19th Issue (2018) 139 19.54. What are the chief factors of a finite group in which every 2-maximal subgroup is not m-maximal for any m ⩾ 3? A subgroup H of a group G is said to be m-maximal if there is a chain of subgroups H = H 0 < H 1 < . . . < H m−1 < H m = G in which H i is maximal in H i+1 for every i. Note that for every m ⩾ 3 there is a finite group in which some 2-maximal subgroup is m-maximal. V. S. Monakhov 19.56. Let A = {A r | r ∈ Φ} be an elementary carpet of type Φ over a commutative ring K (see 7.28), and let Φ(A) = ⟨x r (A r ) | r ∈ Φ⟩ be its carpet subgroup. Define the closure of the carpet A to be the set of additive subgroups A = {A r | r ∈ Φ}, where A r = {t ∈ K | x r (t) ∈ Φ(A)}. Is the closure A of a carpet A always a carpet? An affirmative answer is known if Φ = A l , D l , E l . Ya. N. Nuzhin 19.62. Let A = {A r | r ∈ Φ} be an elementary carpet of type l ⩾ 2 (see P Φ of rank 7.28). For p ∈ Φ, define a set of additive subgroups B p = C ij,rs A ir A js , where the sum is taken over all natural numbers i, j and roots r, s ∈ Φ such that ir + js = p. It is known that the set B = {B p | p ∈ Φ} is a carpet called the derived carpet of A. It is also known that for Φ = A l the set B is a closed (admissible) carpet, which means that its carpet subgroup does not contain new root elements. Is every derived carpet of type Φ over a commutative ring closed (admissible)?
