Let G be a residually finite 2-group and let x ∈ G be a left 3-Engel element of order 2. Is ⟨x G ⟩ locally nilpotent? It is known that in any group a 3-Engel element of odd order belongs to the locally nilpotent radical (E. Jabara, G. Traustason, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc., 147, no. 5 (2019), 1921–1927). Comment of 2025: An element a ∈ G is called a strong left 3-Engel element if ⟨a, a g ⟩ is nilpotent of class at most 2 and ⟨a, a g , a h ⟩ is nilpotent of class at most 3 for all g, h ∈ G. (This is equivalent to a being left 3-Engel when a is of odd order.) It is proved that if a is a strong left 3-Engel element in an arbitrary group G, then ⟨a⟩ G is locally nilpotent (A.
