20th Issue (2022) 161 20.92. This question is about existence of an analogue of the Lazard correspondence for pre-Lie algebras and braces. A pre-Lie algebra A is a vector space with a bilinear operation (x, y) → xy satisfying (xy)z − x(yz) = (yx)z − y(xz) for every x, y, z ∈ A. A pre-Lie algebra A is said to be left nilpotent if, for some n ∈ N, A·(A·(A · · · A))) = 0 where A appears n times in the product. Recall that a set A with binary operations + and ◦ is a left brace if (A, +) is an abelian group, (A, ◦) is a group, and a ◦ (b + c) + a = a ◦ b + a ◦ c for every a, b, c ∈ A. Let p be a prime, and F p the field of p elements. A left brace A is called an F p -brace if its additive group is an F p -vector space such that a ∗ (αb) = α(a ∗ b) for all a, b ∈ A, α ∈ F p , where a ∗ b = a ◦ b − a − b. The idea of a connection between braces and pre-Lie algebras comes from a paper by W. Rump (2014). a) Let A be an F p -brace of cardinality p k for some k.
