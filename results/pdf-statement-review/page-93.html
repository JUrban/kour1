Plotkin 15.76. b) If Θ is a variety of groups, then let Θ 0 denote the category of all free groups of finite rank in Θ. It is proved (G. Mashevitzky, B. Plotkin, E. Plotkin J. Algebra, 282 (2004), 490–512) that if Θ is the variety of all groups, then every automorphism of the category Θ 0 is an inner one. The same is true if Θ is the variety of all nilpotent groups of class ⩽ d, for d ⩾ 2 (A. Tsurkov, Int. J. Algebra Comput., 17 (2007), 1273–1281). Is this true for the varieties of solvable groups? of metabelian groups? An automorphism φ of a category is called inner if it is isomorphic to the identity automorphism. Let s : 1 → φ be a function defining this isomorphism. Then for every object A we have an isomorphism s A : A → φ(A) and for any morphism of objects µ : A → B we have φ(µ) = s B µs −1 A . B. I. Plotkin 15.77. Elements a, b of a group G are said to be symmetric with respect to an element g ∈ G if a = gb −1 g. Let G be an infinite abelian group, α a cardinal, α < |G|. Is it true that for any n-colouring χ : G → {0, 1, . . . , n − 1} there exists a monochrome subset of cardinality α that is symmetric with respect to some element of G? This is known to be true for n ⩽ 3. I. V. Protasov 15.78. (R. I. Grigorchuk). Is it true that for any n-colouring of a free group of any rank there exists a monochrome subset that is symmetric with respect to some element of the group? This is true for n = 2. I. V. Protasov ∗ 15.80. A sequence {F } of pairwise disjoint finite subsets of a topological group is n called expansive if for every open subset U there is a number m such that F n ∩ U ̸ = ∅ for all n > m. Suppose that a group G can be partitioned into countably many dense subsets. Is it true that in G there exists an expansive sequence? I. V. Protasov ∗ Not necessarily. One example is G = Q R under the box topology. It is well- N known that G does not have a countable dense subset (hence has no expansive se- quence). However, the topology on G has a basis having 2 ℵ 0 elements, each element of the basis having 2 ℵ 0 points, and one can partition G into countably many dense subsets (via a transfinite induction of length 2 ℵ 0 ). (Letter of S. Corson of 19 May 2025.) Another example: any infinite abelian group G with finite set of elements of order 2 can be partitioned into countably many subsets dense in every non-discrete group topology on G according to Corollary 12.21 in (Y. Zelenyuk, Ultrafilters and topologies on groups, De Gruyter, 2011) (Letter of I. V. Protasov of 19 May 2025).
