                                           1    Introduction

                                           Let S be a finite set. A Coxeter matrix over S is a square matrix M = (ms,t )s,t∈S indexed by the
                                           elements of S, with coefficients in N ∪ {∞}, such that ms,s = 1 for all s ∈ S, and ms,t = mt,s ⩾ 2 for
                                           all s, t ∈ S, s ̸= t. Such a matrix is usually represented by a labeled graph, Γ, called a Coxeter graph,
                                           defined by the following data. The set of vertices of Γ is S. Two vertices s, t ∈ S are connected by an
                                           edge if ms,t ⩾ 3, and this edge is labeled with ms,t if ms,t ⩾ 4.
                                           If a, b are two letters and m is an integer ⩾ 2, then we denote by Π(a, b, m) the alternating word aba. . .
                                                                                              m                                     m−1
                                           of length m. In other words, Π(a, b, m) = (ab) 2 if m is even, and Π(a, b, m) = (ab) 2 a if m is odd.
                                           Let Γ be a Coxeter graph and let M = (ms,t )s,t∈S be its Coxeter matrix. With Γ we associate a group,
                                           A[Γ], called the Artin group of Γ, defined by the presentation
                                                           A[Γ] = ⟨S | Π(s, t, ms,t ) = Π(t, s, ms,t ) for s, t ∈ S , s ̸= t , ms,t ̸= ∞⟩ .
                                           The Coxeter group of Γ, denoted by W[Γ], is the quotient of A[Γ] by the relations s2 = 1, s ∈ S. We
                                           say that Γ is of spherical type if W[Γ] is finite.
                                           The natural problem of determining automorphism groups of Artin groups has been solved only for cer-
                                           tain classes: spherical types An [DG81, CC05], Bn [CC05], I2 (m) [GHMR00, CP02], D4 [Sor21], and
                                           Dn for n ⩾ 6 [CP25]; affine types A en and C
                                                                                      e n [CC05]; some 2-dimensional Artin groups [Cri05, AC23];
                                           large-type free-of-infinity Artin groups [Vas25]; some other classes of large-type Artin groups [BMV25,
                                           HOV24]; and right-angled Artin groups, for which a uniform description and finite presentation of au-
                                           tomorphism groups is known [Ser89, Lau95, Day09].
                                           In this paper we fill in the missing case n = 5 in the Dn sequence, i.e. we classify automorphisms of
                                           the Artin group A[D5 ] with the Coxeter graph given in Figure 1 on the left.
                                           Let t1 , . . . , t5 denote the standard generators of A[D5 ], and let χ be the inversion automorphism of
                                           A[D5 ], defined on the generators as χ(ti ) = ti−1 , for i = 1, . . . , 5. Let Z(G) denote the center of a
                                           group G. We prove the following theorems.

Theorem 1 Let A = A[D5 ] and C2 = Z/2Z. Then
                                 Aut(A) = Inn(A) ⋊ ⟨χ⟩ ≃ A/Z(A) ⋊ C2 .
In particular, Out(A) ≃ C2 .

Theorem 2 Let A = A[D5 ], A = A/Z(A), and χ be the automorphism induced by χ. Then
                                 Aut(A) = Inn(A) ⋊ ⟨χ⟩ ≃ A/Z(A) ⋊ C2 .
In particular, Out(A) ≃ C2 .

Thus the descriptions of Aut(A[D5 ]) and of Aut(A[D5 ]/Z(A[D5 ])) have the same form as the correspond-
ing descriptions obtained by Castel and Paris for A[Dn ] with odd n ⩾ 6; see [CP25, Corollary 2.6 (2)].
This paper is a continuation of the series of articles [CP25, PS25a, PS25b] devoted to classification of
automorphisms and endomorphisms of Artin groups that admit geometric representations as subgroups
of mapping class groups of surfaces. The key ingredients of the proofs are descriptions of homomor-
phisms from braid groups to themselves and to mapping class groups, obtained in a series of earlier
works [Cas09, Cas16, CKM23, Ore24].
Our paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we prove that the kernel of the natural quotient map
A[D5 ] → A[A4 ] is fully characteristic, and use the Crisp–Paris description of automorphisms preserving
this kernel to prove Theorem 1. In Section 3 we prove Theorem 2. The main point is to show that a lift
of an automorphism of A[D5 ]/Z(A[D5 ]) can be modified by a central transvection so as to become an
automorphism of A[D5 ]. Finally, in Section 4 we apply Theorem 1 to fill the missing type D5 case in
a result of Jones–Mangioni–Sartori [JMS26] on the strong twist conjecture.

If G is a group and g ∈ G, then we denote by conjg : G → G, h 7→ ghg−1 , the conjugation by
g. We say that two homomorphisms φ1 , φ2 : G → H are conjugate if there exists h ∈ H such that
φ2 = conjh ◦φ1 . A homomorphism φ : G → H is called cyclic if its image is a cyclic subgroup of H .
If G is either A[A4 ] or A[D5 ], then a homomorphism φ : G → H is cyclic if and only if there exists
h ∈ H such that all standard generators of G are sent by φ to h.

Lemma 3 Let φ : A[D5 ] → A[A4 ] be a surjective homomorphism. Then φ is conjugate either to π or
to π ◦ χ. In particular, Ker(φ) = F .
4                                                                                      L Paris and I Soroko


Proof By [CP25, Theorem 2.1], an arbitrary homomorphism φ : A[D5 ] → A[A4 ] has one of the two
forms:
    (1) either φ is cyclic, or
    (2) there exist ψ ∈ ⟨χ⟩ and p ∈ Z such that φ is conjugate to αp ◦ ψ .
Since φ is surjective, case (1) is impossible, so φ is conjugate to αp ◦ ψ for some p ∈ Z and ψ ∈ ⟨χ⟩.
Since ψ is an automorphism of A[D5 ], it does not affect surjectivity of φ, hence it is enough to analyze
when αp is surjective. Arguing as in the proof of [CP25, Corollary 2.5], define the exponent sum
homomorphism ℓ : A[A4 ] → Z which sends si 7→ 1 for all i. It is known that ∆2 = (s1 s2 s3 s4 )5
and hence ℓ(∆2 ) = 20. In particular, ℓ(αp (ti )) = ℓ(sj ∆2p ) = 1 + 20p, for all i, j. Therefore,
Im(ℓ ◦ αp ) ⊆ (1 + 20p)Z. Since αp is assumed to be surjective, this is only possible if p = 0. Hence φ
is conjugate to α0 ◦ ψ , which is equal to either π or π ◦ χ. Since π ◦ χ = χA ◦ π , and since conjugation
in the codomain does not change the kernel, we conclude that Ker φ = Ker π = F .

Recall that a subgroup N of a group G is called characteristic if for every automorphism Φ ∈ Aut(G)
we have Φ(N) = N , and N is called fully characteristic if for every endomorphism φ ∈ End(G) we
have φ(N) ⊆ N .

Lemma 4 Let π : A[D5 ] → A[A4 ] be the epimorphism defined above, and let F = Ker π . Then F is
fully characteristic in A[D5 ].

Proof Let φ : A[D5 ] → A[D5 ] be an arbitrary endomorphism, and consider λ = π ◦ φ : A[D5 ] →
A[A4 ]. By [CP25, Theorem 2.1], either λ is cyclic, or λ is conjugate to αp ◦ ψ for some p ∈ Z and
ψ ∈ ⟨χ⟩.
Suppose first that λ is not cyclic. Write αp = γp ◦ π , and observe that γp is injective. Indeed, consider
again the exponent-sum homomorphism ℓ : A[A4 ] → Z, which sends si 7→ 1 for all i. Since ∆2 is
central, for any g ∈ A[A4 ] we have γp (g) = g∆2p ℓ(g) . Suppose that γp (g) = 1. Then g = ∆−2p ℓ(g) .
Applying ℓ and using ℓ(∆2 ) = 20, we get ℓ(g) = −20p ℓ(g). Hence (1 + 20p) ℓ(g) = 0, so ℓ(g) = 0.
Hence g = ∆−2p·0 = 1, and γp is injective.
We have: λ is conjugate to γp ◦ π ◦ ψ , γp is injective, ψ(F) = F (since ψ ∈ ⟨χ⟩ and π ◦ χ = χA ◦ π ),
and conjugating the image does not change the kernel. Therefore Ker λ = Ker π = F . On the other
hand, Ker λ = Ker(π ◦ φ) = φ−1 (F). Hence φ−1 (F) = F , and, in particular, φ(F) ⊆ F .
Suppose now that λ is cyclic. Then there exists h ∈ A[A4 ] such that λ(ti ) = h for all i. In particular,
λ(t5 t4−1 ) = 1. The subgroup F = Ker π is the normal closure of t5 t4−1 in A[D5 ], since π is the quotient
map obtained by identifying t4 and t5 . Therefore λ(F) = 1, i.e. π(φ(F)) = 1, so φ(F) ⊆ F .
Thus every endomorphism of A[D5 ] preserves F .

Proof of Theorem 1 Crisp and Paris proved in [CP05, Theorem 4.9] that the subgroup Aut(A[D5 ], F)
consisting of all automorphisms of A[D5 ] preserving F , has the form:
                       Aut(A[D5 ], F) = Inn(A[D5 ]) ⋊ ⟨χ⟩ ≃ A[D5 ]/Z(A[D5 ]) ⋊ C2 .
It follows from Lemma 4 that the subgroup F is characteristic, hence Aut(A[D5 ], F) = Aut(A[D5 ]),
and the theorem follows.

Remark 5 In addition to automorphisms, Castel and Paris also give a classification of all endomor-
phisms of A[Dn ] for n ⩾ 6. Their proof combines two ingredients:
    • a classification of homomorphisms A[Dn ] → A[An−1 ], [CP25, Theorem 2.1],
    • a classification of homomorphisms A[An−1 ] → A[Dn ], [CP25, Theorem 2.2].
The first ingredient is still available for D5 , since [CP25, Theorem 2.1] applies for n ⩾ 5. It is based
on a classification of endomorphisms of A[An−1 ], which is given for n = 5 independently in [CKM23]
and in [Ore24]. However, the second ingredient, Theorem 2.2 of Castel–Paris, essentially uses Castel’s
classification of maps from A[An−1 ] to the mapping class group of the surface of genus ⌊(n − 1)/2⌋
with one or two boundary components, after applying the Perron–Vannier embedding of A[Dn ]. This
classification is available to us only for n ⩾ 6, via the earlier work of Castel [Cas16, Theorem 1]. To
describe endomorphisms of A[D5 ] by the same route, one would need the genus 2 analogue of Castel’s
result: namely, the statement that every non-cyclic homomorphism from A[A4 ] to the mapping class
group of a genus 2 surface with one boundary component is obtained from the Birman–Hilden 4-chain
Dehn twist representation by conjugating with a homeomorphism, applying a central transvection, and
possibly precomposing with the inversion automorphism of A[A4 ]. We do not have a proof of such a
statement at present. However, surjective endomorphisms of A[D5 ] admit a complete description and
are given in Theorem 1, since all of them are automorphisms. Indeed, by the results of Digne [Dig03] and
Cohen–Wales [CW02], Artin groups of spherical type are linear, and, being finitely generated, they are
Hopfian by Malcev’s theorem (which means that every surjective endomorphism is an automorphism).

4    An application

We finish with an application to the strong twist conjecture of Jones–Mangioni–Sartori [JMS26]. We
recall only the terminology needed here. An Artin system is a pair (A, S), where A is a group and
S ⊂ A is an Artin generating set, i.e. S is identified with the standard generating set of an Artin group;
see [JMS26, Definition 2.1]. We denote by ΓS the corresponding defining graph. In this section we
use the convention of [JMS26]: two vertices s, t ∈ S = V(ΓS ) are joined with an edge whenever the
corresponding Artin exponent ms,t is finite, including the case where ms,t = 2, while a missing edge
means ms,t = ∞. This differs from the Coxeter graph convention used earlier in the paper, where edges
labeled by ms,t = 2 are omitted. For X ⊆ S, we denote by AX the subgroup of A generated by X .
The reflection set of (A, S) is RS = {gsg−1 | s ∈ S, g ∈ A}. An elementary twist is the operation defined
in [JMS26, Definition 3.2]: one cuts the defining graph along a separating spherical indecomposable
subgraph and conjugates one side by the corresponding Garside element. Two Artin generating sets are
called twist equivalent if they are related by a finite sequence of elementary twists and conjugations.
The Artin system (A, S) satisfies the strong twist conjecture if every Artin generating set U for A with
RU = RS is twist equivalent to S. A 1-chunk of ΓS is a connected induced subgraph without separating
vertices, maximal with these properties; see [JMS26, Definition 2.11].
Jones–Mangioni–Sartori proved the strong twist conjecture for spherical-type Artin systems of type An ,
Bn for n ⩾ 2, and Dn with n ⩾ 4, n ̸= 5. The case D5 was omitted because the automorphism group
of A[D5 ] was not known [JMS26, Lemma 5.3]. We now fill this gap.

Proposition 7 Let (A, S) be an Artin system of type D5 . Then (A, S) satisfies the strong twist
conjecture.

Proof Let U be an Artin generating set for A such that RU = RS . As in the proof of [JMS26,
Lemma 5.3], the equality RU = RS implies that (A, U) is again of spherical type and has defining graph
of type D5 . Hence there exists an automorphism ψ ∈ Aut(A) such that ψ(S) = U .
Choose an isomorphism from the standard Artin group A[D5 ] onto A which sends the standard gen-
erating set to S. Through this identification, Theorem 1 applies to ψ . Thus ψ is either inner or the
10                                                                                       L Paris and I Soroko


composition of an inner automorphism with the inversion automorphism χS , where χS (s) = s−1 for all
s ∈ S.
The second case is impossible. Indeed, if ψ = conjh ◦χS , then U = {hs−1 h−1 | s ∈ S}. Since
RU = RS , every element of U is conjugate to some element of S. Hence, for each s ∈ S, the element
s−1 is conjugate to some t ∈ S. This contradicts the exponent-sum homomorphism ℓ : A → Z, defined
by ℓ(s) = 1 for all s ∈ S, because ℓ is constant on conjugacy classes, whereas ℓ(s−1 ) = −1 and
ℓ(t) = 1. Therefore ψ is inner. Hence U is conjugate to S, and in particular U is twist equivalent
to S.

Corollary 8 The conclusion of [JMS26, Corollary E] remains true with type D5 included. More
precisely, let (A, S) be an Artin system whose defining graph ΓS is connected. Suppose that, for every
X ⊆ S spanning a 1-chunk in ΓS , the Artin system (AX , X) is one of the types listed in [JMS26,
Corollary E], with item (5) replaced by spherical type An for n ⩾ 2, Bn for n ⩾ 2, or Dn for n ⩾ 4.
Then (A, S) satisfies the strong twist conjecture.

Proof The proof is the same as the proof of [JMS26, Corollary E]. The only missing input for
including type D5 in item (5) was the strong twist conjecture for Artin systems of type D5 , supplied
by Proposition 7.
