## Logical audit

1. The exact supplied question was visually checked on page 14. It
   requires periodicity, not local finiteness or residual finiteness.
   It has no restriction on the size of an involution centralizer.
2. The construction supplies actual nonidentity involutions: the
   initial elliptic C2 subgroups embed in the final quotient.
3. The exponent is 2p, for p an odd prime; consequently an arbitrary
   2-subgroup has exponent at most two.
4. Exponent-two groups are abelian by the displayed two-element
   identity. Two distinct involutions would then give V4. Thus the
   maximal 2-subgroups have order two, including under definitions
   that permit arbitrary infinite periodic 2-subgroups.
5. Exclusion of V4 alone would not prove that an arbitrary 2-subgroup
   is cyclic. The proof also uses the exponent bound; no infinite
   Sylow classification has been silently imported.
6. The definition of O_{2'} uses odd element orders, not odd finite
   group order or local finiteness. Closure of normal 2-prime
   subgroups under products is justified by the quotient and the
   ambient periodicity, then extended to their directed union.
7. The involution survives in G/O. No assertion O=1 is made.
8. One conjugacy class contains all three generators. Therefore each
   involution normally generates the group, even if its cyclic
   subgroup is very small.
9. Centrality of its image makes every conjugate image equal. It
   follows that the entire quotient is generated by one element.
10. Quotients of perfect groups are perfect, since the commutator
    subgroup of the quotient is the image of the commutator subgroup.
    A nontrivial cyclic quotient is impossible. This is the exact
    contradiction and does not assume simplicity.
11. The 10.62 proof establishes perfectness using the prescribed
    odd relation (abc)^p=1. Pair-product relations by themselves
    permit a C2 quotient, as that packet's retained GAP controls show.
12. The proof does not assert finite or abelian involution
    centralizers, a sharply two-transitive action, or a solution of
    15.54. No theorem needing any such hypothesis is used.
13. The group is not locally finite because it is finitely generated
    and infinite. Finite-group centrality theorems are not applied
    to it or to arbitrary finitely generated subgroups.
14. No finite computation certifies this infinite counterexample.
    Existing finite controls check only the elementary dihedral and
    abelianization steps in its dependency. No new computation is
    required for the central-image lemma.
