# 20.18: an exact-exponent obstruction at the prime two

11 September 2026. This is a formulation qualification, excluded from
the 25 possible-new candidate resolutions. The printed statement on
page 150 was checked visually; the image is cached with this note.

The question asks, for every prime p and k>=2, for infinitely many
subquasivarieties of the class-two exponent-p^k variety, each generated
by a finite group whose derived subgroup has exponent p^k and whose
quasivariety has no independent quasiidentity basis.

If the additional derived-subgroup requirement means **exactly** p^k,
the assertion is false for p=2: no such generating group exists.

## Elementary exponent bound

Let G have nilpotency class at most two and satisfy x^n=1 for every
x in G. Write [y,x]=y^-1 x^-1 y x. Since all commutators are central,
induction, moving the final x past n copies of y, gives

    (xy)^n = x^n y^n [y,x]^(n(n-1)/2).

Thus each commutator c satisfies both c^n=1 and
c^(n(n-1)/2)=1. Bezout's identity gives

    order(c) divides gcd(n,n(n-1)/2).

For n=2^k this greatest common divisor is 2^(k-1), since 2^k-1
is odd. The derived subgroup is generated by these central
commutators; a product of commuting elements each killed by
2^(k-1) is again killed by that integer. Consequently

    exp(G') divides 2^(k-1).

This proof does not require G to be finite or finitely generated.
It applies whether the ambient exponent is required to equal 2^k
or merely to divide it. In either case exp(G') cannot equal 2^k.
Already p=2,k=2 contradicts the universal exact-exponent assertion.

The bound is sharp. Put m=2^(k-1) and take UT_3(Z/mZ), with coordinates
(a,b,c) and multiplication

    (a,b,c)(a',b',c') = (a+a', b+b', c+c'+ab').

Its derived subgroup is all (0,0,c), of exponent m. Every element
has 2m-th power one. The element (1,1,0) has m-th power
(0,0,m(m-1)/2), which is the nonzero central element with last
coordinate m/2 modulo m. Its order is therefore 2m=2^k.
Here m is even because k>=2.

For odd n, the same greatest common divisor is n, so this argument
does not reduce the possible derived exponent. For example UT_3(Z/p^kZ)
has both group and derived-subgroup exponent p^k for odd p. The
odd-prime independent-basis question is not resolved here.

## Convention and prior source

In a variety, “exponent p^k” ordinarily specifies the identity
x^(p^k)=1 and hence means exponent dividing p^k. If the later
derived-subgroup phrase also means only “dividing p^k”, it is a
redundant condition, and the obstruction above does not refute that
different reading. No unprinted restriction p odd is assumed.

There is an especially relevant prior result of Budkin,
*On the quasivarieties generated by a finite group and lacking any
independent bases of quasi-identities*, Siberian Math. J. 61(6)
(2020), 983–993, [primary journal record](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/smj6049),
[DOI 10.1134/S0037446620060038](https://doi.org/10.1134/S0037446620060038).
The primary abstract defines a smaller class-two exponent-p^k
variety by additionally imposing derived exponent p. It states that
for every k>=2 this variety has infinitely many subquasivarieties
generated by finite groups and lacking independent bases.

Those subquasivarieties are also subquasivarieties of the larger
class-two exponent-p^k variety. Thus the abstract already asserts
the affirmative result under the redundant, divisibility-only reading.
The full 2020 proof has not been read or independently audited here;
that source observation is separate from the self-contained negative
exact-exponent argument. The two readings must not be conflated.

## Internal audit and scope

1. Both occurrences of p^k, and “every p and k”, were checked against
   the printed PDF, not inferred from text extraction.
2. Class at most two is sufficient for the collection identity.
3. The order of an individual commutator divides the group exponent,
   including when the exponent is only a bound.
4. The Bezout step uses two actual annihilating integers; it does not
   divide an equality in an arbitrary group.
5. Passing from individual commutators to G' uses centrality, so the
   exponent of a noncommuting product is never assumed bounded.
6. The contradiction is to exact exponent; the convention is explicit.
7. The sharpness example has exact exponent 2^k and exact derived
   exponent 2^(k-1), and is not misreported as a Notebook example.
8. Neither the quasivariety construction nor its independent-basis
   property is needed for the negative exact-exponent argument.
9. Only the primary abstract of the prior Budkin theorem was read.
10. No new priority or odd-prime resolution is claimed. No finite
    computation can replace the all-group argument above.
