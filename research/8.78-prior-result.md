# Problem 8.78: the periodic locally nilpotent case

2026-09-11. Complete affirmative deduction for the last named class
in Problem 8.78 from Maier's published uniqueness theorem. This does
not answer the periodic locally soluble case or classify all classes
with countable universal groups. Excluded from the possible-new count;
outside review remains pending.

## Statement and plan

There is a countable periodic locally nilpotent group U containing
an isomorphic copy of every countable periodic locally nilpotent
group. More generally the same holds after restricting the allowed
primes to any prescribed set pi.

The argument has three steps: embed a countable locally finite
p-group in a countable existentially closed one; invoke uniqueness
of the latter; assemble the universal p-groups by a restricted
direct product. Countability and local nilpotence are checked at
each step. No finite computational experiment is used as evidence
for the universal assertion.

The exact Notebook question on printed page 30 was visually checked
in `references/cache/notebook-page30-878.png`. It asks for embeddings,
not epimorphisms, and explicitly requires a countable ambient group.

## 1. Imported uniqueness theorem

For each prime p there is, up to isomorphism, exactly one countably
infinite existentially closed locally finite p-group E_p.

Here existential closure is relative to the class of locally finite
p-groups: every finite system of group equations and inequations
with coefficients in E_p that is solvable in an overgroup in this
class is already solvable in E_p. Inequations are essential; the
different notion of algebraic closure is not being substituted.

This is B. Maier, *Existenziell abgeschlossene lokal endliche
p-Gruppen*, Archiv der Mathematik 37 (1981), 113--128, Satz 2,
DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01234334 . The original proof has not
been independently read in this session. The exact theorem is
explicitly stated and attributed to Maier in the primary paper
F. Leinen, *Existentially closed LX-groups*, Rendiconti del Seminario
Matematico della Universita di Padova 75 (1986), 191--226, printed
page 192:
https://www.numdam.org/item/RSMUP_1986__75__191_0.pdf .

Leinen's introduction, printed pages 191--194, was read in full;
page 192 was also visually checked because the extracted text loses
some class notation. The general existential-extension assertion
is on page 191 and the Maier bibliographic entry on page 226. Other
selected passages were consulted, but no full-paper proof audit is
claimed. Maier's uniqueness theorem is the sole substantial imported
group-theoretic input to the deduction below.

## 2. Countable existential extensions

Let C_p be the class of locally finite p-groups. It is closed under
subgroups and unions of ascending chains: a finite set in a chain
union lies in one member, and generates a finite p-group there.

For completeness the usual countable construction is as follows.
Start with a countable H in C_p. At an outer stage n, list all finite
systems of equations and inequations with coefficients in the
current countable group H_n. Go through this countable list. At
each inner step, if the listed system is solvable in a C_p-overgroup
of the current group, choose one such solution and adjoin its
finitely many entries. The subgroup generated by the current group
and these entries is again countable and belongs to C_p. If no such
overgroup exists, make no extension at that step. Take the union
of the inner chain to form H_(n+1), and finally take H_infinity as
the union over n. This is an existence construction, with no claim
that the solvability decisions are effective.

The final group is countable and in C_p. If a finite system with
coefficients in H_infinity has a solution in a C_p-overgroup of
H_infinity, all its coefficients occur in some H_n. When that system
was examined at outer stage n, this final overgroup was also an
overgroup of the then-current group. Consequently a solution was
adjoined at that step. Thus H_infinity is existentially closed.

An existentially closed C_p-group cannot be finite. If it were
finite, its direct product with C_p would be an allowed overgroup
and the finite system requiring an element different from every
element of the original group would be solvable only in the
overgroup. Thus the extension above is countably infinite, even
when H is finite.

By the imported uniqueness theorem H_infinity is isomorphic to E_p.
The inclusion H -> H_infinity followed by that isomorphism embeds
H in E_p. Therefore E_p is universal for all countable locally
finite p-groups. No prescribed embedding on a previously chosen
subgroup, or amalgamation property over arbitrary infinite groups,
is needed for this conclusion.

## 3. Decomposition of periodic locally nilpotent groups

Let G be periodic and locally nilpotent. Each finitely generated
subgroup is finitely generated, nilpotent, and torsion, hence finite.
One elementary verification of the last implication uses the lower
central series: its finitely many factors are finitely generated
abelian groups, generated by commutators in a finite generating
set. As torsion quotients they are finite, so their successive
extensions are finite. These finite subgroups are nilpotent.

For each prime p let G_p be the set of elements of p-power order,
including the identity. Given x,y in G_p, the group generated by
x,y is finite nilpotent. Its unique Sylow p-subgroup contains x,y,
and consequently xy and x inverse. Thus G_p is a subgroup. It is
characteristic because element orders are preserved by
automorphisms, and every finite subset generates a finite p-group.

If p and q are distinct, an element of G_p commutes with an element
of G_q: both lie in a finite nilpotent group, where the distinct
Sylow subgroups commute. Every element g of G has finite order and
has its unique commuting primary decomposition inside the cyclic
group generated by g. Uniqueness can also be checked in a finite
nilpotent group containing any proposed finite product of primary
elements. Hence

    G = restricted direct product over primes p of G_p.

When G is countable each G_p is countable. The decomposition uses
finite support element by element, not the unrestricted Cartesian
product of the Sylow subgroups.

## 4. Assembly and embedding

Choose one E_p for each prime p and set

    U = restricted direct product over all primes p of E_p.

There are countably many finite sets of primes, and for each such
set the corresponding finite product of countable groups is
countable. Their union U is countable.

Any finite subset of U has support in finitely many primes. In
each coordinate its entries generate a finite p-group. The subgroup
generated by the original finite set is therefore contained in a
finite direct product of finite p-groups for distinct primes. This
product is finite nilpotent, as is its subgroup. Thus U is periodic
and locally nilpotent.

For a countable periodic locally nilpotent G choose embeddings
i_p:G_p -> E_p from Section 2. The coordinatewise map from the
restricted direct product of the G_p into U is a homomorphism
because distinct primary factors commute. It is injective because
each coordinate map is injective. Section 3 identifies its domain
with G, proving the assertion.

For any set pi of primes, restrict the product to pi and repeat
the same argument. For pi empty the trivial group is universal.

## 5. Internal audit and limits

1. The imported theorem concerns existential closure, including
   inequations, in locally finite p-groups, not arbitrary periodic
   p-groups and not merely algebraically closed groups.
2. The extension construction stays countable and within the
   required class at every inner and outer limit.
3. The final existential test may use coefficients introduced late;
   repeating the outer stages handles every such finite tuple.
4. Uniqueness supplies an isomorphism after the extension is built;
   it is not being assumed to fix any particular earlier subgroup.
5. A periodic finitely generated nilpotent group is finite, which
   justifies the use of finite Sylow decompositions.
6. The primary factors are characteristic subgroups and commute
   across different primes. Finite support is indispensable.
7. The constructed ambient group belongs to the requested class
   and has cardinality aleph_0, rather than continuum.
8. No analogue of Maier's uniqueness theorem for all periodic
   locally soluble groups has been established here. That named
   case of 8.78 remains unresolved in this work.
9. Results denying universal locally nilpotent groups at cardinals
   lambda=lambda^aleph_0 do not cover lambda=aleph_0. Likewise an
   epimorphism-universal group would not suffice for this question.
10. This elementary assembly of a published 1981 theorem is recorded
    as a prior consequence, with no assertion of new priority. The
    packet still awaits outside review.
