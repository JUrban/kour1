# Problem 4.55: a candidate failure of unique projective decomposition

Status: proof draft; independent modular-data verification and prior-work audit
are in progress. Not yet included in the complete-candidate count.

The exact statement on Notebook p.14 was checked visually on 11 September
2026. It asks for uniqueness up to isomorphism and permutation of summands,
not for local endomorphism rings. The counterexample below addresses the
stated uniqueness question, already for finitely generated projectives.

## Statement

Let `G = 3.A7`, the perfect central extension of A7 of order 7560, and let
`R = Z_(5)`. There is a finitely generated projective RG-module with two
decompositions into nonzero indecomposable projectives such that no summand
in one decomposition is isomorphic to a summand in the other.

The proof uses ordinary and 5-modular character data, a rational descent
lemma, and the four rays of `a+d=b+c` in the nonnegative orthant. It does
not assume that rational-valued characters have Schur index one.

## 1. Three elementary facts about lattices

Write `Rhat=Z_5`, `Khat=Q_5` and `k=F_5`.

**Projectivity detected modulo 5.** Suppose L is a finite free R-module
with a G-action and L/5L is projective over kG. Let S be a Sylow 5-subgroup.
Restriction of a projective kG-module to kS is projective. Since kS is a
finite-dimensional local ring, `(L/5L)|S` is free, say of rank d. Lift a
free kS-basis to L to obtain an RS-map `(RS)^d -> L`. Modulo 5 it is an
isomorphism. Nakayama's lemma makes the map surjective, and equal R-ranks
make it injective. Thus L is RS-free. Finally `[G:S]` is a unit in R. The
map `RG tensor_RS L -> L` has an RG-section

`l |-> [G:S]^-1 sum_(t in T) t tensor t^-1 l`,

where T is a left transversal for S. Consequently L is RG-projective.

**Isomorphism detected modulo 5 for projectives.** If P and Q are finite
projective RG-modules with `P/5P ~= Q/5Q`, lift the indicated map through
`Q -> Q/5Q` by projectivity of P. Its cokernel vanishes by Nakayama over
R. It is surjective between free R-modules of the same rank, hence is an
isomorphism.

**Rational descent of a completed projective.** Let M be a finite
projective Rhat G-module. Suppose its generic fibre is the scalar extension
of a QG-module V. Identify `Khat tensor_Q V` with `Khat tensor_Rhat M`.
The rational vectors V are dense in this finite-dimensional Khat-space.
Approximate an Rhat-basis of M by rational vectors `v_1,...,v_s` modulo
`5M`. These vectors are another Rhat-basis of M and a Q-basis of V. Set
`L=sum_i R v_i`. The matrices of each g in this basis have entries both
in Q and in Z_5, hence in R. Therefore L is G-stable, and its completion
is M. In particular `L/5L ~= M/5M` is projective, and the first fact shows
that L is RG-projective.

Here the existence of a rational generic fibre is essential. Merely
lifting composition factors is insufficient; compare the obstruction in
`21.60-review.md`.

## 2. The character data used

All numbering in this section is the local GAP Character Table Library
numbering for `CharacterTable("3.A7")` and its reduction modulo 5. It is
not the numbering across all covering groups in the printed modular Atlas.

The four Frobenius pairs of absolutely irreducible Brauer characters used
are

| Symbol | GAP indices | Degrees in the pair | Degree of the F5-simple |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 9,10 | 3,3 | 6 |
| B | 11,12 | 6,6 | 12 |
| C | 17,18 | 18,18 | 36 |
| D | 19,20 | 21,21 | 42 |

Let `P_A,P_B,P_C,P_D` be their projective covers over F5. Their dimensions
are respectively 90,60,90,90. Finite fields have no nontrivial division
algebras of finite dimension over their centres, so extension to an
algebraic closure splits each cover into the two covers in the indicated
Frobenius pair, each once.

For one of the two faithful central characters, the relevant block of
the decomposition matrix is

| Ordinary character | Degree | A | B | C | D |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| chi_10 | 6 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| chi_16 | 21 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| chi_18 | 21 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| chi_20 | 24 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| chi_22 | 24 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 |

The conjugate block has row indices 11,17,19,23,21, respectively. All other
ordinary rows have zero entries in these eight columns. The ordinary
Galois orbits of the rows occurring here are

`{10,11}, {16,17}, {18,19}, {20,21,22,23}`.

In particular the two degree-21 pairs are different Galois orbits, whereas
the four degree-24 characters form a single orbit.

These data are provided by the [GAP Character Table Library](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Thomas.Breuer/ctbllib/ctbltoc/data/3.A7.html)
and the [published 5-modular decomposition tables](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~MOC/decomposition/tex/A7/A7mod5.pdf),
blocks 9 and 10 on p.1. The ordinary table has also been recomputed from
the actual permutation group `PerfectGroup(IsPermGroup,7560,1)` and found
equivalent to the library table. The independent modular checks are to be
recorded in the review file.

By Brauer reciprocity, the generic ordinary character of a completed
projective with reduction

`P_A^a directsum P_B^b directsum P_C^c directsum P_D^d`

has multiplicities b on the degree-6 pair, d on the first degree-21 pair,
`a+c` on the second degree-21 pair, `a+d` on chi_20 and chi_23, and `b+c`
on chi_21 and chi_22. Thus this character is rational-valued exactly when

`a+d=b+c`.                                                     (1)

Necessity of (1) therefore holds for every finite projective RG-module
whose reduction is supported on these four covers.

## 3. Existence on all four rays, with Schur indices allowed

Idempotents lift over the complete ring Z_5 G (also in matrix rings), so
each P_X lifts to a finite projective Z_5 G-module. We use their direct
sums with multiplicity vectors

`r_1=(1,1,0,0), r_2=(1,0,1,0),`
`r_3=(0,1,0,1), r_4=(0,0,1,1)`.

Each vector satisfies (1), so the generic characters psi_i are
rational-valued. Their degrees are 150,180,150,180.

Set N=168, a common multiple of 6,21 and 24. Each N psi_i is the character
of a QG-module. Here is a direct reason which avoids any Schur-index
assumption. For an ordinary Galois orbit O, the sum of its primitive
complex central idempotents is an idempotent e_O in QG. The rational
module `QG e_O` has complex character

`sum_(chi in O) chi(1) chi`.

All constituents of psi_i have degree 6,21 or 24, with multiplicities
constant on O. Hence N psi_i is a nonnegative integral sum of the
characters of these rational modules. Two semisimple Q_5 G-modules with
the same character over an algebraic closure are isomorphic: the
multiplicities of each simple Q_5 G-module can be recovered after
extension, using the disjoint sets of constituents of different simples.
It follows that the generic fibre of the completed projective with vector
N r_i has a rational form. The descent lemma produces a finite projective
RG-module L_i whose reduction has vector N r_i.

The vectors satisfy `r_1+r_4=r_2+r_3=(1,1,1,1)`. Therefore the second
elementary fact gives

`L_1 directsum L_4 ~= L_2 directsum L_3`.                       (2)

The common R-rank is `168*(150+180)=55,440`.

## 4. Incompatible indecomposable refinements

Every nonzero finite projective RG-module decomposes into finitely many
nonzero indecomposable modules: split successively, using finite positive
R-rank to ensure termination.

Consider any nonzero direct summand U of L_1. Reduction modulo 5 and the
Krull--Schmidt theorem for the finite-dimensional algebra F5G show that
its cover multiplicity vector has support contained in {A,B}. Rationality
of its generic character gives (1), so its vector is `(u,u,0,0)` for an
integer u>0. Positivity follows because a nonzero finite R-projective
cannot have zero reduction. In the same way all nonzero summands of L_i
have vectors on the positive ray through r_i.

These four rays are distinct and meet only at zero. Consequently no
nonzero summand of L_1 or L_4 is isomorphic to a nonzero summand of L_2
or L_3. Refining the two sides of (2) into indecomposables proves the
claimed failure of uniqueness. In particular, the arbitrary-cardinality
version asked in Problem 4.55 also has a negative answer.

## Scope and outstanding audit

This is an existence proof of incompatible finite decompositions with
an explicit group, prime, rank bound, modular multiplicities and rational
generic characters. It does not assert that the L_i themselves are
indecomposable. It does not determine their Schur indices or their
smallest possible ranks. Neither determination is needed in Section 4.

The modular and ordinary character data must be independently checked;
the four-ray linear algebra alone is not a counterexample. Novelty is
not yet established. Johnston--Rumynin's 2025 paper explicitly addresses
their Problem 2 (semiperfectness), and distinguishes their Problems 3
and 4 (the uniqueness statements disproved by this argument).
