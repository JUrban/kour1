# Internal audit of the candidate negative answer to 14.22

Date: 2026-09-11. This is an internal audit, not independent external
review. Novelty remains unconfirmed.

## Exact target

The supplied PDF page 75 was visually inspected. The proposed equality
is Rad_G(T)=sqrt(T), where sqrt(T) is explicitly the least normal isolated
subgroup of G * F(X) containing T. The same fixed variables appear in
S and T. The statement assumes G torsion-free and linear but does not
assume finite generation. Its heading is unstarred. A nearby editorial
star does not label this problem solved.

The counterexamples use G=Q and G=Q * Z, respectively. The latter is
included to meet even a nonabelian CSA interpretation of the coefficient
group. Neither is finitely generated. No result for a strengthened
finitely generated formulation is asserted.

## Potential failure points checked

| Point | Audit |
|---|---|
| Meaning of irreducible | BMR define the topology for arbitrary coefficient groups, using algebraic sets as a closed subbasis, and use topological irreducibility. The Q line is cofinite. In the nonabelian version its centralizer line is also cofinite; this is proved by polynomial specialization. |
| Abelian group versus domain | An irreducible algebraic set over an abelian group need not give an irreducible ideal in the stronger arbitrary-ideal sense. No such equivalence is used. The separate nonabelian example avoids this distinction. |
| T can have new coefficients | All rational syllables of all coefficients of T are included before choosing d. Thus arbitrary additional finite coefficients are covered. |
| Finite equation versus finite system | The starting S is one nontrivial word [x,a_1], although it vanishes identically on Q. The conclusion is disproved for every finite equivalent T, not just for this S or for its finite subsystems. |
| Radical containment direction | A torsion-free quotient killing T gives sqrt(T) contained in its kernel. A nonzero image of the commutator then proves exclusion from sqrt(T), in the correct direction. |
| Isolated versus unique roots | A normal isolated subgroup gives a torsion-free quotient. Such a quotient need not have unique roots. Centralizing a square therefore does not force centralizing its root. |
| Coefficient embedding | Amalgam normal form embeds both factors. In the nonabelian version, taking the additional free product with Z embeds the whole original G, not just its rational subgroup. |
| Target torsion | Every finite-order element in an amalgam is conjugate into a factor; both factors are torsion-free. The added free factor is torsion-free as well. No quotient with torsion is used to establish isolatedness. |
| Nonabelian linearity | An explicit embedding of Q * Z into SL_2(Q(t)) is proved. A cyclically reduced alternating word has trace with positive t-degree and nonzero leading coefficient. |
| Discrimination | The coordinate extension (Q x Z) * Z has a faithful representation over Q(z,t). Every nontrivial element survives all but finitely many rational z-specializations. These maps fix every element of G. |
| Finite coefficient reduction | (C x Z) * Z embeds in (Q x Z) * Z. Thus a T-word trivial in the coordinate group is already trivial in this smaller group, which embeds in the torsion-free separating target. |
| Universal quantifier | The denominator d is chosen for an arbitrary finite T. The escaping rational root 1/(2d) may depend on T. This is exactly what is needed to refute the existence of any suitable finite T. |
| Irreducibility is not matrix irreducibility | The coefficient representation is allowed to be reducible. “Irreducible” in the question modifies the equation system, not the linear representation. |

## Reproducible exact controls

Run:

    python3 scripts/verify_14_22.py

Output: `results/14.22-verification.json`, status PASS.

The verifier compares normal forms in Z^2 * Z against independently
computed matrices over Z[z,t]. It checks all freely reduced words of
length at most six in six signed generators, including the empty word,
and 500 longer deterministic random words: 23,937 words altogether,
65 representing the identity. The 500 long nonidentity words each have
an explicit separating integer specialization. A further 500 exact
calculations verify the trace leading coefficient for alternating words
with upper entries q+n*z and lower entries m*t.

A separate rational-carry normal form for Q *_Z (Z x Z) checks 4,000
associativity and inverse cases, sampled nonzero powers, and 1,000
finite rational coefficient supports. For roots of degrees 2 through
32, the root has the prescribed central power, its commutator with x
has four reduced syllables, and powers 1 through 32 have lengths 4n.
The exact number of sampled power checks is reported by the verifier.

These are controls on the algebra and proof ingredients. They do not
replace the normal form theorem or prove any assertion about all finite
systems by exhaustive search. No probabilistic test is treated as proof.

## Primary-source and novelty audit

1. Kharlampovich--Myasnikov (1998), *Irreducible affine varieties over
   a free group. I: Irreducibility of quadratic equations and
   Nullstellensatz*, J. Algebra 200, 472--516.
   https://web.stevens.edu/algebraic/alexeim/Publications/All_files_new/pp1_34.pdf
   Read introductory definitions through the discrimination criterion
   and the start of the centralizer-extension discussion (author PDF
   pages 3--6). Their sqrt notation is precisely the normal isolated
   closure used here. Their preliminary geometry restricts G to
   nonabelian CSA groups. The paper's full quadratic-equation proof
   was not audited and is not an input to our proof.
2. Baumslag--Myasnikov--Remeslennikov (1999), *Algebraic geometry over
   groups I: Algebraic sets and ideal theory*, J. Algebra 219, 16--79.
   https://web.stevens.edu/algebraic/alexeim/Publications/All_files_new/FINAL_AL_51_38.pdf
   Read Sections 1.6--1.8, the relevant topology statement and abelian
   example in Section 3.1, and all of Section 6. The topology definition
   is for arbitrary G-groups. Section 6 uses ordinary normal closure
   in its version of the Nullstellensatz, unlike the isolated closure
   in the Notebook. Its examples concern algebraically closed groups
   and quadratic equations over hyperbolic groups; no result read
   there supplies or rules out the present counterexamples.
3. Searched combinations of the exact problem number, Kourovka,
   Nullstellensatz, torsion-free linear groups, normal isolated
   subgroups, rational coefficient groups, centralizer extensions,
   and finite presentations. No prior resolution or exact matching
   example was located in this search. The searches were conducted
   on 11 September 2026. This does not prove novelty.
4. Located the abstract of Amaglobeli--Remeslennikov, *G-identities
   and G-varieties* (2000), https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/al276 . It
   concerns reduced G-identities and relatively free groups. Its
   full text has not been read and is not used to assign priority.

Downloaded reading copies of the first two sources and extracted text
are in the ignored `references/cache/` directory, with descriptive
filenames. The public links above preserve provenance in the repository.

## Current conclusion

The proof gives a complete candidate negative answer to the printed
question. The non-finite-generation feature must remain prominent in
any account. Mathematical outside review and a broader literature audit
are still required before describing it as a verified new solution.
