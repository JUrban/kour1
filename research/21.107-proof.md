# A countable counterexample to Problem 21.107

Date: 11 September 2026. Complete candidate proof; novelty and outside
review remain pending. The underlying filter topology is standard.

## Statement

There is a countably infinite Hausdorff topological Boolean group which
is a union of countably many pairwise disjoint dense subsets but admits
no expansive sequence. In fact, for every sequence of pairwise disjoint
finite subsets, some open subgroup misses infinitely many of them.

This gives a negative answer to the exact countable-group question in
Problem 21.107, printed page 183 of the supplied Notebook. As usual,
the open sets in the definition of an expansive sequence are understood
to be nonempty. The construction uses only ZFC, through the existence of
a free ultrafilter on a countable set; no selective ultrafilter or
additional set-theoretic hypothesis is needed.

## 1. The group and its topology

Let N be the positive integers, and fix a free ultrafilter U on N.
Thus U is closed under finite intersections and supersets, contains no
finite set, contains every cofinite set, and contains exactly one of
A and N minus A for each A subset of N. Such a U exists by extending
the cofinite filter to an ultrafilter using Zorn's lemma. The extension
cannot contain a finite set because it also contains that set's
cofinite complement.

Let

    B = [N]^{<omega}

be the group of finite subsets of N under symmetric difference. Its
identity is the empty set. This is the direct sum of countably many
copies of C2, and is countably infinite.

For A in U put

    H_A = [A]^{<omega}.

Use the subgroups H_A as a base of neighborhoods of the identity, and
their cosets as a base for the topology. This is well defined because
H_A intersect H_C = H_(A intersect C). Addition is continuous: for
x, y in B, the sum of the neighborhoods x+H_A and y+H_A is exactly
x+y+H_A. Inversion is the identity map. Hence B is a topological group.

It is Hausdorff. If x differs from y, choose i in their symmetric
difference and take A=N minus {i}. Then A belongs to U and x+H_A,
y+H_A are disjoint neighborhoods. Every H_A is infinite because A is
infinite, so the topology is nondiscrete. The basic subgroups are also
closed: the complement of a subgroup is the union of its other open
cosets.

## 2. An explicit partition into countably many dense sets

For k>=0 let

    D_k = {s in B : v_2(|s|+1)=k},

where v_2 is the exponent of 2 in a positive integer. The sets D_k
partition B, including the empty set in D_0.

Fix k, x in B, and A in U. Choose an integer r>=|x| such that
v_2(r+1)=k; arbitrarily large such integers exist, since
r=2^k(2q+1)-1 has the required valuation. The set A minus x is
infinite. Choose a subset t of A minus x of size r-|x|. Then t is in
H_A, x+t is the disjoint union of x and t, and

    |x+t|=r,       x+t in (x+H_A) intersect D_k.

Every D_k therefore meets every basic open coset and is dense. This
proves exactly the countable resolvability hypothesis of the problem.

## 3. No expansive sequence exists

Let (F_n) be any sequence of pairwise disjoint finite subsets of B.
If infinitely many F_n are empty, the open set B already misses
infinitely many terms. Otherwise discard the finitely many empty terms
and the at most one term containing the identity. We retain an infinite
subsequence of nonempty finite sets of nonempty supports.

For each remaining index n define the nonempty finite subset

    S_n = {max(s) : s in F_n}

of N. For every r, only finitely many indices n have
S_n intersect {1,...,r} nonempty. Indeed, each such F_n contains a
nonempty subset of {1,...,r}, there are only 2^r-1 such subsets, and
the F_n are pairwise disjoint.

It follows recursively that there are increasing indices n_1<n_2<...
for which the finite sets S_(n_j) are pairwise disjoint. More strongly,
after n_j is chosen, take n_(j+1) beyond all indices whose S_n meets
{1,...,max S_(n_j)}. Then

    max S_(n_j) < min S_(n_(j+1)).

Put E = union over even j of S_(n_j). If E belongs to U, take A=E.
Then A misses S_(n_j) for every odd j. If E does not belong to U,
take A=N minus E, which belongs to U; this A misses S_(n_j) for every
even j. In either case there is one A in U such that

    A intersect S_(n_j) is empty for infinitely many j.

For each of those j, no s in F_(n_j) can be a subset of A, since its
maximum lies outside A. Thus

    H_A intersect F_(n_j) is empty for infinitely many j.

The open subgroup H_A is nonempty, since it contains the identity.
Therefore (F_n) is not expansive. The argument covers every proposed
sequence, and completes the proof.

## Scope and provenance

The topology on finite supports generated by a free filter is a standard
linear Boolean group topology. We claim no new underlying group or
topology. The proof above supplies the dense partition and the complete
obstruction to expansive sequences directly, without importing a
selective-separability theorem.

The Boolean case is useful because the map s -> max(s) has finite
fibers: all supports with maximum at most r lie in a finite subgroup.
Pairwise disjointness of the F_n is used at precisely this point.
Replacing finite subsets by arbitrary integer vectors would invalidate
that finite-fiber justification.

Literature scope and independent-style logical checks are recorded in
`21.107-review.md`. No finite computation establishes the ultrafilter
or the universal nonexistence assertion; these are proved above.
