# Problem 21.76: irreducible closed nets that cannot be completed

**Affirmative candidate, pending independent review and priority checks.**
There is such a net of every order n>=3 over k(x,y), for every field k.
In particular this answers the question for every odd characteristic.
A second construction below uses just F9(t) in characteristic three.
Neither argument depends on the candidate solutions of 19.61 or 19.62.

## 1. Definitions and the construction

An elementary net consists of additive subgroups sigma_ij of a field,
for i!=j, satisfying sigma_ik sigma_kj contained in sigma_ij whenever
i,k,j are pairwise distinct. It is irreducible when every level is nonzero.
Write t_ij(a)=I+aE_ij and E(sigma)=<t_ij(a):a in sigma_ij>.
Closedness means t_ij(a) in E(sigma) implies a in sigma_ij.
Completing a net means choosing additive diagonal groups so that the
same product condition holds for all triples of indices, repetitions included.
These are the definitions in 19.48 and 21.76, on printed pages 138 and 178
of the supplied Notebook; the statement image is retained.

Let k be any field and let x,y be algebraically independent over k.
Put R=k[x,y], K=k(x,y), J=yR, and define

    sigma_12 = sigma_21 = kx + J,
    sigma_ij = J                 for all other i!=j.                 (1)

All these groups contain the nonzero ideal J. For pairwise distinct
i,k,j, at least one of the positions (i,k),(k,j) is outside {(1,2),(2,1)}:
two successive exceptional positions would return to i. Therefore
sigma_ik sigma_kj is contained in RJ=J, which is contained in sigma_ij.
Thus (1) is an irreducible elementary net of every order n>=3.

## 2. The closed rank-one pair

**Lemma.** In SL2(k(x)), put

    H = <U(a),V(b): a,b in k>,
    U(a) = [[1,ax],[0,1]],    V(b) = [[1,0],[bx,1]].

Then H intersects either elementary root subgroup precisely in the
corresponding displayed subgroup: its upper and lower parameters are kx.

**Proof.** Every element of H is represented by a word in U(a),V(b).
The identities U(a)U(b)=U(a+b), V(a)V(b)=V(a+b), together with deletion
of zero parameters, reduce it to an alternating word with all parameters
nonzero, or the empty word. No free-product theorem is needed here.

For an alternating word of length m>=2 starting with U, write its factors
as I+c_s x N_s, where c_s is nonzero and N_s alternates E12,E21.
Expand the product by choosing either I or c_s x N_s in each factor.

If m is even, the product of all m chosen N_s is E11. It gives the unique
term of degree m in entry (1,1), with coefficient c_1...c_m != 0.
That diagonal entry therefore differs from 1.

If m is odd, the full product of the N_s is E12, so it has no diagonal
entry. A contribution of degree m-1 must omit exactly one N_s. Omitting
the last gives E11 with coefficient c_1...c_(m-1). Omitting the first
gives E22. Omitting an interior factor leaves two adjacent equal matrix
units in the chosen product; their product is zero. Thus entry (1,1)
has degree exactly m-1 with nonzero leading coefficient. It again
differs from 1. For a word starting with V the same argument uses entry
(2,2), interchanging E12 and E21.

Consequently no alternating word of length at least two is an elementary
transvection, since such a matrix has both diagonal entries equal to 1.
The empty word is the identity, and a word of length one has parameter
in kx in its own root subgroup. This proves the lemma, including root
parameters that were initially allowed to lie anywhere in k(x). QED.

This lemma is a special case of Koibaev's 2011 *Closed pairs* theorem,
which treats arbitrary additive subgroups of the polynomials with zero
constant term. The elementary proof above is supplied so that the present
candidate does not import that theorem's proof.

## 3. Reduction proves closedness of the irreducible net

Reduction at y=0 is a ring homomorphism pi:R -> k[x]. Under the induced
matrix homomorphism, every generator of E(sigma) outside positions (1,2)
and (2,1) maps to the identity. The remaining generators map to the
rank-one group H of the lemma in the upper-left block. In particular,

    pi(E(sigma)) = {diag(h,I_(n-2)): h in H}.                        (2)

Let t_ij(a) belong to E(sigma), initially with a in K. Every generator
and its inverse has entries in R, so E(sigma) is contained in SL_n(R).
The (i,j) matrix entry then gives a in R. This justifies applying pi
to the parameter; no evaluation map on the whole rational-function
field K is being asserted.

If (i,j) is outside {(1,2),(2,1)}, (2) implies pi(a)=0, so a in J.
For the two exceptional positions, the lemma and (2) imply pi(a) in kx.
Then a is in kx+J. In either case a belongs to sigma_ij. Hence sigma is
closed over K.

## 4. No diagonal completion exists

Suppose diagonal additive groups completed sigma to a full net tau.
Since x belongs to sigma_12 and sigma_21, the full-net product rules give

    x^2 in tau_11,          x^3 in tau_11 tau_12 contained in sigma_12.

But reduction at y=0 would imply x^3 in kx, which is impossible because
x is transcendental over k. This contradiction proves noncompletability.

Taking k=F_p for any odd prime p proves the asserted affirmative answer.
The same construction also works in characteristic zero and two; those
existence cases were already known and are not counted separately.

## 5. A construction with one variable in characteristic three

Let F=F3[j], where j^2=-1=2, so F has nine elements. Put

    U = [[1,1],[0,1]],       V = [[1,0],[j,1]],       H0=<U,V>.

The explicit finite certificate in `21.76-finite-table.md` proves
|H0|=120. It exhibits 40 representatives r_i for the cosets <U>r_i,
gives all transitions r_i U=U^e r_k and r_i V=U^e r_k, and supplies
generator words for the representatives. The 120 matrices U^e r_i
are distinct. Since U,V have order three, invariance under their right
multiplication is also invariance under their inverses. The set contains
the identity, so it contains H0; the representative words give the
opposite inclusion. Thus the finite table proves the order exactly.

Each root subgroup of SL2(F) has order nine. Its intersection with H0
has order dividing both 9 and 120, hence at most three. The upper one
contains <U> and the lower one contains <V>. Therefore the parameters
of these intersections are exactly F3 and jF3, respectively. Direct
checks of all nine parameters in both roots independently confirm this.

For every n>=3 put R0=F[t], K0=F(t), J0=tR0, and define

    rho_12=F3+J0,       rho_21=jF3+J0,
    rho_ij=J0            for every other i!=j.                       (3)

The same pairwise-distinct-index argument proves the elementary-net
condition, and J0 proves irreducibility. Any elementary transvection in
E(rho) has parameter in R0. Reduction at t=0 lies in diag(H0,I_(n-2)),
so the root intersections just established imply its parameter belongs
to its original level. Thus rho is closed. A full completion would
force 1*j*1=j into rho_12, whereas j is not in F3+J0. Hence (3) cannot
be completed either.

The finite-field certificate is checked by a separate implementation
using 4-by-4 matrices over F3 and by GAP. No identification of H0 with
a named finite group, subgroup classification, or simplicity assertion
is needed for this proof.

## Scope and priority

The original question permits an arbitrary field of odd characteristic;
it does not impose a transcendence-degree restriction. Construction (1)
gives every odd characteristic. Construction (3) already suffices for the
literal existential question and uses transcendence degree one.
The known polynomial-pair lemma is credited above. The reduction argument
and its application to irreducible nets are the proposed resolution;
their prior publication has not been ruled out. See `21.76-source-audit.md`
and `21.76-report.md` for the exact audit scope and reproducible evidence.
