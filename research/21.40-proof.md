# 21.40: rational linear groups with finitely many automorphism orbits

Status: complete candidate affirmative answer, with a stronger conclusion.
Outside review and further novelty checks remain pending. The supplied
Notebook page 173 was visually checked: no finite-generation assumption
is imposed on G, and the coefficient field is Q.

**Theorem.** Let n>=1 and let G be a subgroup of GL(n,Q). If Aut(G) has
finitely many orbits on G, then G has a normal torsion-free nilpotent
subgroup U of class at most n-1 and index at most (2n+1)^(n^2).
In particular, G is virtually soluble.

For n=1 the indicated nilpotent subgroup is trivial. The proof uses
abstract group automorphisms; they need not arise from conjugations of
matrices or extend to automorphisms of their rational linear span.

## 1. Powers in one orbit give arbitrarily deep roots

Fix g in G. Among g, g^2, g^4, ... there are two powers in the same
Aut(G)-orbit. Choose integers 0<=i<j and alpha in Aut(G) such that

    alpha(g^(2^i)) = g^(2^j).

Put h=g^(2^i) and r=2^(j-i)>1. Then alpha(h)=h^r. Since automorphisms
commute with taking powers, alpha^k(h)=h^(r^k) for every k>=1. Applying
alpha^(-k) to this identity gives

    X_k = alpha^(-k)(h) in G,        X_k^(r^k)=h.             (1)

This reasoning also applies when g has finite order. The matrices X_k
need not be simultaneously diagonalizable or commute with one another.

## 2. Bounded-degree roots force roots of unity

We prove an elementary matrix lemma.

**Lemma.** Suppose h belongs to GL(n,Q), r>1 is an integer, and h has
an r^k-th root in GL(n,Q) for every k>=1. Then every eigenvalue of h
is a root of unity.

**Proof.** Fix an eigenvalue lambda of h in the algebraic closure of Q.
For each k, spectral mapping gives an eigenvalue mu_k of X_k with

    mu_k^(r^k)=lambda,             [Q(mu_k):Q]<=n.           (2)

Choose a positive integer D for which Dh has integer entries. Then
D lambda is an algebraic integer. Let M be the maximum of 1 and the
complex absolute values of all eigenvalues of h.

Each Q-conjugate mu'_k of mu_k satisfies that (mu'_k)^(r^k) is a
Q-conjugate of lambda, and hence is also an eigenvalue of h. It follows
that |mu'_k|<=M. Furthermore,

    (D mu_k)^(r^k)=D^(r^k-1)(D lambda)

is an algebraic integer. Therefore D mu_k is an algebraic integer:
it is integral over the ring of algebraic integers by the displayed
monic equation, and integrality is transitive over Z.

There are only finitely many algebraic integers of degree at most n
all of whose conjugates have absolute value at most DM. Indeed, the
monic minimal polynomial of one of degree d<=n has integer coefficients,
and its coefficient of degree d-s has absolute value at most
binomial(d,s)(DM)^s. There are only finitely many such polynomials,
and each has finitely many roots.

Consequently the numbers mu_k range over a finite set. There are
k<l with mu_k=mu_l=mu. Equation (2) gives

    mu^(r^l-r^k)=1.

As lambda=mu^(r^k), lambda is a root of unity. This holds for every
eigenvalue of h and proves the lemma. QED.

Apply the lemma to (1). Since h is a positive power of g, every
eigenvalue of g is also a root of unity. This conclusion holds for
every g in G. In particular, tr(g) is both rational and an algebraic
integer, so it is an integer; its absolute value is at most n. Thus

    tr(G) is contained in {-n,-n+1,...,n}.                  (3)

The denominator bound above concerns algebraic eigenvalues after a
fixed scaling. There is no asserted bound on denominators of the
entries of the root matrices X_k. Unipotent rational matrices can have
root towers with unbounded entry denominators.

## 3. A finite trace set gives a nilpotent kernel of finite index

Let A=span_Q(G), a unital associative subalgebra of M_n(Q). Its dimension
d is at most n^2. Choose a vector-space basis g_1,...,g_d from G, and set

    R = {a in A : tr(ab)=0 for every b in A}.

The cyclic property of matrix trace shows that R is a two-sided ideal:
if a is in R and b,c are in A, then

    tr((ca)b)=tr(a(bc))=0,      tr((ac)b)=tr(a(cb))=0.

Every a in R is nilpotent. In fact, tr(a^s)=0 for all s>=1, since
a^(s-1) belongs to A, including s=1 because 1 belongs to A. Newton's
identities in characteristic zero imply that the characteristic
polynomial of a is t^n. Cayley--Hamilton then gives a^n=0.

We give the required uniform ideal bound R^n=0 explicitly. Let V=Q^n
with its natural faithful A-action. The spaces

    V, RV, R^2 V, ...

are a descending chain of A-submodules. Suppose W=R^j V=R^(j+1) V
is nonzero, so W=RW. Choose a generating list w_1,...,w_s for W as
an A-module with s as small as possible. Such a finite list exists
because W is finite-dimensional over Q. Expanding W=RW in these
generators, and using that R is an ideal, gives

    w_s = a_1 w_1 + ... + a_s w_s,      a_i in R.

The element 1-a_s is invertible in A, with inverse given by a finite
geometric series, because a_s is nilpotent. Hence w_s is in the
A-span of w_1,...,w_(s-1), contradicting minimality. Therefore the
descending chain cannot stabilize at a nonzero space. Its dimension
starts at n, so R^n V=0. The faithful action gives R^n=0.

It follows that 1+R is a group and that

    [1+R^a,1+R^b] is contained in 1+R^(a+b)    (a,b>=1).

For the inclusion, pass to the quotient algebra modulo R^(a+b),
where the two elements commute, and lift back. Thus 1+R is nilpotent
of class at most n-1. It is torsion-free: a finite-order element
1+a, with a nilpotent, has minimal polynomial dividing both (t-1)^n
and t^m-1 for some m>=1. In characteristic zero their common monic
divisor is t-1, so 1+a=1.

Define U=G intersect (1+R). This is the kernel of the homomorphism
G -> (A/R)^*, and hence is normal in G. It is torsion-free nilpotent
of the required class. To bound the index, associate to g in G the tuple

    (tr(gg_1),...,tr(gg_d)).                               (4)

Every product gg_i belongs to G. By (3), at most (2n+1)^d tuples occur.
Two elements g,h give the same tuple if and only if g-h belongs to R,
because the g_i form a basis. Since R is an ideal and h^-1 belongs
to A, this is equivalent to gh^-1-1 belonging to R, or to gh^-1 in U.
The tuples therefore distinguish exactly the cosets of U. Consequently

    [G:U] <= (2n+1)^d <= (2n+1)^(n^2).

This proves the theorem. QED.

## Scope and checks

The proof does not assume G finitely generated or residually finite.
It does not assert that G itself is soluble or torsion-free. For example,
A5 times the additive group Q is a nonsoluble infinite rational linear
group with finitely many automorphism orbits, and has Q as a normal
abelian subgroup of index 60. A concrete matrix model and the trace
radical are among the exact controls recorded in the accompanying audit.

By restriction of scalars, the same conclusion of virtual torsion-free
nilpotence holds over every number field K: embed GL(n,K) in
GL(n[K:Q],Q) and apply the theorem. No claim over arbitrary fields of
characteristic zero is needed for the printed question.

The argument is self-contained apart from elementary linear algebra
(spectral mapping, Newton identities, and Cayley--Hamilton) and elementary
integrality of algebraic numbers. Computer checks are controls on
examples and on tempting stronger claims, not the proof of the theorem.
See `research/21.40-review.md` for those checks and the exact scope of
the literature inspected.
