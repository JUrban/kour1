# Problem 19.61: closure is a carpet and preserves the derived carpet

12 September 2026. Complete affirmative candidate, subject to outside
review and priority checking.

**Theorem.** Let A be an elementary carpet over a commutative ring K,
let H=E(Phi,A) be its carpet group, and put

    D_r = {t in K : x_r(t) belongs to H}.

Then D is a carpet. Moreover, its derived carpet is the derived carpet
of A. There is no restriction on the characteristic of K.

The argument uses the known square closedness criterion and 300 short
integer certificates in root systems A2, B2 and G2. The certificates
prove an elementary adjoining lemma. An explicit countable completion
then places D between two additive families with the same derived
carpet, which proves the result. No assumption that arbitrary
root-generated subgroups have carpet root intersections is made.

## One adjoining step

For any family F of additive subgroups, write partial(F) for the family
additively generated, at each destination root p, by all Chevalley
commutator parameters c u^i v^j with i r+j s=p and u in F_r, v in F_s.
Using absolute constants c makes no difference to these subgroups.
If F is a carpet, then partial(F) is contained in F. Monotonicity of
partial holds for arbitrary families.

**Lemma.** Let A be a carpet and B=partial(A). Fix a root r, a in A_r
and b in A_-r. Replace A_r by A_r+Z a^2b, leaving every other subgroup
unchanged, to obtain A'. Then partial(A')=B and A' is a carpet.

To prove the lemma, consider any ordered Chevalley rule with destination
p=i r+j s. When its first input is changed from x to x+n a^2b, its
parameter expands as

    c (x+n a^2b)^i u^j
       = sum_(k=0)^i c binomial(i,k) n^k x^(i-k) a^(2k) b^k u^j.   (1)

The k=0 term belongs to B_p by definition. For each k>0 the certified
implication is

    c binomial(i,k) x^(i-k) a^(2k) b^k u^j belongs to B_p,           (2)

where x,a belong independently to A_r, b belongs to A_-r and u belongs
to A_s. Integer multiplication by n^k is allowed in B_p, including
when n is negative. The corresponding expansion is checked separately
when the second input of each original ordered rule is changed. Its
original constant is retained: merely reversing a G2 source pair can
change an absolute constant. Nontrivial commutator rules have distinct,
nonopposite sources, so at most one input subgroup of a rule changes
in this adjoining step.

All the root labels in (2) lie in the plane of r,s. Intersecting that
plane with Phi gives an irreducible reduced crystallographic root
system A2, B2 or G2. Reflections preserve the plane, and every ambient
root string between its roots remains in the plane. Restricting A
therefore preserves the carpet rules and their absolute constants.
Every derived element formed in the restriction belongs to the original
B. Consequently it suffices to check (2) in those three systems.

The complete certificate totals are:

| Type | Targets | Integer derivation nodes | Largest derivation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A2 | 12 | 72 | 6 |
| B2 | 48 | 380 | 12 |
| G2 | 240 | 2,739 | 19 |
| Total | **300** | **3,191** | **19** |

All targets pass. Every initial certificate node is one of the variables
in its specified A subgroup. From f in A_alpha and g in A_beta, an
applicable Chevalley rule gives c f^i g^j in B_(i alpha+j beta), and
hence in A at that root. Two such applications with the same monomial
and integer coefficients u,v give their integer gcd times that monomial
in B, by Bezout. Every noninitial node records and verifies these two
applications. The final node is noninitial and its coefficient divides
the coefficient requested in (2). Thus these are universal integer
proofs, valid in K without division by two, three or any other integer.

Equations (1) and (2) give partial(A') contained in B. Monotonicity
gives the reverse inclusion. Since B is contained in A and hence A',
every defining commutator parameter for A' belongs to its designated
A' subgroup. This is exactly the carpet condition, proving the lemma.

## Completion without changing the derived family

Set C^(0)=A and define, for n>=0,

    C^(n+1)_r = C^(n)_r
                 + <u^2 v : u in C^(n)_r, v in C^(n)_-r>_Z.        (3)

Each C^(n) is a carpet with derived family B. For the induction step,
adjoin any finite collection of the generators on the right side of
(3), one at a time. The parameters used belong to C^(n), so they are
still available at every intermediate step. The lemma shows that each
finite extension is a carpet with derived family B. These finite
extensions form a directed family: adjoining the union of two finite
lists contains both. Their union is C^(n+1).

A directed union of carpets is a carpet, because the finitely many
elements in any particular carpet condition lie in a common member.
Its derived family is the union of the corresponding derived families:
each derived generator uses only two elements, and each additive sum
uses finitely many generators. Here every one of those families is B.
This proves the induction assertion, including equality of derived
families, without a uniform bound on the number of adjoining steps.

Now set C_r=union_(n>=0) C^(n)_r. The same directed-union argument shows
that C is a carpet and partial(C)=B. For u in C_r and v in C_-r, choose
a common stage n containing both. Equation (3) places u^2v in
C^(n+1)_r, so

    C_r^2 C_-r is contained in C_r for every r.                    (4)

The known square closedness criterion makes C closed. A direct proof
of that criterion, valid over arbitrary commutative rings and for all
symplectic ranks, is supplied in `19.62-closedness-criterion.md`.

## The root closure lies between A and C

Since A is contained in C, H is contained in E(Phi,C). Closedness of C
implies that x_r(t) in H forces t in C_r. Thus

    A is contained in D, and D is contained in C.                  (5)

Each D_r is an additive subgroup, by the root-group addition law and
inverses. For any Chevalley commutator parameter built from elements
of D, (5) places that parameter in partial(C)=B, which is contained
in A and hence in D. Thus D is a carpet. Monotonicity also gives

    B=partial(A) contained in partial(D)
       contained in partial(C)=B,

proving the asserted equality of derived carpets.

In rank one there are no nonopposite commutator conditions, so every
additive family, including D, is a carpet and its derived family is
zero. The substantive proof above handles every irreducible type of
rank at least two, the setting of the Notebook.

The completion C need not equal the actual root closure D. The proof
uses only (5) and partial(C)=B. It does not assert that the newly
adjoined root elements in (3) already belong to H.

## Computational and literature audit

The target generator uses binomial coefficients. Its independent
coverage checker instead multiplies the sparse polynomial
(X0+X1^2 X2)^i X3^j repeatedly, doing this for both inputs of every
ordered rule. This covers 416 substitutions and 600 nonconstant terms,
which give the 300 distinct targets after variable renaming. Variables
with equal root labels remain independent. Duplicate target requests
are replaced by their coefficient gcd, a stronger sufficient request.
Twelve deliberately corrupted proofs are rejected.

The exact integral root models and commutator constants come from the
audited 19.62 rank-two packet. Its polynomial identities over Z[t,u]
are replayed. The optimizer is only a proof finder; its algorithm and
finite-ring experiments are not mathematical assumptions. A separate
finite-ring control iterates (3) and compares it to the previously
computed actual group closures.

Troyanskaya's 2024 paper proves the earlier odd-characteristic cases,
excluding characteristic divisible by three in G2:

Its complete mathematical text was read. The square closedness
criterion is Nuzhin's 2026 Theorem 2:

That full article was read and its theorem page inspected visually.
The 2021 Badin--Nuzhin--Troyanskaya abstract concerns separation of
weakly supplemented and supplemented carpet classes; only that
abstract was inspected in this follow-up. None of these source checks
