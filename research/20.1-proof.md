# Problem 20.1: an affirmative answer by a coprime direct product

12 September 2026. Complete candidate, pending independent review and priority checks.

**Theorem.** The finite group \(G=J_1\times C_{13}\) is insoluble and
totally 2-closed, and \(F(G)=C_{13}\ne1\).

We use the published theorem that the first Janko simple group \(J_1\)
is totally 2-closed. The new argument needed for the application is the
following elementary product lemma. No computational result is used in
the proof.

**Pair criterion.** For a faithful permutation group \(X\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)\),
a permutation \(s\) belongs to its 2-closure precisely when, for every
\(\alpha,\beta\in\Omega\), there is \(x\in X\) such that
\(\alpha^s=\alpha^x\) and \(\beta^s=\beta^x\).
Indeed, this is exactly preservation of each orbit of \(X\) on ordered
pairs. In particular, every such \(s\) preserves each point orbit, by
considering \((\alpha,\alpha)\). We use right actions throughout.

**Lemma 1 (finite versus arbitrary sets).** For a finite group, being
2-closed in every finite faithful action implies being 2-closed in
every faithful action on an arbitrary set.

Let a nontrivial finite group \(X\) act faithfully on \(\Omega\). For each
nonidentity element choose a point it moves, and let \(\Omega_0\) be the
union of the orbits of these finitely many points. This is a finite,
faithful, invariant subset. If \(s\in X^{(2),\Omega}\), the pair criterion
shows that \(s\) preserves \(\Omega_0\) and restricts to its 2-closure.
Thus it agrees there with a unique \(x\in X\). For each \(\alpha\in\Omega\),
apply the same argument to the finite faithful invariant set
\(\Omega_0\cup\alpha^X\). The resulting element agrees with \(x\) on
\(\Omega_0\), so equals \(x\) by faithfulness. Consequently \(s=x\)
everywhere. The trivial group is immediate from the pair criterion.

**Lemma 2 (coprime products).** If finite groups \(A,B\) are totally
2-closed and \(\gcd(|A|,|B|)=1\), then \(A\times B\) is totally 2-closed.

Take any faithful action of \(X=A\times B\) on \(\Omega\). First, for
every \(\alpha\in\Omega\),

\[
\alpha^A\cap\alpha^B=\{\alpha\}. \tag{1}
\]

To prove this, suppose \(\alpha^a=\alpha^b\), where \(a\in A,b\in B\).
Then \(ab^{-1}\) fixes \(\alpha\). The orders of \(a,b\) are coprime,
and they commute. The Chinese remainder theorem supplies an integer
\(m\) with \(m\equiv1\pmod{|a|}\) and \(m\equiv0\pmod{|b|}\), giving
\((ab^{-1})^m=a\). Thus \(a\) also fixes \(\alpha\), proving (1).

Let \(\Delta_B\) denote the set of \(B\)-orbits on \(\Omega\).
Normality of \(B\) gives an induced \(X\)-action on \(\Delta_B\), on
which \(B\) acts trivially. The resulting \(A\)-action is faithful:
if \(a\in A\) fixes every \(B\)-orbit setwise, then
\(\alpha^a\in\alpha^A\cap\alpha^B\) for every \(\alpha\). By (1),
\(a\) fixes every point, hence \(a=1\). Likewise \(B\) acts faithfully
on the set \(\Delta_A\) of \(A\)-orbits.

Now take \(s\in X^{(2),\Omega}\). The equivalence relation of lying in
the same \(B\)-orbit is \(X\)-invariant, since \(B\) is normal.
It is therefore a union of \(X\)-orbits on ordered pairs. Since \(s\)
preserves each of those orbits, it permutes the \(B\)-orbits and induces
a permutation \(s_B\) of \(\Delta_B\).

For arbitrary \(D,E\in\Delta_B\), choose \(\alpha\in D,\beta\in E\).
The pair criterion gives \(ab\in X\) agreeing with \(s\) on
\(\alpha,\beta\). Both permutations preserve the orbit partition, so

\[
D^{s_B}=D^a,\qquad E^{s_B}=E^a.
\]

Thus \(s_B\) lies in the 2-closure of the faithful \(A\)-action on
\(\Delta_B\). Total 2-closure of \(A\) supplies a single \(a_0\in A\)
inducing \(s_B\) on the whole quotient set. The same argument, with
the factors interchanged, supplies \(b_0\in B\) inducing the action
of \(s\) on \(\Delta_A\).

Since \(B\) acts trivially on \(\Delta_B\), and \(A\) trivially on
\(\Delta_A\), the permutation \(s(a_0b_0)^{-1}\) fixes each orbit of
each factor setwise. Equation (1) now implies that it fixes every
point. Hence \(s=a_0b_0\in X\), proving the lemma. This uses neither
transitivity of the action nor finiteness of \(\Omega\); Lemma 1 also
allows either finite-set convention in the imported hypotheses.

**Lemma 3.** A cyclic group of prime order is totally 2-closed.

A faithful action of \(C_p\) has a nonfixed point \(\alpha\), whose
stabilizer is trivial. For \(s\) in the 2-closure, preservation of
\(\alpha^{C_p}\) gives a unique \(c\in C_p\) with
\(\alpha^s=\alpha^c\). For any \(\beta\), the pair criterion on
\((\alpha,\beta)\) forces the witnessing element to be this same
\(c\). Thus \(\beta^s=\beta^c\) for every \(\beta\), as required.

**Proof of the theorem.** Arezoomand, Iranmanesh, Praeger and Tracey,
*Totally 2-closed finite groups with trivial Fitting subgroup*,
Theorem 1.1 (also Proposition 4.8), proves that \(J_1\) is totally
2-closed. Its order is

\[
|J_1|=175560=2^3\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\cdot19.
\]

By Lemmas 2 and 3, \(G=J_1\times C_{13}\) is totally 2-closed.
It is finite, of order \(2282280\), and insoluble because it has the
nonabelian simple quotient \(J_1\).

The subgroup \(C_{13}\) is central and nilpotent, so lies in \(F(G)\).
Conversely, any normal nilpotent subgroup of \(G\) projects to a
normal nilpotent subgroup of \(J_1\). Simplicity forces that projection
to be trivial: a nonabelian finite simple group cannot be nilpotent.
Thus every normal nilpotent subgroup lies in \(C_{13}\), and
\(F(G)=C_{13}\). This answers the printed existence question.

More generally the same proof gives \(J_1\times C_p\) for every prime
\(p\nmid175560\). No minimal-order or direct-indecomposability claim
is needed or made.
