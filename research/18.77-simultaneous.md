# Stronger partial: simultaneous detection

12 September 2026. The class-two case is already Isaacs--Passman (1968),
Theorem 1.5, including this stronger assertion. The derivation below is
retained as an algebraic proof and as a partial for groups of class less
than p. Priority of the latter deduction has not been established.

**Theorem.** Let G be a finite p-group of class less than p, and let
p^e be its largest irreducible character degree, with e<p. For any
p-e nonidentity elements g_1,...,g_(p-e), some irreducible representation
of degree p^e has none of those elements in its kernel.

The same conclusion holds for all class-two p-groups, by the elementary
character-algebra application in `18.77-partial-proof.md`. This second
assertion is the cited prior theorem.

We prove a strengthening of the alternating-form lemma in that file.
Keep R=Z/p^k, the finite parameter group D and its additive family M_d.
Let
ell_1,...,ell_t be nonzero homomorphisms D -> F_p, where t<=p-e.
There is a parameter d with maximum image order and with every ell_i(d)
nonzero.

Choose generators of D and the resulting surjection Z^m -> D. Lift the
coefficient matrices of the family to integral alternating matrices.
Choose a parameter u attaining the maximum and make a fixed congruence
change putting its matrix modulo p^k in alternating normal form, with
r nonzero blocks p^(a_i) J. Then r<=e and s=sum a_i=rk-e. The case e=0
uses r=s=0 and the empty Pfaffian one. Let P(X_1,...,X_m) be the
Pfaffian of the leading 2r-submatrix of the linear family after this
congruence change and coefficient lifts. This is homogeneous of degree r.

At u the valuation of P is exactly s. For clarity, this remains true
for any integral lift of the normal form: in its alternating reduction
over Z_p the first r block valuations are a_i<k, so their sum is s.
Equivalently, the difference from the canonical Pfaffian has valuation
at least k+sum_(i<r) a_i > s, after ordering a_i increasingly.

At every integral parameter x, inequality (1) in the companion proof
gives v_p(P(x))>=rk-e=s. The degree in each variable is at most r<p.
Multivariate interpolation on {0,...,p-1}^m, one variable at a time,
therefore shows that every coefficient is divisible by p^s. Write
F=(P/p^s) mod p. It is a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree r,
since its value at u mod p is nonzero. If F(x mod p)!=0, the same
inequality forces e_x>=rk-s=e, so x is a maximum parameter.

The ell_i pull back to nonzero linear forms over F_p. Hence

    H(X)=F(X) product_(i=1)^t ell_i(X)

is a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree r+t<=e+t<=p. Such a
polynomial cannot vanish at every point of F_p^m. Indeed, if its degree
is less than p, it has degree less than p in each variable and the
usual induction using the root bound applies. At homogeneous degree p,
the only monomials with an exponent at least p are the pure powers
X_j^p. Replacing them by X_j gives the unique reduced polynomial
representing the same function, of degree less than p in every variable.
Those linear monomials cannot cancel any of the remaining monomials,
which all have degree p. Thus the reduced polynomial is nonzero and
cannot vanish identically. The constant case is immediate.

Choose x with H(x)!=0. Its image d in D is maximum and avoids all
specified kernels, proving the strengthened algebraic lemma.

For the group application, any nonidentity element g in a nilpotent
group gives a nonidentity central iterated commutator (or is already
central): take its least upper-central-series position and successively
choose a commutator lowering that position without becoming trivial.
Raise the resulting central element to obtain a central element z of
order p. Every normal subgroup containing g contains z. It therefore
suffices to detect the resulting central z_i simultaneously.

For the Lazard Lie ring L, the conditions lambda(z_i)!=1 correspond to
nonzero homomorphisms D=Hom(L,C^*) -> F_p. Apply the strengthened lemma
and the Kirillov central-character formula from the companion proof.
The class-two application uses D=Hom(Z(G),C^*) in the same way.
For an abelian group one can directly apply the degree-zero case, so
p=2 and the possible zero-dimensional Lie ring cause no exception.

This result still leaves general finite p-groups outside the class
hypotheses untreated. Isaacs--Passman's Proposition 1.4 already shows
that the number p-e cannot be increased in general, even in class two.
