# 20.52: exhaustive bounds from the commuting probability

The question asks whether every positive integer m occurs as
(|G|-k(G))/16 for a nonabelian finite group of odd order.

For such a group put z=|Z(G)| and n=|G|. Every noncentral conjugacy
class has at least three elements, so k(G)<=z+(n-z)/3. Moreover G/Z(G)
is noncyclic; since its order is odd, its order is at least9. Therefore
z<=n/9 and k(G)<=11n/27. Consequently |G|-k(G)=16m forces n<=27m.
This makes nonexistence for any fixed m a finite classification problem.

Initial experiment: enumerate every SmallGrp group of every odd order
from3 through1727 and calculate k(G). All orders are within the
library's complete range. This covers every possible example for m<=64,
because the largest allowable order1728 is even. Retain every group ID,
class number and difference; assert the elementary bound on every
nonabelian input. Confirm any missing m independently before claiming
a resolution.

Motivation for64: fixed-point-free C3 actions on an abelian group A give
(|A:C3|-k(A:C3))/16=(|A|-1)/6. This realizes m=1,2,4,8,16,32 with
|A|=7,13,25,49,97,193. At m=64, the analogous kernel order385 contains
5 and11 to odd powers and does not permit a fixed-point-free automorphism
of order3. This failure of one construction is not nonexistence of G.

Subsequent reading found that Buckley--MacHale2013 already supplies

This is retained as a bounded verification, not a new partial theorem.
The general question remains unresolved here.
