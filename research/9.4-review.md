# Problem 9.4: internal mathematical review

This is an internal review, not an independent referee report.
The candidate uses no unproved structural existence theorem.

1. **Exact quantifier.** The claim in the Notebook concerns one finite
   generating quasigroup for the entire isotope class. The witness is
   allowed to depend on the proposed generator's size.
2. **Nontrivial source.** M generated by C2 alone suffices. Every witness
   group is a finite direct power of that very group.
3. **Term validity.** t_N is an ordinary two-variable multiplication
   term with N nested left multiplications. It is not a group power.
4. **Permutation bound.** Every cycle of a permutation on n points has
   length dividing n!, even when n is composite or n=1.
5. **No named parameters.** The universal identity contains no symbol
   for 0. Testing x at the additive zero is a legitimate assignment.
6. **Finite witness.** m=n!+1 is finite and >=2, and 2^m is finite.
   No asymptotic or finite approximation claim enters the contradiction.
7. **Isotopy direction.** From Q_m to A_m the bijections id,T,id obey
   alpha(a)+beta(b)=gamma(a*b). This is precisely an isotopy.
8. **Division signs.** The formulas use subtraction over general F_p.
   Direct substitution verifies both left-division and right-division
   laws. The binary map is Latin without an associativity assumption.
9. **Failure.** T^N(e_0)=e_N for m=N+1 and N>=1, so the two sides
   of the proposed identity are distinct basis vectors.
10. **Variety preservation.** Every identity of a generating algebra
    holds under H, S and P, independently of whether the class of all
    group isotopes has already been proved to be a variety.
11. **Quasivariety preservation.** Identities are quasi-identities;
    only subgroup and product closure is used on the group side.
    There is no erroneous appeal to quotient closure of quasivarieties.
12. **Pseudovariety preservation.** All counterexamples are finite;
    ordinary identities are preserved under the finite HSP operations.
13. **Finite families.** Their maximum size supplies the same n! law.
    No assertion of a uniform identity for all finite quasigroups is made.
14. **Arbitrary finite G.** An element of order d>1 yields C_p by
    taking a power for a prime p dividing d. Cauchy's theorem is not
    needed, and p need not equal two.
15. **Infinite backup.** The shift on the direct sum indexed by Z is
    bijective, and e_0\\e_0=0. Both positive and negative shifts and
    addition are generated by the three quasigroup operations.
16. **Local finiteness.** Equality of term functions on Q is exactly
    the identity relation defining its generated variety. Thus the
    bound |Q|^(|Q|^k) proves finiteness, without a finite-basis claim.
17. **Signature boundary.** The same obstruction works in the
    multiplication reduct, but not with iterated ill-typed expressions
    in a three-sorted algebra. Original Gvaramiya Theorem 8 and the
    modern account both use ordinary quasigroups for this premise.
18. **Other notion of generation.** If generation is defined inside
    the lattice of isotope-closed varieties, identities of a generator
    need not survive the added isotopies. That is a different problem;
    the supplied statement does not specify that convention.
19. **Computations.** Exhaustive Latin-square controls test the
    factorial identity independently of the affine construction.
    Integer-coordinate products and table-derived divisions check
    conventions independently. The proof for arbitrary n is elementary
    and does not extrapolate from those checks.
20. **Priority.** The limited searches found no explicit prior answer.
    This is not evidence enough to assert novelty. The simplicity of
    the argument makes further priority and formulation review
    particularly appropriate. No one has been contacted.

Disposition: complete negative candidate for ordinary variety,
quasivariety and pseudovariety generation as stated in the Notebook.
Independent review and priority remain pending.
