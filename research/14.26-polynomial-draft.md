# Restricted wreath products: candidate local nilpotent models

Working derivation, 11 September2026. Not frozen or counted.

Let C be the class of torsion-free nilpotent groups. The proposed theorem
is that Q(C) is closed under restricted wreath products with Z.

## 1. A finite algebra with prescribed shifted zero products

Fix integers c>=1, R>=1 and a finite nonempty set I of integer positions,
of size M. Choose m>=2R(c-1)+M-1. Let V have rational basis
a_0,...,a_m. On the symmetric algebra Sym(V) put

    D(a_j)=a_(j+1) (j<m),  D(a_m)=0,
    f(t)=sum_(j=0)^m (t^j/j!) a_j = exp(tD)a_0.

Let J be the homogeneous ideal generated by all coefficient vectors
of f(t)f(t+d), d=1,...,R. Quotient further by all homogeneous terms
of ordinary a-degree>c. Call the resulting finite-dimensional unital
commutative rational algebra A; write A_+ for its positive-degree ideal.

D preserves J because D(f(t)f(t+d)) is its derivative in t, so the
derivative's coefficients are scalar multiples of the same coefficients.
D also preserves the degree cutoff and descends to A. It is nilpotent
on A: on every homogeneous degree<=c it raises the sum of the indices
j, which is bounded by cm. Therefore exp(sD) is a polynomial algebra
automorphism, and exp(sD)f(t)=f(t+s).

In A we have f(i)f(j)=0 whenever 0<|i-j|<=R. This follows by
substitution into a defining polynomial and commutativity.

For each 1<=k<=c, the elements f(i)^k, i in I, are linearly
independent in A. Here is a proposed exact dual certificate.

Pair Sym^k(V) with symmetric rational polynomials Q(t_1,...,t_k)
of degree at most m in each variable, using

    Q(t_1,...,t_k)=ell(f(t_1)...f(t_k)).

This identifies symmetric k-linear forms with such polynomials;
factorials are nonzero over Q. A functional kills the degree-k part
of J if Q(t,t+d,t_3,...,t_k)=0 identically for each d=1,...,R.
Indeed, the vectors f(t_3),...,f(t_k) span V by Vandermonde, and
extracting coefficients in t supplies every generating relation.

Define

    Delta_k(t_1,...,t_k)
      = product_(a<b) product_(d=1)^R ((t_a-t_b)^2-d^2).

It is symmetric, has degree 2R(k-1) in each variable, kills the
required specializations, and has the same nonzero rational value
C_k=product_(a<b) product_(d=1)^R (-d^2) on every diagonal.
For each i in I let L_i(z) be the Lagrange polynomial with
L_i(j)=delta_ij for j in I. Then

    Q_i=Delta_k * L_i((t_1+...+t_k)/k) / C_k

has degree at most 2R(k-1)+M-1<=m in each variable and satisfies
Q_i(j,...,j)=delta_ij. It descends to A_k and separates f(i)^k.
For k=1 the empty Delta product and C_1 are one. The degree cutoff
introduces no further degree-k relation. This proves independence.

## 2. Unitriangular copies and the shift: direct matrix construction

There is no need for a Lie semidirect product or BCH here. Put
U=UT_(c+1)(A_+), the matrices with diagonal1 and entries in A_+
above the diagonal. Let alpha=exp(D), acting entrywise on U, and
form H=U semidirect Z, with t u t^-1=alpha(u).

For g in UT_(c+1)(Q), define rho_i(g) by

    (rho_i(g))_(a,b) = g_(a,b) f(i)^(b-a), a<b,

and diagonal1. This is a group homomorphism: exponents add in every
term of ordinary matrix multiplication. It is injective because
f(i)^k is nonzero for each 1<=k<=c. Equivariance is immediate:
alpha(rho_i(g))=rho_(i+1)(g).

If distinct i,j have |i-j|<=R, then

    (rho_i(g)-I)(rho_j(h)-I)=0
      =(rho_j(h)-I)(rho_i(g)-I),

since each scalar product contains f(i)f(j). Thus the two copies
commute. For a set I of diameter<=R, their product is exactly

    product_(i in I) rho_i(g_i) = I+sum_(i in I)(rho_i(g_i)-I).

Independence of f(i)^k for each superdiagonal k makes this direct
product map injective.

The group H is torsion-free and nilpotent. Here are elementary details.
Let U_j consist of matrices whose first j-1 superdiagonals vanish.
Then U=U_1>...>U_(c+1)=1, and U_j/U_(j+1) is an additive direct
sum of c+1-j copies of A_+. Conjugation by U is trivial on this
factor; t acts by alpha. Since D is nilpotent on A_+, delta=alpha-1
is nilpotent there. Choose L with delta^L=0. Between U_j and
U_(j+1), take inverse images of

    A_+^(c+1-j) > delta A_+^(c+1-j) > ... > delta^L A_+^(c+1-j)=0.

Every successive factor is central in H: U acts trivially and the
commutator with t moves it one step farther. These inverse images
are H-normal subgroups because delta commutes with alpha. Together
with H/U=Z, they give a central series of length at most cL+1.

A finite-order element of H maps trivially to Z, hence lies in U.
In U, its first nonzero superdiagonal would be multiplied by its
integer order upon taking that power, impossible in a rational
vector space. Thus H is torsion-free. Its quotient by U is exactly
Z, and t projects to1.

This avoids the earlier false temptation to descend the index-weight
grading through the nonhomogeneous index relations. Only ordinary
a-degree and the genuine superdiagonal filtration are used.

## 3. Local embedding of a wreath product

First take a finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent base N, embedded
in some UT_(c+1)(Q). The classical embedding theorem needs a primary
source or a proof. Use the left-shift convention

    (f,n)(h,m)=(f * shift^n(h),n+m),
    shift(h)(i)=h(i-1),

where f,h:Z->N have finite support. Fix a finite subset S of this
wreath product, including1. Collect all positions occurring in the
supports of its elements, and all positions in shifted supports used
in products u v=w with u,v,w in S. This is a finite set I; enlarge
R to at least its diameter and1. For those finite elements define

    Psi(f,n)=(product_i rho_i(f(i))) t^n.

All required rearrangements take place among copies indexed in I
and hence commute. Equivariance makes Psi respect every prescribed
product u v=w. The t exponent separates different n. For equal n,
the injective product over I separates different base functions.
Thus Psi is a local embedding of S into a torsion-free nilpotent group.

For a non-finitely-generated base in C, restrict to the finitely
generated subgroup of N containing all coordinates used in S.

## 4. Passing from C to Q(C)

A group in Q(C) has every finite partial multiplication table locally
embeddable in a member of C, since C is closed under finite products.
Proof: for each inequality between two named elements, the finite
equations of the table must have a solution in a C-group separating
that pair; otherwise the corresponding quasi-identity would hold in
C and fail in the group. Take the finite product of these separating
solutions. Include the identity and the required inverse equations.

For a finite part S of G wr Z with G in Q(C), collect a finite base
table containing all its coordinate values and the products needed
for relations u v=w among S, together with every inequality needed
to separate the coordinate functions. Locally embed this base table
in one N in C, and replace its coordinates. This locally embeds S
into N wr Z. Section3 then locally embeds its image into C.

Since every finite part has such an embedding, G wr Z satisfies
every quasi-identity of C. This is exactly membership in Q(C).

## Audit flags

- Check the exact direct/restricted convention and novelty.
- Section2 now uses actual unitriangular matrices and a refined
  superdiagonal central series. No index-weight grading, Engel theorem
  or BCH semidirect construction is used.
- The source group need not be positively graded. Its given matrix
  entries are scaled by powers according to their superdiagonal.
- The construction gives local embeddings, not global homomorphisms
  from the entire wreath product into a nilpotent group.
- No finite sample can replace the all-parameter dual-polynomial proof.
