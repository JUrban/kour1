# Problem 13.53: locally graded p-groups are already covered

Checked 11 September 2026. Prior partial theorem, no new count. The
general two-torsion-generator question remains unresolved here.

Sozutov--Alexandrova (2017), Theorem 2 and Corollary 1, prove the
stronger assertion that if G=<a,b> is an infinite locally graded
p-group, then <a,b^g> is infinite for every g in G. Here locally
graded means that every infinite finitely generated subgroup has a
proper subgroup of finite index. This restriction is substantive.

## Short proof of the cited special case

Fix g and set H=<a,b^g>. For any finite quotient Q of G, the group Q
is a finite p-group. Conjugating b does not change its image modulo
the Frattini subgroup Phi(Q), since Q/Phi(Q) is abelian. Thus the
images of a and b^g span Q/Phi(Q), as do a and b. The finite p-group
Burnside basis theorem implies that H maps onto Q.

Finite quotients of G have unbounded orders. Indeed, start with G_0=G.
Every G_i of finite index in G is infinite and finitely generated
(Schreier's theorem). Local gradedness gives a proper finite-index
subgroup G_(i+1) of G_i. The indices [G:G_i] grow at least as 2^i.
Taking the core K_i of G_i in G gives a finite quotient G/K_i with
order at least [G:G_i]. Since H maps onto every G/K_i, H cannot be
finite. This proves the assertion for every g.

This proof is a simplification of the finite-index/Frattini argument
in the cited paper, not a new theorem. It also explains the obstacle
to extending it: a general finite quotient need not be a p-group,
and changing a generator by conjugation need not preserve generation.

An elementary separate sufficient condition is that C_G(b) is
infinite: then b^g=b and <a,b^g>=G for every g in C_G(b). If C_G(a)
is infinite the same conclusion follows after conjugating the pair.
Neither centralizer need be infinite in the stated general problem.
