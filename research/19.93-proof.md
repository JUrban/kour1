# Candidate negative answer to Problem 19.93

12 September 2026. Complete argument submitted to internal audit;
independent review and priority checks remain pending.

For every prime p>=7 and every integer K there is a finite p-group Q
with exactly two generators, with no quotient isomorphic to C_p wr C_p,
and with a lower p-central factor of order greater than p^K. Thus the
function in the printed conjecture does not exist, already for (p,d)=(7,2).

We use P_1(Q)=Q and P_{i+1}(Q)=P_i(Q)^p[P_i(Q),Q]. The quotient
condition is on Q itself, exactly as printed. No assertion is made
that subgroups of Q avoid wreath-product quotients.

## 1. Three power relations exclude the wreath quotient

Write W=C_p wr C_p as V semidirect <t>, where V=F_p^p and t cyclically
permutes its p coordinates. Let s(v) be their sum. The map

    rho: W -> F_p^2,       rho(v,t^a)=(s(v),a)

is a surjective homomorphism. If a!=0, its action on the p coordinates
is a single cycle, and therefore

    (v,t^a)^p=(s(v)(1,...,1),1).

If a=0, (v,1)^p=1. Consequently u^p=1 if and only if the two coordinates
of rho(u) have product zero.

Suppose u,v generate W and u^p=v^p=1. Their images under the surjective
map rho must form a basis of F_p^2. Each lies on a coordinate axis,
so they lie on different axes and both are nonzero. Their sum has
both coordinates nonzero. Hence (uv)^p!=1.

It follows that any group generated by x,y satisfying

    x^p=y^p=(xy)^p=1                                      (1)

has no epimorphism onto W. This observation works for every prime p.

## 2. A complete filtered algebra of exponential growth

Fix p>=7. Let B=F_p<<X,Y>> be the algebra of noncommuting formal power
series, with m its ideal of series with zero constant coefficient.
Let J be the closed two-sided ideal generated by

    X^p,       Y^p,       (X+Y+XY)^p,

and set A=B/J, A_n=(m^n+J)/J. This is a complete separated filtered
algebra, A_0=A, with A_i A_j contained in A_{i+j}. Each A/A_n has
finite dimension. Put a_n=dim_Fp(A_n/A_{n+1}) and H(t)=sum a_n t^n.

Each defining relation has initial degree p. For the third relation,
its degree-p component is (X+Y)^p, the sum of all 2^p distinct
length-p words, each with coefficient one. In particular this
component is nonzero even in characteristic p. Also J is contained
in m^p, so a_0=1 and a_1=2.

The filtered Golod--Shafarevich inequality gives the coefficientwise
inequality

    (1-2t+3t^p) H(t)/(1-t) >= 1/(1-t).                   (2)

This is the complete filtered version, with the denominator 1-t;
we do not apply the stronger homogeneous version to these relations.
A precise source is Ershov, *Golod--Shafarevich groups: a survey*,
Theorem 2.6 and Proposition 2.7(a), pp.9--10,
https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.0490. This classical inequality is the
only substantial imported theorem in the argument.

For tau=2/3,

    1-2tau+3tau^p <= 1-4/3+3(2/3)^7 = -115/729 < 0.

Thus H(tau) diverges. Indeed, convergence would allow evaluation of
(2), giving a negative left side and a positive right side.

## 3. Finite p-groups spanning the algebra truncations

For N>=2 set R_N=A/A_N and let M_N be the image of A_1 in R_N.
This is a finite-dimensional algebra with R_N=F_p 1 + M_N,
M_N^N=0. The set 1+M_N is a group: multiplication stays in the set,
and a finite geometric series supplies the inverse of 1+z. Its
cardinality is |M_N|, a power of p. Define

    Q_N = <1+X,1+Y> <= 1+M_N,

where X,Y now denote their images in R_N. These generators satisfy
(1), since in characteristic p, (1+z)^p=1+z^p, and
(1+X)(1+Y)=1+(X+Y+XY). Section 1 excludes a wreath quotient of Q_N.

The map from Q_N into (A_1/A_2,+), sending 1+z to z modulo A_2,
is a homomorphism and sends the two displayed generators to a basis
of F_p^2. It is well defined for N>=2 and is surjective. Thus Q_N
requires exactly two generators.

The natural algebra map

    theta_N: F_p[Q_N] -> R_N

is onto. Its image is a unital algebra containing 1+X and 1+Y, hence
X,Y; these generate R_N as an ordinary algebra because series
truncate to polynomials. If I_N is the augmentation ideal of F_p[Q_N],
then theta_N(I_N) is contained in M_N, so theta_N(I_N^N)=0. In particular

    dim R_N <= dim F_p[Q_N]/I_N^N.                       (3)

Neither an identification of A with a completed group algebra nor a
residual-finiteness theorem for triangle groups is needed here.

## 4. Bounded lower p-central factors bound augmentation growth

Let Q be any finite p-group, I the augmentation ideal of F_p[Q], and

    b_i = dim_Fp P_i(Q)/P_{i+1}(Q).

The lower p-central series terminates. For completeness, induction
on |Q| applies to Q/Z(Q); after the image series terminates, the
remaining terms lie in Z(Q) and repeated p-th powers kill them.
Every factor is elementary abelian.

Choose lifts g_{i,1},...,g_{i,b_i} of a basis of each factor, ordered
first by increasing i. Iterating the quotient normal forms shows
that the products

    product_(i,j) g_{i,j}^{e_{i,j}},      0<=e_{i,j}<p,

give every element of Q uniquely. Expanding each g^e=(1+(g-1))^e
in this same fixed order shows that the elements

    product_(i,j) (g_{i,j}-1)^{e_{i,j}},  0<=e_{i,j}<p,   (4)

span F_p[Q]. There is no rearrangement of noncommuting factors in
this expansion.

We claim g-1 belongs to I^i for every g in P_i(Q). This follows
inductively. If h-1 is in I^i, then

    h^p-1=(h-1)^p belongs to I^{ip} subset I^{i+1}.

For g in Q, the identity

    [h,g]-1 = h^{-1}g^{-1}(hg-gh),
    hg-gh = (h-1)(g-1)-(g-1)(h-1)

uses the convention [h,g]=h^{-1}g^{-1}hg and puts the commutator
difference in I^{i+1}. The set of elements with difference in an
ideal is closed under multiplication and inversion, completing
the induction.

Hence (4) lies in I^{sum i e_{i,j}}. Modulo I^N, only products of
weight less than N are needed. If every b_i<=K, and c_n are the
nonnegative integer coefficients of

    P_K(t)=product_(i>=1)(1+t^i+...+t^{(p-1)i})^K,

we obtain the uniform bound

    dim F_p[Q]/I^N <= sum_(n=0)^(N-1) c_n.               (5)

The finite product with b_i copies counts (4); adding unused copies
up to K only increases coefficients. The infinite product is
well defined coefficientwise. It converges at every real 0<t<1,
because

    P_K(t) <= product_(i>=1)(1-t^i)^(-K) < infinity.

For example, -log(1-t^i)<=t^i/(1-t) makes the logarithm of the last
product summable.

## 5. Contradiction and the quantifiers

Suppose that for one integer K every lower p-central factor of
every Q_N had dimension at most K. Combining (3) and (5), for all
N>=2 we would have

    sum_(n=0)^(N-1) a_n = dim R_N
       <= sum_(n=0)^(N-1) c_n.

The same bound holds for N=1, since a_0=c_0=1. In particular,
a_n<=sum_(j=0)^n c_j for every n, and therefore, for 0<t<1,

    H(t) <= P_K(t)/(1-t) < infinity.

This contradicts H(2/3)=infinity. Thus for every integer K some
finite Q_N has a factor of order greater than p^K. Section 3 proves
that all these groups have precisely d=2 and satisfy the quotient
exclusion. Any proposed value f(p,2) would give a uniform integer
K with p^K>=f(p,2), which is impossible. This disproves 19.93.

The construction provides an effective finite family: R_N can be
computed by linear algebra on words of length less than N, and Q_N
by closing the two units under multiplication. The proof establishes
termination of a search for width greater than any given K; it does
not give a practical upper bound on the required N or group order.
