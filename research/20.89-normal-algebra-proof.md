# Almost Engel elements in normal subgroups of algebra unit groups

This is further partial coverage of 20.89 / 19.83, including positive
characteristic. No complete-candidate increment or priority claim is
made. The normal-subgroup hypothesis below is essential to this proof;
an arbitrary linear group has not been shown to satisfy it.

Let A be a finite-dimensional associative unital algebra over a field F.
Write A^* for its unit group, and let G be a normal subgroup of A^*.
Use [x,a]=x^-1 a^-1 x a.

**Theorem.** If F is infinite, the almost Engel elements of G are exactly
its Engel elements. Consequently they form the Hirsch--Plotkin radical
of G. In particular 20.89 has an affirmative answer for every normal
subgroup of GL_n(F), and for SL_n(F), over every infinite field.

## 1. The two-parameter identity

Fix a in G. Put c_t=1+t*a and let T be the set of t in F for which c_t
is a unit. Every c_t commutes with a and normalizes G. If a is almost
Engel, its unique minimal finite sink E is therefore invariant under
conjugation by c_t. Indeed conjugation preserves every iterated
commutator with a and bijects G onto itself. Equivalently, it preserves
the periodic points of x->[x,a], which are exactly E.

Suppose e is in E and two distinct s,t in T give the same conjugate f:

    c_s^-1 e c_s = f = c_t^-1 e c_t.

Multiplying gives

    e+s*e*a = f+s*a*f,       e+t*e*a = f+t*a*f.

Subtract the equations and divide by the nonzero scalar s-t. We obtain
e*a=a*f. Substitution in either original equation gives e=f, and
therefore e*a=a*e. Thus for an e not commuting with a, the map
t -> c_t^-1 e c_t is injective on T.

## 2. Infinitely many units and collapse of the sink

Let d=dim_F(A), and represent A by left multiplication on itself.
This representation is faithful: left multiplication by b sends 1
to b. The polynomial det(I+t*L_a) has degree at most d and value 1
at t=0. It is therefore nonzero and has at most d roots.

Moreover L_b is invertible exactly when b is a unit in A. If L_b is
invertible, choose c with bc=1; injectivity of L_b applied to cb-1
gives cb=1. The reverse implication is immediate. Consequently T
contains all but finitely many elements of F and is infinite.

For each e in the finite set E, two parameters in T give the same
conjugate. Section 1 implies that e commutes with a. Thus commutation
with a sends every member of E to 1. Since the commutator map permutes
its minimal sink, E={1}. This proves that a is Engel in G. The converse
is part of the definition.

The regular representation embeds G into GL_d(F). Gruenberg's theorem
identifies the Engel elements of a linear group with its Hirsch--Plotkin
radical, proving the subgroup assertion. This last classical input is
stated in Shumyatsky, *Almost Engel linear groups*, preliminaries,
[arXiv:1610.03126v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03126v1).

The proof actually needs only that c_t normalizes G for infinitely many
parameters t, separately for each almost Engel a under consideration.
Normality in A^* is a convenient sufficient condition. Embedding an
arbitrary group in A^* does not provide that condition.

## 3. A finite-field lower bound

The same two-parameter identity works over a finite field F_q. Suppose
a has a nontrivial finite minimal sink E relative to G. Some e in E
does not commute with a: otherwise the sink would collapse to {1}
as before. Section 1 supplies |T| distinct conjugates of this e in E.
None is 1, whereas 1 always belongs to E. Therefore

    |E| >= |T|+1 >= q-d+1.

If A is a unital F_q-subalgebra of M_n(F_q), use det(I_n+t*a) instead
of the regular representation to obtain the sharper bound q-n+1.
Invertibility in M_n implies invertibility in A by the characteristic
polynomial, so this change of representation is legitimate.

More precisely, let s(a) be the number of distinct eigenvalues of a
that belong to F_q. Since a is invertible all these eigenvalues are
nonzero, and det(I_n+t*a)=0 holds at exactly their negative reciprocals.
Thus

    E != {1}  implies  |E| >= q-s(a)+1.

This assertion concerns a normal subgroup G of the unit group, not
every finite linear subgroup. It does not assert that every nonscalar
matrix is non-Engel over a finite field. Small general linear groups
have nonscalar elements in their Fitting subgroups.

## 4. Scope of the controls

The verifiers check GL_2(F_p) and its normal subgroup SL_2(F_p) for
p=2,3,5,7, using one representative of every GL_2-conjugacy class
that lies in the group. Such representatives suffice for this
conjugacy-invariant assertion even when an SL_2 class splits. The
classes are scalar, nontrivial Jordan, split semisimple, and irreducible
quadratic companion classes. Each representative's entire finite sink
is computed. For every nontrivial sink, an element not commuting with
the representative has all its parameter conjugates checked for
distinctness and membership in that sink.

GAP uses native finite-field matrices and iterated images of the group.
Python uses integer residue matrices and deletes the noncyclic vertices
of the commutator functional graph. It imports neither group tables nor
sink data from GAP. Finite agreement tests the bound and algebraic
identities; the infinite theorem follows from Sections 1--2.

An additional normality control uses the matrices

    a=[[0,1],[1,0]],       b=[[0,-1],[1,-1]].

They generate S3 over Q, with b of order three and b^a=b^-1. Thus
[b,_n a]=b for every n>=1: a is almost Engel, because the group is
finite, but is not Engel. This disproves dropping the normality or
parameter-normalizer hypothesis from the infinite theorem. Over F7
the same group has a-sink {1,b,b^2} of size three, although five of
the parameters 1+t*a are invertible. The parameter t=2 does not
normalize this S3, and its conjugate of b leaves the sink. Both
verifiers check this explicit failure of the omitted hypothesis.
