# Problem 20.112: saturation of the Sylow-normalizer formation

2026-09-11. Exploratory; no claimed solution.

The source preprint arXiv:1904.06986v1 has been read in full (11 pages).
Its Example 2.1 takes F=N^3 (Fitting height at most three) and a
faithful irreducible extension of S4 in characteristic 3 to show
w*F need not equal F. This does not answer whether w*F is saturated.
Theorem 3.4 and Corollary 3.4.2 settle the smaller F=N^2 case as equality.

Try F=N^3 first, screening the installed groups of orders 648,1296,1944
for G/Phi(G) in w*F but G outside w*F. All are soluble by Burnside.
Only groups outside F with nontrivial Frattini subgroup can be hits.
These orders cover a small first family of potential Frattini lifts;
negative results do not prove saturation.

For a hereditary formation F, F-subnormality of H in G can be tested
by repeatedly replacing the current K by <H,K^F>. If this reaches H,
the chain can be refined to a maximal F-subnormal chain. If it stalls
above H, no maximal F-normal overgroup of H in K exists. Conversely,
an F-subnormal chain restricts to <H,K^F>, so a valid chain must descend.
For F=N^3, K^F is obtained by applying the nilpotent residual three
times; each nilpotent residual is the terminal lower central subgroup.

The GAP screen records residual/normalizer chains for any hit and
requires an actual successful exit and terminal sentinel. A hit needs
an independent subgroup-chain check and an explicit proof before any
candidate status. Maintain the existing six n=7 verifiers undisturbed.
