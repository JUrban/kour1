# A bounded saturation screen for 20.112

2026-09-11. No solution or counterexample obtained.

The class F=N^3 consists of soluble groups of Fitting height at most
three. It is a hereditary saturated formation. The screen checks the
potential saturation failure G/Phi(G) in w*F with G outside w*F.

Thus all 8,339 groups in these three orders pass the tested implication.
Only 12 require both quotient and ambient normalizer computations after
the necessary filters. All are soluble by the two-prime order theorem.

F-subnormality is checked by iterated residual closure, as justified in
`research/20.112-plan.md`; the source's hereditary-formation restriction
lemma supplies the reverse implication of that criterion. The algorithm
uses terminal lower central groups three times for the N^3-residual.
The S3,N and S4,N^2 negative controls and S4,N^3 positive control pass.
No independent all-maximal-chain implementation has been run, so these
remain exploratory computational evidence rather than a proof packet.

The run completed in 16.43 seconds with actual exit 0 and the unique

## Primary source and limitations

Vasil'ev, Vasil'eva and Melchenko,
[arXiv:1904.06986v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06986v1), 15 April 2019,
was read in full (11 pages). Definitions of the different generalized
subnormalities, the hereditary restriction lemmas, Theorem 2.4, and the
distinction between w*F=F and saturation are retained. Its general
formation theorem is not a saturation theorem. Example 2.1 is a
proper containment example for N^3; it is not a saturation failure.

The 2020 publication metadata use a different third-author surname
and title; we cite the exact cached preprint rather than silently
identifying versions. The proof of Theorem 3.4 has been read, but its
many imported formation-theoretic lemmas have not all been independently
reproved. None is used to assert a new arbitrary-group solution here.

Potential further reduction: in a minimal failure of saturation, a
unique minimal normal subgroup lies in Phi(G), hence is elementary
abelian of some prime p. Sylow p-normalizers lift immediately. For
q different from p the obstruction is the passage from N_G(Q)N to
N_G(Q). This lifting step has not been proved in general and must not
be assumed merely because N is contained in Phi(G).
