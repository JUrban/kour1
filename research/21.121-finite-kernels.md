# Finite kernels with a generalized p-Jordan ambient group

12 September 2026. Addendum to the
[permanence proof](21.121-permanence-proof.md). This is a partial
structural result for 21.121, with no new complete-candidate count,
no established priority and no outside review.

Fix a prime p. Suppose Gamma is **generalized p-Jordan**: every
finite p'-subgroup has an abelian normal subgroup of index at most
a fixed J_0>=1. Let N be a finite normal subgroup of Gamma, of
arbitrary order.

**Theorem.** Gamma and Gamma/N have exactly the same set of
admissible p-Jordan exponents. In particular, if Gamma is p-Jordan,
then any finite normal quotient has the same optimal exponent and
the same attainment or nonattainment of its infimum.

The development route is to take the preimage M of the abelian
p'-subgroup in a finite quotient, centralize its finite kernel K,
and apply the already proved soluble bound to C_M(K). This centralizer
has bounded index and nilpotency class at most two. The preliminary
lifting argument below also checks that the generalized p-Jordan
hypothesis passes to the finite normal quotient.

## 1. Lifting p'-sections of finite groups

Let E be finite, K normal in E, and suppose E/K has order prime
to p. Then there is a p'-subgroup H of E mapping onto E/K.

Choose a Sylow p-subgroup P of K. It is also a Sylow p-subgroup
of E. The Frattini argument gives E=K N_E(P): conjugating P by
an element of E gives another Sylow p-subgroup of K, and Sylow
conjugacy inside K corrects that conjugation by an element of K.
In N_E(P), the subgroup P is a normal Sylow p-subgroup. By
Schur--Zassenhaus it has a p'-complement H. Thus

    E=K N_E(P)=K P H=K H,

and H maps onto E/K, as asserted. If P=1, simply take H=E.

It follows that a uniform generalized p-Jordan constant for a
finite group also works for all its quotients and their p'-subgroups:
apply the preceding argument to the preimage E of the p'-subgroup,
then map down the normal abelian subgroup of H.

In particular Gamma/N is generalized p-Jordan with the same constant
J_0. A finite p'-subgroup of Gamma/N has a finite full preimage because
N is finite, so the finite-group argument applies. This assertion
does not claim the same lifting property for infinite kernels.

## 2. Passing a bound downward

Suppose e is admissible for Gamma, with constant J. Every finite
subgroup Gbar of Gamma/N has a finite full preimage G with

    |G_(p)|=|N_(p)| |Gbar_(p)|.

A normal abelian p'-subgroup of G maps to such a subgroup in Gbar,
with index at most its old index. Thus e is admissible for Gamma/N
with constant J|N_(p)|^e, enlarged to one if necessary. No
generalized p-Jordan hypothesis is needed for this direction beyond
the assumed full p-Jordan bound.

## 3. Passing a bound upward under the stated hypothesis

Suppose e is admissible for Gamma/N with constant J. Let G<=Gamma
be finite, put K=G intersect N, and let Gbar=G/K be its image in
Gamma/N. Choose Abar normal abelian of p'-order in Gbar such that

    [Gbar:Abar] <= J |Gbar_(p)|^e.

Let M be the preimage of Abar in G, and set C=C_M(K). Both M and
K are normal in G, so C is normal in G. The conjugation action on K
gives

    [M:C] <= |Aut(K)| <= |N|!.

Since M/K is abelian, [C,C] is contained in K. Every element of
C centralizes K, so [C,C] is central in C. Consequently C is
nilpotent of class at most two, in particular soluble. Moreover
M/K has order prime to p, so, putting s=|K_(p)|, we have

    |C_(p)| <= |M_(p)|=s,
    |G_(p)|=s |Gbar_(p)|.

Every p'-subgroup of C is a p'-subgroup of Gamma. The
[soluble theorem](21.121b-soluble-proof.md), with the given uniform
constant J_0, supplies a characteristic abelian p'-subgroup D of
C such that

    [C:D] <= J_0^3 |C_(p)|^2 <= J_0^3 s^2.

Since C is normal in G, D is normal in G. Therefore

    [G:D] = [G:M][M:C][C:D]
           <= J J_0^3 |N|! s^2 |Gbar_(p)|^e
           = J J_0^3 |N|! s^(2-e) |G_(p)|^e
           <= J J_0^3 |N|! max(1,|N|^(2-e)) |G_(p)|^e.   (1)

This constant is independent of G. The estimate holds for real e,
including negative values if they are allowed in the definition,
because 1<=s<=|N|. It proves upward preservation of e. Together
with Section 2 this proves equality of the full admissible sets.
Equality of infima and of attainment follows whenever the infimum
lies in the allowed range.

## 4. The hypothesis and the earlier kernel result

The generalized p-Jordan hypothesis is automatic when Gamma is
already p-Jordan. Thus the theorem gives invariance of exponents
under all finite normal kernels **within the p-Jordan class**.

If N is a p-group, this generalized hypothesis is also automatic
from a p-Jordan bound for Gamma/N: every finite p'-subgroup of
Gamma maps injectively to the quotient. The earlier unconditional
upward result for a finite p-kernel is therefore consistent with
this more general conditional assertion; its earlier constant is
sharper and does not need a factorial.

For a finite kernel at another prime, the hypothesis cannot simply
be deleted. The infinite extraspecial construction in Section 4
of the [permanence proof](21.121-permanence-proof.md) has a finite
central q-kernel and abelian quotient, for q different from p, but
fails the generalized p-Jordan property. It is not a counterexample
to the present theorem because it lacks exactly its extra hypothesis.

## 5. Source, review and computational limits

The complement theorem is the standard Schur--Zassenhaus input
already identified in the [source audit](21.121b-source-audit.md).
The Frattini argument and the class-two centralizer argument are
proved here. The soluble theorem and characteristic construction
are the previously proved project lemmas, with their precise
classical dependencies and finite controls retained.

The primary comparison remains Chen--Shramov's published 2024
paper, with the exact reading scope in the
[permanence report](21.121-permanence-report.md). Additional bounded
queries combined p-Jordan with “finite kernel,” “finite normal”
and “quotient exponents.” No exact prior version of this conditional
finite-kernel assertion was located in those searches. This is not
a priority certificate.

The same-agent review checked finite full preimages, Sylow orders,
the Frattini factorization, the use of Schur--Zassenhaus only for
a normal Sylow subgroup, simultaneous normality of M and K,
centrality of [C,C], characteristic invariance of D, and the
constant in (1). No gap was identified. No new mathematical
verifier was run for this short deduction; the prior controls are
not described as an exhaustive check of this arbitrary-group theorem.

The complete-candidate count remains 46. Nothing here supplies the
universal exponent-three bound asked for in 21.121(b).
