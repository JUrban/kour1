# Problem 16.14: central involutions and generator rank

Status updated September 12, 2026: the full printed inequality follows
from the prior theorem in `16.14-prior-result.md`. The historical search
and planning record below is retained. The elementary exponent-four
argument is now extended to arbitrary exponent in class two in
`16.14-class2-proof.md`; no new complete solution is counted.

The supplied PDF page98 was visually read. Let G be a finite 2-group
with Omega_1(G)<=Z(G). Does d(G)=rank(G/G^2) satisfy
d(G)<=2 rank(Z(G))? For an abelian finite 2-group, its rank is its
minimal number of generators. Since G^2=Phi(G), the left side is
log_2 |G:Phi(G)|.

## First computation

Search all SmallGroups at orders2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256. For each group,
compute d(G) and z=rank(Z(G)). If d<=2z, it cannot be a counterexample,
regardless of the central-involution hypothesis. Otherwise look for an
actual noncentral involution. Finding one excludes the hypothesis;
failure after exhausting the group gives a candidate requiring an
independent Omega_1 calculation and a structural description.

Keep every excluded rank-violating case's group ID and a noncentral
involution's polycyclic exponent vector. Record complete order ranges
and totals. Run one additional GAP process initially; fourteen current
19.20 workers leave ample room within the20-core limit. Do not infer
anything for larger groups from a negative result.

## Structural discriminator

For class-two exponent-four groups, squaring is a quadratic map from
the elementary abelian central quotient into the central involutions.
The central-involution hypothesis constrains its zero fiber. A possible
Chevalley--Warning argument should be checked against central direct
factors and the distinction between G/Z(G) and G/Phi(G); no general
bound is asserted here before those details are resolved.

The term p-central has conflicting p=2 conventions in the literature:
some authors require Omega_2(G)<=Z(G), a stronger condition than this
question. The power-endomorphism usage of n-central can instead mean
G^n<=Z(G), also different. Every imported theorem must be checked
against the actual central-involution assumption.

## Completed range and verification

The search covered all58,760 SmallGroups of orders2 through256 that
are powers of two. In55,177 cases the rank inequality itself holds.
Every one of the remaining3,583 groups has a recorded noncentral
involution, excluding the question's hypothesis. There are no hits.

Run `bin/gap scripts/search_16_14.g`, then the independent small
control `bin/gap scripts/verify_16_14.g`, and finally
`python3 scripts/verify_16_14_log.py` with the redirected log names
used in `results/16.14-*.log`. The last script validates all completed
ranges and replays every involution as a product of pc generators.
It tests noncentrality by commutation with generators and checks both
ranks using alternative descriptions. All3,583 certificates pass.
The independent small control checks all340 groups through order64,
using direct squares, element counts, and a separate Omega computation;
104 have central involutions, including45 of exponent at most four.
All checks pass. The summary is `results/16.14-summary.json`.

The proof in `16.14-exponent4.md` handles the distinction between
central involutions W and the square subgroup P explicitly. Squaring
is an anisotropic quadratic map G/W -> P, so a parity count gives
log_2|G/W|<=2 log_2|P|. This yields the desired generator bound even
with central direct factors. No reduction from larger exponent is known
here. No extension to the over ten million groups of order512 has
been launched; structural work should precede that larger commitment.

## Source-reading update

Jezernik, https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.3467 , introduction read: his
p=2 definition requires Omega_2(G)<=Z(G). Kuhn,
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0612133 , introduction and selected Section9
examples read: he uses all elements of order p central, matching the
Notebook for p=2. His d_0 and d_1 are cohomological invariants, not
the generator rank d(G). Neither paper's full proof was audited, and
neither provides a resolution used here. Both reading copies are cached.
