# Problem14.24: exact obstruction to a bound uniform in rank

Status: complete negative deduction for the rank-uniform interpretation,
using a prior family. The fixed-rank question is not resolved here.
Excluded from the count of new complete resolutions.11September2026.

For every r>=3 there are conjugate automorphisms alpha_r,beta_r of a
free group of rank2r-1 with norms3 and2, respectively, for which the
minimum norm of gamma satisfying gamma^-1 alpha_r gamma=beta_r is
**exactly r**. Thus no function of these two norms alone can work
simultaneously in all ranks. Recursiveness is irrelevant to this
negative rank-uniform conclusion.

## Construction and upper bound

Use the free basis X={x_1,...,x_r,t_1,...,t_(r-1)}. Define alpha by

    alpha(t_i)=t_i,
    alpha(x_1)=x_1 t_1,
    alpha(x_i)=t_(i-1)^-1 x_i t_i       (2<=i<=r-1),
    alpha(x_r)=t_(r-1)^-1 x_r.

This is the exact family in the28August2026 note on14.23 cited below.
Its inverse reverses the signs of the inserted t-letters, so it is an
automorphism of norm3. Define a second automorphism beta by

    beta(t_i)=t_i,
    beta(x_i)=x_i t_i                   (1<=i<=r-1),
    beta(x_r)=x_r.

Its inverse sends x_i to x_i t_i^-1 for i<r, and fixes x_r and the
t_i, so beta has norm2. Let

    gamma(x_i)=x_1 x_2 ... x_i,         gamma(t_i)=t_i.

The inverse sends x_1 to x_1 and x_i to x_(i-1)^-1 x_i for i>1,
fixing all t_i. Thus gamma is an automorphism of norm r. Telescoping
gives, on each free generator,

    alpha(gamma(x_i))=gamma(x_i)t_i=gamma(beta(x_i))  (i<r),
    alpha(gamma(x_r))=gamma(x_r)=gamma(beta(x_r)),
    alpha(gamma(t_i))=t_i=gamma(beta(t_i)).

Consequently gamma^-1 alpha gamma=beta, using ordinary function
composition. This proves the upper bound r for the minimal norm.

## Lower bound for every conjugator

Work in the integral abelianization L=Z^(2r-1), and write e_i=[x_i],
f_i=[t_i]. The fixed lattices of the induced maps A and B are

    ker(A-I) = Z(e_1+...+e_r) direct-sum <f_1,...,f_(r-1)>_Z,
    ker(B-I) = Z e_r          direct-sum <f_1,...,f_(r-1)>_Z.

Indeed, A adds (c_i-c_(i+1)) f_i to a vector with x-coordinates c_i,
whereas B adds c_i f_i for i<r. These formulas also show that the
displayed bases are bases of the full integer kernels.

Let theta be any automorphism satisfying theta^-1 alpha theta=beta,
and let T be its induced unimodular map on L. The intertwining equation
A T=T B implies

    T(ker(B-I))=ker(A-I).

Therefore T(e_r),T(f_1),...,T(f_(r-1)) generate ker(A-I). At least
one of these vectors has nonzero coefficient of e_1+...+e_r: otherwise
they would generate only the proper subgroup spanned by the f_i.
Such a vector has all r x-coordinates equal to a nonzero integer, and
hence has l1-norm at least r. It is the exponent-sum vector of one of
the words theta(x_r),theta(t_1),...,theta(t_(r-1)). Reduced word length
is at least the l1-norm of its exponent-sum vector. Thus ||theta||>=r.

Together with the explicit gamma, this proves the exact minimum r.
The argument uses only fixed lattices in abelianization; it does not
assume that every fixed abelianization vector lifts to a fixed group
element, nor require a computation of the full fixed subgroup.

## Quantifiers and attribution

If a single f:N x N->N were required to work for every rank, choose
r>f(3,2), contradicting the exact minimum above. For a fixed rank n,
the example does not rule out a rank-dependent function f_n. The
printed wording, which begins with Aut(F_n), must not be silently
interpreted as requiring uniformity in n. The question for each fixed
rank remains unresolved in this report.

## The fixed-rank algorithmic question

For any one fixed n, existence of a recursive bound f_n is equivalent
to decidability of conjugacy in Aut(F_n), when automorphisms are given
by their images on the specified basis. Given a bound, enumerate the
finitely many n-tuples of reduced words of length at most the bound,
use the decidable free-basis test to retain automorphisms, and test
alpha theta=theta beta on each basis generator. This decides both
positive and negative conjugacy instances.

Conversely, a conjugacy decision algorithm identifies all conjugate
pairs in the finite effectively enumerable sets of automorphisms of
norm at most a and at most b. For each positive pair, enumerate
automorphisms until a conjugator is found. There are only finitely many
positive pairs, so this procedure terminates; the maximum of the found
norms, with zero when no pair occurs, is a recursive bound f_n(a,b).
This elementary equivalence is not itself a conjugacy algorithm.

The primary abstract of Dahmani--Touikan,
[*Reducing the Conjugacy Problem for Relatively Hyperbolic Automorphisms
to Peripheral Components*](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnaf311),2025,
describes a reduction and new special cases for Out(F_n). Only that
abstract was read here. It does not supply an audited general algorithm
for Aut(F_n) in this report. Conjugacy of elements of a free-by-cyclic
group must also not be confused with conjugacy of automorphisms.
