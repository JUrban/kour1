# Problem 9.45: a finite coordinate divisibility criterion

Started 2026-09-11 at 16:53 UTC. A general candidate is being

Write a=r/m modulo Z^n, with m its least positive denominator.
The proposed test lists centered representatives of the m cyclic
cosets, and recognizes vectors b for which b/||b||^2 lies in the
dual lattice. Such vectors generate orthogonal rank-one summands.
Two distinct summand lines must be orthogonal, by integrality of
both projections and strict Cauchy--Schwarz. Thus counting them
should replace any search through combinations of possible bases.

1. Prove the splitting and mutual-orthogonality lemmas in full.
2. Prove that every nonunit splitting vector is centered, with
   at most two coordinates equal in absolute value to 1/2. This
   is essential: arbitrary tie choices lose legitimate summands.
3. Give an explicit necessary and sufficient test consisting only
   of residue calculations, squared norms and integer divisibility.
   State complexity in m, without claiming polynomial time in log m.
4. Compare with independent exhaustive orthogonal-basis enumeration
   in small primal lattices, using covolume rather than duality as
   the acceptance test. Include nonrectangular and tie controls.
5. Replay every positive basis and every negative projection
   obstruction in actual integer lattice arithmetic in GAP.
6. Audit the prime-power reduction and composite examples as
   additional structural checks, without conflating the cases.
7. Audit literature. Bardakov 1998 explicitly solves n=2, while
   Chandrasekaran--Gandikota--Grigorescu 2015 handles Construction A
   from arbitrary binary and ternary codes. Record exact reading
   scopes and make no claim that these prior results are new.
8. Complete a logical review, bind proofs and test outputs by hashes,
