# Problem 21.42: a negative answer from existing grading results

Date: 2026-09-10. Status: literature-based resolution, excluded from the
new-theorem candidate count. No outside review has yet checked this note.

## Conclusion and existing sources

Every three-generated torsion-free nilpotent group of class three is
self-similar. In fact it admits a faithful transitive recurrent action.
Thus the answer to the existence question in 21.42 is negative.

The following two established results imply this conclusion.

1. Every three-generated three-step nilpotent Lie algebra has a positive
   grading. This is due to K. Dekimpe, P. Igodt and H. Pouseele,
   *Expanding Automorphisms and Affine Structures on Nilpotent Lie
   Algebras with Few Generators*, Communications in Algebra 31 (2003),
   5847--5874,
   [DOI 10.1081/AGB-120024857](https://doi.org/10.1081/AGB-120024857).
   The publisher abstract was checked. The positive, rather than merely
   integer, grading assertion is explicitly restated in Dekimpe--Deré,
   [arXiv:1407.8106v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.8106v1), page 13, in the
   proof of Theorem 6.1, with reference [8] pointing to that 2003 paper.
   That full preprint was downloaded and the relevant passages read.
2. For a finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group, a positive
   grading of its rational Malcev Lie algebra is equivalent to being
   freely fractal, hence implies self-similarity. This is Proposition 6,
   Section 7.4, of O. Mathieu,
   [*Which Nilpotent Groups are Self-Similar?*, arXiv:2101.11291v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11291v1),
   page 27. The definitions on pages 6--7 and the proof immediately
   preceding Proposition 6 were checked in the downloaded full paper.
   The result applies to the original lattice, not just to an unspecified
   finite-index subgroup. A positive complex grading suffices as well.

For a three-generated group G, its rational Malcev Lie algebra is
generated by at most the logarithms of those three generators. Its class
is three. Apply the first result, then the second. If the minimal number
of rational generators is two, the same cited grading result covers
two-generated class-three algebras (indeed, class four); alternatively
the short argument below covers that case directly.

The supplied Notebook page 173 was inspected visually. It asks for self-similarity, with no
finite-state or prescribed-degree restriction. Berlatto--Santos's
[2025 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16947v1), introduction, poses the
same question as Problem 1. Its introduction was checked in full. The
conclusion above is an inference from the cited older results; neither
older paper has been found explicitly phrasing it as Kourovka 21.42.
Accordingly we retain it as a useful literature consequence, without
claiming the grading theorem or the self-similarity implication as new.

## Independent derivation of the grading assertion

This derivation was found before locating the 2003 result. It is retained
as a self-contained rediscovery. All vector spaces and changes of basis
are over Q. Positive integer generator weights specify the gradings.

Let L be a nilpotent Lie algebra of class at most three with minimal
number of generators at most three. Class at most two has the usual
positive grading: a complement to L' has degree one and L' degree two.
We therefore assume class exactly three.

Choose three generators when the minimal number is three, and present
L as a quotient of the free class-three Lie algebra F on their space V.
Write F=V direct_sum F_2 direct_sum F_3 in its ordinary grading. The
relation ideal I lies in F_2 direct_sum F_3. Let U be its projection to
F_2=exterior_square(V), and let J=I intersection F_3. Then [V,U] is
contained in J. The relations are a graph U -> F_3/J, together with J.

If U=0, I is homogeneous, so L has its ordinary positive grading. If
dim U=3, then every commutator lies in gamma_3(L), forcing
gamma_3(L)=[L,L']=0 and then L'=0, contrary to class three.

### Case dim U=2

Any plane in exterior_square(Q^3) has the form
span([x,z],[y,z]) in a suitable rational basis x,y,z. Put q=[x,y].
In L the elements [x,z] and [y,z] lie in gamma_3(L) and are central.
Jacobi implies [z,q]=0. Hence gamma_3(L) is spanned by [x,q] and [y,q].
The relations express [x,z] and [y,z] as linear combinations of these
two triple brackets, with possible additional relations between them.
Weights (x,y,z)=(1,1,2) make all these relations homogeneous and positive.

### Case dim U=1: reduction to five triple brackets

Every nonzero element of exterior_square(Q^3) is decomposable, so put
U=span([x,y]) after a rational basis change. The relations [V,U]=0 leave
the five-dimensional triple-bracket space with basis

    A=[x,[x,z]],   B=[y,[x,z]]=[x,[y,z]],
    C=[z,[x,z]],   D=[y,[y,z]],   E=[z,[y,z]].

Now regard J as a subspace of this five-dimensional space, after
factoring out [V,U]. The remaining relation is [x,y]=w modulo J.
Write S=span(A,B,D). Adding degree-two terms to x and y changes w by
an arbitrary element of S and changes no triple bracket. Indeed the
changes to [x,y] from these modifications independently span A,B,D.
Thus only the image of w in (S+span(C,E))/(S+J) matters.

If that image is zero, one can take w=0, and the ordinary grading works.
Otherwise the projection of J to span(C,E) has dimension zero or one.
A change of basis in span(x,y), together with rescaling, gives w=C;
when the projection has dimension one it can simultaneously be made
span(E). Adjustments by J and S make these statements exact.

If the projection of J is zero, then J is an arbitrary subspace of S.
Weights (x,y,z)=(2,2,1) make [x,y]=C homogeneous of degree four, while
all of S has degree five, so they work for every such J.

### The remaining one-dimensional projection

Write

    J=J_0 + span(E+Q),       J_0 subset S,       Q in S.

Replace z by z+alpha*x+beta*y. Modulo the central relation [x,y]=C,
this leaves A,B,D unchanged and changes E by alpha*B+beta*D, while
the change in C belongs to S and can be absorbed by degree-two changes
of x and y. It follows that the B and D components of Q can be removed.

If Q has no remaining A component, then J=J_0+span(E), and weights
(2,2,1) again work. The same conclusion holds if J_0 contains a vector
with nonzero A component: subtract a multiple of that relation from Q,
then remove its B and D components as above.

The only remaining case is

    J=J_0 + span(E+lambda*A),
    lambda nonzero,       J_0 subset span(B,D).

A shear x -> x+t*y sends B to B+t*D and fixes D. It puts any line
J_0 in the form span(B) or span(D); the zero and full-plane cases need
no change. Such a shear only adds B and D components to the relation
E+lambda*A and S-components to [x,y]=C. The preceding changes remove
those additions. Thus one may assume

    J_0 = 0, span(B), span(D), or span(B,D).

Weights (x,y,z)=(3,4,2) now work: [x,y] and C have degree seven,
E and A have degree eight, B has degree nine, and D has degree ten.
Every remaining relation is homogeneous of positive degree.

This exhausts the three-generator cases. For two generators, the
degree-two free component has dimension one. A nonzero degree-two
relation would put the whole derived algebra in gamma_3 and destroy
class three. Hence all relations are in degree three and the ordinary
grading works. One generator cannot generate a class-three Lie algebra.

## Why the grading yields a faithful action

For completeness, the mechanism in the cited self-similarity criterion
is as follows. A positive rational grading gives dilations acting by
q^j on degree j. For a suitable integer q>1 a dilation delta preserves
the given Malcev lattice G and delta(G) has finite index in G. The
inverse f:delta(G) -> G is a virtual endomorphism. If K is any
f-invariant subgroup contained in delta(G), then every f^n(k) stays in
G. Its logarithm is delta^(-n)(log k), which tends to zero because all
weights are positive. The lattice is discrete, so k=1. In particular
the normal f-core is trivial. The usual action on the coset tree is
therefore faithful and state-closed. The lattice-preserving choice of
delta for the original group is supplied by Mathieu's Proposition 6
and the preceding lattice argument; it is not inferred merely from
existence of a dilation on the ambient real Lie group.
