# Problem 16.95: permutation of columns to a cyclic matrix

Status: all binary matrices through dimension6 covered, with no
counterexample. Independent controls pass. The general problem remains
unresolved here; no novelty claim is made for this bounded computation.

For every A in GL(n,F), does some column permutation AP have minimal
polynomial of degree n? The printed conjecture is due to J. G. Thompson.

## Literature warning

John D. Dixon, *Recognizing cyclic matrices and a conjecture of J. G.
Thompson*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02238 , initially claimed a proof.
The paper was withdrawn in version2 on14 November2017. Its current
primary abstract explicitly includes an erratum crediting
Alexander Stasinski: a proposed cyclicity criterion fails for J-I,
where J is the all-ones matrix. The determinant-preservation step is
identified as the error. Consequently this paper is not evidence of a
solution to16.95. The primary abstract/erratum was read; the current
PDF endpoint returned404, and the erroneous full proof was not read.

## Bounded search

Over F_2, a matrix is invertible precisely when its columns form a
basis. The property being tested is invariant under permuting the
original columns, so enumerate unordered bases of F_2^n, not all
ordered bases. For each one, test all column permutations until a
cyclic matrix is found. This is an exact reduction, not a random
sample. Through n=6 there are27,998,208 bases at n=6 and84,200 at
n<=5.

Cyclicity is tested by linear independence of I,A,...,A^(n-1) in the
space of n by n matrices. This is equivalent to minimal-polynomial
degree n and avoids assuming a standard basis vector is cyclic.
The latter assumption can fail even for a cyclic matrix. An independent
small verifier should instead search all possible cyclic vectors.

## Completed run and independent validation

`scripts/search_16_95.cpp` completed in one C++ process. It enumerated
28,082,408 unordered bases, performing50,718,594 cyclicity tests.
There were no hits. The dimension counts were1,3,28,840,83,328,
27,998,208, agreeing with |GL(n,2)|/n! in each case. At most155
permutations were needed for any basis at n=6. The output is retained
in `results/16.95-f2-through6.log`.

`scripts/verify_16_95.py` compiles a harness containing the actual C++
cyclicity function and compares it with an independent all-vector
Krylov criterion and row reduction. It covers all20,335 invertible
matrices in dimensions1,...,4, another2,000 random matrices in
dimensions5,6 (including singular matrices), and12 scalar controls.
All22,347 comparisons pass. It also verifies the search's exact
dimension and total counts. Result: `results/16.95-verification.json`.

Reproduce the search with

    g++ -O3 -std=c++20 scripts/search_16_95.cpp -o /tmp/search_16_95
    /tmp/search_16_95 6
    python3 scripts/verify_16_95.py

The column-permutation quotient covers all invertible binary matrices
in this range. No nonbinary field or dimension above6 is covered.
No larger run was launched without reassessing its cost and purpose.
