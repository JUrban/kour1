# Problem 14.3: square obstruction and restricted positive criteria

Status: partial analysis, not a solution of the arbitrary finite-group
question. No novelty claim. 11 September 2026.

The problem asks whether every central unit u of ZG is h v with
h in Z(G) and v a symmetric central unit. Here star inverts group
elements and fixes the integer coefficients. It is not enough to find
an abstract copy of the quotient unit group among the symmetric units.

## Exact obstruction

Set w=u(u*)^-1. Centrality gives ww*=1. If w=sum a_g g, the identity
coefficient of ww* is sum a_g^2=1. Thus w=plus or minus one group element.
Its augmentation is one, so w=z in Z(G). This also proves that the map

    q: Z(U(ZG)) -> Z(G),  q(u)=u(u*)^-1

is a homomorphism. Its restriction to Z(G) is h -> h^2.

The required factorization of u exists if and only if q(u) belongs to
Z(G)^2. Indeed q(hv)=h^2 for symmetric v. Conversely, if q(u)=h^2,
then v=h^-1 u satisfies v*=h u*=h^-1 u=v.

Consequently the exact obstruction group is

    q(Z(U(ZG))) / Z(G)^2,

an elementary abelian 2-group. The problem is immediately affirmative
when the finite centre has odd order. This includes centreless groups.
These observations are not asserted to be new.

## Two necessary conditions on a possible obstruction element

Let z=q(u).

1. Every real-valued irreducible character chi satisfies chi(z)=chi(1).
   A central element acts as a scalar in an irreducible representation.
   That scalar for u is nonzero because u is a unit, and is real because
   chi is real-valued. The scalar for u* is the same. Thus their quotient,
   the scalar for z, is one. Equivalently, every possible z lies in the
   intersection of the kernels of all real-valued irreducible characters.

2. There is g in G with g^2=z and with odd conjugacy-class size.
   Write u=sum a_g g. The equation u=z u* makes its coefficients invariant
   under the involution T:g -> z g^-1. Centrality also makes them constant
   on each conjugacy class. The augmentation is plus or minus one.
   Reduce the sum by classes modulo two. Even classes contribute zero;
   distinct classes paired by T also contribute zero. Hence some odd
   conjugacy class fixed by T has an odd coefficient. The involution T
   on this odd finite class has a fixed point, which satisfies g^2=z.

The second condition is also equivalent to z having a square root in
Z(P) for a Sylow 2-subgroup P, after taking the 2-part when necessary.
Only its direct class-size formulation is used by the proposed screen.
In particular, for a 2-group the odd conjugacy class must be a singleton,
so this argument proves the required factorization for every finite
2-group. For a finite nilpotent group, split off its odd-order direct
factor. The 2-component of g is central and the odd component of z has
a unique square root in the odd-order centre. Together these give a
square root of z in Z(G). The result for nilpotent groups is a partial
positive case, with no priority assertion.

Both conditions are necessary, but neither is claimed sufficient to
produce a central unit. A table flag is an element still allowed by an
obstruction test, not a counterexample to the Notebook problem.

## Why the square-in-G shortcut fails

In Dic3=C3 semidirect C4, with inversion action, the square z of a
generator of C4 is central of order two. It has no square root in the
centre, which is C2. Its displayed square root has conjugacy class size
three. Thus condition 2 alone does not force a central square root.
The real faithful degree-two character instead excludes this z by
condition 1. The group is not a counterexample to the unit question.

## Primary-source scope

Bakshi--Maheshwary--Passi, *Group rings and the RS-property*,
[arXiv:1805.08965v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08965v1),
published in Communications in Algebra,
[DOI 10.1080/00927872.2018.1498876](https://doi.org/10.1080/00927872.2018.1498876),
Theorem 13 gives q(u) in Z(G), and an exact sequence from the central
units to the symmetric norm units with kernel plus or minus Z(G).
The complete statement and proof of Theorem 13 and Corollary 14 were
read in the primary author manuscript, along with the introductory
summary. The rest of the paper was not audited. Its norm map is u -> uu*;
the existence of this exact sequence does not prove that q(u) is a square
in Z(G), which is the extra requirement in the Notebook question.

The 2019 ICTS author slides initially located state that weaker abstract
isomorphism consequence as well. The primary article supersedes the
slides as our source for the precise theorem. No prior general resolution
was established by this limited search.

## Computation in progress

`scripts/screen_14_3.g` tests condition 1 on the 2,750 named installed
ordinary character tables. Central group elements are the singleton
classes; their squares are obtained from the second power map. Possible
nonsquares that survive all real characters are recorded explicitly.
Completion, independent controls, and any further filtering will be
reported separately. This is not an enumeration of central units.
