# A negative answer to Kourovka Notebook 13.19

Status: complete independent counterexample, developed 10 September 2026,
but **excluded from the new-solution count**. The literature audit found that
a negative answer is already implied by Kearnes--Mayr--Ruskuc (2018); see
`13.19-review.md` for the precise implication. Our smaller finite example
has been checked in GAP and independently with binary matrices.

## Statement being answered

Problem 13.19 (Yu. M. Gorchakov), supplied Notebook PDF page 67, asks whether
Q/H must be a regular p-group when H is normal in Q, both H and Q are
subdirect subgroups of the same finite direct product G_1 x ... x G_n,
and Q/H is a p-group. The printed question places no restriction excluding
p=2 or requiring n=p.

We construct an example with n=4, every G_i dihedral of order 8,
|Q|=256, |H|=32, and Q/H dihedral of order 8. This quotient is not regular.

## Construction

All coordinates and sums below are over F_2. Let D be the group of matrices

    d(a,b,c) = [1 a c]
               [0 1 b]
               [0 0 1].

Thus

    d(a,b,c) d(a',b',c')
      = d(a+a', b+b', c+c'+a b').                         (1)

It is the dihedral group of order 8: s=d(1,0,0) and t=d(0,1,0)
are involutions, st has order 4, and they generate D. Its derived subgroup
is generated by z=d(0,0,1), a central involution.

Define Q to consist of the following elements of D^4, with the four factors
indexed by (1,1), (1,2), (2,1), (2,2):

    q(a,b,C) = (d(a_1,b_1,c_11), d(a_1,b_2,c_12),
                d(a_2,b_1,c_21), d(a_2,b_2,c_22)),

where a,b range independently over F_2^2 and C=(c_ij) ranges over all
2 by 2 matrices. There are exactly 2^8=256 such elements. Formula (1) gives

    q(a,b,C) q(a',b',C') = q(a+a', b+b', C+C'+a (b')^T). (2)

Consequently Q is closed under multiplication and contains the identity;
being a finite nonempty subset of a group, it is a subgroup.

Define

    phi(q(a,b,C)) = d(a_1+a_2, b_1+b_2, sum_{i,j} c_ij).

The identity

    sum_{i,j} a_i b'_j = (a_1+a_2)(b'_1+b'_2)

and (1)--(2) show that phi is a homomorphism. It is surjective, as its
three output coordinates can be prescribed independently: use a=(a_1,0),
b=(b_1,0), and C with only c_11 possibly nonzero.

Let H=ker(phi). Explicitly,

    H = {q(a,b,C): a_1=a_2, b_1=b_2, sum_{i,j}c_ij=0}.

It is normal in Q and has order 2^(1+1+3)=32. The first isomorphism
theorem gives Q/H isomorphic to D.

Both H and Q project onto every factor D. Indeed, H contains the entire
diagonal subgroup {(d,d,d,d): d in D}: the repeated a and b coordinates
sum to zero, and four identical c coordinates also sum to zero. The
projection of this diagonal subgroup onto any factor is already D.

## Failure of regularity

For a regular 2-group, the square of a product of two elements must equal
the product of their squares times a product of squares of elements in the
derived subgroup of the subgroup they generate. (The equivalent definition
using one derived-subgroup square gives the same obstruction here.)

In D, s^2=t^2=1, whereas (st)^2=z is not the identity. Every element of
D'=<z> has square 1. Thus no allowed correction can make the regularity
identity hold for s,t. Therefore D, and hence Q/H, is not a regular
2-group. All the hypotheses of 13.19 are satisfied, so its answer is **no**.

## Small certificate in a direct product

The same Q is generated inside D^4 by

    x_1=(s,s,1,1),  x_2=(1,1,s,s),
    y_1=(t,1,t,1),  y_2=(1,t,1,t).

Their four commutators [x_i,y_j] supply z in the respective single
coordinate. Therefore they generate all central coordinates as well as
all a and b coordinates in the displayed model. The homomorphism phi
sends x_1,x_2 to s and y_1,y_2 to t.

H can equivalently be generated by the diagonal s, the diagonal t, and

    (z,z,1,1), (z,1,z,1), (z,1,1,z).

The last three elements span the even-parity central coordinates. GAP
identifies Q as SmallGroup(256,8935), H as SmallGroup(32,46), and
H as D_8 x C_2 x C_2. These identifiers are supplementary checks, not
assumptions of the proof.

## Sources and scope

The actual supplied PDF was visually checked on page 67. Gorchakov also
states the regularity conjecture immediately after his main theorem on
page 801 of the Russian original of *Quotients of subdirect products of
groups*, Sibirsk. Mat. Zh. 50 (2009), 800--805; English translation,
Siberian Math. J. 50 (2009), 632--636,
https://doi.org/10.1007/s11202-009-0070-z . The full Russian original is
available at https://emis.de/ft/10761 . Its later construction chooses an
odd prime, but the conjecture itself has no such restriction.

No result from that paper is needed for the counterexample. In particular,
we do not count the familiar nilpotency bound for subdirect quotients as a
new result. This example answers the stated question at p=2; it makes no
claim to settle an additional question restricted to odd primes.
