# Problem 17.16: a prior positive consequence for type D5

12 September 2026. The full question is not settled here. Paris--Soroko,
[arXiv:2606.26082v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26082v2), Theorem 1,
combined with the already known linearity of spherical Artin groups,
gives a positive answer for A[D5]. This adds no new candidate.

## Elementary deduction

We use the following general observation. Suppose a linear group L has
infinite cyclic centre Z=<z> and a homomorphism e:L->Z with e(z)=m!=0.
Put K=ker(e). Then K intersects Z trivially, so its image in L/Z is
isomorphic to K and is linear. This image has finite index: the quotient
L/(KZ) is e(L)/mZ. Consequently L/Z is virtually linear and hence linear.

For completeness, linearity passes from a subgroup H of finite index r
to a group E as follows. Let rho:H->GL(V) be faithful. The induced
representation of E on the direct sum of r copies of V permutes those
summands by its action on E/H. If an element acts identically, it fixes
the summand corresponding to H, belongs to H, and acts there by rho.
Thus it is the identity. This constructs a faithful finite-dimensional
representation over the same field; no normality of H is needed.

For A=A[D5], let e send every standard generator to 1. Artin relations
have equal lengths, so e is well-defined. The facts recorded on page 3
of Paris--Soroko give Z(A)=<Delta_D^2> and pi(Delta_D)=Delta^2, where
pi:A[D5]->A[A4] preserves exponent sum and Delta^2=(s1 s2 s3 s4)^5.
It follows that e(Delta_D)=20 and e(Delta_D^2)=40. The preceding
observation therefore proves that A/Z(A) is linear, using the known
linearity of A. Theorem 1 identifies Aut(A) with (A/Z(A)) semidirect C2.
A second application of the finite-index argument proves Aut(A) linear.
