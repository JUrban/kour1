# Exact infinite controls for the characteristic-zero theorem

These are proved examples and bounded arithmetic checks, not computational
proofs of the imported structure theorems.

Let B=Z[1/2] and let H be the Heisenberg group on B, with multiplication

    (u,v,z)(a,b,c)=(u+a,v+b,z+c+u*b).

The map delta(u,v,z)=(2u,4v,8z) is an automorphism. Define

    G=(H semidirect_delta Z) times S4.

With k in Z and p in S4, an element (u,v,z,k,p) has the faithful
rational matrix representation given by a block sum of the matrices

    [[8^k, u*4^k, z], [0, 4^k, v], [0,0,1]]  and  P_p,

where P_p has its entry 1 in row i, column p(i). Thus G is a
characteristic-zero linear group of degree seven.

**Its almost Engel subgroup is exactly L=H times S4.** If l is in L,
the first commutator with any x in G has its first component in H.
The second has that component in Z(H), and the third has identity
there. The S4 component of every commutator belongs to A4. Hence
K={1} times A4 is a common sink, with entry by the third commutator
in this particular example.

Conversely take g with k nonzero. On the first coordinate of H/Z(H),
commutation with g is multiplication by r=2^(-k)-1. For k!=-1 this
nonzero rational number has absolute value different from 1, so the
iterates of 1 are all distinct and have no finite eventual sink.
For k=-1 the multiplier is 1, and every element of the infinite group
B is fixed on this quotient. Every proposed common finite sink would
therefore need to contain infinitely many distinct first-coordinate
images. In either case g is not almost Engel. This also explains why
testing just one orbit would miss the k=-1 obstruction.

L is not metabelian because S4 has derived length three. It is not
virtually abelian: every finite-index subgroup of H contains nonzero
powers of (1,0,0) and (0,1,0), whose commutator is a nonzero central
element. Thus this example lies beyond both earlier covered classes.

For a nonsplit lift, take R=Z times S3 and N=2Z times {1}. The quotient
R/N is finite of order twelve, but its full preimage R is infinite.
The torsion subgroup T(R)={0} times S3 has order six and intersects N
trivially. There is no subgroup mapping isomorphically onto R/N:
all torsion elements have even first coordinate zero, so such a
subgroup could not surject onto the C2 factor. In the matrix model
[[1,m],[0,1]] direct-sum P_p, the sixth power has upper entry 6m.
This checks why the proof takes torsion elements in a finite extension
of N rather than declaring that extension itself finite.

Finally the union of the diagonal cyclic groups of orders 3^r in
GL_1(C) is an infinite torsion abelian group. Every element is Engel
and K=1 works for the whole group. Its full torsion subgroup is
infinite. This rules out replacing the finite kernel in the theorem
by all torsion elements of L.

The two verifiers independently construct the first example using
GAP rational matrices and Python rational normal forms. They print
every coordinate of three successive commutators in 720 pairs, then
compare all 2,160 resulting group elements exactly. They also check
the two kinds of excluded dilation, the finite S4 sink union, the
nonsplit lift, and the central torsion controls. The finite examples
and bounded tests supplement the preceding infinite proofs.
