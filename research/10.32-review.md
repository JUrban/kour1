# Internal audit of the logarithmic symmetric-group bound

Completed 2026-09-11. Candidate number30. No outside review and no
assertion of historical priority. The mathematical proof is
`10.32-proof.md`; this audit records its logical and evidence boundaries.

## Logical audit

1. **Exact question.** The supplied Notebook's printed/physical p.40 was
   rendered and visually checked. Problem10.32 is unmarked there and asks
   for an analogous logarithmic degree bound for the whole symmetric
   group, assuming at least one exponent odd.
2. **Exponent convention.** Nonzero negative exponents are permitted.
   The cases with either exponent +/-1 are immediate, so the slightly
   awkward printed definition of m in these cases causes no ambiguity.
3. **Both odd.** Reversal on each cycle gives an involution t with
   tgt=g^{-1}. Thus gt is an involution and g=(gt)t is its own odd-power
   factorization, in every degree.
4. **The imported cycle theorem.** Its cycle counts include every fixed
   point. With m_1=L-1, its positive-genus equation is precisely
   2g=L-h-k+1. No genus-zero exception is suppressed.
5. **Parity.** L==h+k-1 modulo2 ensures integral genus; the strict
   cycle-count margin gives genus at least one.
6. **Product convention.** The source's tau_1 tau_2 sigma=1 gives a
   product equal to sigma^{-1}, which has exactly the desired cycle
   type. All classes used are invariant under inversion.
7. **Small odd targets.** A transposition joins the two disjoint q-cycles
   of B^{-1}; its product with B^{-1} is one2q-cycle. There are enough
   letters because2q<=n.
8. **Defect.** For any other odd g, e=s_0-t is odd and at least3.
   The only permutation of defect one is a transposition.
9. **Invariant subset.** Padding the support with fixed points to
   d=max(s_0,2q) is possible since both arguments of the maximum are<=n.
   This avoids the invalid full-degree restriction on small supports.
10. **Counts.** On this d-set, h=t+d-s_0 and k=d-2q+2. These are
    positive total cycle counts for actual conjugacy classes.
11. **Small support inequality.** If s_0<=2q, then h+k+1=2q-e+3<=2q.
12. **Large support inequality.** If s_0>2q, t<=s_0/2 gives
    h+k+1<=3n/2-2q+3<2q under the stated strict interval condition.
13. **Remaining hypotheses.** L=2q<=d and h+k-1=2d-e-2q+1 is even.
    Thus the theorem applies in both support cases.
14. **Return to the target.** From alpha beta=A, write alpha=A beta^{-1},
    simultaneously conjugate alpha to the restricted g, then extend all
    factors by fixed points. This preserves both required cycle types.
15. **Even targets.** With u=v=q>=5, the strict interval implies the
    floor inequality in Brenner--Evans--Silberger Lemma5. Both factors
    have orderq; no assumption about a single alternating-group class
    or the splitting of symmetric-group classes is needed.
16. **Root orders.** For odd r and q not dividing rs, order2q is coprime
    to r, and orderq is coprime to s. The corresponding assertions for
    even targets hold as well.
17. **Negative roots.** Bezout inverses modulo finite element orders
    give (A^R)^r=A and (B^S)^s=B regardless of exponent signs.
18. **Interchanging exponent positions.** The explicit conjugation
    identity in the proof preserves the target and places the powers
    in the requested order. It is independently checked in the replay.
19. **Prime interval.** theta(n/2)-theta(3n/8+1) is exactly the sum
    over primes in the open-left, closed-right interval used by the
    factorization lemma.
20. **Analytic domains.** n>=1126 puts n/2>=563, where the published
    lower estimate is valid; the upper estimate applies at every
    positive real argument. The natural logarithm is used throughout.
21. **Rational margins.** The bounds e<11/4, (11/4)^6<563,
    1.01624<49/48 and all coefficient subtractions were checked exactly.
    No rounded numerical logarithm supports the proof.
22. **Strict contradiction.** The resulting prime sum exceeds n/16.
    If every interval prime divided m, log m would exceed n/16, even
    when n=16log m. Thus a missing prime exists.
23. **Asymptotic quantifiers.** For each fixed c>8, theta(z)~z gives
    one threshold N_0(c) valid for all later n. It depends only on c,
    not on r,s. The claim makes no assertion at c=8.
24. **Scope of conclusion.** The roots can have two disjoint cycles;
    the Notebook word problem imposes no single-cycle restriction.
    Sufficient constants are given, with no optimality claim.
25. **Proof versus evidence.** Imported published/preprint theorems
    and the above deductions prove the unbounded statement. The
    finite certificates test signs, cycle types, support parameters,
    ordering and arithmetic; they do not verify the imported theorems.

## Finite checks and failed runs

The initial character-table exploration covers3,621 odd target classes
at15 pairs (n,q),6<=n<=25, for the weaker floor interval. All coefficients
are positive. It separately checks every A5 element as a product of
two elements of order dividing3. This does not prove the weaker
interval lemma in general.

The final GAP computation searches for actual factors in every
symmetric-group conjugacy class at the seven eligible pairs
(10,5),(14,7),(15,7),(22,11),(23,11),(24,11),(25,11). Its fixed-seed,
bounded randomized search finds6,143 witnesses in978,066 trials.
Of these,3,047 targets are odd (3,040 positive-genus cases and seven
transpositions), and3,096 are even. Both exponent signs and both
orders of the exponents give49,144 exact root identities.

`replay_10_32.py` uses its own integer partitions, tuple permutations,
cycle decompositions, powers and modular inverses. It verifies the
complete expected set of target types, uniqueness, all products and
root identities, and six rational estimates. GAP lists partitions
in ascending order; the replay normalizes only the lookup key while
retaining the original order for the target permutation.

The first attempt used the nonexistent GAP global InverseMod, emitted
warnings and an error, and was rejected despite GAP's actual exit0.
Its script, transcript, status and partial output are retained under
`10.32-first-failed-*`. The second completed all mathematics but GAP
inserted backslash/newline formatting into the output string. Its
status records mathematical completion only; the independent JSON
parser rejected that artifact. The second script, status, transcript
and raw text remain under `10.32-second-format-*`.

The final script disables formatting on an explicit output stream.
Its actual process exit is0, the log is clean, the sentinel is present,
and independent JSON parsing/replay passes. The transcript counts are
identical to the second run because the random seed and mathematics
are unchanged. `results/10.32-summary.json` binds the final evidence
and retained failures to the proof by SHA256. Packet auditing checks
integrity and finite completion, not the mathematical theorem itself.
