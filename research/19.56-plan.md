## Historical working route before completion

The notes below preserve the initial unresolved steps. Each was
subsequently completed in the proof and review linked above.

The Notebook assumes |ab|>=|a||b| for coprime-order star-commutators,
where a star-commutator is [x,y] with x,y of distinct-prime-power
orders. The order of the output need not be a prime power.

Monakhov's 2017 theorem, arXiv:1704.01746v1, concerns ordinary
commutators whose outputs have prime-power orders. The restrictions
are different. The full four-page preprint has been read. Its
normalizer lemma and minimal-counterexample strategy motivate the
following attempt; they do not directly solve this problem.

1. Normalizer lemma: if a is a star-commutator of q-power order and
   R is a p-group, p!=q, normalized by a, then a centralizes R.
   For y in R, [a,y] is another star-commutator and lies in R;
   a[a,y]=a^y contradicts the order inequality unless [a,y]=1.
   This elementary lemma is checked.
2. Possible replacement for ordinary focal generation: the hyperfocal
   subgroup theorem should imply that, in a finite perfect group,
   each Sylow q-subgroup is generated by q-elements which are
   star-commutators. Verify the exact theorem, lift p'-automorphisms
   to p'-elements of normalizers, and split these into commuting
   prime-power parts. This is an imported-theorem dependency still
   being checked, not an assumption already certified.
3. In a smallest nonsoluble example, G is perfect and G/Phi(G) is
   minimal simple. For every Sylow p-subgroup R of Phi(G), Step 2
   and Step 1 would make all other-prime Sylows centralize R.
   The centralizer is normal with p-power index; perfection then
   implies R central. Consequently Phi(G)=Z(G).
4. To finish, it would suffice to construct a star-commutator a of
   2-power order with aZ(G) a nonidentity involution. Baer–Suzuki
   in the simple quotient yields a dihedral subgroup of order 2r,
   r odd prime, containing this involution. Its central preimage
   has a normal Sylow r-subgroup; Step 1 then contradicts the
   inversion action in the quotient.
5. A candidate way to obtain a in Step 4: every group on Thompson's
   minimal-simple list has a nontrivial elementary abelian 2-subgroup
   normalized nontrivially by an odd-prime-power element. The PSL2
   even, PSL2 odd, PSL3(3), and Suzuki cases must each be justified,
   including lifting the two inputs through the central kernel.
