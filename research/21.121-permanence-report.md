## Result

The [proof](21.121-permanence-proof.md) shows that a p-Jordan bound
J|G_(p)|^e can be realized by a **characteristic** abelian p'-subgroup,
with bound J^3|G_(p)|^e. Solubility is unnecessary for this deduction.
The proof applies the bound first to G and then to O_(p')(G), and
uses the Chermak--Delgado construction only inside the latter.

It follows that finite-index subgroups have exactly the same set
of admissible exponents as their ambient groups, including the
attainment or nonattainment of the infimum. More generally this
holds for a normal subgroup whose quotient has bounded finite
subgroups. Quotienting by a finite normal p-subgroup also preserves
the full admissible set. A classical infinite extraspecial group
shows why an arbitrary finite central kernel cannot replace the
p-kernel in this assertion.

All finite constants, normality steps and the convention allowing
negative exponents are handled in the proof. These results supply
reductions for part (b); they do not prove its universal bound three.

Lemma 2.8 already proves preservation of p-Jordanity under an
extension with bounded finite subgroups in the quotient. Its displayed
bound uses exponent B e. The present deduction preserves e itself,
by first obtaining a characteristic subgroup inside the finite
intersection with the kernel. The source's preceding discussion
correctly warns that applying Chermak--Delgado in a mixed-order group
does not control the resulting subgroup's divisibility. Our construction
avoids that use by applying it in O_(p')(G).

The introduction also states that the converse from generalized
p-Jordan to p-Jordan does not hold. No example or proof of that
assertion was identified in the sections read here. We neither use
that assertion nor claim its negation. Our soluble theorem imposes
a separate hypothesis, and Theorem 1 of this follow-up starts with
an actual p-Jordan bound. None of these deductions asserts the
unrestricted converse.

## Same-agent review and existing controls

The internal review checked the following possible failure points:

1. A normal abelian p'-subgroup of G is contained in O_(p')(G).
   The latter is itself a subgroup of the original ambient group,
   so the same constant applies to it with Sylow p-order one.
2. Characteristic in O_(p')(G), then characteristic in G, gives the
   needed invariance. For an ambient extension, characteristic in
   the finite normal intersection implies normal in the full finite
   subgroup; no transitivity of ordinary normality is assumed.
3. A finite-index subgroup need not be normal. Its core has bounded
   index, and the original bound restricts to that core.
4. For negative e the p-part of the finite index produces the
   extra bounded factor max(1,B^(-e)); the exponent is unchanged.
5. For a finite p-kernel, a preimage of a finite subgroup is finite.
   In the reverse direction O_(p')(M) maps injectively into the
   abelian quotient M/P_0, so it really is abelian and is normal in G.
6. The infinite extraspecial boundary example has a fixed central
   kernel of order q different from p. Its finite subgroups E_d
   have unbounded minimum abelian indices despite trivial Sylow
   p-subgroups. This establishes the claimed failure with a general
   finite kernel.
