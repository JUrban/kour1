# Problem18.120: an abelian-normal-closure criterion

12 September2026. Complete elementary partial, not a general answer.

Let G=AB, where A is abelian and B has nilpotency class at most two.
No finiteness assumption is needed in the following criterion. Put
K=<A,B'> and C=< (B')^a : a in A >. The subgroup C is normalized
by A and contains B', so it is normal in K and K=AC. In fact
C=(B')^G: write G=BA and use normality of B' in B.

**Criterion.** If C is abelian, then B0=K intersect B is abelian and
K=AB0.

**Proof.** Any x in K intersect B has the form ac, with a in A and
c in C. Since x lies in B, it centralizes B'. Since C is abelian,
c also centralizes B', and hence a centralizes B'. The commutativity
of A implies that a centralizes every A-conjugate of B', and therefore
all of C. Thus, for any two elements ac,a'c' in K intersect B,
both a and a' centralize C. Since A and C are abelian, these two
elements commute. Finally, any k in K can be written k=ab using
G=AB; as a belongs to K, b=a^-1 k belongs to K intersect B.
Consequently K=A(K intersect B). QED.

**Corollary.** The answer to18.120 is affirmative if G has nilpotency
class at most four, even without A intersect B=1.

**Proof.** Write gamma_i=gamma_i(G). For b,c in B' and a,d in A,
we have b^a=b u and c^d=c v with u,v in gamma_3. Since B' lies in
gamma_2, the cross-commutators [b,v] and [u,c] lie in gamma_5=1,
and [u,v] lies in gamma_6=1. Also [b,c]=1 because B' is abelian.
Hence b^a and c^d commute. All generators of C commute, so apply
the criterion. QED.

Another sufficient case is when B is subnormal in G with defect at
most two. This is a direct consequence of the prior McCann2016
Lemma4: C is abelian in that case. The short argument is as follows.
Choose B normal in N normal in G. Since N=(N intersect A)B,
N/B is abelian, so N'<=B. Then C<=N'<=B. Consequently B'<=Z(C);
normality of C makes Z(C) normal in G, so all conjugates of B'
lie in Z(C), proving C abelian. In a finite p-group, index at most
p^2 implies subnormal defect at most two, giving that further case.
These conclusions do not prove abelianness of C in general.
