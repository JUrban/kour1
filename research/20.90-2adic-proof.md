# 20.90: a separate four-generator 2-adic construction

Status: complete companion candidate proof. This answers the same Notebook
entry as `20.90-proof.md`, so it is not counted as another problem solved.
It gives a compact 2-adic analytic CN-group; abelian centralizers are not
asserted for this example. Outside review and priority checks remain pending.

## Construction

Let O=Z2[omega], where omega^2+omega+1=0, and let K=Q2(omega). The
polynomial is irreducible modulo2 and hence over Q2. Thus O embeds in
the field K. As a topological Z2-module it is Z2+Z2 omega, and O/2O=F4.
It is a compact local ring with maximal ideal2O: an element nonzero in
F4 has an inverse modulo2, which lifts by the convergent geometric series
for 1+2a. Put

    S=SL2(O),         H=S/{I,-I}.

Congruence kernels S_n={s in S : s=I modulo2^n}, n>=1, are open normal
subgroups with trivial intersection. Their successive quotients embed
in the additive group M2(F4), by I+2^n A -> A modulo2. Consequently S_1
is pro-2. Compactness and the congruence kernels show S and H are profinite.

## Four topological generators, infinitude and non-prosolubility

The upper and lower elementary matrices

    U(1), U(omega), L(1), L(omega)

topologically generate S. Indeed their integer powers and products give
U(a) and L(a) for a in Z+Z omega, which is dense in O. Closure therefore
gives U(a),L(a) for every a in O. Elementary matrices generate SL2(O):
one entry of a primitive first column is a unit, because O is local;
interchanging rows if necessary and adding multiples of one row to the
other reduces to a diagonal matrix. For every unit a this diagonal matrix
is elementary, using

    W=U(1)L(-1)U(1)=[[0,1],[-1,0]],
    U(a)L(-a^(-1))U(a)W^(-1)=diag(a,a^(-1)).

Thus H is topologically generated by at most four elements. The image
of U(1) in H has infinite order: for a nonzero integer n, U(n) is neither
I nor -I in characteristic zero.

Reduction maps S onto SL2(F4), since all elementary matrices lift. Both
I and -I reduce to I, so the map factors through H. The finite quotient
SL2(F4) is nontrivial perfect, as explicitly proved in `20.90-proof.md`.
Thus H is not prosoluble.

## Centralizers in the projective quotient

Take a nonidentity h in H and any lift g in S. Then g is nonscalar:
a scalar of determinant1 over K is I or -I, both representing1 in H.
The matrix algebra lemma proved in `20.90-proof.md` gives

    C_S(g) <= K[g]^*,

so C_S(g) is abelian. The preimage in S of C_H(h) is

    P_g={s in S : s g s^(-1) is g or -g}.

The sign gives a homomorphism P_g -> {1,-1}, with kernel C_S(g).
We treat the two trace cases separately.

If tr(g) != 0, trace invariance rules out the negative sign, because
tr(g)=-tr(g) would imply tr(g)=0 in characteristic zero. Therefore
C_H(h)=C_S(g)/{I,-I}, an abelian profinite group, hence pronilpotent.

Suppose now tr(g)=0. We claim its reduction g_bar in SL2(F4) is not I.
If g=I+2A for a matrix A over O, trace zero gives tr(A)=-1. The exact
two-dimensional determinant identity gives

    1=det(I+2A)=1+2 tr(A)+4 det(A)=1-2+4 det(A),

so 2 det(A)=1, which is impossible in O. This proves the claim.

Cayley--Hamilton in characteristic2 shows (g_bar-I)^2=0. Thus g_bar is
a nonidentity unipotent. Its centralizer in SL2(F4) has order4: after a
change of basis the matrix is U(1), and a commuting determinant-one
matrix must have form [[a,b],[0,a]], with a^2=1. In F4 this forces a=1,
and there are four choices for b. The change of basis may be in GL2(F4);
it still preserves determinants and the centralizer size.

Reduction of C_S(g) is a subgroup of this order4 group, and its kernel
is contained in the pro-2 congruence subgroup S_1. It follows that
C_S(g) is pro-2. Since P_g/C_S(g) has order at most2, P_g is pro-2 too.
Finally C_H(h)=P_g/{I,-I} is pro-2. A pro-2 group is pronilpotent.

Every nonidentity centralizer in H is therefore either abelian or pro-2.
This proves that H is a finitely generated infinite profinite CN-group
which is not prosoluble. QED.

## Audit notes

- The quotient by {I,-I} is essential: otherwise the nonidentity central
  element -I would have the nonsoluble whole group as its centralizer.
- An abelian centralizer of a lift does not by itself imply an abelian
  projective centralizer. The possible sign change is explicitly included.
- At trace zero, reduction is nonidentity by the determinant argument;
  trace zero alone would not justify this assertion.
- The reduction centralizer has order4, and the kernel is pro-2. These
  are the two separate inputs needed for pro-2-ness of the strict
  centralizer. Extending it by a group of order at most2 preserves that.
- No ramification calculation in K(i), nor classification of p-adic Lie
  groups, is used. The local ring and matrix arguments above suffice.
- `scripts/verify_20_90_2adic.py` independently enumerates all65,536
  matrices over O/4O. It finds3,840 determinant-one matrices, generated
  by the four stated elementary matrices, and checks that all240
  trace-zero matrices have nonidentity reduction modulo2. The reduction
  image has order60 and its kernel has order64. The exact output is
  retained in `results/20.90-2adic-controls.log`; all checks pass.
- The general literature and exact source question are documented in
  `20.90-proof.md`. The2019 source's PSL2(Z2) example has soluble quotient
  SL2(F2); here the residue field is F4, and the conclusion needed is CN.
