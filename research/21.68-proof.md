# A semi-abelian non-monomial group of order 2592

Status: candidate negative resolution of Notebook 21.68, developed
10 September 2026. Internal proof audit and two independent exact
computations pass; see `21.68-review.md`. Further novelty checking and
outside review remain.
The argument is finite and does not depend on a group database.

## The group of order 96 and its subgroup of order 24

Let E be the even-weight subspace of F_2^4. Let A_4 act on E by
permuting coordinates, and put K=E semidirect A_4, of order 8*12=96.
We use left actions and multiplication

    (v,g)(w,h)=(v+g(w),gh).

Write z=(1111,1), and set

    x=(1010,(12)(34)),
    y=(1001,(13)(24)),
    r=(0000,(234)).

Here a permutation acts on the support of a vector in the usual way.
Direct calculation gives

    x^2=y^2=(xy)^2=z,  z^2=1,
    r^3=1,  r x r^(-1)=y,  r y r^(-1)=xy.

The first relations imply yx=zxy. The eight elements
1,z,x,zx,y,zy,xy,zxy are distinct: their permutation parts form the
Klein four-group, with two different vectors above each part. Thus
Q=<x,y> is quaternion of order 8. Conjugation by r preserves Q and
cycles x,y,xy. Consequently H=<x,y,r>=Q semidirect <r> has order 24.

Its abelianization has order 3. Indeed, in the abelianization the two
conjugation relations give x=y=xy, hence x=y=1, while the quotient by
Q is C_3. In particular H has no subgroup of index 2.

H has an irreducible complex representation of degree 2. Identify Q
with the quaternion units {+/-1,+/-i,+/-j,+/-k}, sending x to i and y
to j. The unit

    s=(-1-i-j-k)/2

has order 3 and conjugates i to j and j to k. Thus it represents r.
Embed the quaternions into 2-by-2 complex matrices by

    i -> diag(sqrt(-1),-sqrt(-1)),
    j -> [[0,1],[-1,0]].

The restriction to Q is irreducible: its possible invariant lines under
i are the coordinate lines, which j interchanges. Denote this
representation of H by rho.

## An abelian extension with a prescribed character stabilizer

Let Omega be the four left cosets K/H, with the natural left K-action.
Let

    B={b in F_3^Omega : sum_(omega in Omega) b_omega=0}.

K acts on B by coordinate permutations. Define G=B semidirect K.
Then |B|=27 and |G|=27*96=2592.

This group is semi-abelian under the exact definition in 21.68: there
is a subgroup chain

    1 < C_3 < A_4 < K < G,

where A_4=V_4 semidirect C_3, K=E semidirect A_4, and
G=B semidirect K. At each step the new normal factor is abelian.
No quotient operation is even required.

Fix the point omega_0=H and a primitive complex cube root zeta. Define
the linear character lambda(b)=zeta^(b_omega_0) of B. The four coordinate
characters restricted to B are distinct. For two distinct points u,v,
choose a third point w and a vector with b_u=1,b_w=-1 and all other
coordinates zero; the u and v evaluations differ. Therefore the K-orbit
of lambda has exactly four elements and its stabilizer is exactly H.
Its inertia subgroup in G is I=B semidirect H.

The character lambda extends to I by lambda_tilde(b,h)=lambda(b),
because H fixes it. Form the degree-2 representation

    tau(b,h)=lambda(b) rho(h),

and let V=Ind_I^G tau, of dimension 8. This representation is irreducible.
Here is a direct argument. Its restriction to B has four distinct weight
characters, each with a two-dimensional weight space. G permutes these
spaces transitively. Every G-invariant subspace splits into B-weight
spaces, by the elementary averaging projections for the finite abelian
group B. In the lambda-weight space the stabilizer H acts by the
irreducible representation rho. Thus any nonzero invariant subspace
contains that whole weight space after translating, and then contains
all four. This proves irreducibility.

## This degree-8 irreducible representation is not monomial

Suppose instead that V=Ind_L^G mu for a linear character mu of a subgroup
L. Comparing dimensions gives [G:L]=8. Since B is a normal 3-group,

    [B:B intersect L]=[BL:L]

is a power of 3 dividing [G:L]=8. Hence B is contained in L.

In the induced representation, restriction to B is the sum of the
G-conjugates of mu restricted to B, using a transversal of L. Therefore
mu restricted to B is one of the four known weights of V. Conjugating
L and mu if necessary, we can assume mu restricted to B=lambda.

For l in L and b in B, a one-dimensional character satisfies
mu(l b l^(-1))=mu(b). Thus L stabilizes lambda and L is contained in
I. It follows that

    [I:L]=[G:L]/[G:I]=8/4=2.

As B is contained in L, L/B would be an index-2 subgroup of I/B=H.
This contradicts the abelianization computation above.

Thus G is a finite semi-abelian group with a non-monomial irreducible
character, giving a negative answer to 21.68.

## Source comparison and scope

The exact Notebook statement was visually checked on supplied PDF page177.
Kida, *On semiabelian groups*, J. Group Theory28 (2025),697--712,
https://doi.org/10.1515/jgth-2024-0010 , Conjecture1.3, asks the same
question. The published paper was downloaded from
https://d-nb.info/1364524341/34 and its introduction, definition, relevant
lemmas, and Example5.5 inspected. It reports checking the conjecture
through order240 and explicitly notes that the semi-abelian group
SmallGroup(96,204) contains SL(2,3). Our general extension idea was
developed before reading that example; the example then supplied a small
base group, realized here without relying on its database ID.

The new ingredient claimed here is the abelian extension producing the
degree-8 non-monomial character. No priority or independent-review claim
is made. Targeted searches have not yet located an earlier resolution.
