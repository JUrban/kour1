# 20.122(b), and its soluble version in (c): a counterexample

September 12, 2026. Complete elementary negative candidate for these two
assertions. Part (a), including its soluble version, is not resolved here.
Priority and outside review remain pending.

The printed statement is on page 165 of `docs/21tkt.pdf`. The page image
was visually checked. It imposes no requirement that the three nilpotent
subgroups be equal, conjugate, or maximal nilpotent subgroups.

## The example

Write permutations on the right, so that a product applies its left factor
first. In the permutation group on six letters put

\[
r=(1\,2\,3\,4),\qquad s=(2\,4),\qquad z=(5\,6),
\]
\[
G=S_{\{1,2,3,4\}}\times\langle z\rangle,
\qquad A=\langle r,s,z\rangle,
\qquad B=\langle rz,s\rangle,
\qquad C=\langle r,z\rangle.
\]

Then

\[
|G|=48,\quad A\cong D_8\times C_2,\quad B\cong D_8,
\quad C\cong C_4\times C_2.
\]

Here subscripts give group orders. Indeed, conjugation by `s` inverts `r`
and `rz`; the first four coordinates show that their generated dihedral
groups have order eight. The factor `z` is independent of the first four
coordinates. Thus all three input subgroups are nilpotent 2-groups.

Let

\[
t_1=r^2=(1\,3)(2\,4),\quad
t_2=(1\,2)(3\,4),\quad t_3=(1\,4)(2\,3),
\quad V=\{1,t_1,t_2,t_3\}.
\]

We will prove the exact identities

\[
\boxed{\quad
\min_G(A,B,C)=F(G)=V\times\langle z\rangle
\ <\ \operatorname{Min}_G(A,B,C)=A.
\quad}
\]

## All conjugates and all intersections

For each `i` let `P_i=C_{S_4}(t_i)`. It has order eight: a permutation
commuting with the two disjoint transpositions may interchange their two
supports and independently switch each support. Its elements consist of
`V`, two transpositions, and two mutually inverse 4-cycles. In particular,
the `P_i` are the three Sylow 2-subgroups of `S_4`, and

\[
P_i\cap P_j=V\quad(i\ne j).
\]

Their two transpositions have disjoint supports and product `t_i`.
Consequently, these two transpositions together with `1,t_i` form a
subgroup `K_i` of index two in `P_i`. Define the homomorphism
`chi_i:P_i -> C_2` to have kernel `K_i`. It takes value zero at `t_i`
and value one at the other two nonidentity elements of `V` and at the
4-cycles of `P_i`.

The three conjugates of `B` in `G` are exactly

\[
B_i=\{p z^{\chi_i(p)}:p\in P_i\},\qquad i=1,2,3.
\]

To justify completeness, `B=B_1`, since `chi_1(r)=1` and `chi_1(s)=0`.
Conjugation permutes the double transpositions, their centralizers, and
the transpositions in those centralizers; therefore it permutes precisely
these three graphs. All three occur because the double transpositions
are conjugate. Central conjugation by `z` changes nothing.

Choose either 4-cycle `r_i` in `P_i`, with `r_1=r`. Its square is `t_i`.
The three conjugates of `C` are exactly

\[
C_i=\langle r_i\rangle\times\langle z\rangle,
\qquad i=1,2,3.
\]

The cyclic subgroup is independent of the choice between the two inverse
4-cycles. The same conjugation argument proves completeness.

Put `L(u)=<u>` for the involutions used below, and put `R=<rz>`.
Because `A=P_1 x <z>`, the complete intersection table is

| `A intersect B_i intersect C_j` | `j=1` | `j=2` | `j=3` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `i=1` | `R` | `L(t_2 z)` | `L(t_3 z)` |
| `i=2` | `L(t_1 z)` | `L(t_2)` | `L(t_3 z)` |
| `i=3` | `L(t_1 z)` | `L(t_2 z)` | `L(t_3)` |

Here is a direct derivation of every entry. For `(i,j)=(1,1)`, restricting
the graph of `chi_1` to `<r>` gives exactly `<rz>`. If `j=1` and `i!=1`,
the projection of the intersection to `S_4` is contained in
`P_i intersect <r>={1,t_1}`. The graph selects `t_1 z`, since
`chi_i(t_1)=1`. If `j!=1`, first intersecting with `A` gives
`A intersect C_j=<t_j,z>`. Intersecting this subgroup with `B_i` leaves
`<t_j>` when `i=j`, and `<t_j z>` otherwise. This proves the table
without relying on a subgroup catalogue or any computation.

## The two notions of minimum

Thus the family of distinct intersections is exactly

\[
\mathcal F=\{R,L(t_1z),L(t_2),L(t_2z),L(t_3),L(t_3z)\}.
\]

The five `L` groups have order two and are distinct. The cyclic group
`R` has order four and its unique subgroup of order two is `L(t_1)`.
That subgroup is absent from the displayed family. Hence **all six
members are minimal by inclusion**, while exactly the five `L` groups
have minimum order. This checks minimality in the full family, allowing
all independent `x,y in G` as required by the problem.

The five groups of order two generate `V x <z>`: their generators include
`t_2,t_3,t_2 z`, hence also `z` and `t_1=t_2 t_3`. Adding `R` gives `r`
and therefore gives `A`, since `<r,V>=P_1`. This proves the two claimed
minimum-subgroup identities.

## Solubility and the Fitting subgroup

The normal chain `1 < V < A_4 < S_4` has abelian factors, so `G` is
soluble. Also `F(S_4)=V`. For completeness, the conjugacy-class sizes
in `S_4` are `1,6,3,8,6`, for identity, transpositions, double
transpositions, 3-cycles and 4-cycles respectively. A normal subgroup is
a union of these classes and its order divides 24. The only possible
orders are `1,4,12,24`, giving `1,V,A_4,S_4`. The last two groups are
not nilpotent, since their subgroups of order three are not normal.
This proves the assertion about `F(S_4)`.

The image in `S_4` of any normal nilpotent subgroup of `G` is normal and
nilpotent, and is therefore contained in `V`. Conversely, `V x <z>` is
abelian and normal. Thus `F(G)=V x <z>` as claimed. In particular, `rz`
belongs to an inclusion-minimal intersection and does not belong to
`F(G)`. The answer to (b), and to its soluble restriction in (c), is no.

## Independent finite verification and scope

`scripts/verify_20_122_native.g` constructs the stated permutations
directly in GAP, checks nilpotency, solubility and the Fitting subgroup,
and enumerates all 2,304 ordered conjugating pairs.
`scripts/verify_20_122_permutations.py` separately constructs the 48
permutations and all 2,304 multiplication entries without GAP or a
catalogue. It checks all 110,592 associativity triples, enumerates all
98 subgroups, derives the nine normal subgroups and four normal
nilpotent subgroups, and recovers the same Fitting subgroup and full
intersection family. All exported data agree. Five deliberately corrupt
certificates are rejected. These checks supplement the elementary proof.

The related prior pair-intersection example and the limited novelty
search are recorded in `20.122-source-audit.md`. The present result
does not answer the minimum-order assertion in (a), or that assertion
for soluble groups. It is counted as one problem/subpart candidate,
not as two separate new results.
