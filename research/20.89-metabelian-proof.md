# Almost Engel elements in metabelian and virtually abelian groups

This extends the partial coverage of20.89 /19.83. The full linear-group
question remains open here. There is no new complete-candidate count
and no established priority. The metabelian sink-normality lemma and
product argument already occur in Khukhro--Shumyatsky2017, Lemma2.3
and the proof of Theorem4.1. Section1 is a self-contained rediscovery.
The virtually abelian assertion below is a consequence of
Shumyatsky2016, Lemma3.2, explicitly credited below.

We use [x,a]=x^-1 a^-1 x a. An almost Engel element has one finite
set containing every sufficiently late term of every sequence
x,[x,a],[x,a,a],...; the starting time may depend on x. Write E(a)
for its minimal sink. For any self-map with a finite common eventual
sink, its minimal sink consists exactly of all periodic points. Indeed,
every orbit eventually enters a cycle in the finite sink, and every
periodic point must belong to every eventual sink. In particular the
self-map permutes its minimal sink.

## 1. An elementary metabelian theorem

**Theorem.** Let G be any metabelian group, with no linearity or
finiteness assumption. Its almost Engel elements form a characteristic
subgroup. Each minimal sink E(a) is a finite normal subgroup of G, and

    E(a^-1)=E(a),       E(ab) <= E(a)E(b)

whenever a and b are almost Engel.

Put A=G', written additively, and let alpha_g(x)=x^g on A. The
automorphisms alpha_g commute: their commutators are induced by
elements of G', which acts trivially on A. Put delta_g=alpha_g-1.
On A the commutator map x->[x,g] is the group endomorphism delta_g.

Every first commutator in G lies in A. Consequently g is almost Engel
in G exactly when delta_g on A has a finite common eventual sink;
the two minimal sinks agree. One direction restricts starting points
to A. For the other, apply the restricted assertion after the first
commutator. This also proves equality of the two minimal sinks.

The periodic points of an endomorphism of an abelian group form a
subgroup: if delta^r(x)=x and delta^s(y)=y, use the common period
lcm(r,s) for x-y. Hence E(g) is a finite subgroup of A. Every
alpha_h commutes with delta_g and therefore preserves its periodic
points. Thus E(g) is normal in G.

Let a,b be almost Engel and K=E(a)+E(b). This is a finite normal
subgroup. On A/K the commuting endomorphisms delta_a and delta_b
are locally nilpotent: each vector is killed by some power of each.
Moreover

    delta_ab = delta_a + delta_b + delta_a delta_b.

For x in A/K choose i,j>=1 with delta_a^i(x)=delta_b^j(x)=0.
Every monomial in the expansion of delta_ab^(i+j-1) has total
degree at least i+j-1. One of its two exponents is therefore at
least i or j. Commutativity makes that monomial vanish on x.
Thus delta_ab is locally nilpotent. After the first commutator,
every ab-Engel sequence in G consequently enters K and stays in K.
This proves that ab is almost Engel and E(ab)<=K.

For inverses,

    delta_(a^-1) = -alpha_a^-1 delta_a.

These factors commute, so local nilpotence modulo E(a) is preserved.
It follows that a^-1 is almost Engel with E(a^-1)<=E(a). Apply the
same argument to a^-1 to obtain equality. The identity is Engel;
closure, inverse closure, and invariance under automorphisms prove
the characteristic-subgroup assertion. This proof is elementary
and does not require a theorem about Engel elements in soluble groups.

## 2. A general finite-kernel criterion

Say a group N is finite-by-(locally nilpotent) when it has a finite
normal subgroup K with N/K locally nilpotent. Such an N has a
**finite characteristic** subgroup with this property. Choose K of
least order among the finite normal subgroups with that property.
For any automorphism sigma, the quotient N/(K intersect K^sigma)
embeds in N/K times N/K^sigma. Finite direct products and subgroups
of locally nilpotent groups are locally nilpotent. Minimality of |K|
therefore forces K=K intersect K^sigma; equality of orders gives
K=K^sigma. This proves the characteristic assertion.

Suppose that in a group G every almost Engel element a has normal
closure N_a=<a^G> finite-by-(locally nilpotent). Choose the finite
characteristic kernel K_a just described. Since N_a is normal in G,
K_a is normal in G. Modulo K_a the first commutator [x,a] lies in
N_a/K_a, and this element together with a generates a nilpotent
subgroup. Thus every a-Engel sequence eventually enters K_a.

For two almost Engel elements a,b put K=K_a K_b, finite and normal
in G. The images of N_a,N_b in G/K are normal locally nilpotent
subgroups. Their product is locally nilpotent, by the usual
Hirsch--Plotkin radical theorem. It contains ab, and the same
first-commutator argument proves that K is an ab-sink in G.
The kernel K_a also works for a^-1. Thus the almost Engel elements
form a characteristic subgroup, and each lies in

    union { preimage of HP(G/K) : K finite and normal in G }.

Conversely every element of this union is almost Engel, by the same
argument. This gives an equality under the stated normal-closure
hypothesis. We do not assert that this hypothesis holds in all
linear groups.

## 3. Virtually abelian groups: a prior-theorem consequence

Shumyatsky, *Almost Engel linear groups*, arXiv:1610.03126v1,
Lemma3.2 (PDF pages5--7), says: if G=H<a>, H is a virtually abelian
normal subgroup, and a is almost Engel, then <a^G> is
finite-by-(locally nilpotent). The lemma does not assume linearity.
For any virtually abelian G we may take H=G; the statement does not
require H to be proper. Section2 therefore applies.

**Corollary (prior consequence).** In every virtually abelian group
the almost Engel elements form a characteristic subgroup and are
exactly the union displayed in Section2. Each minimal sink is
contained in a finite normal subgroup of the whole ambient group.

The minimal sink itself need not be normal in a general virtually
abelian group: every finite group is virtually abelian. This is why
Section2 uses a finite characteristic kernel, not an assertion that
each sink is already normal. The stronger assertion in Section1
depends on metabelianity.

## 4. Exact infinite dihedral controls

Let A be any abelian group, written additively, and let
G=A semidirect <t>, where t has order2 and x^t=-x. Rotations in A
are Engel: their first commutators lie in A and their second are1.
For t, the first commutator of x is -2x and that of xt is 2x. Thereafter
the commutator map is delta(x)=-2x. The same conclusion, with a
translated starting coordinate, holds for every reflection at.

**Proposition.** The reflections are almost Engel if and only if A
is torsion and its odd-primary part A_odd is finite. In that case
the minimal sink of every reflection is exactly A_odd. Otherwise
the almost Engel subgroup is precisely A.

For necessity, a finite eventual sink makes each delta-orbit
eventually periodic. Thus each x is killed by
2^n((-2)^k-1), for some n>=0 and k>=1; this nonzero integer proves
that A is torsion. Every odd-order element is periodic under -2,
since -2 acts invertibly on its finite cyclic subgroup. Hence all
of A_odd belongs to the finite minimal sink. For sufficiency write
A=A_2 direct-sum A_odd. Every element of A_2 is killed by a power
of2, while -2 permutes the finite A_odd. The periodic-point
description proves the claimed exact sink.

In particular Dih(C_(2^infinity) direct-sum C_15) has sink C_15
for every reflection, with no uniform bound on when all starting
points enter it. Dih(C_(3^infinity)) has no almost Engel reflection:
its finite subgroups have reflection sinks of unbounded size.
Both are metabelian linear groups over C, using diagonal matrices
diag(z,z^-1) and the matrix interchanging the coordinates. These
infinite conclusions follow from the proof, not finite experiments.

## 5. Scope of the verification

The accompanying native GAP and independent integer computations
check the finite sink identities and normality on eight explicit
metabelian models. They also test the dihedral sink formula over a
family of cyclic groups, and reject the stronger false claims that
all sinks in all finite groups are normal subgroups, that an almost
Engel element must have trivial sink, or that finite dihedral tests
give a uniform sink in the infinite union. Exact finite checks
support the formulas; Sections1--4 prove the infinite assertions.
