## Logical audit

1. **Exact question.** The supplied PDF, printed/physical p.43, asks for a
   group generated by one conjugacy class of involutions with odd pairwise
   product orders and no subgroup of index two; periodicity is the stronger
   parenthesized request. The proposed group is periodic.
2. **Initial group.** F=C2*C2*C2 has the concrete trivalent Cayley tree. The
   action is on the metric realization, so an involution fixes an edge
   midpoint. Edge inversions cause no obstruction to Theorem 6.7, whose
   hypothesis is an isometric action on a length space, not a tree action
   without inversions. Barycentric subdivision would also remove inversions.
3. **Finite subgroups of F.** A finite subgroup fixes the center of a finite
   invariant subtree. Point stabilizers have size at most two. This proves
   the order-four exclusion directly.
4. **Acylindricity.** The uniform bound on elements moving one point a
   bounded distance follows from finite Cayley balls and free vertex action.
   It implies the required two-point bound.
5. **Non-elementarity.** The axes of ab and ac have different endpoint
   pairs; the action is not elementary.
6. **Tameness.** An axis stabilizer acts faithfully on a simplicial line.
   Its image is infinite cyclic or infinite dihedral and has trivial finite
   normal subgroup. Both parts of Definition 4.46 are checked.
7. **Injectivity radius.** Every infinite order element has cyclically
   reduced length at least two, and ab attains two. Rescaling leaves this
   radius positive.
8. **The parameter nu.** An elliptic subgroup generated by g and h^(-1)gh
   has at most two elements. If g!=1 it would be centralized by h, forcing
   the loxodromic h to fix its unique midpoint. Thus nu<=1 and tau=3.
9. **Omega and positive delta.** The first draft used the source's
   zero-hyperbolicity convention. The final proof instead takes a small
   positive delta, because Definition 4.28 has a strict inequality. The
   almost-fixed sets have radii 4 delta and their enlarged sets radii at
   most 17 delta. Distinct midpoints have distance at least one, and
   delta<1/100 makes the relevant intersections empty. Also L_S delta<1
   forces all elements in the defining tuples to be elliptic. This gives
   Omega=0 without relying on a degenerate definition at delta=0.
10. **Strong stability.** Q is all loxodromic elements, the explicitly
    permitted choice in Remark 6.6. It is not merely a chosen set of
    translations or a set of words of odd length.
11. **Primitivity.** The label pattern on the axis of abc has least period
    three. Its orientation-preserving stabilizer is exactly <abc>.
    The reverse pattern is not a cyclic shift, excluding a reflection.
    This establishes primitivity in the maximal loxodromic group, the
    source's actual definition, not just the absence of an apparent word
    power in a presentation.
12. **Prescribed relation.** Proposition 6.1 allows any maximal admissible
    selection. The eligible word abc can be included first. The quotient
    kills its p-th power. This feature is read from the construction, not
    invented as an extra conclusion of Theorem 6.7.
13. **Initial rescaling.** Remark 6.8 expressly permits a smaller scale and
    larger critical exponent. Take 3 lambda<=L_S delta_1 and the existing
    initialization inequalities. The positive injectivity-radius bound
    can be met by enlarging n_1 since its required lower bound tends to
    zero. No explicit value of n_1 is asserted.
14. **Parameter order.** The initial group, delta, and lambda are fixed
    before p. Any sufficiently large odd prime p can then be used. The
    condition p=3 mod 4 belongs to the paper's WST/affine application;
    Theorem 6.7 itself allows any large odd exponent.
15. **Generator survival.** Each of <a>,<b>,<c> is elliptic, so embeds in
    the final group. Quotient maps preserve the assertion that their images
    generate. Distinctness of these particular images is not assumed.
16. **Infinitude.** This is Theorem 6.7(4), with the allowed initial scale
    and maximal selection. No finite computation substitutes for it.
17. **Exponent.** Every element of F is elliptic or in Q. The former has
    order at most two; the latter maps to an element of order dividing 2p
    or to an elliptic image. Hence every element of G has order dividing
    2p. The prime hypothesis is used to make odd nonidentity product orders
    equal to p, not merely divisors of an odd integer.
18. **Order four at the limit.** This is not in the theorem's headline
    conclusion. Every stage is tame by Proposition 6.1. The explicit finite
    multiplication-table lifting argument embeds any hypothetical finite
    subgroup of the limit in a stage, excluding both C4 and V4.
19. **Even product obstruction.** If t=xy has even order, its middle power
    z is an involution commuting with x. The assertion x not in <t> follows
    because x and y are distinct involutions and a cyclic group has at most
    one involution. Thus <x,z> is V4. This also handles the case |t|=2.
20. **Every involution is covered.** The argument in item 19 is internal
    to G. It does not assume that an arbitrary involution is the image of
    an original involution. This avoids an unnecessary lifting hypothesis.
21. **Conjugacy.** With t=xy of odd order d, the explicit conjugator is
    t^((d+1)/2) under the convention x^g=g^(-1)xg. The sign/exponent were
    checked in actual dihedral permutation groups.
22. **Generation by one class.** All involutions form one class, and that
    class contains the three generating images. No assertion about the
    number of involution classes in the original free product is needed.
23. **No index two subgroup.** All generator images under a map to C2
    coincide; (abc)^p=1 for odd p kills that common value. Every index-two
    subgroup supplies just such a nontrivial map. Perfectness follows by
    the same argument in the whole abelianization.
24. **Exact exponent.** There are involutions, and at least two of them
    are distinct (otherwise G would be C2, contrary to infinitude or the
    odd relation). Their product has order p. Thus exp(G)=2p.
25. **Unneeded stronger statements.** The proof claims neither a simple
    group nor a sharply 2-transitive action, neither a finite presentation
    nor an explicit numerical exponent. These do not follow from the
    presented argument and are unnecessary for 10.62.
26. **Dependency boundary.** The deep input is Amelio's geometric quotient
    construction. Its applicable hypotheses and the additional features
    extracted from its proof have been audited; the underlying cone-off
    estimates have not been independently reproved here.

## Exact finite controls

Command: `python3 scripts/run_10_62_gap.py`.

The retained run returned actual process status 0, had no GAP error or
syntax warning, and ended with `PASS_1062_GAP`. Duration was about 3.3s.

- 50 odd exponents and 50 even exponents, n=2,...,101. For each, GAP
  computes the abelian invariants of both the pair-product presentation
  and the presentation with the extra relation (abc)^n. For odd n the
  invariants change from [2] to []; for even n both remain [2,2,2]. These
  are 200 finitely presented abelianization computations, not enumerations
  of the groups defined by those presentations.
- All 8 assignments of the three generators to C2 are checked separately.
  Pair-product constraints alone allow the zero and all-one assignments;
  the odd triple relation eliminates the latter.
- 21,318 ordered pairs of distinct reflections in actual permutation
  dihedral groups of degree 3 through 40. All 13,488 odd-product pairs
  satisfy the explicit conjugator formula; all 7,830 even-product pairs
  give a checked subgroup with order four and abelian invariants [2,2].
- A separate V4 control covers a pair whose product has order two.

The launcher `bin/gap` limits this extra job to a 4GiB GAP workspace. It
ran alongside the six pre-existing 14GiB certificate jobs. No geometric
claim, including infinitude, is inferred from these finite checks.
