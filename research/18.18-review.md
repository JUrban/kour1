## Audit of the symmetric-group sentence

1. The witness t is an ordinary group element. Its conjugacy class,
   noncommutation relation, four-tuple domain Q, incidence M and
   equality-of-incidence formula E are all first-order definable.
2. Vertex quantification is a fixed four-variable abbreviation, never
   quantification over arbitrary subsets. Five distinct vertices uses
   twenty variables. The quotient notation does not introduce a new
   sort into the final language: all formulas have explicit finite
   expansions using E and Q. Incidence is well-defined by the very
   definition of E.
3. Conjugation preserves these formulas, so it induces the vertex
   action without an additional group-action assumption. Uniqueness
   of an edge between two vertices identifies C_t with all two-element
   subsets. Thus a permutation fixing the vertices fixes C_t pointwise.
4. The trivial-centralizer axiom is essential for faithfulness. Without
   it a central direct factor could survive. It is itself a first-order
   sentence, even though generating the group by an unbounded number
   of transpositions would not be a permissible first-order shortcut.
5. Each c fixes itself under conjugation, hence permutes its own two
   endpoints. The action axiom forces both to move and every other
   vertex to be fixed. Thus every pair's transposition lies in the
   faithful image, which is the whole finite symmetric group.
6. In S_n, the four-clique argument excludes the three-edge triangle.
   This is why four entries and n>=5 are used. The incidence sets are
   exactly the stars at points, with no additional vertices. The
   exceptional outer automorphism of S6 causes no problem: the sentence
   only requires an appropriate conjugacy class to exist, not uniqueness.

## Centralizer and computability audit

7. H need not be two-generated. All its elements are placed in separate
   levels, and two permutations encode their right multiplications.
   The fixed set of the second permutation distinguishes level zero;
   the first ties all levels to the same map u. The centralizer is
   exactly the left regular copy of H, not merely a group containing H.
8. The two padded orbits have different sizes. Therefore no commuting
   permutation can exchange them. The second orbit is a full symmetric
   action with trivial centralizer. This produces the prescribed H in
   every S_n after one threshold, which is stronger than occurrence in
   infinitely many S_n and is necessary for the cofinite conclusion.
9. A false input sentence supplies a finite H only in the correctness
   proof. The transformation does not search for H. Relativization and
   substitution into the fixed sigma are effective on sentence codes.
10. In the second reduction, all nonsymmetric finite groups satisfy the
    first disjunct automatically. Large symmetric groups satisfy the
    second disjunct when H exists. Hence there are finitely many possible
    exceptions, not merely infinitely many successes.
11. If the input is valid in all finite groups, the existential disjunct
    fails everywhere and the output fails in infinitely many symmetric
    groups. Thus the reduction lands in the complement exactly in the
    required case. Negating the earlier universal-centralizer reduction
    would not have established this fact.

## Completed exact controls

GAP evaluates the incidence axioms on actual S5 through S9 using their
transposition classes, and on the fixed-point-free involution class of
S6. All six positive cases pass. Twelve involution classes in seven
negative targets are rejected: S4, A4, A5, A6, D10, SL2(5), and S5 x C2.
The rejection reasons include too few vertices, three distinct endpoints
for one edge, and nontrivial common centralizer. These controls do not
replace the proof that all other finite groups are excluded.

For every one of the 14 groups of orders at most eight, GAP constructs
both permutations and the actual centralizer in S_(h^2). It then checks
two padded degrees, 2h^2+3 and 2h^2+4, up to degree 132. The computed
centralizer equals the explicitly constructed left-translation group;
its SmallGroups identifier agrees with H. All 14 base and 28 padded
centralizers pass. The final GAP process was observed to exit zero.

Independent Python uses literal permutation tuples and computes the
same six positive incidence structures. It constructs the 14 small
groups from cyclic, product, dihedral, quaternion and permutation laws,
checking all 3,624 associativity instances. For centralizers it uses
unique propagation of a chosen point image through the two generating
permutations and their inverses. This exhausts commuting permutations
on each transitive orbit and independently recovers precisely the left
translations in all 42 cases. Its process was observed to exit zero.

Two earlier GAP control runs were explicitly stopped as incomplete,
with observed nonzero exits and retained source hashes and logs. Stage
diagnostics identified redundant full star enumeration on the A6
negative target. The completed version stops once an edge already has
three distinct incidence sets, an exact violation of the two-endpoint
axiom. Positive cases still enumerate all qualifying quadruples. Neither
stopped run is counted as a pass.

`results/18.18-summary.json` checks both final markers, compares the
independent positive and centralizer counts, and binds the proof,
scripts and retained artifacts by hash. Numerical tests cannot establish
non-enumerability; that conclusion rests on the two proved reductions.

## Priority scope

First-order reconstruction of symmetric-group actions is classical.
Shelah, *First order theory of permutation groups*, Israel Journal of
Mathematics 14 (1973), 149–162,
[author archive](https://shelah.logic.at/papers/24/),
[DOI 10.1007/BF02762670](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02762670), was read for
its introduction, interpretation definitions and Lemma 2.1's statement
and proof hint, which credits McKenzie. The paper explicitly sets finite
sets aside. The opening statement of the associated 1973 errata was
also read; its corrected classification was not audited. None of those
classification results is imported here, and no novelty is claimed for
the existence or general technique of a symmetric-group interpretation.

Searches for the exact Notebook question, cofinite finite-group theories,
and the centralizer route did not locate the complete two-reduction
answer. This is a limited priority check, not proof of novelty. The
candidate count records a full internally audited argument under the
stated cofinite interpretation, awaiting outside review.
