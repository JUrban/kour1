# A formulation problem in 12.69

Status: source inconsistency requiring clarification of the intended
mathematics; **not counted as a new solution**.

The supplied PDF page 63 was visually checked. It asks about a countably
infinite field F, a finite group G of automorphisms, and a subfield S such
that for every x there is a nonzero f in ZG with x^f in S. It asks whether
F=S or F/S is purely inseparable, and says the uncountable analogue has
an affirmative answer.

Under the usual multiplicative action, let F=Q(i), let G consist of the
identity and complex conjugation sigma, and let S=Q. The fixed nonzero
element f=1+sigma of ZG gives x^f=x sigma(x) in Q for every nonzero x,
and its nonnegative exponents also give value 0 at x=0. But Q(i)/Q is
proper and separable. More generally, the group-ring norm sum_{g in G}g
maps every nonzero element into the fixed field F^G.

If an additive action were intended instead, the same group-ring element
acts by the trace x+sigma(x), and the same counterexample works. Thus
the typography alone cannot repair the issue.

The identical example with F=C, S=R also contradicts the printed assertion
about uncountable fields. This makes a missing qualification or other
formulation issue a substantial concern. Possibilities such as restricting
the allowed group-ring elements must not be silently supplied: the exact
intended correction has not been established.

We record the literal counterexample and the inconsistency instead of
claiming a substantive resolution of the intended problem. No author or
editor has been contacted. The same wording was located in an older
English Notebook, but no reliable correction has yet been found.
