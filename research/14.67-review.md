# Internal review of the 14.67 partial results

This is an internal logical audit, not external review. The general
problem remains unresolved and novelty of the prime-case proof is
unestablished.

## Statement and scope

The supplied page 79 uses a non-strict largest-centralizer condition
among all nonidentity elements. It does not exclude central elements
from that comparison. Thus groups with nontrivial centre are indeed
immediate positive cases; replacing the condition with smallest
*noncentral* class would invalidate that reduction.

The finite search covers orders 2 through 511. The trivial group is
vacuous. Every subgroup in the search is an actual subgroup, not merely
an isomorphism type; all conjugacy classes of eligible elements and all
overgroups of their centralizers are included. The direct controls use
all actual nilpotent subgroups, and test elements in ineligible classes
as well, giving nontrivial negative controls.

## Prime-case proof audit

1. Passing to a prime-order power preserves the entire centralizer,
   because containment and maximal cardinality force equality. It
   therefore preserves both eligibility and the counterexample H.
2. If the index r is prime and H is not contained in C, then HC=G.
   Normalization by C makes H normal in HC; normality in G is a
   conclusion at this stage, not an extra initial assumption.
3. A nilpotent transitive permutation group of prime degree has cyclic
   regular image. The normal Sylow orbit argument proves this without
   a classification of primitive groups.
4. D=H intersect C equals H intersect ker(phi). In particular D is
   normal in G and centralizes the entire normal closure A of a,
   not just a itself.
5. The conjugation action of G normalizes the cyclic regular image of
   H. An element fixing the distinguished point has only a multiplier
   in the resulting affine action; multiplier one means trivial image.
6. In the nontrivial-multiplier branch, the outer class of conjugation
   by h has both r-th and (k-1)-th power trivial. These exponents are
   coprime, so the class is trivial. This works for arbitrary D,
   including the trivial group.
7. The resulting B=C_H(D) is abelian because its quotient by Z(D) is
   cyclic. Its normalization by C follows from normality of both H
   and D. Applying the elementary abelian lemma is therefore valid.
8. In the multiplier-one branch, a centralizes every conjugate, and
   conjugation then shows that every pair of conjugates commutes.
   Prime order gives an elementary abelian normal closure A.
9. Sylow subgroups of H are normal in G because H is normal and
   nilpotent. The coprime orders of A and H_q force commutation. This
   is the exact reason that p=r; no unproved coprime-action theorem
   is being imported.
10. A is generated linearly by a under H/D, since G=HC and C fixes a.
    Thus the nilpotent operator delta=T-I has a nonzero last vector
    delta^(d-1)(a), and d>=2. D acts trivially on A, which justifies
    replacing h^c by h^k in the polynomial calculation.
11. The last vector z is fixed by H and scaled by C. Its full G-orbit,
    not merely its H-orbit, has at most r-1 elements. It is nonidentity,
    so this contradicts the exact largest-centralizer hypothesis.

## Necessary hypotheses and computational controls

The order-six group S3 supplies simple controls for three assumptions.
Its three-cycle has the largest nonidentity centralizer, of index two.
Taking H=S3 shows that merely requiring H to be soluble would not
suffice. Taking a transposition and H=C3 instead preserves nilpotence
and normalization but drops the largest-centralizer hypothesis, and
the conclusion fails. Taking the three-cycle and a subgroup C2 shows
why normalization cannot simply be omitted. S3 and every one of its
nonidentity conjugacy classes are in the independent control set.

The direct controls returned zero successfully: 147 groups, 1,966
element classes and 67,289 literal subgroup cases. Both positive and
negative predicates agree with the overgroup/Fitting reduction. The
completed main search and independent catalogue also exited zero;
`summarize_14_67.py` checks their coverage and source hashes.

Only the primary abstract of the related Moretó2023 article was read.
The source explicitly reports the soluble prime-class case, but its
full proof and precise implications remain unaudited. This note does
not assert that the arbitrary-finite-group formulation is new.
