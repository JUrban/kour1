## Construction and audit

For p>3 and n>=1 put

    G = <a,b | b^-1 a b = a^(1+p), b^(p^n) = a^(p^n)>.

Write M=p^(n+1), N=p^n and q=1+p. Conjugating the second relation
by b and comparing it with itself gives a^M=1. Taking its p-th
power then gives b^M=1. Since q is invertible modulo M, the first
relation permits collection into a^i b^j; the second reduces this
to 0<=i<M, 0<=j<N. Thus |G|<=MN.

For the lower bound take A=<alpha> and B=<beta>, both cyclic of
order M. Let beta act by alpha^beta=alpha^q. The congruence
q^N=1 modulo M makes this action well defined and makes beta^N
central. Also alpha^N is central, because N(q-1)=M. Consequently

    z = beta^N alpha^(-N)

is a central element of order p in S=A semidirect B. Its nontrivial
powers have nontrivial components in both cyclic factors, so <z>
meets A and B trivially. The quotient S/<z> has order M^2/p=MN,
and its two generators satisfy the displayed relators. Therefore
the map from G onto this quotient is an isomorphism; in particular
both a and b have order M. This also rules out hidden relations in
the collection argument.

The cyclic subgroup <a^p> is normal, the quotient by it is abelian,
and [a,b]=a^p. Hence G'=<a^p>. For any integer k,

    [a^k,b]=a^(pk),  [a^k,a]=1.

Induction gives gamma_r(G)=<a^(p^(r-1))> for 2<=r<=n+2.
Its last nontrivial term is gamma_(n+1)=<a^N>, of order p.
Thus the nilpotency class is n+1. The group is nonabelian, so its
minimum generator number is exactly two. Its abelianization is
C_p x C_(p^n).

The two-relator presentation has deficiency zero. To check the
group-level convention, any presentation of a finite group with
d generators has a relation exponent matrix of rational rank d;
therefore it has at least d relators. No presentation can have
positive deficiency. These arguments prove the full assertion for
every n, independently of the finite controls below.

## Small primes

The preprint states p>3 and mentions the odd-prime extension.
Its construction actually works for p=2 as well: only q^N=1 mod M
is needed, not exact order p^n of q. Induct on k to obtain

    (1+p)^(p^k) = 1 mod p^(k+1)

for every prime p, including2. All central-quotient and lower-central
arguments above remain valid. For p=2,n=1 the group is Q8. This is
a short extension of the prior construction, not another Notebook
resolution or a novelty claim.

## Independent controls and their limits

`scripts/check_17_113.py` implements integer coordinates on the
semidirect product and its central quotient, without GAP. In normal
form a^i b^j the quotient multiplication is

    (i,j)(k,l) = (i + q^(-j)k + N floor((j+l)/N) mod M,
                  j+l mod N).

It checks the projection homomorphism, its diagonal kernel, the
relators, generator powers and lower-central commutators. Its15
parameter pairs have p in {2,3,5,7,11}, 1<=n<=3. Projection checks
are exhaustive on both semidirect coordinates for M<=25 and use
4,000 fixed-seed pairs otherwise; 1,000 associativity triples are
checked per pair. These are bounded controls of the symbolic proof.

`scripts/check_17_113.g` starts from just the two free-group
relators and computes finite p-quotients. It verifies order, full
lower central series, abelianization, generator orders and relators
for p=2,3,5,7 with 1<=n<=6 and p=11 with 1<=n<=4: 28 cases.
The quotient orders reach the independently proved upper bound,
so in these cases the computed quotients are the entire groups.
