# Problem 19.20: coprime direct-product exploration

11 September 2026. No equality example or new candidate. This is a
read-only follow-up to the completed counts through order 511.

Write R(G)=|End(G)|/|PIso(G)| for a finite group. If A and B have
coprime orders, then

    R(A x B) = R(A) R(B).

Indeed every subgroup of A x B is a product of subgroups of the two
factors. For any element (a,b), a power whose exponent is 1 modulo
|A| and 0 modulo |B| isolates (a,1), and another power isolates
(1,b). These powers lie in any subgroup containing (a,b), proving
the subgroup assertion. An isomorphism between two such subgroups
preserves their unique Hall factors for the two disjoint prime sets.
Thus partial isomorphisms correspond bijectively to pairs of partial
isomorphisms in A and B. A homomorphism from either factor to the
other is trivial, again by coprime orders; this gives the corresponding
bijection for endomorphisms. Both counts therefore multiply.

Consequently, groups of coprime orders with reciprocal nonunit ratios
would give a nonabelian equality example. It is essential to check
coprimality; the multiplicativity argument fails without it.

`scripts/explore_19_20_coprime_ratios.py` extracts the COUNTS,
CLOSEST and STRONG_INEQUALITY_COUNTEREXAMPLE rows of the retained
logs, checks agreement of duplicated IDs, and compares exact rational
ratios. The earlier logs did not print a row for every group, so this
is deliberately not another assertion of catalogue coverage.

The retained rows cover 85,343 distinct IDs and give 70,983 distinct
nonunit ratios. There are no reciprocal pairs, even before imposing
coprimality. Thus this particular construction supplies no equality
example from the logged groups. It excludes neither products with
three or more factors nor any unlogged group or parametric family.

Reproduce with `python3 scripts/explore_19_20_coprime_ratios.py`.
The JSON result records the script hash and the hash of every input
log containing a parsed row. Actual process exit was zero and the
completion sentinel was present. The argument is elementary; no
new mathematical priority is asserted.

A separate quick exploration of the order-256 rows showed that the
2-adic valuations of the two counts are equal in 2,800 nonabelian
cases. Therefore merely proving unequal valuations cannot be a
general argument for the strict inequality of the counts. This is
an exploratory obstruction, not an invariant theorem.
