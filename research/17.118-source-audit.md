## Laffey's finite 3-group theorem

Thomas J. Laffey, *The number of solutions of x^3=1 in a 3-group*,
Mathematische Zeitschrift 149 (1976), 43-45, DOI 10.1007/BF01301629.
The [publisher PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/BF01301629.pdf)
was retrieved successfully and is cached as `laffey-1976-cubes.pdf`.
All three pages of extracted text were read. Page images of the theorem
and proof pages 43 and 44 were viewed; page 45 contains references and
the receipt date. The theorem is exactly the bound 7/9 for the fraction
of roots of x^3=1 in a finite 3-group not of exponent 3. The printed
sharp example has order 243 and 189 roots; it is not the example used
in our characteristic-index lower bound.

The proof reduces a minimal counterexample to a group with center of
order 3 and exponent-3 central quotient, then uses commutator identities
and root-set intersections. It imports results of Levi--van der Waerden
and Heineken via Huppert's *Endliche Gruppen I*, III.(6.5),(6.6),(6.9).
Those imported proofs have not been independently checked here. The
application in Section 3 of our proof therefore depends on Laffey's
published theorem, rather than being fully self-contained. The
fractions in the OCR are unreliable; the page images confirm 7/9 and
the other relevant constants.

## Modern restatement

Khaled Jaber and Frank O. Wagner, *Largeness and equational probability
in groups*, Annales mathématiques Blaise Pascal 27 (2020), 1-17,
[journal PDF](https://ambp.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/ambp.388.pdf).
Fact 1.3 on printed page 2 restates Laffey's exact 3-group theorem.
That page was both read and viewed (`17.118-probability-theorems.png`).
Relevant statements were inspected; this is not a full-paper proof
audit. Its separate bound for groups that are not p-groups does not
apply to the setting of 17.118. No general positive root-proportion
gap for p-groups at p >= 5 is inferred from this paper.

## The known infinite counterexample

E. I. Khukhro, A. A. Klyachko, N. Yu. Makarenko, Yu. B. Melnikova,
*Automorphism invariance and identities*, Bulletin of the London
Mathematical Society 41 (2009), 804-816; cached
[arXiv:0812.1359](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.1359), version 2 dated
31 August 2010, eight PDF pages. The main theorem statements and the
complete construction in Section 6, pages 7-8, were read. The entire
paper's proofs have not been audited.

Theorem 6 constructs, for sufficiently large primes, a group of
exponent p^2 with an exponent-p subgroup of index p and no
characteristic exponent-p subgroup of finite index. The construction
has an infinite elementary abelian quotient with basis a,x_1,x_2,... .
It uses graded periodic presentations and imports Olshanskii's theorem
26.4 to establish the exact orders of the periods. Shears x_i -> a^k x_i
extend to automorphisms; an invariant image in the elementary abelian
quotient is either zero or contains a. The first alternative gives
infinite index, and the second violates exponent p.

The finite-group condition in the Notebook excludes this example.
Neither residual finiteness nor preservation of the essential order
conditions in finite quotients is established. Our finite squarefree
algebra has a different, fully specified construction. The infinite
theorem is background, not an imported ingredient of its proof.
