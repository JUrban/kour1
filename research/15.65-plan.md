# Problem 15.65: nonrationality of the unitary cyclic limit

11 September 2026, 19:20 UTC. Active proof and source audit.

The first formula in Fulman--Neumann--Praeger, Theorem 2.1.9,
expresses C(U;q) as an infinite product. With t=1/q its degree-one
self-conjugate factor is

    ((1+t-t^2)/(1+t))^(1+1/t).

At alpha=(1-sqrt(5))/2 this factor has a simple base zero and exponent
alpha. Every other factor should form a holomorphic nonvanishing
function on |t|<3/4. If this is justified, the limit has a nonintegral
power singularity at alpha and cannot be rational. Equality on the
prime-power sequence q=2^k forces equality of analytic germs at t=0,
so complex continuation is legitimate even though q originally counts
field elements.

Tasks:

1. Check the product and all polynomial-count formulas against the PDF.
2. Prove normal convergence, removability at zero, and nonvanishing of
   the product after removing the degree-one factor.
3. Prove the exact local exponent and the rational-function contradiction.
4. Check formal coefficients by two independent methods and compare the
   published coefficients. Check the finite unitary cyclic counts in GAP
   against the finite generating formula for modest dimensions.
5. Record source dependencies, reading limits, and bounded novelty search;
   obtain a manual adversarial review before adding a portfolio candidate.

The source defines separable using the CHARACTERISTIC polynomial; the
Notebook says MINIMAL polynomial, which is semisimplicity in the source.
The proposed primary result concerns CYCLIC matrices, whose definition
agrees, and therefore does not depend on correcting that discrepancy.
A further result for source-separable matrices may be recorded separately.

No numerical computation is proposed as proof of nonrationality.
