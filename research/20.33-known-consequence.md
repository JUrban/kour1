# 20.33: a universal oracle-presented group gives an affirmative answer

Status: complete deduction from standard embedding constructions, excluded
from new-priority claims. The exact statement on printed page152 was
visually checked. Outside review remains pending.

We prove a stronger assertion: for every oracle X there is a
three-generated group G_X with an X-c.e. presentation such that a
finitely generated group has an X-c.e. presentation if and only if it
embeds directly in G_X. Here X-c.e. means computably enumerable with
oracle X, exactly as in the question; decidable word problem is not
assumed.

## A uniform countable presentation containing all the groups

Fix effective codings of finite words and oracle programs. For every
positive integer n and program index e, let R_(n,e)^X be the words on
n generators enumerated by the e-th oracle program. Ill-formed outputs
can be ignored. Every such set defines a group

    P_(n,e)^X = <a_(n,e,1),...,a_(n,e,n) | R_(n,e)^X>.

Take the free product U_X of these groups over all pairs (n,e). Its
generator alphabet is computable and countable; its defining relators
are X-c.e., by dovetailing the program runs. Each P_(n,e)^X embeds as a
free factor. Thus every finitely generated X-c.e.-presented group embeds
in U_X. Repetitions, trivial factors, and programs that never output
anything cause no difficulty. In particular no oracle is used to decide
whether two presentations define the same group.

Enumerate U_X's generator alphabet effectively as g_1,g_2,... and write

    U_X = <g_1,g_2,... | R_X>.

## An explicit three-generator embedding preserving the oracle

The following elementary HNN construction works for any countably
presented group U=<g_1,g_2,... | R>. In B=U*F(a,b), put

    q_i = a^i b a^(-i),       c_i = g_i q_i       (i>=1).

The q_i freely generate a subgroup A of F(a,b): they are part of the
Schreier basis of the kernel of F(a,b)->Z sending a to1 and b to0.
The c_i freely generate a subgroup C of B. Indeed, the retraction
B->F(a,b) killing U sends c_i to q_i, so any nonempty freely reduced
relation in the c_i would give the same nonempty relation in the free
basis q_i. This argument permits arbitrary torsion or trivial g_i.

Consequently q_i->c_i extends to an isomorphism A->C. Form the HNN
extension

    H = <B,t | t^(-1) q_i t = g_i q_i for every i>=1>.

The HNN normal-form theorem embeds B, hence U, in H. The defining
relations express every g_i as the explicitly computable word

    w_i(a,b,t) = t^(-1) a^i b a^(-i) t a^i b^(-1) a^(-i).

Thus H is generated by a,b,t. Eliminating the g_i gives exactly

    H = <a,b,t | r(w_1,w_2,...) for r in R>.

There are no additional relations: substituting g_i=w_i makes every
displayed HNN relation a free-word identity. Conversely, these words
and a,b,t define the inverse presentation maps. This verifies the
elimination even for infinitely many generators and relations.

Apply this construction to U_X and call the resulting group G_X. The
substitution i->w_i is computable without an oracle. Applying it to
the X-c.e. relator stream R_X gives an X-c.e. defining presentation
of G_X, uniformly in X. No word-problem or subgroup-membership decisions
enter the construction.

## The converse and the exact Notebook formulation

Finite free products of copies of an X-c.e.-presented finitely generated
group have X-c.e. presentations on the union of the generator alphabets.
Adding finitely many relators preserves this property.

Every finitely generated subgroup of an X-c.e.-presented group also
has an X-c.e. presentation. To see this explicitly, let Q=<S|R> with
finite S, let N be the normal closure of R in F(S), and represent the
chosen subgroup generators by words v_1,...,v_m. Enumerate N with X by
enumerating finite products of conjugates of enumerated relators and
their inverses. For every word u in F(z_1,...,z_m), the substitution
u(v_1,...,v_m) is effectively computable. Dovetailing these comparisons
against the enumerated, freely reduced words of N enumerates exactly
the kernel of F(z_1,...,z_m)->Q. That kernel is a defining relator set
for the subgroup. This is an enumeration, not a membership decision.

If a finitely generated group has an X-c.e. presentation, the first
two sections embed it directly in G_X. In the Notebook's required
expression take one copy of G_X and the empty finite extra relator
set (or the singleton containing the identity). Conversely, any
finitely generated subgroup of any of the permitted finite-relator
quotients has an X-c.e. presentation by the preceding paragraph.
This proves both directions. QED.

## Prior construction and reading scope

The underlying effective countable-to-finite-generator embedding is
classical. An explicit two-generator version is Theorem1.1 of
V. H. Mikaelian, [Embeddings using universal words in the free group of
rank2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09433v4), first posted2020, v4 revised
28 July2023. It replaces every generator by a computable fixed word
and every relator by the corresponding substitution. Applied to U_X,
it improves the bound from three generators to two. The theorem and
its normal-form proof in Sections3.1--3.2 were read; the later variants
and applications were not audited.

The three-generator proof above spells out the embedding and all
oracle bookkeeping independently. The exact20.33 implication was not
found already stated in the limited literature searches; that does not
establish novelty. We retain it as a standard-construction consequence,
consistently with the treatment of19.9,20.8 and20.11. The printed question
does not require G_X to have decidable word problem or finite presentation,
nor does it require a nontrivial extra quotient.
