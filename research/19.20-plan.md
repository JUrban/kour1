# Endomorphisms and partial isomorphisms: 19.20

For a finite group G and an isomorphism type T write s_T for the number
of actual subgroups of G isomorphic to T, q_T for the number of normal
subgroups N with G/N isomorphic to T, and a_T=|Aut(T)|. Then

    |PIso(G)| = sum_T s_T^2 a_T,
    |End(G)|  = sum_T q_T s_T a_T.

The first identity chooses the domain and image of a partial isomorphism
and an isomorphism between them. The second chooses an endomorphism's
kernel and image, and the induced isomorphism from its quotient. Both
count every map exactly once. Counts of conjugacy classes alone cannot
replace s_T: the search weights each class by its actual cardinality.

For the search through order 255, all subgroup and quotient types are
identified by SmallGrp. Automorphism-group orders are computed once per
type and cached. Every abelian group is retained as a positive equality
control. An equality for a nonabelian group is a counterexample to 19.20.
An example with |End|>|PIso| would refute the stronger inequality but
would not on its own answer the Notebook equality question.

Primary source: Peter J. Cameron, *Endomorphisms and partial isomorphisms*,
Semigroup Forum (2025), https://doi.org/10.1007/s00233-025-10514-5 . Full
two-page paper read from the author's institutional deposit:
https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/handle/10023/31654/Cameron_2025_SF_Endomorphisms-and-partial-isomorphisms_CC.pdf?sequence=1 .
It proves the abelian equality by duality and reports the strict inequality
for nonabelian groups of order at most 63. Our 2--63 block reproduces that
range as a control; subsequent blocks extend it. No new theorem follows
from a finite negative search.
