# 20.11: all four assertions follow from subgroup rigidity

Status: complete affirmative deductions from prior theorems, excluded
from the new-priority candidate count. Outside review remains pending.
The printed statement on page 148 was visually checked.

Let F<=H be as in the question, and let C be the nonempty family of
subgroups G with F<=G<=H and rank(G)=r, where r is the least possible
finite rank of an overgroup of F in H. Neither F nor H is assumed
finitely generated.

The required input is Jaikin-Zapirain,
[Free groups are L^2-subgroup rigid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09515v2):
Corollary 1.2 makes every finitely generated compressed subgroup inert,
and Corollary 1.5 closes the family of minimum-rank overgroups of a
finitely generated subgroup under pairwise joins and intersections.
We give the steps handling the broader quantifiers in the Notebook.

## Pairwise intersection: (ii')

Take G_1,G_2 in C and put T=<G_1,G_2>, a finitely generated free group.
The subgroup G_1 is compressed in T: every overgroup of G_1 in T
contains F and hence has rank at least r. By Corollary 1.2 it is inert,
so rank(G_1 intersect G_2)<=rank(G_2)=r. This intersection contains F;
its rank is therefore exactly r. In particular it is finitely generated.

## Pairwise join: (i')

Put K=G_1 intersect G_2. We just proved that K has finite rank r.
Every overgroup of K in T contains F and has rank at least r. Thus
G_1 and G_2 are minimum-rank overgroups of the finitely generated
subgroup K. Corollary 1.5 now gives rank(T)=r. This use of K avoids
assuming, without justification, that the original F is finitely generated.

## A greatest member: (i)

Every chain in C has a union U of rank at most r. To see this, U is
free by the subgroup theorem. If a free basis of U had r+1 elements,
choose r+1 of them. They generate a free factor B of U and all lie in
one member G of the chain. A free factor of U contained in G is also
a free factor of G, by the free-product subgroup theorem. This would
force rank(G)>=r+1, a contradiction.

Since F<=U, its rank is exactly r and U belongs to C. Zorn's lemma
gives a maximal member M of C. For every G in C, the pairwise-join
result puts <M,G> in C. Maximality forces G<=M. Thus M is the
greatest member, as requested. This works even when H is uncountable.

## A least member: (ii)

Fix G_0 in C and let I be the intersection of all members of C.
The group G_0 is countable. For every element z of G_0 outside I,
choose a member M_z of C not containing z. Consequently a countable
subfamily, together with G_0, has intersection I. Its finite
intersections form a descending sequence

    G_0 >= L_1 >= L_2 >= ...,

each of rank r by (ii'), with intersection I.

We use the elementary bounded-rank intersection fact: the intersection
of a descending sequence of subgroups of a finitely generated free
group, each of rank at most r, has rank at most r. Here is a graph
proof to make the limit step explicit. Take Schreier graphs over a
finite free basis of the ambient group G_0. On every finite set of
words, membership in the descending subgroups eventually agrees with
membership in their intersection. Thus each finite labeled
neighborhood in the Schreier graph of I appears unchanged in the
Schreier graph of L_n for all sufficiently large n. If I had rank
greater than r, its Schreier graph would contain a finite connected
subgraph of first Betti number greater than r. The same finite graph
would then occur in a Schreier graph of L_n, forcing rank(L_n)>r.
This is a contradiction.

It follows that I has rank at most r. It contains F, so its rank is
exactly r and I belongs to C. By construction I is the least member.
The cases where C's intersection is already a finite intersection,
or r=0, are included (one may repeat the last L_n). QED.

## Scope and audit

All four assertions are affirmative, including when F is infinitely
generated and H has arbitrary free rank. The deep input is prior
work, and the full proof of subgroup rigidity was not independently
audited. Corollaries 1.2, 1.4, 1.5 and Section 3.7's deduction of
Corollary 1.5 were read in the primary v2 paper, revised February 2026.
The Notebook already credits this work for 19.102--105.

The additional limit arguments above are given explicitly. In particular,
pairwise intersection closure alone was not silently assumed to imply
closure under arbitrary intersections, and a finite-generation condition
on F was not added to the printed question. The greatest and least
members need not be equal: for F=<a^2> in H=<a>, both have rank one,
with least member <a^2> and greatest member <a>.
