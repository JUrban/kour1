# Problem 11.78: connectedness and prior results

12 September 2026. This is a scope audit, not a counted new resolution.
The printed question does not say that the algebraic groups are connected.
Under that literal wording both parts have elementary negative examples.
The connected interpretation has substantial prior positive results, and
is the one retained as the substantive unresolved target here.

## A disconnected obstruction to the printed wording

Let K be the algebraic closure of Q and put

    G_n = (Ga)^n semidirect C_2,

where the involution acts by inversion. Concretely its points are (v,e),
with v in K^n and e in {1,-1}, and

    (v,e)(w,f)=(v+e w,ef).

These are affine algebraic groups of dimension n with two components,
realized by matrices [[e I_n,v],[0,1]], with e^2=1. Their centres are
trivial. A translation centralizing an involution has 2v=0 and hence
v=0; an element outside the translation group does not centralize it.

They are directly indecomposable even as abstract groups. Suppose
G_n=H times J with both factors nontrivial. If both factors project
nontrivially to C_2, take h in H and j in J outside K^n. Both are
involutions, and they commute, so hj has order at most two. But hj
is a translation and K^n is torsion-free. Hence h=j, contradicting
H intersect J=1. Thus one factor lies in K^n; it is abelian and
commutes with the other factor, so it is central and therefore trivial.

The Q-vector spaces K and K^2 both have countably infinite dimension.
Any Q-linear isomorphism between them gives an abstract isomorphism
G_1(K) -> G_2(K). There is no semialgebraic isomorphism: a field
translation preserves dimension, and an injective surjective rational
group morphism between algebraic groups over an algebraically closed
field preserves dimension. Here the dimensions are one and two.
This disproves the literal disconnected version of part (a).

For part (b), choose a Q-basis of K containing 1 and sqrt(2). Let T
fix all basis elements except sqrt(2), which it sends to 2sqrt(2).
It is a Q-linear automorphism, so (v,e) |-> (T(v),e) is an abstract
automorphism of G_1(K). If it were semialgebraic, its restriction to
the identity component would have the form c sigma(v), where sigma
is a field automorphism of K and c is nonzero in K: in characteristic
zero, a rational group homomorphism Ga -> Ga is multiplication by a
scalar. T(1)=1 forces c=1, but sigma(sqrt(2)) must be plus or minus
sqrt(2), whereas T(sqrt(2))=2sqrt(2). This is impossible. The field K
is algebraic over its prime field as required in the printed part (b).

These elementary examples expose a connectedness issue; they do not
refute a version in which connectedness is intended as a convention.
No author or editor has been contacted to resolve that convention.

## Prior connected results

Olivier Frécon, *Algebraic Qbar-groups as abstract groups*, preprint dated
12 October 2013, https://modnet.imj-prg.fr/preprints/papers/archive/637.pdf ,
Theorem 10.1 (pp.72–73), states that if G is nontrivial and connected
over an algebraically closed field K of positive characteristic or
K=Qbar, every abstract isomorphism from G is standard if and only if
there is no nonzero homomorphism G -> Z(G) and G is not a central
product of two proper closed subgroups with finite intersection.
The source defines standard as field translation followed by an isogeny,
which gives the semialgebraic form required here.

For centreless, directly indecomposable G, both conditions hold. A
central product with finite intersection would have that intersection
in Z(G), hence trivial, and would be a direct decomposition. Thus this
prior theorem settles the connected algebraically closed algebraic-field
case of (b), as well as the connected positive-characteristic case of
(a). It does not, by itself, cover every algebraic field that is not
algebraically closed.

Corollary 1.3 (p.5) also says that abstractly isomorphic connected
Qbar-defined algebraic groups over any algebraically closed field of
characteristic zero have algebraically isomorphic quotients by their
centres. Consequently centreless such groups are algebraically
isomorphic, giving that prior subcase of (a). Arbitrary groups defined
using transcendental coefficients are not included in that corollary.

Examples 3.1(2)–(3), and Theorem 10.1 with its short deduction were
read in extraction. Pages 5,72,73 were rendered and viewed, confirming
Qbar rather than Q in the hypotheses. The long model-theoretic proof
and its imported inputs were not independently audited.
