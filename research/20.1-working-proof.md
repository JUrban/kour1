# Problem 20.1: working proof

12 September 2026. Historical working draft. The frozen candidate is in `research/20.1-proof.md`.

For a faithful group G acting on a set Omega, its2-closure consists
exactly of the permutations s for which, for every alpha,beta, some
g in G satisfies alpha^s=alpha^g and beta^s=beta^g. This is just
preservation of each orbit on ordered pairs.

**Lemma.** If finite groups A and B have coprime orders and are each
totally2-closed, then A x B is totally2-closed.

Let G=A x B act faithfully on any set Omega. For every alpha,

    alpha^A intersect alpha^B = {alpha}.                 (1)

Indeed, if alpha^a=alpha^b then alpha^(ab^-1)=alpha. Since a,b
commute and have coprime orders, a and b belong to the cyclic subgroup
generated by ab^-1. Thus each fixes alpha.

Let Delta_B be the set of B-orbits on Omega. Because B is normal,
G acts on Delta_B, with B acting trivially, so this is an A-action.
It is faithful: if a in A fixes every B-orbit setwise, then alpha^a
is in both alpha^A and alpha^B for every alpha, and (1) gives a=1.
Similarly B acts faithfully on Delta_A, the set of A-orbits.

Let s lie in the2-closure of G on Omega. The equivalence relation
of belonging to a common B-orbit is G-invariant, hence a union of
G-orbits on Omega^2. Therefore s permutes the B-orbits and induces
a permutation s_B of Delta_B. Given two such orbits D,E, choose
alpha in D and beta in E. By the pair criterion, some g=ab in G
agrees with s on alpha,beta. Since s preserves the orbit partition,

    D^(s_B)=D^a,       E^(s_B)=E^a.

Thus s_B lies in the2-closure of the faithful A-action. By total
2-closure, s_B is the permutation induced by a single a_0 in A.
Likewise s induces on Delta_A the permutation of a single b_0 in B.

The permutation s(a_0 b_0)^-1 fixes each A-orbit and each B-orbit
setwise. By (1) it fixes every point. Hence s=a_0 b_0 belongs to G,
proving the lemma. No transitivity was used.

For completeness, for finite groups the definition gives the same
class whether one quantifies over finite sets or arbitrary sets.
Suppose G is 2-closed in every finite faithful action and acts
faithfully on Omega. There is a finite faithful G-invariant subset
Omega_0: for each nonidentity element choose a point that it moves,
and take the union of the finitely many finite orbits so obtained.
An element s of the 2-closure preserves each G-orbit, by applying
the pair criterion to (alpha,alpha). It therefore restricts to the
2-closure on Omega_0, where it agrees with a unique g in G.
For any alpha, the finite faithful invariant set Omega_0 union
alpha^G similarly gives an element g_alpha agreeing with s;
faithfulness on Omega_0 forces g_alpha=g. Thus s=g on all of Omega.
(The trivial group is immediate.) This justifies use of a published
finite-set total-closure theorem on either of the quotient sets above.

**Application.** Let T=J1. Arezoomand--Iranmanesh--Praeger--Tracey,
*Totally2-closed finite groups with trivial Fitting subgroup*,
Theorem1.1, says that T is totally2-closed. The prime cyclic group
C13 is totally2-closed: any faithful action has a regular orbit;
the pair criterion on that orbit gives a translation, and the
criterion between that orbit and every other orbit forces the same
translation everywhere. (Every orbit is regular or a fixed point.)

The order of J1 is175560=2^3*3*5*7*11*19, coprime to13. Therefore
G=J1 x C13 is totally2-closed. It is insoluble because it maps onto
J1. Its Fitting subgroup is exactly C13: a normal nilpotent subgroup
projects to a normal nilpotent subgroup of the nonabelian simple J1,
which must be trivial, while C13 itself is central and nilpotent.

The J1 total2-closure theorem is a substantial imported result.
The elementary direct-product argument is written out here, but
priority of this consequence is not established by initial searches.
