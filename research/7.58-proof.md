# Problem 7.58: an explicit equality criterion in the free verbal base

11 September 2026. Explicit consequence of prior work, with a self-contained
proof. Excluded from the complete-candidate count.

## Statement and conventions

Let F=F(X), let R be any normal subgroup, and write Q=F/R with quotient
map x |-> bar(x). No cardinality or finite-presentation assumption is
made. Let V be any variety of groups. Put E=Q x X and

    B = F(E)/V(F(E)).

Write a(q,x) for its distinguished generators. Q acts on B on the left
by s.a(q,x)=a(sq,x). Use the semidirect-product multiplication

    (b,q)(c,r) = (b (q.c), qr).

Then W=B semidirect Q is the V-verbal wreath product of the free V-group
on X with Q. Indeed its base is the coproduct in V of the copies of
that group indexed by Q, hence is the free V-group on their combined
distinguished bases. This also agrees with the usual verbal-product
definition: for factors already in V the verbal subgroup of their free
product lies in the Cartesian subgroup, since it maps trivially into
each factor.

Use the standard embedding in these coordinates,

    mu: F/V(R) -> W,       mu(x V(R)) = (a(1,x),bar(x)).       (1)

A direct proof of its kernel, as well as of the criterion, is given
below, so the embedding theorem need not be imported as a premise.

Form the right Cayley multigraph Gamma with vertex set Q and, for each
(q,x) in E, a distinguished positive edge q -> q bar(x). Each such
edge has its own formal reverse, labelled a(q,x)^-1. Parallel positive
edges remain distinct, even if they have opposite endpoints or equal
generator images. Positive loops are retained. Choose a spanning tree
T of the underlying multigraph and root it at 1. Only one orientation
of each selected tree edge is used as a member of E_T subset E.

For q in Q, let t_q be the word in the free edge letters on the unique
tree path from 1 to q. In particular t_1=1. Define in B

    c(q,x) = t_q a(q,x) t_(q bar(x))^-1,
    rho(a(q,x)) = c(q,x).                                  (2)

Since B is relatively free in V and its proposed images lie in B, the
assignment defines an endomorphism rho of B. It requires only the
tree paths at the two endpoints of each edge being substituted.

**Criterion.** For every (b,q) in W,

    (b,q) is in mu(F/V(R))  <=>  rho(b) = b t_q^-1 in B.     (3)

Thus the criterion is one explicit equality in the ambient relatively
free group B, with no subgroup-membership oracle. The choice of tree
changes rho and t_q but not the set detected by (3).

## A free-basis calculation

First work in the unrestricted free group K=F(E). For a positive tree
edge e=(q,x), one has t_q a(e)=t_(q bar(x)) in K, irrespective of which
endpoint is closer to the root. Hence c(e)=1 for e in E_T.

For the positive edges outside T let C be the subgroup of K generated
by the c(e). The set consisting of the original tree-edge generators
and the c(e) for e outside T is a free basis of K. To prove this, define
alpha on the original free basis by fixing tree generators and replacing
each non-tree generator a(e) by t_q a(e) t_r^-1, where r=q bar(x).
Define beta by fixing tree generators and sending each non-tree a(e)
to t_q^-1 a(e) t_r. The path words use only tree generators, so direct
substitution gives alpha beta=beta alpha=id on every generator.
This works for infinite bases too: each individual path is finite and
both maps are specified by finite words on each generator.

Consequently C is a free factor of K. Killing all the tree generators
and fixing all c(e) in this new basis gives a retraction K -> C.
In the original basis it is exactly (2), because rho(t_q)=1. In
particular rho^2=rho. The induced endomorphism of B is also idempotent,
with image C_bar generated by the images of the c(e). Therefore

    z is in C_bar  <=>  rho(z)=z.                           (4)

One must not identify every subgroup of a relatively free group with
a relatively free group; it is the explicit free-factor retraction
above that makes this particular identification valid. More precisely,

    C intersect V(K) = V(C).                               (5)

The forward inclusion follows by applying the retraction to an element
of C intersect V(K): any homomorphism maps verbal elements into verbal
elements. The reverse inclusion is immediate from C <= K. Thus
C_bar is canonically C/V(C).

## Paths and the embedded kernel

For a word w on X and its inverses, read the path in Gamma starting
at 1 and labelled by w. Let P(w) be its edge word in K. The endpoint
is bar(w), and concatenation gives the cocycle identity

    P(uv) = P(u) (bar(u).P(v)).

Free cancellations of generator letters remove precisely consecutive
formal inverse edges, so the construction is well defined on F. The
map w |-> (P(w),bar(w)) into K semidirect Q is a homomorphism with the
generator images in (1). Reading original X-labels from its edge words
shows that it is injective.

On R the map P is a homomorphism into K. Its image is exactly C.
For completeness, if a loop traverses an edge e from u to v, the
corresponding edge letter equals t_u^-1 c(e) t_v; for a reverse edge
use c(e)^-1 with endpoints reversed. Multiplying around the loop
cancels all intervening tree words and gives a product of c(e)'s.
Conversely each c(e) is the edge word of the loop which follows the
tree from 1 to its initial vertex, traverses e, and returns along
the tree. Reading its X-labels gives an element of R. Injectivity of
the path map shows R is isomorphic to C.

The map into W has kernel contained in R, since its top coordinate is
bar(w). On R its kernel corresponds to C intersect V(K), which is
V(C) by (5), and therefore to V(R). This proves (1) with exactly the
required kernel for every V.

Let tau(q) be the word on X obtained by reading the tree path to q;
then mu(tau(q))=(t_q,q). Every w with bar(w)=q has the form r tau(q)
for some r in R. It follows that the set of image elements over q is

    { (c t_q,q) : c in C_bar }.                            (6)

Equations (4) and (6), together with rho(t_q)=1, immediately give (3):
b t_q^-1 belongs to C_bar if and only if rho(b)=b t_q^-1.

## Explicit reconstruction and effectiveness

Represent b by a finite word a(e_1)^eps_1 ... a(e_m)^eps_m.
For a positive edge e=(u,x), put

    s_e = tau(u) x tau(u bar(x))^-1 in R.

If (3) holds, a preimage word for (b,q) is

    s_(e_1)^eps_1 ... s_(e_m)^eps_m tau(q).                 (7)

Indeed the first product has image (rho(b),1); appending tau(q) gives
(rho(b)t_q,q)=(b,q). This reconstruction also handles negative edge
letters, and it works with any representative word of b.

For a finite input, only finitely many tree paths and substitutions
are needed. If those paths and the word problem in B are effectively
available, (3) is a decision procedure and (7) supplies a witness.
For arbitrary R and V there is no assumed algorithm for their word
problems. The result supplies the requested group-theoretic criterion
and does not claim unconditional algorithmic decidability.

## Relation with the abelian criterion

If V is the variety of all abelian groups, B is the free abelian group
on the positive edges. Define the boundary homomorphism into the free
abelian group on vertices by

    partial(a(q,x)) = [q bar(x)] - [q].

The tree paths satisfy partial(t_q)=[q]-[1]. The subgroup C_bar is
the kernel of partial. Indeed all fundamental cycles have boundary
zero, and the basis change decomposes B as the direct sum of their
span and the tree-edge span. A finitely supported chain of tree edges
of boundary zero is zero: restrict to its finite supporting subtree
and remove a leaf, repeating until no edge remains. Hence partial
is injective on the tree-edge span. Criterion (3) thus reduces to

    partial(b) = [q]-[1],

the usual integral-flow form of the Magnus image criterion. The
nonabelian retraction condition retains the order of the edge letters,
which boundary alone cannot record in a general variety.

## Other indexing conventions

A common source convention writes elements as q d and uses base
generators u_x(h) with u_x(h)^s=u_x(hs). Its standard image is
x |-> bar(x) u_x(1). The following map from our coordinates to that
convention is an isomorphism fixing Q:

    a(q,x) |-> u_x(bar(x)^-1 q^-1).

It is a bijection of the base generators, respects the actions because
s u_x(h) s^-1=u_x(hs^-1), and sends a(1,x) bar(x) to
u_x(bar(x)^-1) bar(x)=bar(x) u_x(1). This gives an explicit conversion
of (3) to that convention, without changing the embedded subgroup.
