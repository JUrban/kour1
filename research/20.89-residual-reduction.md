# A residual-finiteness reduction for almost Engel generators

This is a separate partial observation for 20.89/19.83. It does not
establish product closure relative to an unrestricted ambient group.

Let G be a residually finite linear group, and let a_1,...,a_r have
finite Engel sinks E_1,...,E_r relative to G. Residual finiteness gives
a normal subgroup H of finite index in G disjoint from every
E_i minus {1}. For h in H all commutators [h,_n a_i] stay in H, so
eventually they equal 1. Moreover a_i is Engel in H<a_i>: writing an
element as h a_i^k gives

    [h a_i^k,_n a_i] = [h,_n a_i]^(a_i^k).

Use Gruenberg's theorem that the Engel elements of a linear group
form its Hirsch-Plotkin radical. The normal closure of a_i in
H<a_i> is locally nilpotent. Its intersection with H is a normal
locally nilpotent subgroup of H and hence is contained in R=HP(H).
Consequently [H,a_i] <= R for every i. The subgroup R is characteristic
in H, hence normal in G.

In G/R, the images of all a_i centralize H/R. Put

    C = C_{G/R}(H/R),  S/R = C.

The subgroup C is normal in G/R. Its subgroup C intersect (H/R) is
central in C and has finite index, since (G/R)/(H/R)=G/H is finite.
Thus C is central-by-finite. The linear locally nilpotent group R is
soluble: its Fitting subgroup is nilpotent of finite index, and the
remaining finite locally nilpotent quotient is nilpotent. These
standard linear-radical facts are recorded in Wehrfritz2019,
Remark 3.3. The preimage in S of Z(C) is therefore soluble and has
finite index in S. Hence S is a normal virtually soluble subgroup of
G containing all the a_i.

In particular L=<a_1,...,a_r> is virtually soluble. The a_i remain
almost Engel relative to L (intersect each sink with L). Shumyatsky's
Theorem 3.3 now proves that L is finite-by-nilpotent.

This applies to every finitely generated linear ambient group, which
is residually finite by the classical Malcev theorem. It also applies
to any residually finite linear ambient group without a generation
assumption. For a completely arbitrary linear G, restrict to the
finitely generated group L itself: each a_i is almost Engel there,
L is residually finite, and the same argument proves that L is
finite-by-nilpotent.

Thus, without any residual-finiteness assumption on G, **every subgroup
generated by finitely many elements that are almost Engel in G is
finite-by-nilpotent**. The extra normal-subgroup conclusion for S
requires the original ambient G to be residually finite.

The missing quantifier is substantial. For a product b of the a_i,
this supplies a finite sink in L, and in each enlarged group generated
by finitely many almost Engel elements. It does not supply one finite
sink for [x,_n b] as x ranges over all of G. Nor may arbitrary test
elements x be silently assumed almost Engel. No full candidate is
added from this observation.

## Every finite sink generates a finite subgroup

Here "sink" means the unique minimal finite Engel sink E(a). Its
members are exactly the periodic points of x -> [x,a]; in particular
each e in E(a) satisfies e=[e,_n a] for some n>=1. This characterization
is stated at the start of Wehrfritz2019 and also follows by decomposing
the eventual finite dynamics into cycles.

Every e in E(a) belongs to the normal closure N=<a^G>, because its
periodic identity expresses it as a commutator with a. Express the
finitely many elements of E(a) as words in finitely many conjugates
of a, and include a itself among these generators. Their subgroup M
is finite-by-nilpotent by the result just proved: all the generators
are almost Engel relative to G, since that property is conjugation
invariant. Let K be a finite normal subgroup of M with M/K nilpotent.

In M/K, the identity e=[e,_n a] can be iterated to arbitrarily large
Engel lengths. Nilpotence then forces eK=K. Thus E(a) is contained in
K and **<E(a)> is finite**.

Consequently, in a torsion-free linear group every almost Engel
element is Engel. In particular the answer to 20.89/19.83 is affirmative
for every torsion-free linear group, by Gruenberg's theorem. This
does not assume finite generation, characteristic zero or a triangular
representation. The argument concerns the minimal sink: an arbitrary
larger set called a sink can include unnecessary infinite-order elements.

The finite subgroup <E(a)> need not be normal in G. Replacing it by
its G-normal closure without proving that closure finite would be an
invalid shortcut to the original general question.

For an explicit finite control, let G=F_3^2 semidirect D_8, where
D_8 acts by signed coordinate permutations, and let a act as
(x,y)->(-x,y). The quotient D_8 is nilpotent, so every periodic
commutator point lies in the translation subgroup. On translations,
the commutator map is (x,y)->(x,0). Thus E(a) is exactly the three
x-axis translations. This subgroup is carried to the y-axis subgroup
by the coordinate swap and is not G-normal; its normal closure has
order nine. GAP and independent matrix enumeration check this example.
