# 17.118: bounded-class and small-prime investigation

2026-09-12. The full problem asks for a bound depending only on p
for every finite p-group with an exponent-p subgroup of index p.
No complete answer is currently claimed.

The existing infinite counterexample of Khukhro--Klyachko--Makarenko--
Melnikova (2009), Theorem 6, is not a finite counterexample. Its
graded-periodic construction has an infinite elementary abelian
quotient. Do not infer a finite example by truncating that proof.

Current proof route:

1. If r independent maximal subgroups have exponent p, choose
   generators adapted to their coordinate hyperplanes.
2. For a group of nilpotency class c, prove r>c forces exponent p,
   using commuting deletion maps in a universal nilpotent quotient
   and the lower central Lie ring.
3. If the maximal subgroups additionally have class at most c,
   r>c+1 first forces the entire group to have class at most c.
   This would bound the characteristic intersection by p^(c+1).
4. Give the sharp p=2 bound4. For p=3 compare the class-three
   exponent-three theorem with Laffey's stronger root-proportion
   theorem; the latter would give the bound27.
5. Test whether a class-three group of order3^8, generated by three
   order-three elements with all repeated triple commutators killed,
   realizes the bound27 through a cyclic generator permutation.
6. Preserve exact GAP controls, reconstruct a multiplication model
   independently if an extremal example is found, audit sources,
   and commit a precise partial report. No p>=5 answer follows
   merely from a bound depending on nilpotency class.
