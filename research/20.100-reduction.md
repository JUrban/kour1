# 20.100: torsion-free groups, a finite abelian reduction, and fixed-n certificates

Status: proved structural partial results. Independent certificates verify
n=4,5,6. The n=6 checker verified all3,245,190 collision branches and
19,094,383 relation implications. The full conjecture for arbitrary
n remains unresolved, so this entry does not increase the complete-candidate
count. Novelty and outside review remain pending.

## Exact question and prior scope

The supplied Notebook page162 was visually checked. For a positive integer n,
a group G has no elements of orders2,...,n+1. The question asks whether every
n-element subset can be ordered as a1,...,an so that a1,a2^2,...,an^n are
pairwise distinct. The identity is allowed in the subset.

Sun's [On permutations of {1,...,n} and related topics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10503v6),
version6 dated24 March2021, contains the exact Conjecture4.1. Theorem1.4(i)
proves the torsion-free abelian case; Theorem1.4(ii) gives n<=3. Both proofs
and the exact conjecture/remarks were read. Remark4.1(b) reports cyclic
searches for n=4,...,9 through respective orders100,100,70,60,30,30. Those
are finite-order searches, distinct from the all-group fixed-n certificates
below. The rest of the paper was not audited for all other conjectures.

## 1. Arbitrary torsion-free groups

**Theorem1.** Let a1,...,ar be infinite-order elements of any group, with
repetitions allowed, and let 0<e1<...<er be arbitrary integers. The elements
can be ordered as b1,...,br so that b1^e1,...,br^er are pairwise distinct.
One additional identity element may be included if assigned a distinct
positive exponent.

**Proof.** On the finite list of cyclic subgroups <ai>, use commensurability:
two such subgroups are equivalent when their intersection is nontrivial.
For infinite cyclic subgroups this is an equivalence relation. In fact,
nontrivial subgroups of an infinite cyclic group have finite index; hence
<a> cap <b> and <b> cap <c> have a common nontrivial subgroup inside <b>,
which also lies in <a> cap <c>. Within each of the finitely many equivalence
classes the intersection of all its cyclic subgroups is infinite cyclic.
Choose a nonidentity element c in that intersection. For each a in this
class choose a nonzero integer k_a with c=a^(k_a), and put

    length(a)=1/|k_a| > 0.

If a^i=b^j with i,j positive, then a,b are in the same class. Raising this
equality to the integer k_a*k_b gives

    c^(i*k_b)=c^(j*k_a).

Since c has infinite order, i*k_b=j*k_a. In particular

    i*length(a)=j*length(b).                         (1)

Order the list by nondecreasing length. The products e_i*length(b_i) are
strictly increasing, because the lengths are positive and the exponents
strictly increase. Equation(1) rules out equal powers. Repeated lengths,
or distinct elements with a common power, cause no problem. An identity
power cannot equal an infinite-order power. QED.

**Corollary1.** The assertion in20.100 holds for every torsion-free group,
without a commutativity, orderability, finite-generation or unique-root
assumption. It also holds for any finite subset of an arbitrary group
whose nonidentity elements all have infinite order.

## 2. Reduction to finite abelian groups

Fix n. Let P(n) be the assertion in20.100 for every group satisfying its
order restriction, and let P_ab_fin(n) be the same assertion restricted to
finite abelian groups.

**Theorem2.** P(n) and P_ab_fin(n) are equivalent.

**Proof.** One direction is immediate. Assume P_ab_fin(n), and let A be
an n-element subset of an arbitrary eligible group G.

Partition the nonidentity finite-order elements of A according to the cyclic
subgroups they generate, and call the resulting distinct cyclic groups
C1,...,Cs, with orders m1,...,ms. Every prime divisor of an m_j is greater
than n+1; otherwise a suitable power of a generator would have a forbidden
prime order. Therefore, for a finite-order a in A and 1<=i<=n,

    <a^i>=<a>.                                      (2)

In particular, two finite-order powers from this range can coincide only
when their bases generate the same C_j.

For each C_j take a separate abstract isomorphic cyclic factor D_j. Map
each finite-order a in A to its image in the corresponding coordinate of
D1 x ... x Ds. If 1 belongs to A, map it to the identity. These images are
distinct; equalities of their powers with exponents1,...,n are exactly
the equalities among the corresponding finite-order elements of A. Inside
a common coordinate this follows from the cyclic isomorphism, and between
different coordinates from(2) and nontriviality of all the relevant powers.

Let r be the number of infinite-order elements of A. Choose a prime
p>n+1 and append a separate cyclic factor C_p.
Replace those r elements by r distinct nonidentity elements of this new
coordinate, possible since p-1>=n+1>r. We have constructed n distinct
elements of the finite abelian group

    B=D1 x ... x Ds x C_p,

whose prime divisors are all greater than n+1. The new prime may divide
an m_j: the separate coordinate already keeps the dummy elements distinct
from the earlier images. By P_ab_fin(n), they admit
an assignment of exponents1,...,n giving distinct powers. Keep the assigned
exponents on the elements coming from finite-order members of A. Their
actual powers in G are distinct by construction. Assign the remaining
exponents to the infinite-order elements using Theorem1, in increasing
order of the positive lengths there. Their powers are distinct from each
other, and also from every finite-order power. This proves P(n). QED.

The reduction preserves n and has no bound on the orders m_j. It does not
reduce to finite cyclic groups alone. It is also not the invalid claim that
every group embeds in an abelian group: only the needed finite power
equalities are represented, with infinite-order elements handled separately.

## 3. A finite certificate system for fixed n

The reduction admits an explicit finite-order bound.

**Theorem3.** Let n>=2 and let p_n be the smallest prime greater than n+1.
If20.100 fails at n, it has a counterexample in a finite abelian group of
order at most p_n*n^(n-1). In particular, each fixed-n assertion is decidable
by a finite computation. Bertrand's postulate gives the looser elementary
bound 2(n+1)n^(n-1).

**Proof.** By Theorem2 a counterexample exists in a finite abelian group.
For each permutation of1,...,n choose one pair witnessing its failure,
and impose the corresponding relation i*x_a=j*x_b on a free S-module
on x1,...,xn, where S inverts the primes at most n+1. Call the quotient M.
The map to the original counterexample shows that its n distinguished
elements are distinct. The selected relations make every permutation
fail in M, so M itself is a counterexample as an additive group. Its
torsion orders have no small prime divisors.

Build a multigraph on the n variables with an edge for each relation,
labelled by its two coefficients i,j in1,...,n. For a connected component
with k vertices, use a spanning tree to express each variable as an
S-unit multiple of one generator t. Each further edge gives a cycle
relation (A-B)t=0 up to an S-unit, where A and B are the products of
edge coefficients around the two orientations of that simple cycle.
Its length is at most k, so, if A!=B,

    0<|A-B|<n^k.

If all the cycle relations vanish identically, the component is a free
S-module of rank1. Otherwise it is S/(m) for a positive integer m coprime
to the inverted primes, and m divides the small-prime-free part of one
of these nonzero cycle differences. Hence m<n^k. All the variables in
this component are unit multiples of t and generate the same finite
cyclic subgroup. Different components are direct summands.

If no component is free, M is already a finite abelian counterexample of
order at most n^n, which is less than p_n*n^(n-1). If some component is
free, let s<=n-1 be the total number of variables in the finite components.
Their cyclic factors have product of orders at most n^s. Apply the explicit
counterexample transfer in Theorem2, retaining those finite factors and
replacing all infinite-order variables by distinct elements of a separate
C_(p_n). If the resulting finite set admitted a good assignment, Theorem1
would lift it back to a good assignment in M, a contradiction. Thus this
finite abelian group is a counterexample, of order at most
p_n*n^s<=p_n*n^(n-1). QED.

For example the bounds at n=4,5,6 are448,4,375,85,536. These are bounds
on where some counterexample must exist, not a claim that the certificate
search enumerates all groups or subsets through these orders.

## 4. Certificate rules

Let S=Z[1/P], where P is the product of the primes at most n+1. Every
eligible finite abelian group is an S-module: multiplication by each of
these primes is bijective, so the inverse actions are defined uniquely.
Use additive notation in this section.

Introduce n distinguished variables x1,...,xn. A potential counterexample
has xi!=xj for i!=j, and for every permutation (e1,...,en) of1,...,n it has
at least one collision

    e_i*x_i - e_j*x_j = 0,      i<j.                 (3)

A node of the certificate is an integer presentation matrix R for relations
on these variables, interpreted over S. It asserts that no counterexample
can satisfy all the rows of R. There are two permitted rules:

1. A leaf is valid if e_i-e_j, the difference of two standard coordinate
   vectors, lies in the S-row span of R. Its relations force xi=xj.
2. A branch selects one permutation of1,...,n and includes one child for
   every pair i<j. The child's relations, after the displayed bijective
   relabelling of variables, must all belong to the S-row span of R plus
   the row(3). Each child must already have been proved.

Rule2 is sound because a counterexample to the chosen permutation must
have one of these collisions. The children cover every possible pair.
Relabelling preserves distinctness and the condition that every permutation
has a collision. The empty presentation at the root covers all tuples in
all S-modules. A finite acyclic proof with that root therefore proves
P_ab_fin(n), hence P(n) by Theorem2.

Only implication of the child's relations is required for this soundness
argument. The checker does not need to trust the generator's claim that a
child gives an exact quotient or a canonical normal form.

## 5. Search representation and independent verification

The search uses components in which xi=w_i*t, with a parameter t annihilated
by m. Here m=0 denotes a free S-generator; m>0 is coprime to P. A collision
joining two components merges their generators by a unit ratio and takes
the gcd of their annihilators. A collision within one component adds an
annihilator and again takes a gcd. All coefficients1,...,n are S-units.
The generator normalizes component weights and forgets variable labels
only after emitting the exact relabelling used by the checker.

The GAP checker takes a different route. It translates each component into
integer presentation rows. For every parent/collision it computes

    U M V = D

with U,V unimodular integer matrices and D a Smith diagonal matrix. It
checks the identity and both unimodularity assertions. A row v lies in
the S-row span exactly when the coordinates of vV beyond the rank vanish
and its other coordinates are divisible by the corresponding nonzero
diagonal entries after removing prime factors at most n+1. This is the
standard Smith-normal-form membership criterion after localization.

Every child relation is checked by this criterion, every leaf is checked
for an actual forced equality, and child identifiers must precede their
parent. The root has an empty presentation. These checks are sufficient
to verify a proof even if the generator's component arithmetic or its
canonicalization contained an error.

The scripts are `scripts/prove_20_100.py` and
`scripts/verify_20_100_certificate.g`. The certificates are compressed GAP
data files in `results/20.100-nN-certificate.g.gz`; each node calls the
checker with only integers, rationals, lists, and previously proved nodes.

A negative control weakens the order restriction for n=4 by allowing
elements of order5. The generator then returns C5 with its four
nonidentity elements. This is a genuine counterexample: distinct powered
outputs would be all four nonzero residues, with product -1 modulo5,
whereas the product of the four inputs times1*2*3*4 is1. The incomplete
search prefix is retained with an explicit `COUNTEREXAMPLE` result and
is not a proof certificate for20.100.
