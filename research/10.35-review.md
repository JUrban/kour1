Date: 2026-09-10. This is a self-audit, not an independent review.
The candidate is `research/10.35-proof.md`.

## Statement and quantifiers

- The supplied PDF page 40 was rendered and inspected. Both fields are
  printed without algebraic-closure bars; the degree n is the same
  on the complex and rational sides. The problem is unstarred.
- Taking n=2 suffices to refute the universal assertion. The group is
  a subgroup of GL_2(C), not merely a quotient of a matrix group.
- Residuality would require every nonidentity element to survive in
  some permitted image. The proof supplies one fixed nonidentity
  element a^2 killed by **all** homomorphisms into GL_2(Q).
- A homomorphism into a torsion-free subgroup of GL_2(Q) is in
  particular a homomorphism into GL_2(Q), so the stronger obstruction
  implies precisely the requested negative answer.

## Construction audit

1. T=diag(2i,2), not diag(i,1). Its fourth power is the nonidentity
   scalar 16I, preventing the rotation lift from introducing torsion.
2. The complex ratio of the diagonal entries is i, so conjugation
   acts by z -> iz on the normal unipotent subgroup. The common
   scalar factor 2 affects element orders without changing this action.
3. U(1) and T generate U(i) by conjugation, hence generate all of
   U(Z[i]). Negative powers give negative Gaussian integer entries.
4. The normal forms U(z)T^k are closed under multiplication and
   inversion. Their lower right entries determine k. Their upper
   right entries are 2^k z and therefore determine z.
5. The map G -> Z taking U(z)T^k to k has torsion-free kernel
   and quotient. Every finite-order element must first lie in that
   kernel and then be the identity. Thus torsion-freeness holds for
   arbitrary products of generators, not only the generators.
6. The commutation relation and inversion by T^2 hold exactly.

## Universal matrix lemma audit

1. Scalar X is handled separately. The proof never assumes that
   every nonidentity matrix is nonscalar.
2. Conjugacy to the inverse allows determinant -1 as well as +1.
   In the -1 case equality of traces forces trace zero, and the
   characteristic polynomial forces X^2=I. Omitting this branch
   would be an unjustified assumption.
3. For nonscalar X the centralizer algebra really is F[I,X],
   including both diagonalizable and nondiagonalizable cases, and
   including irreducible characteristic polynomials over F. The
   cyclic-vector proof requires no eigenvalues in the base field.
4. Commutation of X_1=YXY^{-1} with X puts X_1 in this algebra.
   Since conjugacy preserves nonscalarity, I and X_1 span it.
   Hence conjugation by Y normalizes the entire algebra, not just
   the subgroup generated by X.
5. The scalar subspace is invariant and the quotient is precisely
   one-dimensional over F. Its automorphism is multiplication by
   an element q of F, even if eigenvalues of X live in an extension.
6. In determinant one, X^{-1}=tr(X)I-X. Modulo scalar matrices,
   conjugation by Y^2 is therefore -1. Since the class of X is
   nonzero, q^2=-1 follows.
7. This is impossible in Q and R. Over Q(i) it is possible, and
   the proposed complex representation explicitly realizes q=i.

## Boundary controls

The assertion for arbitrary rational images is phi(a^2)=I; it is
not phi(a)=I. An explicit degree-two representation with image D8
separates a. The stronger assertion phi(a)=I is used only when
the image is torsion-free.

Indeed the residual kernels can be determined exactly. The lemma
applied to a and its conjugate b kills 2A in every rational image.
If an element U(z)T^k has k nonzero, the representation
U(z)T^k -> 2^k I separates it. If k=0 and z is not in 2Z[i],
the displayed D8 representation separates it. Thus the intersection
of kernels of all maps to GL_2(Q) is exactly 2A. Restricting to
torsion-free images gives intersection exactly A, using the same
scalar representation to separate every element outside A.

There is an explicit faithful representation of G in GL_3(Q),
so this example does not answer a different question permitting
the rational matrix degree to grow. That distinction is stated
in the proof and agrees with the printed fixed-degree question.

the claim is not that this abstract group is new. The candidate
claim is its application, with the elementary two-dimensional
obstruction, to the exact fixed-degree Notebook problem. Because
the argument is short and the problem is old, further checking
for prior coverage or a historical formulation issue is especially
appropriate. No claim of established novelty is made here.
