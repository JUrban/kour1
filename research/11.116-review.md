# Internal review of the candidate solution to11.116

The proposed answer is yes, with explicit bound2r[G:D], where the normal
quasicyclic subgroup D has r factors at distinct primes. This is a complete
candidate resolution of the printed question. Outside mathematical review
and priority verification remain pending.

## Hypotheses and proof audit

- The actual Notebook page56 was visually checked and retained. The question
  uses order dimension. Shevrin2012 page304 explicitly gives the equivalent
  definition by an order embedding into a product of chains. No stronger
  requirement of preserving lattice joins is being substituted.
- The Chernikov definition used is exactly the one in Shevrin2012 page302:
  a finite extension of a finite product of quasicyclic groups. A repeated
  prime in the normal product would violate the printed hypothesis. The
  case of zero factors is a finite group and is handled separately.
- The map to integers is a bijection of underlying sets, not a group map.
  Only terminating base-p expansions are used. Appending zeros leaves the
  image unchanged, removing the ambiguity from finite expansion lengths.
- A coset of the order-p^n subgroup fixes precisely the digits after the
  nth position. Arbitrary first n digits give an entire integer interval,
  not merely a subset with the same endpoints. Finite singleton cosets
  and the unique coset of the full quasicyclic group are both included.
- Subgroups split into primary components because all their elements
  have finite order and each primary component is an integer multiple
  of the element. The number of distinct primary factors is finite.
  The repeated-prime diagonal obstruction explains why the hypothesis
  is material to this argument.
- Each nonempty fiber of H in a fixed coset of D is exactly a coset of
  H intersect D, as proved by the explicit product in Section3. Neither
  normality of H nor splitting of G over D is assumed.
- All fibers, including empty ones, are retained. The empty-set sentinel
  compares below every nonempty box and cannot equal a nonempty box.
  Inclusion of subgroups is equivalent to inclusion of every fiber;
  hence no information is lost by this encoding.
- The same coordinate system is used for all subgroups of G. It has
  exactly2r[G:D] coordinates, including infinite subgroups. There is no
  passage from a collection of unrelated finite realizers to an infinite
  one and no unproved compactness step.
- The final linear orders are constructed directly by coordinate priority
  and lexicographic ties. Injectivity ensures totality and separates each
  pair which fails the desired order. Joins need not be coordinate maxima.

## Completed exact controls

Run from the repository root:

    python3 scripts/verify_11_116.py
    bin/gap scripts/verify_11_116.g

The Python arithmetic check verifies every coset at the finite levels
2^8,3^5,5^4,7^3:2,056 cosets. It independently compares40,000 pairs of
quasicyclic cosets using exact rational congruence against endpoint
inclusion. These pairs include singleton, finite and full-group cosets,
with levels beyond those needed for the chosen representatives. A false
containment from the ordinary numerical interval hull is rejected; a
diagonal subgroup at a repeated prime is explicitly not rectangular.

GAP enumerates actual subgroups and compares its subgroup-inclusion test
with both the coordinate inequalities and the intersection of the constructed
linear orders. All792 subgroups,2,176 fibers and242,344 ordered subgroup
pairs pass in these six groups:

| G | Order of cyclic D | [G:D] | Subgroups | Ordered pairs | Linear orders |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|Dihedral group of order32|16|2|36|1,296|4|
|Dihedral group of order54|27|2|44|1,936|4|
|Dihedral group of order288|144|2|418|174,724|8|
|Generalized quaternion group of order32|16|2|20|400|4|
|Quaternion group of order32 times S3|48|4|252|63,504|16|
|C9 times S3|9|6|22|484|12|

The quaternion case is a nonsplit extension. The last case explicitly
checks that G/D is nonabelian. Two cases have distinct primary factors.
These finite controls do not prove the infinite theorem; they independently
test the encoding and its inclusion direction in actual group models.

the converse as11.116. The proofs of older structural results cited in
its bibliography were not audited. Our sufficiency proof uses the stated
definition of Chernikov group and elementary arguments only.

resolution. This establishes neither novelty nor absence of prior work.

outside scrutiny, especially the common digit ordering and fiber encoding.
