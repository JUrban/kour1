# Internal audit of the prior-theorem answer to 17.87

Date: 11 September 2026. This is an internal logical audit, not outside
review. The answer is a complete consequence of published results and
is excluded from the working new-candidate count.

1. **Exact target.** The printed question asks for intermediate growth
   and finitely generated Schur multiplier. It does not require residual
   finiteness, simplicity, finite presentation, or a specified torsion
   property of the final group. The constructed multiplier is zero.
2. **Perfect input.** Use the infinite simple finite-index derived
   subgroup of the explicit group in Nekrashevych Theorem 8.1. Finite
   index gives finite generation and preserves intermediate growth.
   Infinite simplicity excludes abelianness and gives perfectness.
3. **Correct covering group.** The universal extension is F'/[F,R],
   not all of F/[F,R]. Its central kernel is (R intersect F')/[F,R].
   The latter is allowed to be infinitely generated.
4. **Finite generation.** Arbitrary lifts of a finite generating tuple
   generate the perfect central extension, since U=HK implies U'=H'.
   This avoids an invalid inference from finite generation of a group
   to finite generation of its derived subgroup.
5. **Second cover.** Two central extensions do not always compose to
   a central extension. Here the upper covering group is perfect;
   each relevant commutator is central and defines a homomorphism to
   an abelian group, hence vanishes. Universality then supplies a
   section, and perfectness eliminates the remaining central factor.
6. **Homology input.** Hopf's formula is stated exactly and identifies
   the kernel of the second universal extension with H_2(U,Z).
   The argument does not infer vanishing from the five-term sequence
   alone, which would be insufficient.
7. **Growth estimate.** For fixed r, D_r is finite despite an arbitrary
   central kernel. Products of m of its elements have polynomially many
   multiplicity vectors in m. The quotient block values stay ordered.
   No commutation of their lifts is used. Empty blocks cover lengths
   not divisible by r; m=ceiling(n/r) handles the limit.
8. **Order of limits.** First n tends to infinity for fixed r, killing
   the polynomial factor; only then r tends to infinity. No uniform
   bound on |D_r| is asserted or needed.
9. **Intermediate rather than polynomial.** A quotient of a group
   with a polynomial ball bound has a polynomial bound. The quotient
   P already has intermediate growth. Together with the upper bound
   this proves exactly the required growth type.
10. **Evidence.** The group is infinite and is defined using a published
    infinite construction. No finite computation certifies its growth
    or universal homology. The evidence is the written deduction and
    the explicitly scoped published input, with source hashes retained.
