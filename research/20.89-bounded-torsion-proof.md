# Almost Engel elements when finite subgroups have bounded order

This is partial coverage of Kourovka 20.89 / 19.83 in every
characteristic. It does not resolve the general positive-characteristic
case. The deduction uses the previously established residual-finiteness
reduction; no new priority claim is made.

Write [x,a] = x^-1 a^-1 x a and iterate on the right. An almost Engel
element a has a finite set E such that, for every x in the ambient
group, all sufficiently long [x,_n a] lie in E. The entry time can
depend on x.

**Theorem.** Let G be a linear group. Suppose that every finite subgroup
of G has order at most an integer b. Then its almost Engel elements
form a characteristic subgroup L. The torsion elements of L form a
finite characteristic subgroup K of G, of order at most b, and L/K is locally
nilpotent. The same set K is a finite Engel sink for every element of
L relative to all of G.

No assertion that the torsion elements of G form a subgroup is intended.

**Proof.** Let S initially denote the set of almost Engel elements of
G and put L = <S>. Automorphisms preserve S, so L is characteristic
in G. We do not yet assume S is closed under multiplication or inverses.

The result in [20.89-residual-reduction.md](20.89-residual-reduction.md)
says that a group generated by finitely many members of S is
finite-by-nilpotent, over any field. Thus every finitely generated
subgroup H of L is finite-by-nilpotent: express its generators as words
in finitely many members of S and their inverses, apply that result,
and intersect its finite normal kernel with H. Call this property (P).

Let y_1,...,y_r be torsion elements of L. Their subgroup H has, by
(P), a finite normal subgroup D with H/D nilpotent. The quotient is
finitely generated by torsion elements, so it is finite. We use here
the standard elementary fact that a finitely generated nilpotent group
generated by torsion elements is finite. For example, its abelianization
is finite; the successive lower-central factors are generated by simple
commutators in those generators and are quotients of tensor powers of
that finite abelianization, so each factor is finite. Consequently H
itself is finite.

It follows that the torsion elements of L form a locally finite
characteristic subgroup K. This argument proves closure under products
and inverses; it does not assume that periodicity alone implies local
finiteness. If K had more than b elements, choose b+1 distinct elements
in it. They would generate a finite subgroup with more than b elements,
contradicting the hypothesis. Therefore |K| <= b. Characteristic
invariance in L and then in G makes K characteristic in G.

For a finitely generated H <= L, choose its finite normal kernel D
as above. Every element of D is a torsion element of L, so D <= K.
Hence H/(H intersect K), a quotient of H/D, is nilpotent. Every
finitely generated subgroup of L/K is the image of some such H;
therefore L/K is locally nilpotent.

Finally take l in L and x in G. Normality of L gives [x,l] in L.
The two elements [x,l]K and lK generate a nilpotent subgroup of L/K.
Repeated commutation of the former with the latter eventually becomes
trivial. Equivalently, [x,_n l] belongs to K for every sufficiently
large n. Thus K is a sink for l relative to all of G, and L <= S.
The reverse inclusion is the definition of L, proving S = L and all
the claims. In particular, the proof does not treat an arbitrary test
element x as almost Engel. QED.

**Corollary.** If a linear group G has a torsion-free subgroup H of
finite index d, the same conclusion holds, with |K| dividing d.

Indeed, any finite subgroup Q of G acts on the d left cosets G/H by
left multiplication. The stabilizer in Q of gH is Q intersect gHg^-1,
which is trivial because H is torsion-free. Each orbit therefore has
size |Q|, giving |Q| dividing d. This supplies the bound in the theorem,
and applying the same argument to the resulting finite subgroup K
gives its divisibility assertion. H need not be normal, and replacing
it by its core is unnecessary.

## Examples and limits

The hypothesis permits a nontrivial finite sink in positive
characteristic. In every characteristic p, the group S_3 times Z
embeds in GL_4(F_p(t)) by

    (s,k) -> diag(P_s, t^k),

where P_s is the faithful three-point permutation matrix. Its
torsion-free subgroup 1 times Z has index 6. Every element is almost
Engel, since a commutator has zero Z-coordinate and the first factor
is finite. The theorem's K is S_3 times 0. A smaller common sink is
A_3 times 0: all first commutators already lie there. For a transposition
the commutator map fixes each element of A_3, so that smaller common
sink cannot be reduced. This example also distinguishes the theorem's
canonical torsion subgroup from a least possible common sink.

The finite-subgroup bound is not automatic for finitely generated
linear groups in positive characteristic. Consider

    U = { [[1,f],[0,1]] : f in F_p[t,t^-1] }

and d = diag(t,1). The group <d, [[1,1],[0,1]]> contains U, because
conjugation produces all monomials t^i, and their products add the
entries. For each r the entries in the F_p-span of 1,t,...,t^(r-1)
give a finite elementary abelian subgroup of order p^r. Consequently
the bounded-order argument cannot be applied to all finitely generated
positive-characteristic linear groups.

The local property (P) and the torsion-subgroup argument also appear
in the characteristic-zero proof. What is used here in their place
after that common starting point is precisely the stated bound on
finite subgroups. No assertion about arbitrary periodic subgroups,
or about unrestricted positive-characteristic almost Engel closure,
has been added.
