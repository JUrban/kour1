# An exact cubic-time algorithm for palindromic length: Problem16.9

Status: complete candidate affirmative resolution. Internal audit and
independent exact controls are recorded separately. Outside review and
further novelty checks remain pending. 11 September 2026.

## Result and algorithm

Let F_n have the specified free basis x_1,...,x_n. A palindrome is an
element whose reduced word equals its reversal. Write pl(w) for the
minimum number of palindromes whose product is w, with pl(1)=0.

**Theorem.** Given a word of length L in the specified basis and its
inverses, one can compute pl(w), and a factorization with exactly
pl(w) palindromes, using O(L^3) arithmetic operations and O(L^2)
integer storage. The integers in the dynamic program have size O(L).
This answers16.9 affirmatively, uniformly in the finite rank.

Here is the explicit algorithm. Use a new alphabet {0,1,...,n}, all
of whose letters are involutions. Replace x_i by the two-letter word
i0 and x_i^-1 by 0i, obtaining a word W of length at most2L. For a
word V on the involution alphabet, let d(V) be the minimum number
of unmatched positions in a noncrossing partial pairing of equal
letters. Then

    pl(w) = min { d(W), d(W0) }.                       (1)

The pairing number is computed by the interval recurrence

    D(i,i)=0,
    D(i,j)=min { 1+D(i+1,j),
                 D(i+1,k)+D(k+1,j) : i<k<j and V_i=V_k }.

Intervals are half-open, [i,j). The first term leaves position i
unmatched; the other terms pair it with k. Apply the recurrence to
V=W0. Its table contains both d(W) and d(W0). There are O(L^2)
intervals and O(L) possible partners for each. The proof of (1)
and the recovery of a minimum factorization follow below.

## Palindromes become involutions in an index-two extension

Let tau be the automorphism of F_n taking every x_i to x_i^-1,
and form H=F_n semidirect <t>, where t^2=1 and t acts as tau.
Put s_0=t and s_i=x_i t for1<=i<=n. A change of presentation gives

    H = <s_0,...,s_n | s_0^2=...=s_n^2=1>,

the free product of n+1 cyclic groups of order2. Indeed x_i=s_i t,
and these substitutions identify the displayed relations with
t^2=1 and t x_i t=x_i^-1, without imposing any further relation.
The original F_n embeds by the semidirect-product construction. It
is the kernel of the parity homomorphism epsilon:H->C2 sending
every s_i to the nonidentity element.

For p in F_n, reversal of its reduced word equals p precisely when
tau(p)=p^-1. Consequently

    p is a palindrome  <=>  (p t)^2=1.

The element pt is always nonidentity, since it has odd parity.
Conversely every nonidentity involution of H has odd parity. To
see this, cyclically reduce a free-product word by conjugation.
A cyclically reduced word of length at least2 has all its positive
powers reduced, hence has infinite order. Thus every involution is
conjugate to some s_i and has odd parity. It can therefore be written
uniquely as pt with p a palindrome. Involutions of H are exactly the
reflections, meaning conjugates of the generators s_i.

Let r(h) denote the minimum number of reflections whose product is h.
We claim

    pl(w)=min { r(w), r(wt) }.                         (2)

If w=p_1...p_k is a product of palindromes, then

    wt^k = (p_1 t)(tau(p_2)t)(tau^2(p_3)t)...(tau^(k-1)(p_k)t)

is a product of k reflections. This gives the inequality from right
to left in (2). Conversely choose j in {0,1} and a minimum
reflection factorization wt^j=r_1...r_m. Write r_i=q_i t with
q_i a palindrome. Parity gives m=j modulo2. Expanding the product
and cancelling t^j gives

    w=q_1 tau(q_2) tau^2(q_3)...tau^(m-1)(q_m).

Each displayed factor is a palindrome, since tau preserves reversal.
Thus pl(w)<=m, proving (2). Identity palindrome factors, if any,
can simply be omitted; no nontrivial palindrome requirement is used
in deriving the two inequalities.

## Reflection length equals the noncrossing pairing number

Consider an arbitrary word V in letters s_0,...,s_n. A partial
pairing uses disjoint pairs of equal-labelled positions. It is
noncrossing if there are no two pairs {i,k},{j,l} with i<j<k<l.
Let d(V) be its minimum number of unmatched positions.

**Lemma1.** Inserting or deleting two adjacent equal letters leaves
d(V) unchanged.

One inequality follows by pairing the two inserted letters. For
the other, take a pairing before deleting the adjacent equal
positions. If they are paired together, delete that pair. If both
are unmatched, just remove them. If exactly one has another partner,
leave that partner unmatched. If both have different partners,
pair those former partners with each other; their labels agree.
In every case the unmatched count does not increase.

The last repair preserves the noncrossing condition. One may draw
the original pairs as disjoint arcs above the ordered positions;
join the two former partners along their old arcs and the short
boundary interval between the adjacent deleted positions. This
curve meets no other arc and can be replaced by a pairing arc.
Equivalently the two partners are either on opposite sides, or on
the same side with their old arcs nested; these exhaust the
noncrossing possibilities for adjacent positions. The other repairs
only remove arcs. This proves the reverse inequality and the lemma.

The normal-form reductions in H delete precisely adjacent equal
letters. Lemma1 therefore makes d(V) a function of the represented
element of H, with no requirement that V was initially reduced.

**Lemma2.** For every h in H, d(h)=r(h).

A word v s_i v^-1 has a pairing of all letters in v with the
corresponding letters in v^-1, leaving only s_i unmatched. A
concatenation of k such words consequently has a noncrossing
pairing with at most k unmatched positions. Lemma1 then gives
d(h)<=r(h).

Conversely choose a pairing of V with m unmatched positions. Delete
those positions. The remaining word represents1: an innermost pair
is now an adjacent equal pair, and removing these pairs inductively
reduces the word to the empty word. Deleting a letter s_i from a
word X s_i Y changes its represented element by

    X s_i Y = (X s_i X^-1)(X Y).

Delete the m unmatched positions successively, from left to right.
This expresses h as a product of m reflections, followed by the
remaining word, which is1. Hence r(h)<=d(h), proving Lemma2.

Combining Lemma2 with (2) proves formula (1). The interval recurrence
computes exactly the required minimum: once the first position is
paired, noncrossing forces all other pairs to lie either inside
that pair or after it. The unmatched case accounts for every
remaining possibility. Empty intervals give the induction base.

## Recovering the minimum palindrome factors

Store a minimizing choice in every interval. Trace it back for the
better of the two endpoints W and W0, obtaining the set of m
unmatched positions. The deletion procedure in Lemma2 explicitly
gives r_1,...,r_m, where each r_i=X_i s_(a_i) X_i^-1 and X_i
is the prefix of the word after the earlier deletions.

Every r_i s_0 has even parity. To rewrite an even-parity word in
the original free basis, scan its positions starting with even
parity. A nonzero letter a contributes x_a at an even position
and x_a^-1 at an odd position; a zero letter contributes nothing.
Every letter toggles parity. Freely reduce the resulting word.
This is precisely rewriting with the coset representatives1,t,
since s_i=x_i t and t x_i=x_i^-1 t.

This recovers q_i=r_i t. Negate every letter in q_i for even i
(indices starting at1), obtaining tau^(i-1)(q_i). Their product
is w by the calculation in (2), and all are palindromes. Formula
(1) proves that their number m is minimum. In particular a minimum
output for w!=1 cannot contain an identity factor, since removing
it would contradict that equality. For w=1 the empty output works.

Each X_i has length at most2L+1, and there are O(L) factors, so
recovering and storing the output takes O(L^2) elementary word
operations and space. It does not increase the cubic running-time
bound of the dynamic program. In bit operations the numeric part
has the corresponding O(log(L+1)) overhead; generator-index input
encoding is separate from the number of word operations.

## Implementation, controls and attribution

`scripts/palindromic_length.py` implements the algorithm and emits
both the exact length and minimum factors. For example,

    python3 scripts/palindromic_length.py 1 2 3 1

returns length3 and an actual factorization in the free group on
the letters1,2,3. The companion controls compare the recurrence
with direct enumeration of all deletion subsets, check exhaustive
free-word families against a recent two-palindrome characterization,
and replay all returned factors in GAP's actual free group. See
`16.9-review.md` and `results/16.9-summary.json` for evidence and scope.

The reflection-length deletion characterization is established prior
work in Coxeter groups, due to Dyer2001; the special free-product
case needed here is proved in full above. No novelty is asserted
for that characterization or for interval dynamic programming.
Frid's2025 primary preprint studies the two- and three-palindrome
cases and discusses the general algorithm question. Its scope and
the earlier literature are recorded in the review. The specific
index-two conversion and resulting general algorithm require further
priority checking; no absence of prior work is asserted.
