# Report: a positive-characteristic unit-group case

September 12, 2026. Complete candidates remain 46; outside reviews
remain zero. Priority is unestablished. This does not solve 20.89
for arbitrary linear subgroups in positive characteristic.

The proof shows that, over an infinite field F of any characteristic,
almost Engel elements in a normal subgroup G of A* are Engel, where
A is a finite-dimensional associative unital F-algebra. Hence they
form HP(G). In particular this covers normal subgroups of GL_n(F)
and SL_n(F). The elementary argument conjugates a finite minimal
sink by the units 1+t*a: two parameters giving the same conjugate
force the sink element to commute with a, so the entire sink is {1}.

For a unital subalgebra of M_n(F_q), it also proves that a nontrivial
minimal sink has size at least q-s(a)+1, where s(a) is the number of
distinct eigenvalues of a in F_q. The same hypothesis on normalization
by the unit group is retained.

The native and independent verifiers compare complete sink sets for
107 GL_2-conjugacy representatives in eight GL_2 / SL_2 cases over
F2,F3,F5,F7. There are 112,692 starting matrices across those cases,
83 nontrivial sinks, and 423 parameter conjugates whose distinctness
and sink membership are checked. Both finite algorithms agree on
every printed matrix, including an S3 control where a parameter unit
does not normalize the subgroup. That example also disproves dropping
the normalization hypothesis from the infinite theorem.

Native GAP uses actual finite-field matrix groups and repeated images
of commutator maps, with an independent finite Fitting-subgroup check.
Python constructs residue matrices separately and removes noncyclic
vertices of each functional graph. The computations verify the
finite bound and identities; the infinite-field proof is explicit
and does not follow just from these samples.
