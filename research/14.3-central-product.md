# 14.3: a limit of the two tests and an additional character-field obstruction

Status: a proved limitation and a positive example, not a resolution of
the arbitrary finite-group problem. No novelty claim. 11 September 2026.

The real-character and odd-class-root conditions in
`14.3-reduction.md` do not jointly force a central element to be a
square in the centre. An explicit group of order96 demonstrates this.
Nevertheless every central integral unit of this group is symmetric.
The proof of the latter assertion uses algebraic integers in a character
field, a constraint that the two earlier tests do not see.

## The group and the element that survives both tests

Put

    K = <r,s | r^8=s^2=1, r^s=r^3>,       z_K=r^4,
    J = <a,t | a^3=t^4=1, a^t=a^-1>,     z_J=t^2,
    G = (K x J)/<(z_K,z_J)>.

Both presentations are the indicated semidirect products, of orders16
and12. Their centres are <z_K> and <z_J>, respectively, each of order2.
The two factors embed in G, since the diagonal subgroup intersects each
factor trivially. Write z for the common image of z_K and z_J.

An element represented by (k,j) is central in G precisely when k is
central in K and j is central in J: its commutator with (h,1) has second
coordinate1, so membership in the diagonal kernel forces that commutator
to be trivial, and likewise for the other factor. Consequently

    |G|=96,       Z(G)=<z> is C2,       Z(G)^2={1}.

For x the image of (1,t), we have x^2=z. The preimage of its centralizer
is K x C_J(t), by the same intersection argument. Since C_J(t)=<t>,
the conjugacy class of x has size3. Thus the odd-class-root test allows z.

We next show that every real-valued irreducible character of G kills z.
The irreducible characters of K consist of four linear characters and
three characters of degree2. The latter arise from the nontrivial orbits
of multiplication by3 on the characters of <r>: the exponent orbits are
{1,3}, {2,6}, {5,7}. Their values at r are respectively

    zeta_8+zeta_8^3=sqrt(-2),       0,       -sqrt(-2).

This list is complete since the sum of squared degrees is16. The four
linear characters and the character from {2,6} kill z_K. The remaining
two are not real-valued. Thus no real irreducible character of K is
nontrivial on z_K.

Every irreducible character of G inflates to a tensor-product character
phi times psi of K x J, trivial on (z_K,z_J). If it is nontrivial on z,
then phi is nontrivial on z_K. Its restriction to the embedded K is
psi(1) phi, which is not real-valued by the preceding list. Therefore
every real-valued irreducible character of G kills z. Both necessary
conditions from the earlier note allow this central nonsquare.

This is a counterexample only to the proposed sufficiency of the two
group-theoretic tests. It is not a counterexample to14.3.

## The additional arithmetic constraint

Let chi be a complex irreducible character of a finite group H, of
degree d and character field F=Q(chi). If u is a central unit of ZH,
Schur's lemma gives its scalar

    alpha = chi(u)/d in F.

Both alpha and alpha^-1 are algebraic integers. Indeed left
multiplication by u and by its integral inverse give mutually inverse
integer matrices on ZH; the scalar on any irreducible constituent of
the complex regular representation is an eigenvalue of the corresponding
integer matrix. Hence alpha is a unit of the ring of integers of F.
Since chi(h^-1) is the complex conjugate of chi(h), the scalar for u*
is bar(alpha). It follows that a possible q(u)=u(u*)^-1 must satisfy

    chi(q(u))/d = alpha/bar(alpha),       alpha in O_F^x.

This is a necessary condition only; choosing allowed scalars in the
separate fields need not produce a unit of the integral group ring.

In particular, every algebraic-integer unit of Q(sqrt(-2)) is +1 or -1.
Here is a proof requiring no formula for its full ring of integers.
The norms of alpha and alpha^-1 are positive rational integers whose
product is1. Thus alpha bar(alpha)=1. Its integral trace T satisfies
-2<=T<=2, and alpha solves X^2-TX+1=0. The possibilities T=0 and T=+/-1
would generate Q(i) and Q(sqrt(-3)), respectively, neither of which is
Q(sqrt(-2)); distinctness follows since2 and3/2 are not rational squares.
The possibilities T=+/-2 give alpha=+/-1. Thus alpha/bar(alpha)=1
for every such unit.

## A degree-four character excludes z

The degree-two representation of K given by

    r -> diag(zeta_8,zeta_8^3),       s -> [[0,1],[1,0]]

is irreducible and has character phi from the exponent orbit {1,3}.
Its values belong to Q(sqrt(-2)) and phi(z_K)=-2.
There is also an irreducible degree-two representation of J:

    a -> diag(zeta_3,zeta_3^-1),       t -> [[0,1],[-1,0]].

Its character psi has integer values: on a^i t^(2e) it has value
(-1)^e(zeta_3^i+zeta_3^-i), and on elements with odd t-exponent it
vanishes. In particular psi(z_J)=-2. Irreducibility of both displayed
representations follows because the diagonal generator has distinct
eigenlines and the other generator interchanges them.

Their tensor product is irreducible on K x J. The diagonal element
(z_K,z_J) acts trivially, so it descends to an irreducible character
chi of G with

    chi(1)=4,       chi(z)=-4,       Q(chi)=Q(sqrt(-2)).

The last equality follows from the displayed values and from
chi(image(r,1))=2sqrt(-2).

For a central integral unit u, the arithmetic constraint forces
chi(q(u))/4=1, so q(u) cannot equal z. The exact obstruction argument
in `14.3-reduction.md` gives q(u) in Z(G)={1,z}. Therefore q(u)=1,
which says u=u*. In particular14.3 is affirmative for this group.

## Independent finite calculation and logical audit

`scripts/verify_14_3_central_product.g` constructs K as an explicit
permutation group, forms the quotient of its direct product with
SmallGroup(12,1), and obtains IdGroup(G)=[96,123]. It checks the centre,
central squares, x^2=z, the centralizer index3, and all real irreducible
characters. The21 Frobenius--Schur indicators are computed independently
by summing over the96 actual element squares; they agree with the
real-valued test. There are15 real characters and six characters
nontrivial on z. Two of the latter have degree4 and field Q(sqrt(-2)).
Exactly six elements square to z and have odd class size, all of size3.
The process exited zero and its complete log is retained as
`results/14.3-central-product.log`.

The proof does not use the database identifier to deduce any group
property. The diagonal-kernel intersection is checked separately for
the centre and the centralizer. Real-valuedness is tested on the embedded
K, so no unsupported rule about products of nonreal character fields is
used. The scalar-integrality argument uses the integer regular matrices,
not the generally false assertion that division of an algebraic integer
by d preserves integrality. Both u and u^-1 are needed to get a field
unit. The final conclusion uses the proved fact q(u) is a group element;
an arbitrary central element of QG would not be restricted to {1,z}.

The theorem is a positive example and a limitation of a proposed method.
The general problem, any novelty of these observations, and outside
review remain pending. The complete-candidate count is unchanged.
