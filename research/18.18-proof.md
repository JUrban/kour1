# Both non-enumerability assertions in Problem 18.18

Status: complete candidate affirmative proof with internal audit and
exact controls passed. Outside review and further priority checking
remain pending. 11 September 2026.

Let T_fin be the group-language sentences true in every finite group,
and T_cof those true in all but finitely many finite groups up to
isomorphism. The latter is equivalent to truth in every finite group
of sufficiently large order. The Notebook recalls Mal'cev's theorem
that T_fin is not computably enumerable. We use that theorem as input.
All other ingredients of the reductions below are proved here.

**Theorem.** Neither T_cof nor its complement in the set of group-language
sentences is computably enumerable.

## Relativizing to common centralizers

For any sentence phi let phi^C(a,b) be its effective relativization to

    C(x;a,b) := xa=ax and xb=bx.

First rename bound variables to avoid capture. Replace each universal
quantifier by a universal quantifier restricted using implication from
C, and each existential quantifier by conjunction with C. Keep atomic
formulas and Boolean connectives unchanged. Since the common centralizer
is a subgroup, induction on formulas proves that phi^C(a,b) holds in G
exactly when C_G(a,b) satisfies phi.

Set P_phi=forall a forall b phi^C(a,b). If phi belongs to T_fin, then
P_phi holds in every finite group. If a finite H fails phi, take
G_n=H x S_n for n>=3 and a=(1,(1 ... n)), b=(1,(1 2)). These two
permutations generate S_n, whose center is trivial, so C_G_n(a,b)=H x 1.
Thus P_phi fails in groups of unbounded order. We have the reduction

    phi in T_fin  iff  P_phi in T_cof.

This proves that T_cof is not computably enumerable. The second
assertion needs a different reduction and the following two lemmas.

## A sentence whose finite models are exactly S_n, n>=5

**Lemma 1.** There is an explicit group-language sentence sigma such
that a finite group G satisfies sigma if and only if G is isomorphic
to S_n for some n>=5.

We give fixed first-order formula abbreviations and five axioms; their
finite expansion is the required sentence. There are no quantifiers
over arbitrary subsets, natural numbers, or variable-length tuples.

Choose a parameter t with t!=1 and t^2=1. Let C_t(c) assert that c is
conjugate to t. Let J(c,d) mean that c,d belong to C_t, are distinct,
and do not commute. Let Q(u) for a four-tuple u=(u_1,...,u_4) assert
that its entries belong to C_t and are pairwise distinct and pairwise
J-related. Put

    M(c,u) := C_t(c) and AND_(i=1)^4 (c=u_i or c u_i != u_i c),
    E(u,v) := forall c (C_t(c) implies (M(c,u) iff M(c,v))).

On the domain Q, E is an equivalence relation, being equality of the
subsets specified by M. We refer to its equivalence classes as vertices.
This is only notation: every vertex quantifier below expands to four
group variables satisfying Q, and vertex equality expands to E.

Require the following five assertions.

1. There exist at least five vertices.
2. Each c in C_t is incident with exactly two distinct vertices, where
   incidence with [u] means M(c,u).
3. Each pair of distinct vertices has exactly one common incident c
   in C_t.
4. For every c in C_t and u satisfying Q,

       E(c u c^-1,u)  iff  not M(c,u),

   where conjugation is applied to the four entries of u.
5. The common centralizer of C_t is trivial:

       forall g ((forall c (C_t(c) implies gc=cg)) implies g=1).

For example assertion 2 expands as: for every c in C_t there exist
Q-tuples u,v with not E(u,v) and M(c,u),M(c,v), and every Q-tuple z
with M(c,z) satisfies E(z,u) or E(z,v). Assertions 1 and 3 use similarly
fixed tuples and ordinary uniqueness. Define sigma by existentially
quantifying t and conjoining these assertions with t!=1 and t^2=1.

Suppose a finite group satisfies sigma. Its vertex set V is finite,
with |V|>=5. Assertions 2 and 3 identify C_t bijectively with the
two-element subsets of V. Conjugation by G preserves every displayed
formula and hence gives an action on V and this incidence structure.
An element fixing V pointwise fixes every c by assertion 3, and is
identity by assertion 5. The action on V is therefore faithful.

For c in C_t, conjugation by c fixes c itself, so preserves the pair
of its incident vertices. Assertion 4 says that it moves both of them
and fixes every other vertex. Thus c acts as the transposition on
its incident pair. Every pair occurs, so the faithful image of G in
Sym(V) contains every transposition. It is all of Sym(V), proving
G is isomorphic to S_|V|.

Conversely let G=S_n, n>=5, with t a transposition. Then C_t is the
set of two-element subsets of {1,...,n}, and J means that the two
subsets intersect. Four distinct pairwise intersecting two-element
subsets have a common point. Indeed, after choosing {a,b} and {a,c},
any third not containing a must be {b,c}; there is no fourth distinct
two-element subset meeting all three. The sets defined by Q-tuples
are therefore exactly the stars of transpositions at individual points.

More precisely M(c,u) holds precisely when c contains that common
point: a two-element subset not containing it cannot meet all four
distinct other endpoints. Every point supplies such a Q-tuple since
n-1>=4. Thus the vertices defined above are exactly the n original
points. Assertions 1–4 follow from ordinary transposition action.
Assertion 5 holds because transpositions generate S_n and its center
is trivial. This proves Lemma 1 in both directions.

## Every finite group occurs as a centralizer in all large S_n

**Lemma 2.** For each finite group H of order h, and every
n>=2h^2+3, there are a,b in S_n with C_S_n(a,b) isomorphic to H.

Enumerate H as h_0=1,h_1,...,h_(h-1). On
Omega=H x {0,...,h-1}, of size h^2, define

    a(g,j)=(g,j+1 modulo h),
    b(g,j)=(g h_j,j).

The fixed points of b are exactly H x {0}. This also holds when h=1.
A permutation f commuting with a and b must preserve that level;
write f(g,0)=(u(g),0). Commutation with a gives f(g,j)=(u(g),j),
and commutation with b gives u(g h_j)=u(g) h_j for all j. Since the
h_j enumerate H, u(g)=u(1)g. Conversely every such left translation
commutes with a and b. Their common centralizer is exactly a regular
copy of H acting identically on all levels.

The group generated by a,b is transitive on Omega: move to level j,
apply b, and move back, realizing right multiplication by h_j on
level zero; then a reaches every level.

For the requested n put k=n-h^2 and add a disjoint set of k points.
Extend a there by a k-cycle and b by a transposition joining consecutive
points. They generate S_k on that set. As k>h^2 and k>=3, these two
orbits have distinct sizes. Any permutation commuting with a and b
preserves each orbit; its centralizer is the product of the two
centralizers, namely H x Z(S_k)=H. This proves Lemma 2 for every
n in the stated final interval, not just for a subsequence.

## Reduction to the complement

With the fixed sentence sigma from Lemma 1, construct effectively

    R_phi := (not sigma) or (exists a exists b not phi^C(a,b)).

If phi belongs to T_fin, the existential disjunct fails in every
finite group. Thus R_phi fails in every S_n for n>=5, and is not
in T_cof.

If phi does not belong to T_fin, choose a finite H failing it.
Every finite group not satisfying sigma satisfies R_phi by its first
disjunct. By Lemma 2, every S_n with n>=2|H|^2+3 satisfies its second
disjunct. The only possible exceptions are the finitely many smaller
symmetric groups. Therefore R_phi belongs to T_cof. We have proved

    phi in T_fin  iff  R_phi not in T_cof.

If the complement of T_cof were computably enumerable, its computable
preimage T_fin would be computably enumerable, contradicting the
stated theorem of Mal'cev. This proves the second assertion.

The sentence R_phi does not need to compute the witness H or its order.
Those are used only to prove eventual truth when a witness exists.
The computable transformation consists of finite syntactic substitution
into one fixed sentence sigma. In particular no decision procedure for
finite-group validity is hidden in the construction.

## Scope

This result uses the cofinite, isomorphism-type meaning of almost all,
not an asymptotic density convention. The earlier partial reduction in
`18.18-partial.md` remains valid and is included above. First-order
interpretations of symmetric groups are classical; Lemma 1 is supplied
in full to avoid depending on an unspecified axiomatization theorem.
Neither that general technique nor Mal'cev's input is claimed as new.
Controls, primary-source reading scope and remaining priority questions
are recorded in `18.18-review.md`.
