# Internal audit of the Problem 21.107 counterexample

Date: 11 September 2026. Internal mathematical review only; no outside
review or established priority claim.

## Exact scope

Printed page 183 of `docs/21tkt.pdf` was rendered and visually inspected.
The question assumes a countable topological group and a partition into
countably many dense subsets. It imposes no metrizability, first
countability, precompactness, or restriction on elements of order two.
It requires the finite sets in the expansive sequence to be pairwise
disjoint. Our group is countably infinite, Hausdorff, and nondiscrete,
and its dense partition is given explicitly.

The use of a nonempty open subgroup to refute the conclusion avoids the
inessential omission of the word 'nonempty' in the printed definition.
We do not use that omission as a counterexample.

## Logical checks

1. **Choice and the ultrafilter.** Every free ultrafilter works. Extending
   the cofinite filter by Zorn gives one in ZFC. No Ramsey property,
   P-point, rapidity, continuum hypothesis, or forcing construction is
   assumed. Freeness follows because every cofinite set remains in the
   extension.
2. **Group topology.** The identity base consists of actual subgroups.
   Finite intersections remain in the base. Translations of these
   subgroups give a topology and addition is jointly continuous. This
   is the linear/Mathias topology, not an arbitrary invariant topology
   for which only separate continuity might hold.
3. **Separation.** Removing one coordinate that distinguishes two
   vectors gives disjoint cosets of a basic open subgroup. Every basic
   subgroup is infinite, so no point is isolated.
4. **Dense partition.** The valuation of support size plus one takes
   every nonnegative value on arbitrarily large support sizes. In an
   arbitrary coset x+H_A, choose extra coordinates from A minus the
   finite support x. This gives exactly the desired support size,
   without cancellation. It works for each color and each coset.
5. **Finite fibers.** The argument uses maxima of supports, not minima.
   There are finitely many Boolean vectors with maximum at most r.
   Pairwise disjointness therefore ensures that only finitely many
   F_n contain any such vector. This remains true when the sets F_n
   have varying, unbounded cardinalities.
6. **Subsequence extraction.** Remove empty terms and the at most one
   term containing zero. At each stage only finitely many future
   indices are forbidden by the current finite bound on maxima, so a
   strictly increasing subsequence with separated maximum sets exists.
7. **One open witness.** The ultrafilter chooses either the union of
   even maximum sets or its complement. The chosen A avoids all odd
   or all even maximum sets. The single subgroup H_A therefore misses
   infinitely many terms. No neighborhood is allowed to vary with n
   after A has been chosen.
8. **Universal conclusion.** The topology and ultrafilter were fixed
   before the proposed sequence. Only the witness neighborhood depends
   on that sequence, exactly as required to negate expansiveness.
9. **Odd-exponent variant.** The direct sum of C3 copies has the same
   proof using finite supports and 3^r-1. The result does not depend on
   the special behavior of Boolean irresolvable groups.

## Literature audit and reading scope

The supplied Archive 15.80 records the 2025 negative answer without the
countability restriction, using the uncountable box product of real
lines. It also mentions universal resolvability for certain abelian
groups. That archive does not supply a countable example without an
expansive sequence. Problem 21.107 explicitly asks the countable case.

Ol'ga Sipacheva, *Free Boolean Topological Groups*, Axioms 4, 492--517,
https://doi.org/10.3390/axioms4040492, describes exactly the topology
used here: its identity neighborhoods are the subgroups generated by
members of a free filter. The publisher page was rate-limited, so the
publisher's collected-volume reprint was downloaded:

https://mdpi-res.com/bookfiles/book/237/Topological_Groups_Yesterday_Today_Tomorrow.pdf

The reprint's discussion on printed pages 85--88 was read, including
the Mathias neighborhood base and the adjacent distinctions among
linear, free, and Laver topologies. No Ramsey equivalence or extremal
disconnectedness theorem from that discussion is used in our proof.
The full survey was searched for 'expansive', 'groupable', and
'resolvable'; no occurrence giving the present conclusion was found.
The full survey and its cited bibliography have not been audited.

Searches for the exact problem number, expansive sequences with
ultrafilters/Boolean groups, groupable dense sets, and Mathias
resolvability found the standard topology and related selective
separability literature, but no matching prior resolution. This limited
search does not establish novelty. The books of Protasov--Zelenyuk and
Zelenyuk merit a broader follow-up check.

No finite numerical test is claimed as evidence for the existence of a
free ultrafilter or for nonexistence of all expansive sequences. The
proof is self-contained; the audit above checks its quantifiers and
topological hypotheses directly.
