# Problem 10.32: a logarithmic symmetric-group universality bound

Started 2026-09-11, approximately 17:36 UTC. The initial exploration
below is retained as history. Final status: complete candidate using
a slightly stronger interval condition; see the closing update.

## Exact target and easy cases

The printed problem on Notebook p.40 asks for an analogue for S_n of
the bound n >= max(N_0,c log(rad(rs))) for universality of x^r y^s
on A_n, when at least one exponent is odd. Droste's stated symmetric
bound is max(6,4 rad(rs)-4). Exponent +1 or -1 is immediate. If both
exponents are odd, every permutation is a product of two involutions,
each of which is its own corresponding odd power. Thus only the
case of exactly one odd exponent needs work.

The possible ambiguity in the Notebook's definition of m when exactly
one exponent is +/-1 has no effect: that case is universal in every
degree directly.

## Source checks and routes that do not suffice

1. Droste, *On the Universality of Words for the Alternating Groups*,
   Proc.AMS96(1986),18--22, Theorem2 and its proof on p.20 were read
   in the author's public ResearchGate upload (uploaded 19 March2014):
   https://www.researchgate.net/publication/238869489_On_the_Universality_of_Words_for_the_Alternating_Groups
   The proof chooses a multiple M of m in a long interval and then
   adjacent admissible cycle lengths near M. Both roots are individual
   cycles. Theorem1's proof and the both-odd introductory observation
   were also read; the whole paper was not read or visually checked.
   A single prime omitted from rs does not by itself replace this
   adjacent-length argument.
2. Brenner--Evans--Silberger, *The Universality of Words x^r y^s*,
   Proc.AMS96(1986),23--28, author copy:
   https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~revans/Universality.pdf . The introduction,
   theorem statements, Lemmas4--10 and bibliography were consulted;
   Section4's full number-theoretic proof was not read. In particular,
   Lemma5 gives A_n as the square of the class with two nontrivial
   cycles of lengths u,v >=4 when floor(3n/4)+1 <= u+v <= n.
   This covers only the even target permutations.
3. Malcolm, *The p-width of the alternating groups*, arXiv1710.04972v2,
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04972 , introductory material through
   Section3.1 was read, not the full proof or Appendix A. A width-three
   theorem cannot supply the required two factors. The finite
   obstruction interval in Remark2.6 does not prove infinitely many
   exceptional degrees for a fixed odd p. The assertion there about
   3-width of A5 is incorrect: all60 elements are products of two
   elements of order dividing3. This is checked independently in GAP;
   (123)(345) is a5-cycle and (123)(124) a double transposition,
   covering all remaining even cycle types by conjugacy. The printed
   assertion was visually checked on p.3; it is not merely an OCR error.
   No claim is made here about the correctness of the main theorem.
4. Replacing the omitted-prime argument by a bound for gaps in pairs
   of coprime adjacent integers requires a separate sieve argument.
   A one-dimensional Jacobsthal bound does not automatically handle
   the two forbidden residues at common odd primes. No such bound has
   been established here.

## Concrete conjectural route

For an odd prime q, let C_(2q) denote the class of a 2q-cycle with
n-2q fixed points, and C_(q,q) the class of two disjoint q-cycles
with the same number of fixed points. Investigate:

    floor(3n/4)+1 <= 2q <= n
       implies C_(2q) C_(q,q) = S_n minus A_n.                 (L)

This is a conjectured sufficient condition, not a proved theorem.
Together with the cited even-target Lemma5 for q>=5, it would solve
the factorization problem whenever such a q does not divide rs:
the first factor has order q or2q (coprime to odd r), and the second
has order q (coprime to s). Inverses modulo these orders provide the
roots. If the odd exponent is s instead, interchange the two factors;
products of conjugacy-invariant sets commute as sets.

Primes q in (3n/8+O(1), n/2] have total logarithm asymptotic to n/8
by the prime number theorem. Thus (L), if proved, would yield the
requested form n >= max(N_0(c),c log m) for every c>8. The constant
would be weaker than the alternating-group one but logarithmic.
The prime-number argument remains conditional on (L).

## Completed route, 2026-09-11

The positive-genus three-partition theorem of Boccara, in the precise
reproof by Song--Xu--Ye 2024v2, proves the needed odd-target lemma for
the stronger strict interval 3n/8+1 < q <= n/2. For small-support
targets first restrict to max(support size,2q) points. Transpositions
are handled directly by merging two cycles. The originally stated
weaker condition (L) is not claimed proved in its full range.

Rosser--Schoenfeld's explicit estimates now give N_0=1126 and c=16;
their asymptotic estimates give every c>8. The modular-root deduction
includes negative exponents and either location of the odd exponent.
This is the thirtieth complete candidate, with priority and outside
review pending. The proof and 25-point audit are separate files.

All 6,143 actual factorizations from seven eligible degree/prime pairs
through degree25 pass independent Python replay, including49,144 root
identities. The first GAP run failed because InverseMod was undefined;
the second completed mathematically but emitted non-JSON continuations.
Both artifacts are preserved. The corrected stream export passes exact
JSON parsing and all independent checks. Finite tests do not substitute
for the general proof or certify the published input theorems.
