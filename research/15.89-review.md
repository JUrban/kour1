# Internal review of the counterexample to15.89

Status: complete internal audit, no external review or established
priority. 11 September 2026.

## Exact statement and graph checks

1. The supplied printed page94 asks about all complex-valued functions,
   not just bounded or square-summable functions. A screenshot of that
   page was read directly. Zero suffices to refute the universal
   assertion about all complex numbers.
2. The free product is C3*C2, not C2*C2. The outer factor in U B U
   needs two nonidentity elements. Using an involution as the outer
   factor would merely conjugate a finite subgroup and could lose
   connectivity or infinitude.
3. The four support words are distinct by reduced free-product normal
   form. In particular the product U B U is an ordinary unweighted
   adjacency operator, with no repeated or identity contribution.
4. The connection set is inverse-closed. Its two conjugates of b
   are involutions; they each contribute one neighbour, not two
   repeated edges. The other two words are mutual inverses.
5. The identities recovering a^2 and b from S establish connectivity
   directly. Infinitude follows separately from the normal forms of
   (ab)^n. The left-regular group action supplies vertex transitivity.

## Operator checks

6. The convention R_g f(x)=f(xg) gives R_g R_h=R_(gh). The displayed
   operator product therefore enumerates neighbours in the specified
   order. No commutation of U and B is assumed.
7. The polynomial U^2-U-2I vanishes solely because a^3=1. Since2 is
   invertible over C, (U-I)/2 is a two-sided inverse of U. B is its
   own inverse, and reversing the three factors gives the stated
   two-sided inverse of U B U.
8. Both the operator and its inverse are finite sums of translations.
   They are defined on every function, and their compositions involve
   only finite sums. Analytic spectral results and convergence arguments
   are unnecessary. Invertibility on a smaller function space alone
   would not have sufficed.
9. The inverse contains negative rational coefficients. The graph's
   adjacency operator itself has exactly four coefficients, all one.
   There is no requirement that an inverse adjacency operator also
   be the adjacency operator of a simple graph.

## Independent exact controls

`scripts/verify_15_89_free_product.py` implements alternating factor
syllables and exact rational convolution. It checks both product
identities, inverse-closure, generation identities, and101 distinct
powers of ab with their exact reduced lengths. At50 vertices it checks
all1,500 coordinate coefficients in the two local operator compositions,
including the full dependency sets outside the selected vertex set.
Removing one connection-set element breaks the asserted inverse, as
required by the negative control. The run exits zero.

`scripts/verify_15_89_free_product.g` uses a different model: integer
matrices modulo their scalar signs, with

    a = [[0,-1],[1,-1]],       b = [[0,-1],[1,0]].

It independently obtains four support matrices, nine inverse-support
matrices, and both identity products. It also checks the generation
identities and that (ab)^n is represented by [[1,0],[n,1]] for0<=n<=100.
These matrix controls do not require assuming that a finite quotient
is faithful. For the actual permutation groups PSL2(3), PSL2(5),
PSL2(7), of orders12,60,168, the script separately finds generating
elements of orders3 and2. Each resulting four-regular simple Cayley
graph has full-rank rational adjacency matrix, and the formula gives
both inverses entry by entry, for63,936 matrix entries in total.
The actual GAP process exits zero and both final markers are present.

The finite quotient matrices corroborate the formula; they do not
prove the infinite claim by extrapolation. The proof on arbitrary
functions is the finite-sum operator calculation above.

## Literature scope and remaining priority work

Trofimov, *On adjacency operators of locally finite graphs*,
[arXiv:2208.01926v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.01926v3),
Izvestiya: Mathematics88 (2024),542--589,
[DOI10.4213/im9408e](https://doi.org/10.4213/im9408e), explicitly
references15.89. The Russian introduction, selected propagator
statements, and Sections8.1--8.5 were read previously;8.4--8.5 were
checked again for this construction. Section8.4 gives a cubic complex
example without the required vertex transitivity. Section8.1 gives
positive-characteristic vertex-transitive examples. Section8.5 concerns
vertices admitting no propagator, and its examples actually have
eigenfunctions; it must not be misread as an absence-of-eigenvalues
claim. Full proofs of the general theory were not audited.

The primary abstract of Trofimov's2025 paper,
[Infinite Locally Finite Connected Graphs with Countable Complements
in C of the Sets of Eigenvalues](https://journal.imm.uran.ru/node/1583),
was located. Its full proof was not read in this pass; the direct
page fetch timed out. No vertex-transitive counterexample was established
from that abstract. The editors' official website, checked11September2026,
lists a September2026 update; the supplied statement still poses15.89.
This is not evidence that no unpublished or unlocated solution exists.

The zig-zag graph product itself is prior work of
Reingold--Vadhan--Wigderson2002. Its Definition3.1 on printed page170
and the three-matrix factorization on pages172--173 were read directly
in the primary paper. Its expansion proofs are neither imported nor
audited. The relation to this finite-sum factorization is credited in
the proof. Cavaleri--D'Angeli--Donno, *Permutational powers of a graph*,
[arXiv:1811.09836](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09836),
Electronic Journal of Combinatorics26(4) (2019),P4.22,
[DOI10.37236/8337](https://doi.org/10.37236/8337), also discusses
invertibility of the small factor. Its finite-graph convention in
Section2 and the complete statement and proof of Lemma3.5 in the
author manuscript were read. The lemma uses invertibility to characterize
when a permutational power is symmetric; it does not state the present
infinite-graph consequence. The rest of its proofs were not audited.
Thus neither the matrix-product construction nor the observation that
its finite factors can be invertible is being claimed as new.
Searches for the exact problem,
Trofimov with Cayley graphs and invertible adjacency, and zig-zag
products with zero eigenvalues did not locate this precise consequence.
The construction should be independently reviewed and its priority
checked more broadly before any claim of a new published solution.
