# Problem14.15: infinite commutator width, a prior-theorem consequence

Status: completely determined by published theorems; excluded from new
resolution priority. Recorded11September2026.

For every fixed n>=3, the answer is k_n=infinity. The same conclusion
holds for n=2, as the Notebook already notes. The exact supplied statement
was visually checked on page74. No assertion about the direct limit is
being substituted for the fixed-rank question.

## Direct deduction

Genevois--Horbez, *Acylindrical hyperbolicity of automorphism groups of
infinitely ended groups*, Journal of Topology14(2021),
[DOI10.1112/topo.12203](https://doi.org/10.1112/topo.12203),
[arXiv:2002.01388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.01388), Corollary1.2,
states that Aut(F_n) is acylindrically hyperbolic for every n>=2.

Bestvina--Bromberg--Fujiwara, *The verbal width of acylindrically
hyperbolic groups*, Algebraic & Geometric Topology19(2019),477--489,
[DOI10.2140/agt.2019.19.477](https://doi.org/10.2140/agt.2019.19.477),
[primary publisher PDF](https://msp.org/agt/2019/19-1/agt-v19-n1-p12-s.pdf),
Theorem1.1, says that a nontrivial word w whose exponent-sum gcd d(w)
is not one has infinite verbal width in any acylindrically hyperbolic
group. Their convention assigns d(w)=0 when every exponent sum is zero.

Apply this with w=x y x^-1 y^-1, for which d(w)=0. Its verbal subgroup
is exactly Aut(F_n)', and its verbal length is exactly commutator length:
the inverse of a commutator is another commutator. Hence the supremum
in14.15 is infinite, for every fixed n>=3.

## Explicit length implication from the imported quasimorphism theorem

For completeness, the needed lower-bound step can be checked without
invoking stable-commutator-length duality. Theorem2.2 of the same2019
paper gives F=<a,b> inside G=Aut(F_n), such that the Brooks counting
quasimorphism associated to any nonempty reduced word extends from F
to G. Choose the cyclically reduced word c=a b a^-1 b^-1. The extended
function H has H(c^m)=m for every m>=1.

Replace H by q(g)=(H(g)-H(g^-1))/2. It is an antisymmetric
quasimorphism, still agrees with the Brooks function on F, and has some
finite defect D. The defect is positive, since a homomorphism would
vanish on c, whereas q(c)=1. For any x,y in G, three applications of
the defect inequality give

    |q(x y x^-1 y^-1)| <= 3D.

Thus a product of k commutators has absolute q-value at most
(4k-1)D. Since q(c^m)=m, this proves

    cl_G(c^m) >= (m/D + 1)/4.

All c^m lie in G', so the stated supremum is infinite. This is simply
a detailed deduction from the prior extension theorem, not a new
construction or an explicit choice of automorphisms a,b.
