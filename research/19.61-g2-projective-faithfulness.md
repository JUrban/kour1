# Faithfulness of the G2(F9) projective action

12 September 2026. This supplies the identification argument requested
in the F9 plan. It does not assert completion of the F9 carpet search.

The integral model is the adjoint Chevalley representation of the
complex simple Lie algebra of type G2, expressed on its Chevalley
lattice and reduced over F9. The rational GAP basis construction is
imported; all required root divided powers and their integral lattice
preservation were checked in the frozen prime-field packet. The
weights of the adjoint representation generate the root lattice, so
the resulting group is the adjoint Chevalley group in the usual sense.
In particular the possible nonsimplicity of the reduced *Lie algebra*
in characteristic three is not the hypothesis of the simplicity theorem.

The primary reference is Robert Steinberg, *Lectures on Chevalley Groups*,
Yale notes (1967), [University of Utah scan](https://www.math.utah.edu/~ptrapa/math-library/steinberg/steinberg-yale-notes.pdf).
Section 3, printed page 21 (PDF page 27), defines precisely the group
generated by the root divided-power exponentials. Theorem 5 in Section 4,
printed page 47 (PDF page 54, also duplicated at PDF page 53), says that
the adjoint group of an indecomposable complex root system is simple,
with explicit exclusions over fields of sizes two and three. F9 is
outside all exclusions. We import that established simplicity theorem.

The F9 preparation constructs a seven-dimensional invariant subspace
W by extending the frozen F3 orbit span. For every one of the 108 root
matrices it checks invariance and reconstructs each image of a basis
vector from the computed seven coordinates. Thus restriction to W
followed by the action on its projective points is a well-defined
homomorphism from the original fourteen-dimensional matrix group.

This homomorphism is nontrivial. Already over the prime subfield, in
zero-based coordinates, the second basis vector e1 is sent by root
matrix number 1 at parameter 1 to e1+2e7+e13. These vectors are not
proportional even over F9, since their supports differ. The exact
vectors are retained in `results/19.61-g2-projective-nontrivial.json`.
Both belong to W by its construction and checked invariance.

The kernel of a nontrivial action of a simple group is trivial. Hence
the projective action, and its restriction to the complete orbit of
the line of e1, is faithful: that orbit contains a point moved by the
same root element, so its permutation action remains nontrivial.
The preparation finds exactly 66,430 points in that complete orbit.
The guard is not reached. No ambient-order comparison is used to
deduce this faithfulness assertion.
