# Working proof for 15.76(b)

Archived first draft. It did not increase the candidate count. The
reviewed version is `15.76-proof.md`; use that version for review.

Write S_d for the variety of all groups of derived length at most d.
We aim to prove that every automorphism of its category of free groups
of finite rank is inner, for every d>=1.

## 1. The affine coefficient model

Let F be the absolutely free group on x_1,...,x_n, let N be a normal
subgroup, put Q=F/N, and let M be the free left Z[Q]-module with basis
e_1,...,e_n. Use multiplication

    (q,U)(r,V) = (qr,U+qV)

in Q semidirect M, with the order of coordinates as displayed. Sending
x_i to (bar(x_i),e_i) gives a homomorphism with kernel [N,N].

Here is a proof of the kernel assertion. Take the covering graph of the
rose with n directed loops corresponding to N. Its vertices are Q, and
its oriented i-edges go from q to q bar(x_i). Its integral edge-chain
group is M, where q e_i denotes that edge. A word traces a path from 1;
its scalar coordinate is the endpoint and its module coordinate is the
signed edge chain. Thus a word is in the kernel precisely when it is a
loop in N with zero integral chain. The first homology of a graph is
the abelianization of its fundamental group. Since a graph has no
two-cells, zero homology class is exactly zero edge chain for a loop.
Consequently the kernel is [N,N]. This applies to infinite covering
graphs as well: every loop and every chain has finite support.

The boundary map sends q e_i to q(bar(x_i)-1). In particular, for any
word w(x,y), write its affine value as

    w((a,U),(b,V)) = (w(a,b), f(a,b)U+g(a,b)V),

where f and g are its integral group-ring coefficient words. They are
obtained by the displayed multiplication and the inverse formula
(q,U)^-1=(q^-1,-q^-1 U). Their universal boundary identity is

    f(a,b)(a-1)+g(a,b)(b-1)=w(a,b)-1.             (1)

All products here are ordered products in the group ring, not a
commutative polynomial ring unless Q happens to be abelian.

## 2. Associative-word rigidity

Claim: if w(x,1)=x, w(1,y)=y, and w(w(x,y),z)=w(x,w(y,z)) are
identities of S_d, then w=xy or w=yx is an identity of S_d.

For d=1, a word is x^r y^s in the free abelian group; normalization
gives r=s=1. Assume d>=2 and the claim is known for d-1.
Passing to S_(d-1), the word is xy or yx. If necessary replace w(x,y)
by w(y,x), which preserves associativity and normalization, so that

    w(a,b)=ab in every S_(d-1) group.             (2)

Let Q be the free S_(d-1) group on a,b,c and let M=Z[Q]^3.
The affine semidirect product M semidirect Q belongs to S_d.
Evaluating associativity on (a,U),(b,V),(c,W), where U,V,W are the
three module basis vectors, and comparing coefficients gives

    f(ab,c) f(a,b) = f(a,bc),                    (3)
    f(ab,c) g(a,b) = g(a,bc) f(b,c),             (4)
    g(ab,c) = g(a,bc) g(b,c).                    (5)

Substitution is legitimate because (2) identifies the scalar coordinate
of each inner word. We shall need only (3) and (5), together with (1).

Put h(t)=f(1,t) in Z[t,t^-1]. Setting a=1 in (3) gives

    f(b,c)h(b)=h(bc).

Then c=b^-1 gives f(t,t^-1)h(t)=h(1)=1. The last equality follows
from the x-exponent sum of w being one. Thus h is a unit of the
ordinary Laurent ring Z[t,t^-1], so h(t)=t^k for some integer k;
the sign is positive because h(1)=1. It follows that

    f(a,b)=(ab)^k a^-k.                          (6)

Similarly put q(t)=g(t,1). Equation (5) with c=1 gives
q(ab)=g(a,b)q(b). Substituting b=a^-1 proves q is a Laurent unit;
q(1)=1 gives q(t)=t^l for some integer l. Hence

    g(a,b)=(ab)^l b^-l.                          (7)

There is no use here of a general unit theorem for Z[Q]. Only units
in the cyclic Laurent subring are used.

Map (1) to the group ring of the free abelian quotient on X,Y.
By (2), (6), and (7) it becomes

    Y^k(X-1)+X^l(Y-1)=XY-1.

Differentiate formally with respect to X and set X=1. Then

    Y^k = l+(1-l)Y.

Comparison of Laurent coefficients forces exactly two possibilities:
(k,l)=(0,1) or (k,l)=(1,0).

In the first case f=1 and g=a. Together with scalar coordinate ab,
these are precisely the affine coefficients of xy. Apply the kernel
assertion of section 1 with F free on x,y and N=F^(d-1): equality
of affine values means equality modulo [N,N]=F^(d). Thus w=xy in S_d.

In the second case f=aba^-1 and g=1. The boundary identity (1)
and (2) give

    ab-aba^-1+b-1=ab-1,

so aba^-1=b in Q. If d>=3, the free S_(d-1) group on a,b is
nonabelian (it maps onto S_3), a contradiction. If d=2, Q is abelian,
and the scalar and module coordinates are exactly those of yx;
the same kernel assertion gives w=yx in S_2. Reversing the initial
replacement, if made, proves the claim in all cases.

## 3. From a category automorphism to a word

Let F_n be a chosen free S_d group of rank n, including F_0=1.
Rank one can be recognized categorically: End(F_1) is an infinite
commutative monoid, whereas End(F_0) is trivial and End(F_n) is
noncommutative for n>=2. For the latter, a projection to the first
basis generator and the automorphism interchanging the first two
generators do not commute. Therefore a category automorphism sends
F_1 to an isomorphic copy of itself. Since F_n is the n-fold coproduct
of F_1, every rank is preserved.

Conjugate by chosen object isomorphisms to make the functor fix objects.
Let iota_i:F_1->F_n select the i-th basis element. The images of these
maps are again a coproduct diagram and hence select a free basis of
F_n. A further conjugation by the corresponding basis automorphisms
makes the functor Psi fix every iota_i as well. The conjugating
automorphism at F_1 is the identity because iota_1 there is the identity.

For a in F_n let f_a:F_1->F_n send x to a, and define

    s_n(a)=Psi(f_a)(x).

Each s_n is bijective, fixes the chosen basis, and sends 1 to 1 because
the zero morphism factors through the zero object. For every homomorphism
u:F_n->F_m, functoriality gives

    Psi(u)=s_m u s_n^-1.                         (8)

Let w=s_2(xy), represented by a group word in the two basis elements.
Apply (8) to the map F_2->F_n selecting a,b. Since the basis maps are
fixed, it follows that

    s_n(ab)=w(s_n(a),s_n(b)).                    (9)

The bijections s_n transport a group law, so w is associative with
identity 1 on every F_n. The corresponding identities in F_3 imply
the same identities throughout S_d. Section 2 therefore applies.

If w=xy, equation (9) makes every s_n a homomorphism fixing the basis,
so s_n is the identity and Psi is the identity functor.

If w=yx, every s_n is an antihomomorphism fixing the basis. Let eta_n
be the ordinary automorphism sending each basis generator to its
inverse, and let j_n be the set map a->a^-1. Then s_n=j_n eta_n.
Every group homomorphism commutes with the inversion set maps, so (8)
becomes

    Psi(u)=eta_m u eta_n^-1.

Thus Psi is inner in either case. Undoing the two earlier inner
conjugations shows that the original category automorphism is inner.

For a category with several named free objects of the same rank, choose
an isomorphism of each to a fixed representative and transport the
argument; the resulting object isomorphisms give the same natural
isomorphism with the identity. No skeletal-category restriction on the
statement is needed.
