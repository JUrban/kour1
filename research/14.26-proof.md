# Problem 14.26: closure under restricted wreath products with Z

Complete affirmative candidate, 11 September 2026. Independent review and
priority remain pending. Let C be the class of all torsion-free nilpotent
groups, with no uniform bound on nilpotency class.

**Theorem.** If G belongs to the quasivariety Q(C), then the restricted
wreath product G wr Z also belongs to Q(C).

This is the direct wreath product in the printed question. Budkin1999,
§1, p258 explicitly defines its base using functions of finite support.
The convention and the one classical embedding input are documented in
`14.26-sources.md`. All other ingredients are proved below.

## 1. Local multiplication tables

A finite subset B of a group, containing 1, locally embeds in K if there
is an injective map theta:B->K with theta(1)=1 and

    theta(ab)=theta(a)theta(b) whenever a,b,ab belong to B.

**Lemma 1.** For a class C closed under finite direct products and
containing the trivial group, a group G belongs to Q(C) if and only if
every finite subset containing 1 locally embeds in a member of C.

**Proof.** Suppose G belongs to Q(C). Name each member a of a finite
B by a variable x_a. Let E be the finite list x_1=1 and x_a x_b=x_ab
for all products that stay in B. For each distinct a,b in B, some
solution of E in a member C_ab of C must have x_a!=x_b: otherwise
E implies x_a=x_b would be a quasiidentity of C violated in G.
Taking the finite product of these solutions separates all pairs
simultaneously and satisfies E. A singleton B is handled by the
trivial group.

Conversely, a violation of a quasiidentity uses finitely many group
words. Include their values, the values of every intermediate product
and inverse, and 1 in B. A local embedding preserves the word
evaluations (use x*x^-1=1 for inverse values) and the inequality in
the conclusion. This would violate the same quasiidentity in C. QED.

Our class C has the stated closure property: a finite product is
torsion-free and nilpotent of class at most the maximum of the classes.

## 2. A finite commutative algebra

Fix c>=1, R>=1 and a nonempty finite set I of integers of size M.
Choose

    m >= 2R(c-1)+M-1.

Let V be the rational vector space with basis a_0,...,a_m, and put
D(a_j)=a_(j+1) for j<m and D(a_m)=0. Extend D as a derivation to
Sym(V). Define the polynomial with vector coefficients

    f(z) = sum_(j=0)^m z^j a_j/j! = exp(zD)a_0.

For d=1,...,R, take all coefficients in z of f(z)f(z+d).
Let J be the ideal they generate, and form

    A = Sym(V)/(J + Sym^(>c)(V)).

Here Sym^(>c)(V) is the ideal consisting of all terms of ordinary
homogeneous degree greater than c. The algebra A is finite dimensional,
unital, commutative, and graded by ordinary degree. Write A_+ for its
positive-degree ideal.

The ideal J is D-stable: D(f(z)f(z+d)) is the derivative of the same
polynomial in z, and its coefficients are scalar multiples of the
original coefficients. The degree-cutoff ideal is also D-stable.
On the truncated algebra before factoring by J, D increases the sum
of the indices in each monomial. That sum is at most cm, so

    D^(cm+1)=0 on A.

This remains true after the quotient; no grading by the sum of the
indices is asserted on A. Thus alpha=exp(D) is a well-defined algebra
automorphism with inverse exp(-D), and

    alpha^s f(i)=f(i+s) for every integer s.

The exponential is a finite sum; its multiplicativity follows directly
from the iterated Leibniz rule. Substitution into the defining
polynomials, and commutativity, give

    f(i)f(j)=0 whenever 0<|i-j|<=R.                         (1)

**Lemma 2.** For each k=1,...,c, the elements f(i)^k, i in I, are
linearly independent in A.

**Proof.** A linear functional ell on Sym^k(V) determines the symmetric
polynomial

    Q(z_1,...,z_k)=ell(f(z_1)...f(z_k)),

whose degree in each variable is at most m. Conversely, every such
symmetric polynomial determines ell: the coefficient on
z_1^j_1 ... z_k^j_k is ell(a_j_1...a_j_k)/(j_1!...j_k!), and symmetry
makes the prescription independent of how the factors are ordered.

If Q vanishes identically after z_1=z, z_2=z+d for every d=1,...,R,
then ell kills J in degree k. To see this, extract the coefficients
in z and use the fact that the vectors f(z_3),...,f(z_k), with each
parameter allowed to vary independently, span V in each factor.
This spanning assertion is the usual Vandermonde argument. It accounts
for every generating quadratic relation multiplied by any element of
Sym^(k-2)(V). For k=1 there are no such relations.

Set

    Delta_k = product_(a<b) product_(d=1)^R ((z_a-z_b)^2-d^2),
    C_k     = product_(a<b) product_(d=1)^R (-d^2).

The first polynomial is symmetric, has degree 2R(k-1) in each variable,
and vanishes on every required specialization. On a diagonal it is
the nonzero constant C_k. Empty products, when k=1, are one.
For each i in I, let L_i be the Lagrange polynomial of degree M-1
with L_i(j)=1 if j=i and 0 for the other j in I. Then

    Q_i = (Delta_k/C_k) L_i((z_1+...+z_k)/k)

has degree at most 2R(k-1)+M-1<=m in each variable. The corresponding
functional kills the degree-k part of J and evaluates to delta_ij
on f(j)^k. The degree cutoff adds no relation in degrees at most c.
These functionals prove the asserted independence. QED.

## 3. A torsion-free nilpotent group with finitely many commuting copies

Let U be the group of (c+1)-by-(c+1) upper unitriangular matrices whose
strictly upper entries lie in A_+. Entrywise alpha acts on U. Form

    H = U semidirect <t>,     t u t^-1 = alpha(u),

where <t> is infinite cyclic. For g in UT_(c+1)(Q), define rho_i(g)
to have diagonal 1 and entries

    rho_i(g)_(a,b) = g_(a,b) f(i)^(b-a),       a<b.          (2)

Ordinary matrix multiplication shows rho_i(gh)=rho_i(g)rho_i(h),
since superdiagonal distances add. Lemma 2 makes rho_i injective.
Moreover alpha(rho_i(g))=rho_(i+1)(g).

For distinct i,j with |i-j|<=R, each term of both matrix products

    (rho_i(g)-1)(rho_j(h)-1),
    (rho_j(h)-1)(rho_i(g)-1)

contains f(i)f(j), and hence both products vanish by (1).
In particular the two copies commute. If the diameter of I is at
most R, their product is exactly

    product_(i in I) rho_i(g_i) = 1+sum_(i in I)(rho_i(g_i)-1).     (3)

Applying Lemma 2 separately to each superdiagonal shows that this
map from the direct product of the copies indexed by I is injective.

**Lemma 3.** H is torsion-free and nilpotent.

**Proof.** Let U_j consist of matrices whose first j-1 superdiagonals
vanish, for j=1,...,c+1. Then U_1=U, U_(c+1)=1, and

    U_j/U_(j+1) = (A_+)^(c+1-j)

as additive groups. Conjugation by U is trivial on this factor,
whereas t acts as alpha. Let delta=alpha-1 and L=cm+1. Since
alpha-1 is D times a polynomial in D, delta^L=0.
Between U_j and U_(j+1) insert the inverse images of

    (A_+)^(c+1-j), delta(A_+)^(c+1-j), ...,
    delta^L(A_+)^(c+1-j)=0.

These are H-normal subgroups. On every successive factor U acts
trivially and t acts trivially, because alpha-1 moves one step down.
Together with H/U, these factors give a central series of length
at most cL+1. Repeated equal terms may simply be omitted.

Any finite-order element of H projects trivially to H/U=Z and lies
in U. If a matrix in U is nonidentity, its first nonzero
superdiagonal is multiplied by n in its nth power. It cannot become
zero for a positive integer n in a rational vector space. Thus U,
and consequently H, is torsion-free. QED.

## 4. Local models for a nilpotent base

We use the classical fact that every finitely generated torsion-free
nilpotent group embeds in some UT_d(Q). To specify the exact input,
Jennings1955, Theorem5.2, gives an s for which

    {g in N : g-1 belongs to Delta^s}=1,

where Delta is the augmentation ideal of Q[N]. The quotient
Q[N]/Delta^s is finite dimensional: for finitely many group generators,
their differences from 1 and the finite geometric expressions for
inverses span it by words of length less than s. Left multiplication
is faithful by the displayed equality, and is the identity on each
successive Delta-power quotient. Choosing a basis adapted to this
filtration gives the required unitriangular representation. This is
the only imported group-theoretic theorem.

First suppose N itself is in C. In N wr Z write elements (f,n),
where f:Z->N has finite support, and adopt the convention

    (f,n)(h,q)=(f * shift^n(h), n+q),
    shift(h)(i)=h(i-1).                                   (4)

Fix a finite subset S containing the identity. Restrict N to the
finitely generated subgroup generated by all coordinates appearing
in S, and embed it in UT_(c+1)(Q), increasing c to 1 if necessary.
Collect a finite set I containing all supports of elements in S and,
for every equality (f,n)(h,q)=(v,r) among elements of S, all positions
in the support of shift^n(h). If I is empty take I={0}. Choose
R>=max(1,diameter(I)) and construct A,H as above.
Define on S

    Psi(f,n) = (product_i rho_i(f(i))) t^n,                 (5)

where only the finite support is used. For every prescribed product
in S, shifting the second matrix product changes rho_i to rho_(i+n).
All involved positions belong to I. The different positions commute,
and multiplication at an equal position agrees with that in N by
(2). Equations (3) and (4) therefore show that Psi preserves the
prescribed product. The identity maps to the identity.

Different integers n are separated by H/U. If n is equal, (3) and
its injectivity separate different coordinate functions. Thus Psi
is a local embedding of S into the torsion-free nilpotent group H.
Notice that H and the map depend on S; no homomorphism from the
whole wreath product to H is claimed.

## 5. Arbitrary bases in the quasivariety

Let G belong to Q(C), and fix a finite S in G wr Z containing 1.
Collect a finite set B in G containing 1, all nonidentity coordinate
values in S, and every coordinate multiplication needed by an
equality uv=w among S. Include the factors and product value in
each such multiplication. By Lemma 1, B locally embeds in some N
in C, through an injective map theta fixing 1.

Replace each coordinate value in every element of S by its image
under theta, keeping its integer exponent. The resulting map into
N wr Z respects all products within S. It is injective: different
integer exponents remain different, and a differing coordinate
remains different because theta is injective. Its support is unchanged
because theta fixes 1 and separates 1 from every other coordinate.

The finite image now locally embeds in a group H in C by Section4.
Composing these two local maps gives a local embedding of S in C.
Since S was arbitrary, Lemma 1 gives G wr Z in Q(C), as required. QED.

## Scope

Nilpotency classes and dimensions may grow with the finite subset;
no uniform bound or residual nilpotence of the whole wreath product
is asserted. The argument uses rational coefficients, finite support,
and unrestricted nilpotency class. It provides a complete candidate
answer to14.26, conditional only on the classical Jennings input
specified above. Exact finite controls and an internal review accompany
it; novelty and independent acceptance remain open.
