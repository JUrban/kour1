# Problem 13.39(f): negative consequence of the power-equation theorem

Status: prior-theorem consequence, excluded from new priority.
Checked 11 September 2026.
The exact supplied subquestion on printed page 69 was visually checked.

Every free rational exponential group on at least two free generators
fails to act freely without inversions by isometries on any Lambda-tree.
The one-generator group is the additive group Q and acts freely by
translations on the Q-line. This resolves the tree-action subquestion;
it says nothing about the other subparts of 13.39.

The imported result is the power-equation theorem of Brady, Ciobanu,
Martino and O Rourke,
[*The equation x^p y^q = z^r and groups that act freely on Lambda-trees*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-08-04639-4),
Transactions of the American Mathematical Society 361 (2009), 223--236.
It states that a solution in a group with such a free action, with all
three integer exponents at least four, has commuting entries. The
[author manuscript](https://math.ou.edu/~nbrady/papers/trees.pdf) and
[journal copy](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/155687/1/lyndon_and_lambda.pdf)
label this Theorem 3.6 in the body, although their introductions call it
3.2. The theorem and its complete main proof were read. The preceding
axis-geometry lemmas and their diagrams were not all independently audited.

Here is the direct implication. Suppose K is any group in which every
element has a fourth root, and K has a free isometric action of the stated
kind. For any x,y in K, choose z with

    z^4 = x^4 y^4.

Applying the cited theorem to these three elements with exponents 4,4,4
gives [x,y]=1. Since x and y were arbitrary, K is abelian. The argument
also works if the imported theorem is phrased only for finitely generated
groups: restrict the action to the subgroup generated by x,y,z. There is
no requirement that this subgroup itself contain all fourth roots.

A free Q-group has all fourth roots by its exponential operations.
For completeness, its noncommutativity in rank at least two follows from
its universal property, without needing an embedding theorem for rational
completions. Let H be the rational Heisenberg group, with coordinates
(a,b,c) in Q^3 and operation

    (a,b,c)(a',b',c') = (a+a', b+b', c+c'+ab').

Its rational powers are

    (a,b,c)^t = (ta, tb, tc + t(t-1)ab/2),   t in Q.

These operations make H a rational exponential group: its roots are
unique, the displayed formula composes according to multiplication of
exponents, and the commuting-product and conjugation identities follow
from uniqueness of roots. In H the two elements (1,0,0) and (0,1,0)
do not commute, since their products have third coordinates one and zero.
Assigning two free Q-generators to these elements, and any remaining
generators to the identity, extends to a homomorphism of Q-groups.
Consequently those two free generators cannot commute.

The general observation about fourth roots now contradicts a free tree
action of the free Q-group. This is an immediate consequence of the 2009
theorem, not a newly discovered obstruction.

The weaker square equation cannot replace the fourth-power equation in
this argument: the nonorientable genus-three surface group is already
Z^2-free. Likewise, combination results requiring an amalgamated subgroup
to be maximal abelian on both sides do not automatically permit adjoining
roots on one side.
