# Problem 16.45: completed p-group pilot

11 September 2026. Bounded evidence only; the general problem remains open here.

All 354 groups in the prescribed orders pass a sufficient condition
for b(G)=mu'(G). The screen covers 30,755 conjugacy classes of subgroups,
representing 45,105 actual subgroups. There are 1,654 subgroup classes
attaining maximal generator rank. Of these, 769 are abelian and 1,646
have Frattini subgroup normal in the ambient group. Every group has
at least one witness of the latter kind. The two sets overlap.

The proof of sufficiency, including the nonfaithful-action convention,
is in `16.45-plan.md`. The enumeration computes the maximum over
all subgroup classes; it does not replace that maximum by d(G).
No failure of the sufficient conditions would itself be a counterexample.

An independent Python computation on twelve multiplication tables
reconstructs 3,243 subgroups by exhaustive successive adjunction of
coset representatives. It verifies 1,146,659 associativity triples,
computes Frattini subgroups as intersections of index-p subgroups,
and matches each GAP subgroup count and maximal rank. It constructs
explicit families of stabilizers whose normal intersection is
irredundant, with the required number of members. The retained
controls include the elementary abelian group of order 64 with
2,825 subgroups and quaternion/other nonabelian examples.

The first GAP process exited zero and printed its sentinel but also
emitted five unbound-global syntax warnings. The wrapper correctly
exited one. That script, wrapper, process record and full output are
archived without alteration. Declaring all screen variables local in
a function removes the warnings. The clean rerun exits zero and its
354 rows are byte-identical to the first run. Both the independent
export and Python control processes also exit zero with clean logs
and their sentinels. The packet auditor checks the closed manifest,
coverage, the archived warning provenance and the original outputs,
then replays the independent controls.

Cameron's primary paper is retained for the precise convention; its
extracted sections on the three maxima and the normal-bottom question
were read. No full-paper audit or priority claim is made. The next
bounded extension can check higher prime-power orders with the same
sufficient conditions. A counterexample would require a complete
audit of all possible normal intersections, beyond this screen.
