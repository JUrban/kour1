# 12.56: the growth scale is n^(Theta(n))

Status: proved quantitative bounds, using the classical isomorphism theorem
for right-angled Artin groups. Novelty of this counting consequence is not
established. This is recorded as a partial answer to the open-ended question
about F(n), not as another complete candidate resolution.

The supplied Notebook page62 was visually checked. Presentation length is
the number of generators plus the sum of the ordinary word lengths of its
cyclically reduced relators. Generator names count as single letters here;
their numerical subscripts do not have a logarithmic bit cost. Let F(n)
count group isomorphism types having a presentation of length at most n.

**Theorem.** With natural logarithms, as n tends to infinity,

    (n/4) log n - (n/2) log log n - O(n)
        <= log F(n) <= n log n + O(n).

In particular F(n)=exp(Theta(n log n))=n^(Theta(n)), and F(n) exceeds c^n
eventually for every fixed c. More explicitly, whenever v>=1,0<=e<=v(v-1)/2
and v+4e<=n,

    F(n) >= binomial(v(v-1)/2,e)/v!.                         (1)

## Upper bound

Fix a number r of generators and total relator length L. Empty relators
can be discarded. For L>0, an ordered list of nonempty relators is encoded
by a string of L letters in a2r-letter alphabet and cuts at any subset
of the L-1 positions between letters. This gives at most

    (2r)^L 2^(L-1) <= (4r)^L

lists. Allowing unreduced words and ordered lists only overcounts valid
presentations. For L=0 there is exactly one list. Every finite presentation
can have its generators renamed to this fixed alphabet. Thus, for n>=1,

    F(n) <= 1 + sum_(r=1)^n sum_(L=0)^(n-r) (4r)^L
         <= 1 + n(n+1)(4n)^n.

The initial1 covers the zero-generator presentation. Taking logarithms
proves the claimed upper bound. No isomorphism algorithm is assumed.

## Lower bound from graphs

For a finite simple graph Gamma with v vertices and e edges, form its
right-angled Artin group

    A_Gamma = <x_1,...,x_v | [x_i,x_j]=1 for each edge {i,j}>.

Each commutator relator is cyclically reduced and has word length4.
This presentation therefore has length v+4e, exactly in the convention
of the question. Droms' classical graph-group isomorphism theorem says
that A_Gamma is isomorphic to A_Delta only when Gamma and Delta are
isomorphic graphs. The converse follows by renaming generators.

There are binomial(v(v-1)/2,e) labeled graphs of this size, and each graph
isomorphism class contains at most v! labeled graphs. They therefore give
at least the right-hand side of(1) distinct group isomorphism types with
presentations of the required length. Automorphisms of graphs can only
improve this lower bound; no assumption of asymmetry is made.

For sufficiently large n choose

    v = floor(n/log n),       e = floor((n-v)/4),
    M = v(v-1)/2.

Then0<e<=M and v+4e<=n. The elementary product formula gives

    binomial(M,e) = product_(i=0)^(e-1) (M-i)/(e-i)
                  >= (M/e)^e,

because (M-i)/(e-i)>=M/e for every i. Also v!<=v^v. Hence

    log F(n) >= e log(M/e) - v log v.

Now

    e = n/4 - n/(4 log n) + O(1),
    M/e = (2n/(log n)^2)(1+O(1/log n)),
    v log v = O(n).

Substitution yields precisely

    log F(n) >= (n/4) log n - (n/2) log log n - O(n).

All these bounds hold for every sufficiently large integer n, not merely
along a sparse subsequence. All groups supplying the lower bound have
explicit finite presentations.

## Source and scope

The group-theoretic input is C. Droms, *Isomorphisms of graph groups*,
Proc. Amer. Math. Soc.100(3) (1987),407--408,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1987-0891135-1).
Direct access to the original AMS PDF returned403. The exact result and
its attribution were checked in the introduction and Theorem4.1 of the
primary paper by M. Gutierrez and A. Piggott,
[*Rigidity of graph products of abelian groups*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972708000105),
Bull. Austral. Math. Soc.77 (2008),187--196. Their Theorem4.1 states
Laurence's stronger form, including the requisite graph isomorphism.
The full earlier isomorphism proof was not independently audited.

The result gives the growth scale, not an exact formula for F(n), an
algorithm computing F(n), or an exact leading coefficient in log F(n).
The Notebook's stated nonrecursiveness of F(n) is compatible with these
computable upper and lower bounds. No novelty claim for the consequence
is made on the basis of a limited search.
