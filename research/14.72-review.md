# Internal review of the 14.72 candidate

This is an internal audit, not independent outside review.

- The supplied page 80 was inspected visually. It says finite cyclic group,
  with no prime-order restriction, and defines the hypersurface condition
  by codimension one. The example uses C4 in characteristic zero.
- X is an irreducible affine variety, not a disconnected union. Both charts
  have coordinate rings of an open subset of one smooth integral curve
  times a polynomial ring. The charts cover because x and x^2-1 have no
  common zero and generate the unit ideal. This also excludes embedded or
  nonreduced components in the three-equation presentation.
- The action preserves the three equations with exact scalar factors.
  Its square is nonidentity. The fixed-point ideal is (x,y,u), with quotient
  k[v], so the full fixed scheme is reduced, connected and smooth.
- The fixed divisor lies in the second chart where its ideal is (y).
  Thus it is a regular effective Cartier divisor. A single global defining
  equation for this divisor is not claimed. This is stated explicitly for
  any reviewer who interprets 'hypersurface' more restrictively than the
  printed parenthetical codimension condition.
- d is a global polynomial xu^2-v^2. The fractions u^2/x and v^2/(x^2-1)
  are used only on the appropriate charts and agree by the equations.
- In the first chart, the displayed Laurent monomials form a basis because
  the curve relation is monic of degree two in y. Every invariant basis
  monomial is expressed in a,a^-1,v,d. The reverse images of the proposed
  normal basis are distinct, proving the full kernel statement, not just
  verifying one relation among invariants.
- In the second chart, taking invariants commutes with the localization
  because the denominator is invariant. The curve invariant ring is k[x^2]
  by the same diagonal monomial basis. Gluing is justified by comaximality
  of a and a-1; it is not an inference from a merely dense open subset.
- The actual quotient ring is proved to be k[a,v,d]/(v^2-(a-1)d).
  At (1,0,0) its local dimension is two and cotangent dimension is three.
  Hence the quotient is singular as an algebraic variety over C.
- The orbit above the node consists of two points with stabilizer C2.
  They are not in X^G. This is the essential reason a local theorem at a
  full fixed point, or a prime-order theorem, does not answer the global
  composite-order question. The construction is not a counterexample to
  those stronger restrictions.
- The finite-field GAP controls use five odd primes with i in the base
  field, and check Jacobians and actual orbits separately. They do not
  assume every rational quotient point has a rational lift. Only fibers
  of rational points actually present in X are compared with G-orbits.
- The exact characteristic-zero proof is self-contained apart from basic
  affine quotient and regular-local-ring facts. The 2013 prime-order paper
  is context, not an input. Only its introductory scope and Theorem 2
  statement are claimed as read; no general composite-order claim from its
  introduction is imported.
- Novelty is unestablished after the initial search. This remains a
  complete candidate under the printed codimension-one formulation and
  awaits outside scrutiny of both formulation and proof.
