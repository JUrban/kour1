# Research plan

## Time and scope

Use the full 48-hour opportunity ending 2026-09-12 20:56:46 UTC. The objective covers the entire supplied Notebook, not only Issue 21. Optimize for rigorous new resolutions, including explicit counterexamples and independently meaningful solved subparts. Do not count known results or restatements as new solutions.

## Phase 1: establish evidence and select a portfolio (initial 1–3 hours)

1. Validate the relocated GAP installation and available packages. Create reproducible launchers with conservative memory settings.
2. Extract all problem statements with issue, number, printed page, and editorial solution markers. Mark starred entries for manual review because they can contain unsolved subparts.
3. Inspect the September 2026 editorial update and recent papers before investing heavily in a candidate. Save citations and exact problem identifiers already addressed.
4. Survey new problems and computationally accessible older problems. Prioritize explicit finite counterexamples, linear-algebra reductions, counting questions, and constructions admitting short verification certificates. Maintain alternatives so a difficult lead does not consume the entire budget.

## Phase 2: rapid experiments and mathematical reductions (roughly hours 2–18)

For each promising problem:

1. Write the precise quantifiers, hypotheses, and requested conclusion.
2. Identify elementary obstructions, smallest plausible examples, and known positive subclasses.
3. Run bounded exact searches with logged parameter ranges, deterministic seeds where applicable, timeouts, and resource limits. Use up to 20 computational processes only when independent search partitions justify them; do not delegate to other agents without explicit authorization.
4. Generalize a counterexample to a transparent construction where possible. Use a second representation or direct arithmetic checker to verify a discovery.
5. Record negative results and why they affect the next step. Stop unproductive exhaustive searches early and move to structural arguments or another problem.

## Phase 3: concentrate on strongest leads (roughly hours 12–40)

Continue alternating computation and proof. For each candidate resolution, seek a counterexample to our own statement, audit conventions and boundary cases, and check that all original hypotheses hold. Recheck literature novelty using primary sources. Keep the original problem in view: a special case is a partial result unless it is a separately asked subpart.

## Phase 4: independent-style audit and final artifacts (last 8 hours, overlapping discovery)

1. Write complete self-contained proofs with exact citations for any imported theorems.
2. Rerun minimal certificate verifiers from clean invocations, preserving versions, commands, outputs, and hashes.
3. Audit every claimed solution against the actual PDF, including all qualifiers and editorial corrections.
4. Produce a final report separating new candidate resolutions, proved partial results, computational evidence, failed approaches, and open leads.
5. Commit all necessary artifacts locally; never push. Preserve substantial progress in frequent commits throughout.

## Review standard

A search hit is not a solution until its hypotheses and conclusion have been checked independently of the search predicate. No randomized non-hit establishes nonexistence. A literature search cannot prove novelty; describe its scope and retain any uncertainty. Reproducible computation complements a proof and must state what it does and does not establish.
