# Problem 14.67: the prime class-size case

Status: proved partial theorem. The general question remains unresolved,
and no new complete candidate or novelty claim is made.

**Theorem.** Let G be a finite group and let a be a nonidentity element
whose centralizer has largest order among all nonidentity elements of G.
If |G:C_G(a)| is prime, then every nilpotent subgroup normalized by
C_G(a) centralizes a.

No solubility assumption on G is needed in the following proof.

## An elementary abelian-subgroup lemma

Write C=C_G(a). Suppose that B is an abelian subgroup normalized by C.
If a does not centralize B, the image W of the homomorphism
b -> b^-1 b^a on B is a nontrivial subgroup, normalized by C, of size

    |W| = |B:C_B(a)|.

For 1 != w in W, its C-orbit is contained in W minus the identity.
Since B centralizes w and C_C(w) normalizes B, we get

    |C_G(w)| >= |B C_C(w)|
              = |C| |B:C_B(a)| / |C:C_C(w)|
              > |C|.

Here B intersect C_C(w)=B intersect C=C_B(a), because B is abelian.
The strict inequality contradicts the choice of a. Thus a centralizes
every such B. This recovers the abelian-H case already printed as known.

## Reduction to a faithful affine action of prime degree

Put r=|G:C|, a prime, and suppose for contradiction that H is a
nilpotent subgroup normalized by C with H not contained in C. As C
has prime index, G=HC. Hence H is normal in G, and it acts transitively
on the r conjugates of a.

Let Omega=a^G, let phi be the conjugation action on Omega, and set
N=ker(phi). Its image phi(H) is cyclic of order r and acts regularly.
To see this, each Sylow subgroup of the nilpotent H is normal in H,
so its orbits all have a common size dividing r. A Sylow q-subgroup
with q!=r therefore fixes Omega pointwise. The image of a Sylow
r-subgroup is a transitive r-subgroup of Sym(r), and a Sylow
r-subgroup of Sym(r) has order r. This proves the assertion.

Consequently

    D := H intersect C = H intersect N

is normal in G, and H/D is cyclic of order r. Since H is normal in G,
phi(G) normalizes the regular cyclic group phi(H). Its normalizer
in Sym(Omega) is the affine group AGL1(F_r). For completeness, label
Omega by F_r so that phi(H) consists of translations. A permutation
normalizing this cyclic translation group has the form x -> kx+b,
with k!=0. The stabilizer phi(C) of the point a thus consists of
maps x -> kx.

Replace a, if necessary, by a nonidentity prime-order power. Its
centralizer contains C, and maximality of |C| forces equality. The
same H still fails the conclusion and the same value r applies.
Restarting the notation above for this power, assume henceforth that
|a|=p is prime.

## Excluding a nontrivial affine multiplier

Suppose phi(a) has multiplier k!=1 in F_r. Choose h in H with hD a
generator of H/D. Then h^a=h^k d for some d in D. The element a
centralizes D, since D is contained in C. Let theta be conjugation
by h on D, viewed in Out(D). We have theta^r=1, because h^r lies
in D. Conjugating by a does not change the induced automorphism on D,
whereas h^a=h^k d gives theta=theta^k. Since k-1 is coprime to r,
theta=1 in Out(D).

Thus some element of the coset hD centralizes D, and

    H = D C_H(D).

The group B=C_H(D) is abelian: its intersection with D is Z(D),
which is central in B, and B/Z(D) is cyclic, being isomorphic to H/D.
Both H and D are normal in G, so B is normalized by C. The abelian
lemma forces a to centralize B. It also centralizes D, and hence H,
a contradiction. Therefore phi(a)=1.

## The remaining unipotent action

Since a belongs to N, it centralizes every conjugate of a. Conjugating
this statement shows that all the conjugates commute with one another.
Their normal closure A is therefore an elementary abelian p-group,
which we regard as a vector space over F_p. It is normal in G.

For q!=p, the Sylow q-subgroup H_q of H is characteristic in H and
normal in G. Hence [A,H_q] lies in A intersect H_q=1. Since H moves a
in an orbit of size r, this forces p=r. (All H_q with q!=r already
act trivially on Omega, and the r-Sylow subgroup must act nontrivially.)

The group D centralizes all conjugates of a, so it centralizes A.
Choose h as above, and write T for its linear action on A and
delta=T-I. We have T^r=I, and in characteristic r this implies
delta^r=0. Since G=HC and C fixes a, the vector a generates A as an
F_r[T]-module. Let d be the least positive integer with delta^d(a)=0.
We have 2<=d<=r, as h does not fix a. Then

    z = delta^(d-1)(a) != 0

is fixed by H. For c in C write h^c=h^k e, where e in D and
k in {1,...,r-1}. The element c fixes a, and e acts trivially on A.
Therefore

    z^c = (T^k-I)^(d-1)(a) = k^(d-1) z.

The last equality follows by expanding T=I+delta: all terms above
degree d-1 annihilate a, and the degree d-1 coefficient is k^(d-1).
It follows that the C-orbit of z has at most r-1 elements. Since H
fixes z and G=HC, the same holds for its G-orbit. This nonzero vector
is a nonidentity element of G with

    |z^G| <= r-1 < r = |a^G|,

contradicting the minimal nonidentity class size of a. This completes
the proof.

## Relation to prior work and verification scope

Moretó, *Conjugacy classes of small size in solvable groups*,
Communications in Algebra 51 (2023), 3051–3055,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/00927872.2023.2176510), states in its
primary abstract a related prime-class-size result: in the soluble,
centreless setting, a small element lies in Z(F(G)). The abstract also
credits linear-algebra methods. Only that abstract, the author's
publication list and the institutional metadata were accessible here;
the full proof and Example 2.6 were not obtained or audited. The proof
above was derived from the overgroup reduction and written independently,
but no assertion of a new theorem, new method, or earliest priority is
made. The related source may already contain stronger implications.

The completed small-group search and independent literal subgroup
controls support this partial theorem in their stated finite ranges.
They are not a replacement for the proof, and no computation here
establishes the full composite-class-size assertion.
