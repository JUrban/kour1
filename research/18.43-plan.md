# Trace-equivalent words: Problem 18.43

## Exact objective and current scope

Find two nonconjugate words in F(a,b) with identical traces for every pair of invertible 3 by 3 complex matrices; this would answer both subparts affirmatively. The initial search uses positive words only, canonicalized by cyclic rotation. In the free group, two positive words of the same length are conjugate exactly when one is a cyclic rotation of the other.

Literature triage found that *Decision problems, complexity, traces, and representations*, https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/29814?nt=1 , reports no SL3-trace-equivalent pairs through length 20. This is a primary paper; inspect its precise search conventions before comparing exhaustive ranges. Start with implementation checks, then lengths 21–32. Negative results remain bounded and do not answer the problem.

## Search and certification

1. Generate each binary necklace of length n exactly once with the standard periodic-prefix recursion. Independently check the count against (1/n) sum_{d|n} phi(d) 2^(n/d).
2. Evaluate its matrix product in two explicit pairs of matrices modulo p=2^31-1. Matrix A is diagonal, B is dense. Store the two traces and word in a sortable array. This needs approximately 16 times the number of necklaces bytes plus recursion storage.
3. A mathematical trace identity must collide under both evaluations. A collision is only a candidate; verify it under further integer/modular assignments, and then symbolically.
4. For symbolic verification put A=diag(a_0,a_1,a_2) and B=(b_ij). If w=A^r_0 B ... A^r_(m-1) B, expand the trace as a sum over closed color sequences i_0,...,i_(m-1) in {0,1,2}. Its monomial records the accumulated A exponents at each color and the directed B-edge multiplicities. Equality of the resulting integer coefficient dictionaries is an exact certificate.
5. Equality on all diagonal A and all B proves the full polynomial identity by conjugation and density of diagonalizable complex matrices. Positive words need no inverses or denominator handling.

Implementation controls: recover the familiar dimension-2 reversal collisions at length 6, compare necklace enumeration with brute rotation canonicalization for small n, and compare modular fingerprints with an independent Python matrix implementation. Check both chosen B matrices have nonzero determinant. All arithmetic uses unsigned 64-bit integers; a sum of three products of residues modulo 2^31-1 is below 2^64, and the Mersenne reduction is exact.

The two 3-dimensional matrix pairs are fixed in the source: diagonals (3,5,7) and (11,13,17), with dense B matrices formed from the first and next nine displayed primes. The reported evaluations can also be viewed as reductions of integer invertible matrices, so distinct modular fingerprints rigorously rule out an identity for that particular pair of words. No probability claim is needed for a completed no-collision search, once enumeration is validated.

Resource plan: run different lengths in separate single-threaded processes, initially at most six at once. This is computational parallelism, not delegation to other agents. Stop or resize a run based on measured cost and remaining research time.

## GL3 and SL3 equivalence

The two subparts are equivalent, even allowing inverses in the words. Let e_a(w)
and e_b(w) be the exponent sums. If u and v have equal trace on all SL3 pairs,
set B=I and A=diag(t,s,(ts)^(-1)). The Laurent polynomials

    t^e + s^e + t^(-e)s^(-e)

are distinct for different integers e. For e=0 this is the constant 3; for
nonzero e the monomial t^e has no counterpart in the polynomial for a different
integer. Thus e_a(u)=e_a(v). Interchanging A,B gives e_b(u)=e_b(v).

Given arbitrary invertible complex A,B, choose nonzero scalars lambda,mu such
that lambda A and mu B have determinant 1. Word evaluation scales by
lambda^e_a mu^e_b, also for negative exponents. These factors agree for u,v,
so the SL3 identity implies the GL3 identity. The converse is immediate by
restriction. This is an elementary reduction, not a resolution of existence.

For positive words the exponent sums also show that trace-equivalent words must
have the same length. Therefore separate exhaustive searches at each length
cover all pairs in the corresponding length interval.

## Primary-source comparison

Lawton–Louder–McReynolds, *Decision problems, complexity, traces, and representations*,
Groups Geom. Dyn. 11 (2017), 165–188, DOI 10.4171/GGD/393, Section 4.3,
reports a length-20 search after discussing positive pairs and the reduction to
SL2 candidates. The paragraph does not give enough implementation detail to
infer exactly which pairs its reported search covers. We use an explicitly
documented positive-word scope, include reversal pairs, and make no claim about
all reduced words with inverses.
