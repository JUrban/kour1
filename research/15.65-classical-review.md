## Mathematical audit

1. The Notebook's printed p.91 says squarefree **minimal** polynomial.
   This is SS in FNP. The proof treats this literal question as well as
   FNP's different squarefree-characteristic-polynomial convention RS.
   Invertibility over a finite field implies SS exactly when the matrix
   order is prime to the characteristic; this is used only in controls.
2. The full U, Sp and O families agree with FNP's definitions. In particular,
   the two even-dimensional O types use the full groups with the factor
   two in their orders in both characteristics. The unitary base parameter
   q is the square root of the actual field size. Rationality in q^2 would
   imply rationality in q and is therefore excluded by the proof.
3. The characteristic parameter e is fixed throughout any analytic
   continuation: e=1 on even prime powers and e=2 on odd prime powers.
   It is never assigned a parity-dependent value at a complex number.
4. Lemma1.3.16's polynomial J_d has odd divisors only. Its denominator
   is 2d, and its constant term is included. K_1=(q-e-1)/2 is exceptional.
   The count bounds hold uniformly for all d with C=8. For example,
   |J_d|<=|t|^(-d), while the N and K formulas give smaller bounds than8.
5. Laurent polynomial exponents are legitimate analytic continuations of
   the integer counts. No complex parameter is asserted to be a field.
   Each logarithm has at least twice the order of the exponent's pole,
   making the apparent singularity at t=0 removable.
6. An analytic germ agreeing with a rational interpolant at t=2^(-k)
   or t=3^(-k) agrees identically near zero. The interpolant cannot have
   a pole there because these values tend to a finite nonzero constant.
   Only sufficiently large k are needed.
7. The local branching lemma permits a zero or pole in its prefactor K.
   A sufficiently small punctured disc excludes other zeros and poles.
   A loop changes log a by 2*pi*i*h. Single-valuedness would force
   exp(2*pi*i*h*P(t)) identically one on an open set, which forces P'=0.
8. A nonconstant exponent does not lose its branching when its value at
   the zero happens to be integral. This matters for RS_U and even RS_Sp
   at t=-1/2. Repeated loops are not replaced with an argument merely
   about the exponent at the central point.
9. For C_Sp the omitted A(t) has its simple zero at alpha=(1-sqrt(5))/2.
   The higher A-factors have the uniform majorant81/112<1 on |t|<3/4.
   All B logarithms are holomorphic on |t|<1. The logarithmic sum is
   normally convergent and its exponential has no zeros.
10. The orthogonal cyclic prefactors are ((1-t)^e+1)/2 and1-t,
    exactly as in Theorem2.3.11. They are nonzero rational functions,
    so rationality of any orthogonal limit would imply that of C_Sp.
11. U,L,P,Q are holomorphic on the unit disc because their numerator
    exponents grow quadratically and their denominator products are
    bounded below on each compact subdisc by a positive infinite product.
    Their continuations are in inverse field size; FNP's entire functions
    of the dimension-generating variable are a different assertion.
12. The explicit majorant210/253 bounds the sum of the absolute values
    of all nonconstant terms of U and L on |u|<=3/7. Thus neither has a
    zero on that closed disc, and their logarithms exist there.
13. For U(-r), the signs are (-1)^(m^2)=(-1)^m and the denominator
    becomes product(1-r^i). The ratio bound is at most r^3/(1-r^2)<1
    for r<=3/5. The alternating estimates9/56>0 and
    -1128797279/26656000000<0 isolate at least one zero between -.6 and-.5.
14. U(-r)>0 for 0<r<=1/2 follows from a_1<=1 and the strictly positive
    paired tail a_2-a_3+... . A first negative zero exists by compactness;
    it has finite positive multiplicity by analyticity and U(0)=1.
    No numerical location, uniqueness, or simple-root assumption is used.
15. L(-r) has a different positive denominator, product(1-(-r)^i).
    Its alternating terms also decrease for r<=13/20. The strict lower
    bound1-r/(1+r)>0 provides a zero-free neighborhood of the path[b,0].
    A sufficiently thin simply connected neighborhood gives a single
    branch of log L along that path.
16. E=(1-u)U and D=(1-u)L have zero linear coefficient. At R=13/20,
    every higher-degree argument has modulus at most169/400<3/7.
    Uniform O(u^2) bounds on their logarithms, followed by the count
    bounds, give a normally convergent nonvanishing higher-degree product.
17. The separated degree-one D factor in the Sp and O cases is nonzero
    near the path by point15. Its exponent has a pole at zero, but
    log D(t)=O(t^2), so this factor extends across zero. It causes no
    local monodromy at the first zero of E.
18. The holomorphic P and Q prefactors need not be nonzero at b. Their
    values at zero show they are not identically zero, hence any zero at b
    has finite order. The branching lemma covers exactly this situation.
19. Q(t)'s exponents m(2m-1) follow by summing the two full orthogonal
    reciprocal orders. The terms (1-t^m)^(-1)+(1+t^m)^(-1) combine
    to2/(1-t^(2m)), cancelling the common factor1/2. No parity correction
    is omitted. The two prefactors in Theorem3.1.18 are transcribed exactly.
20. RS's base V has the single relevant zero at -1/2. All higher factors
    are nonzero on |t|<13/20 and have the requisite logarithmic order.
    Theorem2.3.4 supplies the exact orthogonal multipliers1 and2^(e-2).
21. The analytic branch reaches the relevant real interval from zero:
    A,V are positive up to their first negative roots, and E is positive
    on(b,0]. The higher products are already holomorphic on a full disc.
    Possible ordinary prefactor zeros along the path do not prevent
    analytic continuation. A rational function agreeing initially would
    have to share this continuation, including its impossible monodromy.
22. These are statements about rational functions of field size. No
    assertion about irrationality of any single numerical limit is made.
    The imported limit theorems provide existence; finite tests and
    coefficient growth do not replace those theorems or the branching proof.

## Controls and the published-table discrepancy

The retained GAP run covers fourteen full unitary groups and nine full
symplectic groups. The unitary cases are q=2, dimensions1--4; q=3,4,
dimensions1--3; and q=5,7, dimensions1--2. The symplectic cases are q=2,
ranks1--3; q=3,4, ranks1--2; and q=5,7, rank1. All563 conjugacy classes
are checked, and their sizes sum to the exact group orders.

Each class has three paired tests: minimal-polynomial degree versus rank
of flattened matrix powers; squarefree minimal polynomial versus order
prime to the characteristic; squarefree characteristic polynomial versus
the conjunction of cyclicity and semisimplicity. All tests pass. Separately,
Python computes the finite generating coefficients for all three modes
using exact rational arithmetic and the finite U/GL/Sp centralizer orders.
All69 probabilities agree with the actual matrix groups. No finite
orthogonal matrix groups were enumerated for this extension; their limit
relations are imported and their series checked.

The inverse-field-size series are computed through degree40. A second
implementation expands generalized binomial coefficients of Laurent
polynomial exponents, without logarithmic differentiation. It also builds
the E and D bases by inverting denominator polynomials, independently of
the first implementation's successive geometric divisions. The nine U/Sp
series agree through degree12, giving117 coefficient comparisons. All41
unitary cyclic coefficients agree with the frozen original packet.

Of160 coefficients transcribed from16 rows of Tables3,7,11, **159 agree**.
The exception is the odd-characteristic symplectic SS coefficient at t^9:
the published Table11 gives-195, whereas both exact implementations give
**-193**. This discrepancy is also certified without the count polynomials:
Theorem3.1.18 gives

    SS(Sp;odd) = SS(O odd;odd) P(t)/Q(t).

Using Table11's own odd orthogonal row through t^9, together with

    P(t)=1+t^3+t^5+t^7+t^9+O(t^10),
    Q(t)=1+t+t^3+t^5+t^6+t^7+t^8+t^9+O(t^10),

the quotient again has coefficient-193. Thus the table entry-195 is
inconsistent with the source's exact relation and its other table row.
It is retained verbatim as a documented discrepancy, not silently changed
in the source transcript. The nonrationality proof uses neither value.

The first GAP run exited zero and completed all cases, but printed

Exact rational controls also check the sign and zero-free bounds and
