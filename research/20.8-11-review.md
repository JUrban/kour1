# Internal review of the derived answers to 20.8 and 20.11

These are complete deductions from prior work, not new-priority claims.
No outside verification is implied.

## 20.8

- The ambient free group has rank two, H has rank three, and K has
  rank four. Subgroup rank is not monotone in free groups, so these
  dimensions are compatible with both strict inclusions.
- The three generators of H really form a basis; a six-vertex,
  eight-edge folded graph proves this after elementary basis changes.
  The nonidentity assignment on that basis therefore defines a homomorphism.
- The four displayed fixed-element identities were checked by free
  reduction, in Python and GAP. They imply pointwise fixing of K.
- The intermediate-subgroup argument uses the image of the whole
  finite core graph in any overgroup's core. Its first Betti number is
  a lower bound on overgroup rank. It does not assume that every
  overgroup itself is one of the finite principal quotients.
- Both incoming and outgoing edge folds are included. In particular,
  identifying vertices1 and2 forces vertex0 to merge too.
- The only quotient with rank less than four is the whole rank-three
  rose; its presence forces the intermediate subgroup to be H itself.
  The condition on every proper intermediate subgroup is therefore met.
- Exact controls passed on all five partitions and three distinct
  folded graphs, with457 GAP word controls. The universal claim rests
  on the graph proof, not on enumerating a few overgroups.
- Lei--Zhang's n=3 construction is the source of the example and is
  credited explicitly. Its full general equalizer conclusion need not
  be assumed to verify the specific counterexample here.

## 20.11

- Compression of G_1 is used only inside the finitely generated group
  <G_1,G_2>. Every relevant overgroup contains the original F.
- The finitely generated intersection K, not the potentially infinitely
  generated F, is substituted into Corollary1.5.
- Chain unions are handled by choosing finitely many free-basis
  elements of the union and applying the free-factor subgroup property.
- For intersections, countability is invoked only for one rank-r
  subgroup G_0, which is countable even if H is uncountable.
- Descending subgroup membership stabilizes on every finite set of
  ambient words. This justifies preservation of a finite graph with
  too many independent cycles in the Schreier-graph limit argument.
- Existence of a greatest member and a least member does not imply
  that all minimum-rank overgroups coincide; the final cyclic example
  checks this distinction.
- The full subgroup-rigidity theorem is an external input. Its main
  consequences and the proof of Corollary1.5 were checked for scope,
  not reproduced or independently certified in full.
