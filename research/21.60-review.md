# Internal audit of the order-twelve counterexample to 21.60

Status: complete candidate negative answer, internally audited on
11 September 2026. Independent outside review and further novelty checks
remain pending. The main proof is `research/21.60-proof.md`.

## Exact hypotheses

The printed question on supplied Notebook page 176 was visually checked.
It uses the prime field F_p, rational simple modules, and equality in the
Grothendieck group of F_p G. The ring Z_(p) is the localization of the
integers, not its p-adic completion. No normal-Sylow assumption is imposed.

The example is SmallGroup(12,1), C3 semidirect C4 with inversion action,
at p=2. Its central involution is b^2 and the quotient by this involution
is S3. Its Sylow 2-subgroup is not normal.

## Mathematical audit

- The characteristic-two simple-module list is exhaustive. A central
  involution acts trivially on a simple module by its nilpotent
  difference from 1. The quotient S3 has an explicit algebra
  decomposition kC2 times M2(k), proving that its simple modules have
  dimensions one and two and that both are defined over k=F2 itself.
- The two-dimensional rational lift is simple: its order-three matrix
  has irreducible minimal polynomial t^2+t+1. Its integral matrices
  preserve R^2 and reduce to the simple two-dimensional k-module. The
  lift need not be faithful, and faithfulness is not required in 21.60.
- Since both simple kG-modules lift, the positive-combination condition
  holds for all finite-dimensional modules by composition series. No
  projectivity-preservation assertion for this chosen rational lift is
  being made. The point is precisely that classes alone lose information.
- The quaternion algebra has the positive norm
  x^2-xy+y^2+z^2-zt+t^2. The inverse formula proves division over Q for
  every nonzero element. Bounded enumeration is not used to prove it.
- The order O has the stated four-element R-basis, stable multiplication
  and conjugation. The homomorphism RG onto O sends a to omega and b
  to j, with j^2=-1. This is distinct from the rational simple lift,
  where b is represented by an involution.
- Reducing the quaternion order gives M2(F2), as proved by four linearly
  independent explicit matrices. This does not say that the division
  algebra itself reduces as a field or remains a division algebra.
- The norm-unit calculation gives J(O)=2O. Thus its matrix quotient has
  an idempotent which cannot lift to O. Quotient closure of semiperfect
  rings is proved from the definition in the main note. This establishes
  nonsemiperfectness of RG without a character-field or Schur-index table.

Passing to a completed coefficient ring would change the argument: the
question explicitly concerns the noncomplete rational localization. No
counterexample to semiperfectness over p-adic integers is asserted.

## Exact computations and explicit idempotents

Run:

    python3 scripts/verify_21_60.py
    bin/gap scripts/verify_21_60.g

Python independently implements the quaternion algebra on rational
four-tuples and G on pairs (i,j) in Z/3 times Z/4. It checks the 64
associativity identities on algebra basis triples, 6,561 positive-norm
and inverse examples, all 144 group products in the faithful quaternion
model, and all 256 products in the sixteen-element reduction algebra.

It also constructs F2G by binary group-ring convolution and checks every
one of its 4,096 elements for idempotence, finding 52 idempotents. Their
matrix-quotient images include all eight idempotents of M2(F2). The
explicit quotient to F2 times M2(F2) has nilpotent kernel of dimension
seven, with successive radical-power dimensions 7,2,1,0.

For a more concrete modular witness, in kG set

    e=1+a+a^2,       c=1-e,       z=b^2,
    f=c(a+ab+1+z).

The computed identities e^2=e, f^2=f and ef=fe=0 hold. The two associated
left ideals have dimension four. Their corner algebras and corner
radicals have dimensions (4,3) and (2,1), respectively, proving that
these idempotents are primitive in the finite algebra. The first module
has composition factors 4[k], and the second has factors 2[V]. Thus
their classes have the particularly simple positive preimages 4[Q]
and 2[W]. Under the quaternion reduction, f maps to E22, so f is an
explicit modular idempotent that cannot lift to RG.

GAP independently constructs the actual group, quotient and group ring;
checks the rational and finite-field matrix groups; computes the
Jacobson radical and its powers; computes both left ideals and their
corner algebras; and independently enumerates all 52 idempotents.
The ordinary character table has one quaternionic degree-two character,
consistent with the division-algebra obstruction, but that table is not
an input to the proof.

Both initial control runs passed. Saved outputs are
`results/21.60-python-controls.json` and `results/21.60-gap-controls.log`.
The attempted broad character-table search was superseded by this
explicit construction before any such scan was started.

## A conflicting proposition in the source

The original paper's Proposition 3.3 would imply a positive answer for
this example, because composition series meet its stated filtration
condition. Its proof uses a reduction isomorphism on Ext^1 for chosen
lattices. That step is false without extra hypotheses. The display on
preprint page 10 was inspected visually.

Here is an elementary obstruction to that step alone. Let H=C2 and
let R=Z_(2), k=F2, with trivial actions. Then

    Ext^1_(RH)(R,R) = H^1(H,R) = Hom(C2,(R,+)) = 0,
    Ext^1_(kH)(k,k) = H^1(H,k) = Hom(C2,(k,+)) = k.

Equivalently, an extension of two chosen trivial R-lattices has generator
matrix [[1,t],[0,1]]. Squaring it gives [[1,2t],[0,1]], so the order-two
relation forces t=0. Over k, t=1 gives a nonsplit extension. Thus the
asserted map cannot in general be surjective, let alone an isomorphism.
This explains why that source proposition cannot be used to reject the
explicit quotient obstruction. The order-twelve example also refutes
that proposition as it is written, rather than merely its proof strategy.

independently verified. Conjecture 9 matches the Notebook question.

Searches on the exact question, the authors, semiperfect group rings,
dicyclic groups and C3 semidirect C4 found no prior matching resolution.

are classical; novelty of their use for this exact equivalence remains
to be checked independently. No outside communications were made.
