# 20.100: resuming certificates and dividing independent verification

The original all-group proofs at n=4,5,6 remain checked by
`scripts/verify_20_100_certificate.g`. The following additions support
larger certificates. They do not establish any further fixed-n case until
its final certificate and all its checker parts have passed.

## Reusing completed nodes after an explicit bound

`scripts/prove_20_100.py` now accepts `--resume-certificate PATH`.
The earlier file must be a closed gzip file containing the initial header
and a consecutive prefix of complete postorder nodes. It may also be a
complete certificate. Its n and excluded-prime bound must agree with the
new invocation. The original and new output paths must differ.

The loader parses each node's component state into exact fractions and
rebuilds the completed-node cache. It copies the original node lines
without rewriting them. The search begins at the root, using cached nodes
where available and continuing all unfinished branches. Edges and leaves
are counted only for the completed copied nodes; the earlier run's counts
for unfinished ancestors are not imported. The final output still requires
the independent verifier, so prefix data are never accepted as proof merely
because the generator loaded them.

New bounded stops print `INCOMPLETE_BOUND_REACHED` and close the gzip file,
without writing `FinishProof`. The original n=7 process reached its
10-million-state bound and stopped with its original explicit TimeoutError.
Its closed gzip contains 9,999,993 consecutive completed nodes and no root
footer. The file and log were preserved under the bounded filenames below;
the checked hash and stop evidence are in the bounded summary. Do not read
an active gzip as a completed prefix or overwrite the active process's output.

After verifying the original generator has stopped, preserve its log and
certificate under explicit incomplete/bounded filenames, then use:

    python3 scripts/prove_20_100.py 7 \
      --resume-certificate results/20.100-n7-bounded-certificate.g.gz \
      --certificate results/20.100-n7-certificate.g.gz \
      --max-states 30000000 --seconds 7200

This continuation was started on 11 September 2026 at approximately
06:34:47 UTC as PID 47564, with stdout saved to the canonical n=7 generator
log. Prefix loading completed successfully; at 06:48:58 UTC it had reached
10,780,000 visited states. The time bound includes prefix loading. The loader
reports progress every 100,000 nodes to distinguish loading from DFS work.

Controls resume n=4 after113 complete nodes and n=5 after5,000 complete
nodes; both outputs are byte-identical after decompression to the original
independently verified certificates. A separate actual n=4 stop at50
visited states leaves46 completed nodes; resuming it also reproduces the
verified certificate exactly. Control logs and hashes are in
`results/20.100-resume-controls.json`, the individual resume logs, and
`results/20.100-sharding-controls.json`.

## Dividing the independent GAP check

`scripts/verify_20_100_shard.g` uses the same integer-presentation and
localized Smith membership argument as the original independent checker.
Each of s processes reads every state, but checks precisely those nodes
with (identifier-1) mod s equal to its assigned index. Thus the processes
partition all nodes. Each branch still checks every mapped child relation,
and every child identifier must be smaller than its parent. All leaves in
each part must force an actual equality. The root must have no relations.

The process stores compact component states and uses a fixed65,536-entry
direct-mapped cache of presentation rows, instead of retaining every dense
presentation matrix. A cache entry is used only when its identifier agrees;
collisions merely trigger recomputation. The cache changes neither the
presentation nor its membership test.

Every part prints only `PASS_SHARD`. Such a marker alone does not certify
the root: its earlier children may be assigned to other parts. All parts
must finish on the same certificate and checker, with complete disjoint
coverage. `scripts/run_20_100_shards.py` enforces this:

    python3 scripts/run_20_100_shards.py 7 --shards 6

This command first verifies the completed generator marker, excluded-prime
bound, compressed hash, full gzip, node count and root footer. It records
the exact common certificate/checker hashes and GAP commands in every log,
starts six GAP processes, and checks the hashes again after completion.
It then rejects any error, missing or repeated part marker, wrong index,
wrong node coverage, or mismatch in total nodes/leaves/edges. Only after
all checks does it write a `VERIFIED` aggregate summary. Existing logs or
a completed summary are preserved by refusing to overwrite them; use a
different `--output-prefix` for a deliberate repeat.

The runner uses8GiB GAP workspace ceilings per process and at most10
processes; the planned six-process run allows48GiB combined workspaces.
Existing unrelated workers must still be included in the overall resource
account. The status summarizer now recognizes these part logs and
independently checks their metadata, current certificate/checker hashes,
coverage and aggregate totals. A certificate is reported as `VERIFIED`
only after every assigned part and the final aggregate pass. Completed
parts without the aggregate remain `CHECKED_PENDING_AGGREGATION`.
Missing parts are distinguished from verified live workers by checking
the actual process command and stdout, not merely the recorded PID.

`scripts/verify_20_100_summary.py` passes 19 controls, including missing
parts, a wrong part index, stale hashes, errors after a pass marker,
incorrect aggregate counts, PID reuse, a stopped generator and an
explicit bounded stop. The first negative-control run exposed a partial
duplicate marker being ignored: GAP can wrap a complete `PASS_SHARD`
record across lines, so counting only complete regular-expression
matches missed a second bare prefix. Both the reporter and runner now
also require exactly one marker prefix in each completed part. All
controls pass after this correction. A fresh actual three-part n=4
runner invocation also passes with the unchanged mathematical totals;
its artifacts use the `20.100-n4-summary-integration` prefix.

## Controls and their limits

The three-part checks at n=4 and n=5 pass, with aggregate counts exactly
equal to the original checker: respectively227/10,493 nodes,
51/1,736 leaves,1,056/87,570 edges, and3,953/426,345 relation implications.
The new runner itself is tested on the n=4 certificate with three parts;
its hash and coverage aggregation passes.

A deliberately invalid two-node certificate has a valid forced-equality
leaf and a false implication at its root. The part owning the leaf passes
locally; the part owning the root rejects the false implication. This is
retained explicitly to demonstrate why a local part completion cannot
be counted as a proof. The original deliberately false leaf control also
remains separate from all valid certificates.

No n=7 result is claimed by these infrastructure controls. Its generator
is still active at the time this note is first committed.

## Measured state-storage scale

A separate resource-only probe reads the first million states of the closed
bounded n=7 prefix, retaining the same state objects as the sharded checker
but doing no verification. GAP 4.16.1 reports reachable-object sizes of
20,453,968 bytes at 100,000 states, 102,589,792 at 500,000 states, and
205,051,944 at one million states. The probe completed in 52.5 CPU seconds.
See `scripts/profile_20_100_states.g` and the memory-probe log/summary.

At that observed rate, 30 million retained states alone would take about
6.15 GB per process. This extrapolation excludes the GAP baseline, row
cache, Smith workspaces and garbage-collection headroom, and does not prove
that every later state has the same size. The proposed 8 GiB workspace
ceiling must be assessed against the actual final certificate. If a later
resumption produces a substantially larger certificate, reduce concurrency
and revise workspace ceilings within the 100 GB total budget. No local
resource-probe completion is a certificate-verifier completion.

The closed incomplete prefix remains preserved on the local filesystem,
with its compressed hash and bounded log committed. The large prefix and
the active output gzip are not staged as final proofs.
