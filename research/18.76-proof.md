# 18.76: a nonsplit extension by the additive group of a division ring

Status: complete internally checked candidate negative argument.
Novelty and outside review remain pending. See `18.76-review.md`.

## Statement

There is a countable division ring D and a subgroup

    G = F(a,b) x F(c,d) <= D^*

for which an extension

    1 -> (D,+) -> E -> G -> 1

does not split, although the conjugation action of G on its kernel is
left multiplication in D. The construction works in characteristic
zero and in every positive prime characteristic.

This gives a negative answer to the exact printed Problem 18.76,
visually checked on page 125. Neither the group nor the division ring
is required to be finite in the question.

## 1. The division ring

Let k be Q or a prime field F_p. Free groups are bi-orderable, and a
direct product of bi-orderable groups is bi-orderable, for example
with a lexicographic product order. Choose such an order on

    G=F(a,b) x F(c,d).

The Mal'cev--Neumann theorem embeds k[G] in the division ring k((G))
of formal series with well-ordered support. Let D be the smallest
division subring of k((G)) containing k[G]. It is countable: start
with the countable ring k[G] and repeatedly close under the finitely
many field operations, including inversion of nonzero elements.

All four letters are distinct from 1 in D. Every element of the first
free factor commutes with every element of the second. In particular
c and d commute with a-1, b-1, and (a-1)^(-1). No commutation between
a and b, or between c and d, will be assumed.

Only the standard division-ring embedding theorem and bi-orderability
of free groups are external ingredients. The extension and its
nonsplitting are explicit below.

For the precise external statements, see Sakarovitch, *Rational and
recognisable series*, Theorems 21 and 22, in the author-hosted chapter
at https://perso.telecom-paristech.fr/jsaka/PUB/Files/HBWA.pdf .
Bergman, *Some results relevant to embeddability of rings (especially
group algebras) in division rings*, Journal of Algebra 535 (2019),
503–540, Section 1, explicitly states the division-ring theorem for
two-sided invariant orders and the inclusion of the finite-support
group algebra: https://math.berkeley.edu/~gbergman/papers/kG_in_D.pdf .

## 2. A free-group derivation

Write F=F(a,b). There is a unique map delta:F->D satisfying

    delta(xy)=delta(x)+x delta(y),
    delta(a)=0,  delta(b)=1.

Here elements of F act by left multiplication. Explicitly set
delta(a^(-1))=0 and delta(b^(-1))=-b^(-1), and for a word multiply
each letter's delta value on the left by its preceding prefix, then
add. Deleting adjacent inverse letters does not change this sum, so
it is a well-defined map on the free group. Its values lie in k[F].

Let N=D_add semidirect F, with multiplication

    (r,x)(s,y)=(r+x s,xy).

Define two maps of N:

    gamma_c(r,x)=(c r,x),
    gamma_d(r,x)=(d r+delta(x),x).

Both are automorphisms. For gamma_d, for example, its homomorphism
identity follows from d x=x d and the derivation identity. Its inverse
is (r,x) |-> (d^(-1)(r-delta(x)),x). The inverse of gamma_c is
(r,x) |-> (c^(-1)r,x).

Because K=F(c,d) is free, these two automorphisms extend to an action
gamma:K->Aut(N). Define

    E=N semidirect_gamma K.

This proves existence and associativity without relying on a possibly
inconsistent presentation by four twisted commutation relations.

## 3. The kernel, quotient, and action

The subgroup A={(r,1,1):r in D} is normal in E and is naturally
isomorphic to (D,+). Since both gamma_c and gamma_d induce the identity
on N/A=F, the quotient map is

    pi:E->F x K,    pi(r,x,h)=(x,h).

It is onto, and its kernel is precisely A. On A, gamma_c and gamma_d
act as left multiplication by c and d, respectively. Thus conjugation
by (r,x,h) sends an element v of A to x h v. This is exactly the
prescribed multiplication action of G<=D^*; it does not depend on r.

For a fully explicit multiplication formula, define eta:K->k[K] by
eta(c)=0, eta(d)=1, eta(hk)=eta(h)+h eta(k). Since k[K] commutes
with k[F], the action of h is

    gamma_h(r,x)=(h r+eta(h)delta(x),x).

Consequently

    (r,x,h)(s,y,k)
      = (r+x h s+x eta(h)delta(y), xy,hk).                 (1)

In particular, with the natural zero-kernel lifts of the four letters,
the pairs (a,c),(a,d),(b,c) commute, while

    d_tilde b_tilde = (1_D in A) b_tilde d_tilde.

The element 1_D here is a nonzero element of the additive kernel,
not the identity element of E.

## 4. No section can exist

Suppose a section s:G->E exists. Its values on a,b,c,d have the form

    s(a)=(u,a,1),  s(b)=(v,b,1),
    s(c)=(w,1,c),  s(d)=(z,1,d),

for some u,v,w,z in D. Write

    A_0=a-1, B_0=b-1, C_0=c-1, D_0=d-1.

The four commutation relations in F x K, evaluated using (1), give

    A_0 w - C_0 u = 0,                                    (2)
    A_0 z - D_0 u = 0,                                    (3)
    B_0 w - C_0 v = 0,                                    (4)
    B_0 z - D_0 v = 1_D.                                  (5)

The first two imply

    w=A_0^(-1) C_0 u,  z=A_0^(-1) D_0 u.

Using (4) and the fact that C_0 commutes with B_0 and A_0^(-1),
we obtain

    v=C_0^(-1) B_0 A_0^(-1) C_0 u=B_0 A_0^(-1)u.

Substitution in the left side of (5) gives

    B_0 A_0^(-1)D_0 u-D_0 B_0 A_0^(-1)u=0,

because D_0 commutes with B_0 and A_0^(-1). Equation (5) therefore
asserts 0=1_D, a contradiction. Thus pi has no section and the
extension does not split.

Only A_0 and C_0 were inverted, and both are nonzero because the
group ring embeds in D. The argument never interchanges factors
from within one free factor. It works without change in characteristic 2.

## 5. A cohomological interpretation, not needed for the proof

Formula (1) has factor set

    f((x,h),(y,k))=x eta(h)delta(y).

The derivation identities show directly that f is a normalized
2-cocycle for the left multiplication action. The product of two
two-petal roses is a two-dimensional classifying space for G, with
one vertex, four edges, and four squares. Its degree-one coboundary
map over D is the linear map in the left sides of (2)--(5). The first
three equations have a one-dimensional solution space over the
right division ring D, and force the fourth coordinate to be zero.
Thus this map has rank 3, and H^2(G,D) has right D-dimension 1.
Our factor set represents a nonzero class, as the elementary section
argument already proves.

The distinction between a nested semidirect product and the particular
extension being asked to split is essential. E is a semidirect product
over N, but there is no complement to D_add in E.
