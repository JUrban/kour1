# Problem 18.120: a proved special case and bounded search

12 September 2026. General question unresolved. Candidate increment: zero.

The elementary argument in `18.120-partial.md` proves the requested
factorization whenever the normal closure of B' is abelian. In particular,
it proves the answer for every group of class at most four, without requiring
A intersect B=1. It also applies when B has subnormal defect at most two;
that normal-closure observation is already in McCann (2016), Lemma 4.
No priority claim is made for these special cases.

## Exact search scope

The pilot enumerated all 3,257 SmallGroups of orders 32, 64, 81, 125, 128,
243, 343, 625 and 729. The extension enumerated all 56,092 groups of order
256. GAP's SmallGroups catalogue and subgroup routines are dependencies
of this coverage assertion. The packet checks all expected catalogue IDs,
including rows skipped by the theorem.

Of the 59,349 groups, 58,946 have class at most four and are covered by
the proof. In the other 403, the code takes one representative of every
conjugacy class of class-two subgroups B, and every actual abelian subgroup
A. It tests |A||B|=|G| and A intersect B=1. Simultaneous conjugation carries
any such pair to a tested pair. It is harmless that some simultaneous
conjugacy orbits are tested repeatedly: these are B-representative counts,
not counts of pair orbits. Abelian B needs no search, since B'=1 and B0=1.

Every one of the 723 pilot and 27,432 extension factorizations passes
IsAbelian(<A,B'> intersect B). The 28,155 tests occur in 237 groups.
Class-five and class-six groups contribute successful tests. This does
not exhaust factorizations with nontrivial A intersect B in higher class.

For disjoint factors, any B0<=B satisfying <A,B'>=AB0 must equal
<A,B'> intersect B, by uniqueness of the AB expression. Thus the test
would detect every counterexample in its stated scope. No flag occurred.
For overlapping factors, an abelian intersection is still sufficient but
its failure alone would not refute the problem.

## Independent controls and process evidence

The pilot controls reconstruct complete multiplication tables, inverses,
associativity, all subgroups, conjugacy orbits and the lower central series
for SmallGroup(128,134), (128,995), (729,99), (729,100). They verify 355
B-representative factorizations, representing 435 actual ordered pairs.
Their 1,009 subgroups and 779,035,282 associativity triples pass. The
class-two test uses central generator commutators: their centrality makes
the subgroup they generate exactly the derived subgroup.

Two further controls use (256,503), of class six, and (256,6547), of class
five and the largest factorization count in the extension. The independent
checks use only the exported tables for their group calculations and
compare coverage totals with GAP afterward. These add 1,782 subgroups, 33,554,432 associativity triples and 896
B-representative factorizations, representing 2,816 actual ordered pairs.
The six controls together cover 2,791 subgroups and 812,589,714 triples.

Both screen processes and their outer runners actually exited zero, with
clean logs and completion sentinels. GAP runtimes were 12.21 and 310.01
seconds. Pilot export and controls took 3.38 and 96.19 seconds. Runner
observations retain the actual tool completion identifiers separately from
the child process records. A zero return alone is never treated as success.

An additional exact exploration enumerates all partitions of the positive
roots of UT_n for n=2,...,6 into abelian A roots and a closed B root set
of class at most two. The valid counts are 2, 5, 13, 25, 25; none has
nonabelian B roots in the closure of A and B'. This concerns coordinate
root subgroups only. It is not a proof for arbitrary n, arbitrary matrix
subgroups, or general factorizations.

The SHA-256 manifest binds the mathematics, scripts, source cache and
actual run records. Its audit checks coverage and replays all six table
controls. These checks support a bounded partial result, not a solution
of the general question.

## Next structural route

The derived-length-four examples of Cossey and Stonehewer (1998) show
that the sufficient abelian-normal-closure condition cannot hold in
all factorizations. Their explicit construction has not yet been read.
Testing <A,B'> in those examples is a possible further route; derived
length four for G alone does not refute the requested factorization of K.
