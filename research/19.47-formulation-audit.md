# 19.47: rank-one interpretation requires a definition check

2026-09-11. Conditional elementary obstruction, not a candidate solution.
The printed text was read in the extracted Notebook, but this page has
not yet been visually checked. The meaning of 'one-dimensional
transformation' must be settled from the author's conventions.

If the term means every nonidentity h with rank(h-I)=1, the following
standard quadratic-field normalizer is an immediate counterexample.
For any odd prime power q, put k=F_q, K=F_(q^2), and let

    H = K^x semidirect Gal(K/k) <= GL_2(k).

The nontrivial field automorphism s:x|->x^q fixes exactly the
one-dimensional k-subspace k of K, so rank(s-I)=1. The group contains
the entire multiplication torus K^x and has order2(q^2-1), prime to
the characteristic. Every nontrivial elementary transvection has
order p=char(k), so H contains none.

Explicitly choose a nonsquare d in k and a basis1,alpha with alpha^2=d.
The torus consists of the nonzero matrices

    [[a,d*b],[b,a]],   (a,b)!=(0,0),

and s=diag(1,-1). This s has determinant-1. It would NOT qualify if
the author's term includes a determinant-one or unipotence condition.

The primary abstract of Koibaev, *Transvections in the subgroups of
the general linear group containing a non-split maximal torus*,
Algebra i Analiz21(5)(2009),70--86; English translation St.Petersburg
Math.J.21(5)(2010),731--742, DOI10.1090/S1061-0022-2010-01114-2,
claims that an overgroup with a one-dimensional transformation contains
nontrivial elementary transvections in every row and column. That
claim would also contradict the naive rank-one example, so the
terminology or hypotheses must be checked before assigning an answer.
