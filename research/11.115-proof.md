# 11.115: a proper-power counterexample

September 12, 2026. Complete negative candidate for the supplied question.
Priority and outside review remain pending.

## A short proof

Let X=F(a,b) be the free group of rank two. Fix an integer m>=2 and put

    N = normal closure in X of a^m,
    T = kernel of X -> C_m,  a -> 1 mod m, b -> 0 mod m.

Then N is nontrivial and normal in X, and N<T<X. Indeed a^m is a
nonidentity reduced word and belongs to N; N is contained in T because
the displayed map kills a^m; and a does not belong to T. Finally b is
in T but not in N: the homomorphism X->Z sending a to 0 and b to 1
kills N and does not kill b. In particular N is not maximal in X,
as required in Problem 11.115.

We prove

    [T,N] = [X,N].                                           (1)

Here [U,V] denotes the subgroup generated by all commutators of elements
of U and V. Set K=[T,N]. Since T and N are both normal in X, K is
normal in X: conjugating a generating commutator conjugates each of
its entries back into the corresponding subgroup.

In X/K the image of a^m commutes with b, since a^m is in N and b is
in T. It also commutes with a, since powers of a commute with a in X
itself. As a,b generate X, the image of a^m is central in X/K.
The image of N, its normal closure, is consequently a central cyclic
subgroup. Thus [X,N] is contained in K. The reverse inclusion follows
from T<=X, proving (1).

This contradicts the proposed strict containment. The case m=2 already
suffices. If one additionally wants T not to be maximal in X, take m=4:
the kernel T_2 of the corresponding map to C_2 satisfies
N<T=T_4<T_2<X. This extra condition is not needed in the printed problem.

## The common subgroup and quotient

With commutator convention [u,v]=u^-1 v^-1 u v, one has more explicitly

    K = normal closure in X of [a^m,b],
    X/K = <a,b | [a^m,b]=1>.                                (2)

The displayed normal closure is contained in [X,N]. Conversely, imposing
that one relator makes a^m commute with both generators, so its normal
closure N has central image. This proves the other containment in (2).
In particular K is nontrivial: [a^m,b] is a nonempty reduced word.
Also K<N because every commutator has exponent sum zero in a, whereas
a^m has exponent sum m. Hence the example satisfies the stronger chain

    1 < K < N < T < X.

For a second, concrete model let B=F(s_0,...,s_(m-1)), and let tau be
the automorphism cyclically permuting this free basis. On B x Z define

    (u,k)(v,l) = (u tau^k(v), k+l).                          (3)

This is a group, the semidirect product B semidirect Z. Send a to (1,1)
and b to (s_0,0). These images generate the group, since conjugating b
by powers a^i gives (s_i,0). Also a^m=(1,m) is central, so (2) maps onto
this group. Conversely, in the presentation (2) put s_i=a^i b a^-i.
Conjugation by a cycles these elements, including the final step because
a^m commutes with b. These assignments define a homomorphism from (3)
back to (2), and the two maps fix the generators. They are inverse.

Under this isomorphism N/K is the central infinite cyclic group
generated by (1,m), and

    T/K = B x mZ,       T/N = B,       X/N = C_m * Z.         (4)

These descriptions are supplementary. The proof of (1) does not depend
on a presentation algorithm, a residual property, or a multiplier theorem.

## Schreier basis and finite identities

The subgroup T has free basis

    r=a^m,       s_i=a^i b a^-i  (0<=i<m).

For completeness, the coset graph has vertices 0,...,m-1, an a-edge
from i to i+1 modulo m, and a b-loop at each vertex. The first m-1
a-edges form a spanning tree. The remaining a-edge gives r and the
b-loops give the s_i. Reading reduced loops after collapsing this
tree supplies mutually inverse maps between T and the free group on
these m+1 symbols. This proves the asserted free basis.

It follows that N is the normal closure of r inside T: the resulting
subgroup is also normalized by a, since conjugation by a fixes r and
normalizes T, and X=<T,a>. In the free basis above, K is the normal
closure in T of the m elements c_i=[r,s_i]. Imposing exactly those
commutators makes r central, and therefore centralizes the normal
closure of r; this proves the assertion about K.

Conjugation by a has the explicit formulas

    a r a^-1 = r,
    a s_i a^-1 = s_(i+1)                 (i<m-1),
    a s_(m-1) a^-1 = r s_0 r^-1.

Consequently it sends c_i to c_(i+1) for i<m-1 and sends c_(m-1) to
r c_0 r^-1. The inverse conjugation gives the reverse formulas.
These finite free-word identities provide another direct verification
that this normal closure in T is normal in X. The last conjugation
is essential: omitting its conjugating r would give a false identity
in the original free group, even though it becomes true in X/K.

## Relation to the earlier partial results and priority

The earlier sufficient conditions in `11.115-partial.md` are consistent
with this counterexample. N has infinite index, since X/N maps onto Z.
Moreover N contains no element primitive in X. Its image in X_ab=Z^2
is contained in mZ x {0}, whereas the abelianization of a primitive
element is a nonzero primitive vector. The quotient C_m*Z has torsion;
no torsion-free quotient hypothesis occurs in the supplied statement.

The question on printed and physical page 56 was visually checked.
It says N is not maximal in X, with no finite-index, primitive-element,
torsion-free-quotient, or nonnormal-T restriction. No interpretation
of an omitted qualifier is used in this answer.

The argument above is elementary and self-contained. Limited searches
for the problem number, Shaptala, proper powers and equal commutator
subgroups did not locate an explicit prior answer in the checked
material. This does not establish novelty. The recent Tursunbaev
theorem about equality of derived subgroups addresses a different
question and is not used. Given the short construction, priority and
the intended statement deserve explicit outside scrutiny.
