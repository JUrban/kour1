# Internal review of the coordinate criterion for 9.45

Completed 2026-09-11. This is an internal logical and computational
audit, not an independent external review. Historical priority is
pending. The claimed result is the explicit divisibility-and-count
criterion of `9.45-proof.md`, valid for every rational input vector.

## Logical audit

1. **Exact question.** The printed question on Notebook p.34 was
   visually checked. It asks for necessary and sufficient conditions
   in terms of coordinates; no polynomial bit-complexity requirement
   appears. The scalar product is the standard real one.
2. **Integer shifts.** Replacing a by a+z for z in Z^n leaves S(a)
   unchanged. Thus residue normalization is legitimate.
3. **Minimal denominator.** The gcd condition is essential to unique
   enumeration of the cyclic cosets. A Bezout identity supplies the
   exact order-m argument even when no individual coordinate is a
   unit modulo m.
4. **Dual membership.** Testing the e_j and a suffices because these
   generate S(a). Both parts of dual membership are imposed.
5. **Splitting equivalence.** The projection coefficient is integral,
   and subtracting that integer multiple leaves a lattice vector
   perpendicular to the proposed summand. The converse also holds.
6. **Pairwise compatibility.** Both projection coefficients between
   two splitting vectors are integers. Their product is the squared
   cosine, so distinct lines must be perpendicular.
7. **Same-line uniqueness.** Collinear splitting vectors have ratio
   t with t and 1/t integral, so they differ only by sign.
8. **Counting suffices.** If n mutually orthogonal summands are found,
   every vector has integer coefficients in their orthogonal expansion.
   Linear independence alone would not have established a Z-basis.
9. **Integer dual.** Z^n is a sublattice of S(a), hence every dual
   vector has integer coordinates. This is the key normalization fact.
10. **Reciprocal norm.** A splitting vector is c/d with c integral
    and d=||c||^2 a positive integer. No assumption of primitive c
    is made or needed here.
11. **Unit case.** d=1 gives exactly +/-e_j, present as splitting
    vectors precisely for zero residue coordinates. Such vectors do
    not occur in a nonzero cyclic coset and are added separately.
12. **Coordinate bound.** For d>=2, 2|c_j|<=d follows separately for
    |c_j|=0,1 and |c_j|>=2. Thus all other splitting vectors lie
    in the closed centered cube.
13. **Boundary count.** h half-coordinates imply h d<=4 and hence
    h<=2. Discarding larger tie sets cannot lose any splitting line.
14. **Boundary signs.** Every allowed sign pattern is examined before
    the first-positive filter. Examples with denominator 6 show that
    one sign pattern can pass while the other fails.
15. **Nonzero coset.** Norm squared 1/d<1 excludes a nonzero integer
    vector when d>=2. Every remaining summand is therefore included
    in the k=1,...,m-1 loop.
16. **No duplicates.** Different k give different classes modulo Z^n.
    Within one k, tie choices are distinct. Keeping first-positive
    vectors counts every line exactly once, without a deduplication
    assumption or accidental identification of perpendicular vectors.
17. **Divisibility formulas.** c=mw/Q and a.c=(r.w)/Q, so the two
    printed divisibility conditions are exactly the dual conditions.
    Q is positive for every listed vector by minimality of m.
18. **Complexity.** At most four candidates per coset, O(n) work per
    candidate. O(mn) arithmetic operations apply to the decision;
    dense output adds O(n^2). Dependence on numerical m is explicit.
19. **Input size.** No polynomial bound in log m is claimed. The
    direct test does not factor m. Factorization in the supplemental
    prime-power discussion is not an algorithmic requirement.
20. **Prime-power rank argument.** Cyclic quotient gives rank n-1
    modulo p by Smith form. The product-rank inequality gives rank
    at least n-2 for the diagonal Gram matrix, hence at most two
    nonunit rows when all row norms are powers of p.
21. **Support reduction.** Unit rows are distinct signed coordinate
    vectors; their congruences force the respective residues to zero.
    They are also orthogonal to every other row.
22. **Plane criterion.** An orthogonal integer pair is g(u,v),
    h(-v,u) with primitive (u,v). The first Smith invariant gives
    gcd(g,h)=1; since their determinant is a prime power, one is one.
    The primitive congruence kernel gives the stated unit multiple.
23. **Sufficiency in the plane.** The proposed rows satisfy the
    congruence and have the exact index p^k, so they generate the
    whole dual lattice. Duality then returns the primal basis.
24. **Composite restriction.** The 2 omega(m) support bound is only
    necessary. The explicit m=6 example disproves checking its
    primary intermediate lattices separately.
25. **Example correction.** The initial handwritten residue
    (3,1,2)/6 did not annihilate the stated rows. Independent Python
    searches detected the error. The corrected residue (1,5,2)/6
    gives dot products 0,-6,6 with the dual rows. The former is
    retained as a negative control; the original failed log is kept.
26. **Prior two-dimensional result.** Bardakov's n=2 theorem is
    explicitly credited. The present candidate is the general
    coordinate test; it does not reclassify the n=2 case as new.
27. **Prior method.** The normalized-dual integrality argument occurs
    in CGG2017 Claims 3.1--3.2. Orthogonal decomposition and uniqueness
    have classical antecedents. These are credited, not presented as
    new general lattice theory.
28. **No computational substitution.** The proof covers all inputs
    independently of the finite tests. Tests corroborate arithmetic,
    enumeration, sign handling, and explicit witnesses.

## Independent finite controls

`test_9_45.py` enumerates every primitive residue vector modulo
m up to multiplication by a unit, for these ranges:

| Dimension | Denominators | Cases | Orthogonal |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1--24 | 24 | 24 |
| 2 | 1--40 | 1,240 | 250 |
| 3 | 1--12 | 1,171 | 169 |
| 4 | 1--6 | 932 | 135 |
| 5 | 1--3 | 153 | 21 |

There are 3,520 cases: 599 positive and 2,921 negative. The orbit
coverage is checked independently against J_n(m)/phi(m), because
the unit action on primitive residue vectors is free. All 85
dimension/denominator counts agree in `9.45-coverage.json`.

The independent search enumerates all nonzero lattice vectors of
norm at most one, up to sign, using every coset in the larger cube
[-1,1]^n. It does not use splitting, dual vectors or the divisibility
criterion. This is a complete basis search: if b_i is in an
orthogonal basis, some standard e_j has a nonzero integer coefficient
on b_i, so 1=||e_j||^2>=||b_i||^2. Thus all possible basis members
are in the enumeration.

For scaled integer vectors w=m b, mutually orthogonal n-tuples are
bases exactly when their squared norm product equals m^(2n-2).
Indeed, m S(a) has covolume m^(n-1), and equal covolume of a
contained full-rank lattice forces equality. The recursive search
uses only orthogonality and this exact determinant condition.
It enumerates 93,238 short vectors across cases and visits 40,980
search nodes. Every verdict agrees with the coordinate criterion.
The separately derived prime-power test also agrees in 1,371 cases.

GAP constructs m S(a) by the actual integer generators m I_n and r.
It checks the Hermite normal form and determinant, witnesses each
of the 3,011 returned splitting-vector memberships with integer
coefficients, verifies dual membership against the HNF basis, and
checks both the candidate and independent basis against that HNF.
There are 1,198 complete basis equalities, two per positive case.

For 274 small cases GAP additionally enumerates the entire integer
box [-m,m]^n, checking lattice membership via its inverse HNF, and
performs its own orthogonal-tuple search. All 291,330 box points
produce the expected 2,698 short vectors, and every verdict agrees.
These cases are n=1 throughout; n=2,m<=10; n=3,m<=5; n=4,m<=3;
and n=5,m<=2, within the table's ranges.

The final GAP run has actual process exit 0, no errors or syntax
warnings, and `PASS_945_GAP`. Its 2.66-second result is bound to
the verifier, export, and Python controls in `9.45-gap-status.json`.
The initial GAP run reached the same completion counts but emitted
unbound-global syntax warnings from top-level closures. It was
rejected by the runner despite exit 0 and retained separately.
Wrapping the calculation in a function with explicit locals gave
the clean replacement. The failed Python expected-result check is
also retained; it is not passed off as a successful run.
