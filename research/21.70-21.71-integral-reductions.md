# Integral finiteness reductions for21.70 and21.71

2026-09-12. Conditional deductions and obstructions only. Both original
questions remain unresolved. No novelty or additional complete candidate
is claimed for the standard homological arguments below.

## 1. Duality descends once integral FP is known

**Proposition.** Suppose G is of type FP over Z, meaning that Z has a
finite resolution by finitely generated projective ZG-modules. If G is
an orientable PD_n group over Q and over F_p for every prime p, then G
is an orientable PD_n group over Z.

**Proof.** Put d=cd_Z(G). The FP hypothesis makes d finite and allows
a resolution P_* of length d by finitely generated projective modules.
Indeed, truncate any finite such resolution at its d-th syzygy; this
syzygy is projective and is generated by the image of the original P_d.
Let C^*=Hom_ZG(P_*,ZG). Every term is free as an abelian group: it is
a direct summand of a finite direct sum of copies of ZG. For every
field K there is a natural isomorphism

    C^* tensor_Z K = Hom_KG(P_* tensor_Z K,KG).

Here finite projectivity of each P_i is essential. Tensoring the
resolution with a field remains exact: all its syzygies are torsion-free
as abelian groups, so the short exact sequences are Z-flat sequences.

The top group M=H^d(C^*) is a finitely generated right ZG-module and
is nonzero. To justify the latter assertion when d>0, if the dual of
the last differential were surjective, it would split because P_d^*
is projective. Double duality for finite projective modules would make
P_d -> P_(d-1) a split injection. Removing this summand would shorten
the resolution, contradicting minimality of d. When d=0, P_0 is the
nonzero trivial module Z and its finite-projective dual is nonzero.

Since tensoring bounds the field cohomological dimensions by d, d>=n.
If d>n, rational base change gives M tensor Q=0. Thus M is an abelian
torsion group. Its finite set of ZG-generators has a common finite
exponent, so M has bounded exponent. For each prime p, the exact
sequence of complexes

    0 -> C^* --p--> C^* -> C^*/pC^* -> 0

gives M/pM=H^d(G;F_pG)=0, because C^(d+1)=0. A nonzero bounded
torsion group cannot be p-divisible for every p: its nonzero p-primary
part would be p-divisible and killed by a power of p, forcing it to be
zero. This contradicts M!=0. Therefore d=n.

For any i, the same long exact sequence gives

    0 -> H^i(C^*)/pH^i(C^*) -> H^i(G;F_pG)
      -> H^(i+1)(C^*)[p] -> 0.                       (1)

For 1<=i<=n, the vanishing in degree i-1 over F_p shows that
H^i(C^*) has no p-torsion. H^0(C^*) is torsion-free directly, being
a subgroup of C^0. Hence all H^i(C^*) for i<=n are torsion-free.
Rational base change now shows that H^i(C^*)=0 for i<n, while
H^n(C^*) is torsion-free of rank one. Its G-action is trivial, because
its embedding into H^n(C^*) tensor Q is injective and the rational
action is trivial by orientability. Finally H^n(C^*) is finitely
generated over ZG, as a top cokernel. With trivial action it is
finitely generated over Z, and so it is Z. This proves the proposition.

The proposition identifies the missing implication in21.70: integral
FP still has to be obtained from the actual hypotheses. It is not an
assumption supplied by the Notebook. Finite presentation alone also
does not supply the finite resolution used above.

Even a rank-one integral group is not determined by its field tensors.
For example, let M be the subgroup of Q consisting of rational numbers
with squarefree denominators. It is not cyclic, but M tensor Q=Q and
M/pM=F_p for every prime p. To see the latter, map x to px modulo p
in the localization Z_(p): px is always in Z_(p), the map takes1/p to1,
and its kernel is pM. Thus replacing the finite-generation step at
the end of the proof by only these field calculations would be invalid.
This abelian example is not a group-duality counterexample.

## 2. Euler characteristics agree under finite integral homology

**Proposition.** Let F1,F2 be fields and suppose that H_i(G;Fj) is
finite dimensional and vanishes for i>N, for j=1,2. If H_i(G;Z) is
finitely generated for 0<=i<=N, then chi_F1(G)=chi_F2(G).

**Proof.** Field extension does not change dimensions of homology over
the prime field, so it suffices to consider Q and finite prime fields.
Write r_i=rank_Z H_i(G;Z), and let t_i(p) be the dimension of the
p-torsion subgroup H_i(G;Z)[p]. Take t_(-1)(p)=0. The homological
version of (1), applied to a free integral chain complex for BG, gives

    dim_Fp H_i(G;F_p) = r_i + t_i(p) + t_(i-1)(p),  0<=i<=N.

Finite generation is what makes dim(H_i/pH_i)=r_i+t_i(p).
Moreover H_(N+1)(G;F_p)=0 implies t_N(p)=0, independently of any
finite-generation assumption in degree N+1. Alternating summation
therefore cancels the two torsion contributions and gives

    chi_Fp(G) = sum_(i=0)^N (-1)^i r_i.

The same sum computes chi_Q(G). Apply this separately to the two
prime fields of F1,F2. This proves the proposition.

Consequently a counterexample to21.71, with field resolutions of length
at most N, must have an infinitely generated integral homology group
in some degree i<=N. In particular it cannot be FP_N over Z. Since
FL over any field implies that G is finitely generated, its H_1 over
Z is finitely generated; the offending degree would have to be at
least2. Fields of the same characteristic always give the same Euler
characteristic, without the integral homology hypothesis.

Finite dimensional field homology by itself would not settle the
question. Consider the integral complex with C_0=Z,
C_2=direct_sum_(j>=0) Z f_j, C_3=direct_sum_(j>=0) Z e_j, other
terms zero, and

    d(e_j)=f_j-(j+2)f_(j+1).

The differential is injective by examining the smallest index in a
nonzero finite sum. Its cokernel is Q, under f_j ->1/(j+1)!.
Over Q this complex has homology Q in degrees0 and2, hence Euler
characteristic2. Over any F_p the same differential is injective and
surjective: the relation f_j=(j+2)f_(j+1), iterated until a multiple
of p appears, kills each f_j. Its field homology is just F_p in
degree0, with Euler characteristic1. This is an infinite complex,
not a finite group-ring resolution, and supplies no group of the
required FL types. It pinpoints why homology realization alone is
insufficient.

## 3. The2021 field-finiteness construction does not supply duality

Robert Kropholler's arXiv:2102.13509v1 proves FP_2 over all fields
without integral FP_2. It does not prove FP over all fields: after
Theorem6.1 it explicitly describes that as requiring further work.
There is also a direct obstruction to using that particular group
for21.70. The construction has vertex stabilizers
SL_3(Z)/Gamma(p)=SL_3(F_p) at every odd-prime height p. The matrix
I+E_12 has order p in this stabilizer. At height3 the matrix
diag(-1,-1,1) has order2. Thus the group contains torsion of every
prime order. Restriction of a finite projective F_pG-resolution to
C_p would give finite cd_Fp(C_p), contradicting the elementary
periodic resolution of a cyclic group of order p. The group therefore
has infinite cohomological dimension over every finite prime field.

The construction remains a concrete warning about integral finiteness;
it is neither a PD example nor an FL example of the required type.
No modification removing its torsion while preserving the needed
field properties has been established here.
