## Reduction audit

- No commutativity is used for infinite-order elements. Commensurability
  refers to actual cyclic subgroups, not conjugates of cyclic subgroups.
  Its transitivity follows from finite-index intersections inside the
  common infinite cyclic subgroup.
- A finite collection of commensurable infinite cyclic subgroups has a
  common infinite cyclic subgroup. Negative powers are allowed when
  expressing a chosen common element, and absolute values are used for
  the positive lengths. Equal powers force equal scaled lengths.
- Equal lengths and nonunique roots are permitted. Strict increase of
  the assigned positive exponents makes the scaled lengths strictly
  increasing even when lengths coincide.
- The finite-order reduction uses the global order restriction to show
  every prime factor of an element order exceeds n+1. Thus every exponent
  from1 through n is invertible modulo that order. This is essential for
  identifying the cyclic subgroup from any relevant nonidentity power.
- Separate cyclic factors represent finite-order elements from different
  cyclic subgroups. They need not model multiplication between the
  original elements; the proof uses only their individual powers.
- The identity, if present, is represented exactly once. Infinite-order
  elements are replaced by distinct elements of a fresh cyclic prime
  factor. After obtaining an exponent assignment in the finite abelian
  model, these dummy elements are discarded and the unused exponents
  are assigned by the length argument. No preservation of their dummy
  powers is claimed or needed.
- The construction works for groups of arbitrary cardinality and for
  mixtures of finite- and infinite-order elements. The finite abelian
  group may have arbitrarily large order; there is no catalogue cutoff.

## Certificate logic

- The explicit counterexample bound uses a graph of the selected
  two-variable relations. A spanning-tree elimination is legitimate
  because all coefficients1,...,n are units in the localized ring.
  Each nonzero remaining cycle has length at most its component size k
  and produces a difference of products smaller than n^k. Its cyclic
  torsion order divides that difference after small prime factors are
  removed. Free components are handled by the earlier counterexample
  transfer, not by reducing arbitrary rational numbers without controlling
  their differences. The separate dummy prime may equal a prime already
  occurring in a different factor.
- Every eligible finite abelian group has a unique module structure over
  Z localized at the primes<=n+1. Finite abelian groups suffice by the
  preceding reduction. No unsupported divisibility assertion about an
  arbitrary nonabelian group is used.
- A node refutes a tuple of distinct elements for which every permutation
  has a collision. At a branch, choosing one permutation gives a
  disjunction over every pair of coordinates. All pairs appear exactly
  once in the prescribed order in the independent checker.
- Each child has already been verified. Its variable map is checked to
  be a bijection. Every child relation is proved to follow from the
  parent relations and the selected collision. This implication is
  sufficient; exactness of the proposed quotient is not trusted.
- Leaves are accepted only when Smith-normal-form membership shows an
  actual equality between two distinct coordinate positions. The root
  must have no relations. Strictly increasing node identifiers make the
  proof acyclic and prevent a circular refutation.
- The verifier checks each Smith decomposition by matrix multiplication
  and both unimodularity determinants. Divisibility is tested after
  deleting only the inverted small prime factors. Free coordinates must
  vanish exactly.
- The Python generator uses weighted components and gcds; the GAP verifier
  uses integer presentation matrices and Smith decompositions. Thus a
  normalization or quotient error in the generator cannot silently be
  accepted merely because the same quotient routine is replayed.
- The initial experimental canonicalization compared labels too early,
  producing extra equivalent states. It was corrected before the final
  certificates were generated. The final n=4 and5 state counts agree
  with the original independent prototype's state counts; correctness
  depends on the verifier, not on that agreement.

## Exact independent controls

`verify_20_100_reduction.py` checks1,000 lists in the Klein bottle group,
including repetitions and arbitrary increasing positive exponent sets.
Three distinct elements sharing a common square are explicitly included
as a nonunique-root control. It also verifies400 mixed finite/infinite
cases in the product of the order1331 Heisenberg group and the Klein
bottle group. All69,760 exponent assignments in those cases are checked
for preservation of finite-power collisions, and the lifted assignments
have distinct actual powers.

Allowing order5 at n=4 produces exactly the expected C5 counterexample
with all four nonidentity elements. All24 assignments are independently
checked to fail. A deliberately false equality leaf is submitted to the
GAP checker and must be rejected; it is retained separately from valid
certificates. None of these negative controls is a counterexample to the
Notebook's actual hypotheses.
