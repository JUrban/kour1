# Internal audit of the candidate answer to 16.28(a)

Date: 2026-09-11 UTC. This is an internal audit, not an
independent review. The proof also gives a negative answer
to (b), but that part follows from existing class-product
results and is not counted separately as new coverage.

## Printed statement

The supplied PDF page 99 was rendered and inspected. It
says a field of positive characteristic, not specifically
an algebraic closure of a finite field. The subset in (a)
is closed; it need not be irreducible, contain the identity,
or be a conjugacy class. The latter condition is imposed
only in (b). The group is connected reductive, so SL_2 times
G_m qualifies. The construction also embeds as a closed
subset in SL_4 if a simple ambient group is desired.

The same questions occur in the
[20th edition, page 100](https://alglog.org/20tkt.pdf).
The supplied edition has no editorial solution annotation.
This does not establish novelty or rule out a historical
convention on the intended base field.

## Mathematical checks

1. In characteristic five, x^2-x+1 is separable. Therefore
   the trace-one determinant-one set Y is a single semisimple
   conjugacy class over the algebraically closed base field.
   Scalar adjustment of a GL_2 conjugator makes it an SL_2
   conjugator, with no change to conjugation.
2. Y is closed as a trace fiber. No nonclosed unipotent
   conjugacy class has been substituted for the required
   closed subset.
3. The missing element is -I, not I. In fact Y is inverse
   invariant, and I belongs to Y^2. If AB=-I then B=-A^{-1}
   has trace -1, proving the required exclusion.
4. The explicit factorization D_s=A_s B_s works for every
   s nonzero and different from -1, including s=1. No
   denominator is used at its zero. Each factor has trace
   one and determinant one exactly.
5. The regular semisimple set tr(C)^2!=4 is a nonempty
   Zariski-open subset of SL_2, not just an open subset of
   a torus. Diagonalization and conjugacy invariance put
   the whole set in Y^2.
6. The curve argument for -I in the closure does not require
   the factors A_s,B_s to extend to s=-1. Only their product
   D_s must extend, and it does. This distinction is central
   to the nonclosedness argument.
7. The scalar t in (a) is transcendental. It has infinite
   order in the algebraic closure of F_5(t). It is not an
   element of the algebraic closure of F_5.
8. The special element (I,t) commutes with every other
   factor. Hence the word decomposition by the number k of
   Y factors is an exact equality of product sets.
9. The fiber at t^{n-2} receives contributions only from
   k=2, because all relevant powers of t are distinct.
   Higher products Y^k may contain -I, but they occur in
   different closed fibers and cannot fill this missing point.
10. For every n>=2 the missing point is (-I,t^{n-2}). The
    proof does not merely check finitely many powers, and
    does not assume that the full sequence X^n is increasing.

## Relation to previous class-product results

Guralnick, Malle, and Tiep,
*Products of conjugacy classes in finite and algebraic simple groups*,
Advances in Mathematics 234 (2013), 618--652,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2012.11.005),
[author PDF](https://agag-malle.math.rptu.de/~malle/download/classprod.pdf),
Lemma 2.3(b), proves that the products of two split noncentral
semisimple classes in GL_2(q), under SL_2(q) conjugation,
realize every trace. The introduction, this lemma, and the
opening algebraic-group arguments of Section 5 were read.

This already implies a negative answer to (b) over the
algebraic closure of F_5. Put the two roots of x^2-x+1
in a finite subfield. For any desired trace, enlarge the
finite field to contain it as well and apply Lemma 2.3(b).
A regular semisimple determinant-one matrix is determined
up to conjugacy over the algebraic closure by its trace.
Thus Y^2 contains the regular semisimple open set. The
trace obstruction excludes -I. This is our explicit
inference from the existing lemma, not a claim that the
authors mention Problem 16.28. Accordingly (b) is treated
as a consequence of prior results.

For (a), the proposed additional construction is to retain
that nonclosed product in a distinct scalar fiber of every
higher power. Targeted searches combining Vdovin, 16.28,
closed subsets, product powers, algebraic groups, and
constructible semigroups found no previous occurrence of
this exact counterexample or resolution. These were
limited searches with many unrelated returns; they do
not prove novelty. The need for a transcendental base
field should be checked carefully against any historical
formulation before making a priority claim.

## Computational verification

The GAP script checks rational-function identities over
GF(5)(s), including both inverse identities, and specializes
them over GF(5), GF(25), GF(125), and GF(625). It also
checks the two distinct eigenvalues in GF(25).

The independent Python script enumerates every product
of two trace-one matrices in SL_2(F_p) for p=5,7,11,13,
and verifies the diagonal factorizations. It uses a finite
matrix base with an unbounded integer tag to multiply
all words up to length twelve and check every scalar
fiber against the exact set formula.

Finite-field product sets can be smaller than their
geometric counterparts. For example the square has size
95 in SL_2(5), while the ambient group has size 120.
No conclusion about Zariski density is inferred from these
finite counts. The proof works over an algebraic closure.

Initial GAP attempts hit generic inversion/determinant
dispatch limitations for mixed rational-function matrix
entries. The final script uses explicit two by two entry
formulas and checks the inverse identities themselves.
Completion requires PASS lines, a DONE marker, and no
GAP error text; exit code zero alone is insufficient.
