# Audit and prior-result implication for 13.19

## Mathematical audit

The direct-product construction in `13.19-proof.md` meets every printed
hypothesis. All four factors are D_8; H is an actual normal subgroup of Q
inside that product; both project onto the same factors. Neither a change
of embeddings nor a purely abstract isomorphism is being substituted for
the required simultaneous subdirect representations. The quotient is
finite, of order 8, and the regularity obstruction is explicit.

The supplied PDF page 67 was inspected visually. Its statement does not
exclude p=2. Gorchakov's 2009 Russian paper, page 801, also poses the
regularity conjecture with no odd-prime qualification. The choice of an
odd prime in his subsequent examples is not part of that conjecture.

GAP's `IsRegularPGroup` agrees with the standard Hall convention. Its
implementation returns `IsAbelian(G)` for a 2-group (citing Huppert), but
our argument does not rely on that classification: s,t directly violate
the defining power identity.

## Exact verification

- `scripts/verify_13_19.g` constructs D as a permutation group and Q,H as
  separately generated subgroups of D^4. It checks inclusion, normality,
  all projections, the proposed homomorphism and its kernel, quotient
  isomorphism, and an explicit failure of regularity. All checks passed;
  retained log: `results/13.19-gap-verification.log`.
- `scripts/verify_13_19.py` uses literal 3 by 3 binary matrix multiplication
  and independent finite enumeration. It checks all 65,536 products and
  homomorphism identities in Q, all 8,192 conjugations of H by Q, all
  1,024 products in H, all projections, and the eight quotient cosets.
  All checks passed; output: `results/13.19-matrix-verification.json`.

The proof itself is independent of software. No further brute-force
repetition is needed unless the construction or checker changes.

## Why this is not counted as a new resolution

K. Kearnes, P. Mayr, N. Ruskuc, *Solvable quotients of subdirect products
of perfect groups are nilpotent*, Bull. London Math. Soc. 50 (2018),
1016--1026, https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12196 , already supplies a negative
answer as a consequence of its construction. Sources read:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.02999v2 ;
- the author manuscript in the institutional repository,
  https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/15796/1/Perfect10.pdf .

Here is the implication, which is our inference from the cited statements,
not a claim that the paper explicitly names Notebook 13.19.

1. Section 6 constructs a finite perfect group G=HM, where M is a 2-group
   of upper unitriangular block matrices over F_4, and a normal 2-subgroup
   N contained in M. Lemma 6.1 gives [N,N] > [G,N,N].
2. Set d=2 in their construction from Section 3. Theorem 4.2 gives
   S=Gamma_1(G,N) <= G^4 and T=Gamma_3(G,N)=gamma_3(S), such that S/T
   is nilpotent of class exactly 2, hence nonabelian.
3. Every Gamma_k contains the diagonal G. Lemma 3.3 therefore makes both
   S and T subdirect, and T is normal in S by its lower-central definition.
4. Every non-diagonal generating factor of Gamma_1 lies in N^4, hence in
   the finite normal 2-group M^4. Since the diagonal G is contained in T,
   S/T is an image of S intersect M^4. Thus S/T is a finite 2-group.
5. A nonabelian 2-group is irregular, so this already disproves the
   regularity assertion under precisely the required subdirect hypotheses.

The paper's nonabelian quotient thus predates our example. We retain our
order-256 construction as a particularly small transparent certificate,
and the separate general realization argument in `13.19-universality.md`
as additional mathematical work, while conservatively excluding 13.19
from the count of previously unsolved problems resolved here.

The search also checked the exact problem number, Gorchakov with
regularity/subdirect/dihedral terms, and the SmallGrp identifier. No source
explicitly presenting this order-256 example was located. That limited
search does not prove that the smaller example is new.
