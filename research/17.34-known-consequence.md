# Problem 17.34 is implied by an existing amalgamation theorem

Status: positive answer as a consequence of prior work, excluded from the
new-candidate count. Recorded 2026-09-11. The explicit augmentation-module
proof developed in this session is retained in `17.34-proof.md`.

## Prior theorem

Christian d'Elbée, Isabel Müller, Nicholas Ramsey and Daoud Siniora,
[*Model-theoretic properties of nilpotent groups and Lie algebras*,
arXiv:2310.17595v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17595v3), Theorem 4.35,
proves existence of free amalgams for nilpotent Lie algebras of a fixed
class bound equipped with a Lazard series. Definition 4.14 includes
strongness: the images of the two algebras meet exactly in the common
subalgebra. The field is arbitrary. The theorem, its proof, Definitions
2.8--2.9 and 4.1/4.14, and surrounding scope were read. The full lengthy
inductive construction has not been independently audited.

Here a Lazard series is a descending sequence

    L=P_1 >= P_2 >= ... >= P_(c+1)=0, [P_i,P_j] <= P_(i+j).

It need not be the intrinsic lower central series. This distinction
allows a subalgebra to carry the series induced from its ambient algebra.

## Inference for the precise Notebook problem

Let G be any torsion-free nilpotent group of class at most c, and H a
divisible subgroup. Embed G in its rational Malcev completion D, and
let L=log D, h=log H. The standard Malcev/BCH correspondence gives a
rational nilpotent Lie algebra L of class at most c and a Q-subalgebra h.
The completion and correspondence facts are documented in `17.34-proof.md`.

Equip L with P_i=gamma_i(L), and h with P_i(h)=h intersect P_i.
This is a Lazard series on h, because

    [h intersect P_i, h intersect P_j] <= h intersect P_(i+j).

Take two identical copies of the filtered L, amalgamated over this
filtered h. Their filtrations agree on h by construction. For
finite-dimensional L, Theorem 4.35 supplies a class-at-most-c Lie algebra
P and two embeddings f,g:L -> P with common restriction to h and
f(L) intersect g(L)=f(h). In particular f(x)=g(x) holds exactly on h.

For completeness, only the finite-dimensional form of the theorem is
needed even when G has arbitrary rank. Add constants for two copies of
every element of L, their full Lie-algebra diagrams, equations identifying
the copies on h, and inequalities distinguishing their images at every
x outside h. Also impose the class-at-most-c Lie algebra axioms over Q.
Every finite subset is satisfiable: put all its named elements in one
finitely generated Lie subalgebra A of L. This A is finite dimensional
because L is nilpotent of fixed finite class. Put C=A intersect h, and
give A and C the filtrations induced from P_i(L). Apply the finite-dimensional
strong amalgamation theorem to two copies of A over C. Its target satisfies
all the chosen finite constraints. Compactness therefore supplies the
required two embeddings of all L, agreeing exactly on h, into one
class-at-most-c rational Lie algebra P.

The BCH group of P is torsion-free and nilpotent of class at most c.
The induced maps restricted to G agree exactly on H. Consequently

    dom_(N_c)^G(H)=H.

This application to the Notebook statement is our inference from the
published/pre-existing theorem. The cited theorem need not itself name
17.34. The inference uses no new amalgamation result, so this problem
is excluded from the new-resolution count.

## Further related sources

Shakhova's 2015 paper already explicitly resolves the c<=2 case; see
`17.34-review.md`. The amalgamation paper also cites Maier,
*Amalgame torsionsfreier nilpotenter Gruppen*, J. Algebra 99 (1986),
520--547. Its full text has not yet been read, so we do not assign it an
unverified precise implication here.

The same four authors' new paper,
[*Shirshov's amalgamated free product and generic nilpotent groups*,
arXiv:2609.05789v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05789v1), submitted
5 September 2026, simplifies their amalgam construction and develops
further torsion-free group applications. Its introduction and the
torsion-free application statements were screened. It is not needed
for the implication above, which uses their earlier theorem.
