# Problem 5.25: the metabelian case and an unresolved reduction

11 September 2026. Partial result only; no new-candidate or priority claim.

The question asks whether a nontrivial soluble linearly ordered group
has nonperiodic abelianization. Here linearly ordered means an order
invariant under multiplication on both sides. This investigation does
not assert the general answer or rely on a left-ordering result as a
substitute for that hypothesis.

There is an elementary affirmative argument when G is metabelian.
Suppose G/G' is periodic. For g in G choose a positive m with g^m in G'.
Conjugation by g on the abelian group G' has finite order dividing m.
It preserves the restricted linear order. An order-preserving
permutation of a linearly ordered set cannot have a nontrivial finite
orbit: an increase or decrease would persist around the whole orbit.
Thus g centralizes G'. Hence G' is central. For arbitrary g,h, choose
m with g^m in G'. The class-two commutator identity then gives
[g,h]^m=[g^m,h]=1. Ordered groups are torsion-free, so [g,h]=1. Therefore
G is abelian and periodic, and torsion-freeness forces G=1, a contradiction.

For a hypothetical counterexample of derived length d, every finitely
generated subgroup H has H/(H intersect G') finite, since that quotient
is a finitely generated torsion abelian group. Its normal finite-index
subgroup H intersect G' has derived length at most d-1. Thus it would
suffice to prove that a bi-orderable finite extension of a soluble
group of derived length d-1 has the same bound. This latter assertion
has not been proved here for arbitrary rank. In particular, quotients
by isolated abelian normal subgroups are not automatically equipped
with the required induced order, and that step cannot be used without
an additional theorem. No general induction is claimed.

For context, Linnell's 1999
[publisher abstract](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1112/S0024610799007462)
states local indicability for left-orderable elementary amenable groups.
This concerns finitely generated subgroups; it does not by itself
give the required conclusion for the whole possibly infinitely
generated group. Only the abstract was inspected, and no proof from
that paper is imported. No exact prior general resolution was found
in the limited discovery searches.
