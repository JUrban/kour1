# 21.55: a negative consequence of Hall–Higman

Status: complete deduction from a classical theorem, excluded from the
24 possible new resolutions. No claim of new priority or outside review.
The imported Hall–Higman theorem is not reproved here.

## Exact formulation

The supplied Notebook, printed page 175, was visually checked on
11 September 2026. For a prime power q=p^f, let m_n(q) be the largest
p-length among p-solvable subgroups of GL(n,q). The question asks whether
m_n(q)/log_2(n) tends to 1. The logarithm base is explicitly 2.
Both p and q are fixed as n tends to infinity.

The answer to this assertion as printed is **no**. For example,
for every fixed f>=1,

    limsup_{n -> infinity} m_n(7^f)/log_2(n) <= 1/log_2(7) < 1.

We do not assert existence or an exact value of this limit, and do not
replace the question by a proposed corrected normalization.

## Imported theorem and its scope

The odd-prime exponent part of Hall–Higman's Theorem A says:
if a finite group G is p-solvable, p is an odd non-Fermat prime, and
a Sylow p-subgroup has exponent p^e, then its p-length l_p(G) is at
most e. For odd Fermat primes the general bound l_p(G)<=2e is available.
There is no assumption of complete reducibility, faithfulness of a
specified representation, trivial p-core, or solubility at every prime.
Those are not to be imported from a different Hall–Higman theorem.

The original reference is P. Hall and G. Higman,
*On the p-Length of p-Soluble Groups and Reduction Theorems for
Burnside's Problem*, Proc. London Math. Soc. (3) 6 (1956), 1–42,
[DOI 10.1112/plms/s3-6.1.1](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s3-6.1.1).
The original full text was not obtained: the publisher PDF failed,
and access to the Math-Net translation failed. Thus the original
42-page proof has not been independently audited in this project.

Two accessible academic sources were checked for the precise statement:

- Fletcher Gross, *The 2-length of a finite solvable group*, Pacific
  J. Math. 15 (1965), 1221–1237,
  [publisher PDF](https://msp.org/pjm/1965/15-4/pjm-v15-n4-p11-s.pdf).
  Printed page 1221 defines p-length and Sylow p-exponent, then states
  both odd-prime Hall–Higman bounds with attribution. Pages 1221 and
  the beginning of 1222 were read. Gross's later 2-length proof was
  not audited and is unnecessary here.
- E. I. Khukhro, *Problems of bounding the p-length and Fitting height
  of finite soluble groups*, J. Siberian Federal Univ. Math. Phys.
  2 (2009), 258–270,
  [author manuscript](https://eprints.maths.manchester.ac.uk/2079/1/EART131.pdf).
  The introduction's definition and Section 2's Exponent Theorem,
  Theorem 1, on page 264 and its historical explanation on page 265
  were read. This is a survey statement, not a substitute independent
  proof. The repository cover is dated 2013; the article is from 2009.

## Elementary linear-algebra deduction

Let x be any element of p-power order in GL(n,q), with q of
characteristic p. If x has order p^a, then

    (x-I)^(p^a) = x^(p^a)-I = 0.

Consequently N=x-I is nilpotent. Any nilpotent operator on an
n-dimensional vector space satisfies N^n=0, for example by its
strictly increasing kernel filtration. Let e be the least nonnegative
integer with p^e>=n. The characteristic-p binomial identity gives

    x^(p^e) = (I+N)^(p^e) = I+N^(p^e) = I.

This proves that the exponent of every p-subgroup of GL(n,q) divides
p^e, with e=ceil(log_p(n)). The argument does not assume that all
elements of a p-subgroup share a Jordan basis.

Now let G<=GL(n,q) be p-solvable and let P be a Sylow p-subgroup
of G. Write exp(P)=p^a, allowing a=0 for the trivial subgroup.
The previous paragraph gives a<=e. If p is an odd non-Fermat prime,
Hall–Higman therefore gives

    l_p(G) <= a <= ceil(log_p(n)).

This is uniform in G, so the same bound holds for m_n(q). There
are finitely many subgroups of the finite GL(n,q), and hence its
maximum is defined. For n>1, division by log_2(n) yields

    m_n(q)/log_2(n)
        <= 1/log_2(p) + 1/log_2(n).

Taking upper limits proves the asserted bound. In particular, p=7
is odd and is not a Fermat prime: 7-1=6 is not a power of 2.
Since 7>2, 1/log_2(7)<1. This refutes the proposed universal answer.
An even shorter contradiction uses n=7^k, for which the ratio is
at most 1/log_2(7) for every positive k.

For completeness, the coarser odd-prime bound also gives

    limsup m_n(q)/log_2(n) <= 2/log_2(p).

Thus the printed assertion fails for every p>=5, including Fermat
primes. This note makes no conclusion for p=2 or p=3.

## What is and is not verified

The elementary deduction is proved above, with its separate logical
audit in `21.55-review.md`. No finite matrix search could establish
the asymptotic statement or replace the imported group-theoretic
theorem; none was launched. Public source URLs, exact reading scopes,
and cached-file hashes are retained in the audit record.

Limited exact-question searches did not locate a published discussion
of this particular normalization. That absence establishes no novelty.
The deduction is classified conservatively as a prior-theorem
consequence, not as a new Hall–Higman bound or a newly solved case
with a proved sharp asymptotic constant.
