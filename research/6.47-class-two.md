# Problem 6.47: a self-contained class-two special case

11 September 2026. Partial result, not a new complete candidate.

Let G be any group of nilpotency class at most two, finite or infinite.
Suppose the binary word v defines a group H on the same underlying set.
Then H belongs to var(G).

Use the convention [x,y]=x^-1 y^-1 x y. The original identity 1 is an
idempotent for v, hence is the identity in H. Collecting the word in a
class-two group gives v(x,y)=x^a y^b [x,y]^k for fixed integers a,b,k.
The two unit laws imply x^(a-1)=x^(b-1)=1 throughout G. Consequently

    x * y = xy [x,y]^k.

Conversely this formula is associative for every integer k: centrality
and bilinearity of commutators give, for both bracketings, the expression

    xyz [x,y]^k [x,z]^k [y,z]^k.

It has the original identity, inverses and integer powers. Every element
of Z(G) is central for *, and multiplication involving such an element
is the original multiplication. The quotient modulo Z(G) is abelian for
*, so H has class at most two. Comparing x*y and y*x gives

    [x,y]_H = [x,y]_G^(2k+1).

Now take any group identity w=1 of G, in finitely many variables x_i.
In the free class-two group, collect it as

    w = product_i x_i^(a_i) product_(i<j) [x_i,x_j]^(b_ij),

with a fixed order of factors. Substituting 1 for all variables but x_i
shows that x^(a_i)=1 is an identity of G. Substituting 1 for all variables
but x_i,x_j, and using those unary identities, shows that
[x,y]^(b_ij)=1 is also an identity of G. The unchanged powers transfer
each unary identity to H. The displayed commutator formula transfers
each of the binary commutator identities to H. The same collected
expression, now evaluated in H, is therefore 1. This proves that every
identity of G is an identity of H, which is precisely H in var(G).

No torsion, finite generation, exponent, or finiteness hypothesis was
used. The trivial and abelian cases are included. In particular this
argument does not require the two groups to be isomorphic.

## Source and scope audit

The original question is at printed page 20 of docs/21tkt.pdf. Cooper's
1974 chapter, *Words which give rise to another group operation for a
given group*, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 372, pp. 221–225, states the
same conjecture on p. 223. Its Theorem 1 on p. 222 already classifies
the group words when the original group has class at most three. The
argument above gives a direct class-two proof and carries no novelty
claim. The general problem remains open in this investigation.

Primary chapter: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/BFb0065173

All five pages of the chapter's mathematical text were read; pages
221–223 were also visually inspected. Its class-three classification
and general assertions are not needed as proof premises
above. We have not independently reconstructed all of its omitted
class-three calculations.
