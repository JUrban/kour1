# Internal audit of the 21.55 deduction

All checks below concern the written argument, not an outside review.
The original Hall–Higman proof remains an imported classical theorem.

1. The supplied printed page 175 was viewed. The denominator is
   log_2(n), and the question fixes a characteristic-p prime power q.
2. The theorem used concerns finite p-solvable groups, not only
   soluble groups or completely reducible linear groups. Gross's
   printed page 1221 and Khukhro's Theorem 1 agree on the needed
   non-Fermat odd-prime case.
3. The prime 7 meets the exact exception rule. No p=2 improvement
   or p=3 exceptional case is used in the negative answer.
4. A p-element is unipotent by (x-I)^(p^a)=0. Its nilpotence index
   is at most n without requiring an algebraic closure or a common
   simultaneous triangularization for the whole group.
5. Exponent means the least common multiple of all element orders.
   The same power p^ceil(log_p(n)) kills every element of P, so the
   exponent divides it. No claim that nilpotency class equals
   p-length is made.
6. The Hall–Higman estimate is uniform in the subgroup G. GL(n,q)
   is finite, so taking its finite maximum preserves the inequality.
7. The ceiling contributes at most 1/log_2(n), which tends to zero.
   A strict upper bound on the upper limit suffices to disprove
   convergence to 1; existence of the actual limit is unnecessary.
8. The scalar-field extension q=7^f causes no change in the
   characteristic-p binomial identity. One fixed q=7 already refutes
   the assertion as printed.
9. The optional statement for all p>=5 uses the separately cited
   coarser 2e odd-prime bound. Since p>=5>4, 2/log_2(p)<1.
10. The original theorem was not reproved; source reading scopes are
    explicit. The result is excluded from the 24 new candidates.
    No outside review or proof of novelty is claimed.
