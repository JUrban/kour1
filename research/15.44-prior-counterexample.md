# Problem 15.44(a): a classical Segre counterexample

12 September 2026. The literal question has a negative answer already
implicit in a standard example of a non-Cohen–Macaulay Segre ring.
This is recorded as a prior consequence, not a new candidate resolution.
The local Notebook page 88 was rendered and viewed: it permits a reductive
torus and a general affine variety, and explicitly includes characteristic
zero. No extra smoothness hypothesis is printed.

## Construction and all input hypotheses

Take K=C and let

    S = K[x,y,z,u,v]/(x^3+y^3+z^3),     X = Spec S.

Let G=G_m act on S with weights +1 on x,y,z and -1 on u,v. This
defines an algebraic action on X (using the inverse action on points if
the usual contravariant convention for coordinate rings is adopted).
The cubic relation is homogeneous for the weight grading, so the action
descends. The group G is connected reductive and its Borel subgroup is
B=G itself.

The polynomial z^3+(x^3+y^3) is irreducible over K(x,y): a reducible
cubic over a field has a root, but the valuation of -(x^3+y^3) at the
prime x+y is one and cannot be the valuation of a cube. Gauss's lemma
then gives irreducibility in K[x,y,z]. Thus S is a domain and X is an
irreducible affine variety.

Give every variable ordinary degree one, independently of the torus
weight grading. The algebra S is a homogeneous hypersurface and is
Cohen–Macaulay. More concretely, it is free over K[x,y,u,v], with
basis 1,z,z^2, and x,y,u,v is a regular homogeneous system of parameters.
We use the standard graded Cohen–Macaulay criterion: existence of such
a regular homogeneous system is equivalent to freeness over the
polynomial subalgebra generated by every homogeneous system of
parameters. Thus this also satisfies the explicit formulation in the
Notebook, not merely freeness for a specially chosen subalgebra.

The monomials x^i y^j z^k u^l v^m, with k=0,1,2, form a countable
weight-vector basis of S. Enumerate this basis and take its successive
finite-dimensional spans. This is an ascending exhaustive chain of
G-submodules with one-dimensional character factors. Since B=G,
each such factor V is exactly Ind_B^G V. Hence the precise filtration
condition in the question is satisfied.

## Invariants and an explicit failed parameter sequence

Write A=K[x,y,z]/(x^3+y^3+z^3) and P=K[u,v], with their ordinary
nonnegative gradings. The weight-zero subalgebra is

    R = S^G = direct_sum_{m>=0} A_m tensor_K P_m = A # P.

This equality follows directly from the weight decomposition. R is
generated by the six products

    a=xu, b=xv, c=yu, d=yv, e=zu, f=zv.

Indeed, any monomial of weight zero pairs each of its x,y,z factors
with a u or v factor. Give these six generators degree one.

Consider the three degree-one elements c,b,a-d. In their quotient,
the rank-one relation ad=bc implies a^2=d^2=0, since a=d and b=c=0.
The cubic relations e^3=-a^3-c^3 and f^3=-b^3-d^3 then give
e^3=f^3=0. Consequently R/(c,b,a-d) is spanned by the eighteen
monomials a^i e^j f^k with 0<=i<2 and 0<=j,k<3. In particular it
is finite-dimensional, and dim R<=3.

The elements a,c,b are algebraically independent. They are monomials
xu,yu,xv in the polynomial subring K[x,y,u,v] of S; distinct products
a^i c^j b^k have distinct exponent vectors (i+k,j,i+j,k).
Thus dim R=3 and c,b,a-d is a homogeneous system of parameters.

It is not regular. The element ef is nonzero modulo (c,b): in degree
two this says that z^2uv is not a linear combination of cR_1 and bR_1.
Every monomial in the latter spaces contains x or y, whereas z^2uv
does not. There are no relations in x,y,z-degree two coming from the
cubic defining equation, so these monomials are independent there.
But the polynomial identity

    ef(a-d) = e^2 b - f^2 c

shows that a-d kills this nonzero class. A Cohen–Macaulay graded ring
has every homogeneous system of parameters regular. Therefore R is
not Cohen–Macaulay, as required.

## Independent Hilbert-series calculation

The hypersurface Hilbert function is dim A_0=1 and dim A_m=3m for
m>=1; this follows by subtracting the degree-(m-3) multiples of the
nonzero cubic from the degree-m ternary polynomials. Also dim P_m=m+1.
Thus

    H_R(t) = 1 + sum_{m>=1} 3m(m+1)t^m
           = 1 + 6t/(1-t)^3
           = (1+3t+3t^2-t^3)/(1-t)^3.

The negative coefficient in the numerator gives a second obstruction:
if a linear parameter sequence were regular, its quotient would have
this numerator as its Hilbert series, which is impossible.

## Scope and provenance

The construction works unchanged over every algebraically closed field
of characteristic different from three, but the single choice C already
disproves a claim for arbitrary characteristic. It does not contradict
the Notebook's positive result for X a rational G-module: our X is a
singular cubic hypersurface, not a vector space representation.

The Segre example and failed regular sequence are classical. A source
audit is retained in `research/15.44-sources.md`. The invariant-ring
realization and verification of the printed filtration are elementary
deductions from that example. We claim no new priority and add zero to
the count of complete new candidates.
