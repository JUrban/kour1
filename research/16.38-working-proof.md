# Problem 16.38: working proof

Archived first draft, 12 September 2026. The reviewed proof is
`16.38-proof.md`; use that version for review.

Theorem: let G be soluble and A,B periodic subgroups. Every subgroup
contained in the set product AB is periodic.

Lemma 1. Every periodic soluble group is locally finite.
Proof: it suffices to prove that a finitely generated periodic soluble
H is finite, by induction on its derived length. In the abelian case
this is elementary. If D is its last nontrivial derived subgroup,
H/D is finite by induction. Schreier's theorem makes D finitely
generated; it is abelian and periodic, hence finite. So H is finite.

Lemma 2. Let C be a locally finite group acting on a torsion-free
abelian group D. If delta:C->D satisfies delta(cd)=delta(c)+c delta(d),
then delta(C) cannot contain both t and 2t for nonzero t in D.
Proof: embed D in V=Q tensor_Z D and extend the action. Suppose
there are c_1,c_2 with delta(c_1)=t and delta(c_2)=2t. The subgroup
H=<c_1,c_2> is finite. Set v=|H|^-1 sum_(h in H) delta(h).
The cocycle identity and permutation of the summands give

    delta(h)=v-hv for every h in H.

The rational span W of the finite orbit Hv is finite-dimensional and
H-invariant, and contains t. On W tensor_Q R, average any positive
definite inner product over H. The resulting norm is H-invariant.
Thus v,v-t,v-2t have the same squared norm. Expanding gives

    2<v,t>=<t,t>,       4<v,t>=4<t,t>.

Hence <t,t>=0, so t=0, a contradiction.

Lemma 3. Let D be a torsion-free abelian normal subgroup of any group
E. Suppose A,B are locally finite subgroups of E. Then D intersect AB
cannot contain both g and g^2 for nonidentity g in D.
Proof: use additive notation in D. Let pi:E->E/D, C=pi(A) intersect
pi(B), A_0=A intersect pi^-1(C), and B_0=B intersect pi^-1(C).
Since D is torsion-free, pi restricts to isomorphisms A_0->C and
B_0->C. Write their inverse sections as a:C->A_0 and b:C->B_0.
The subgroup pi^-1(C)=D A_0 splits via a. Write

    b(c)=delta(c) a(c),

so delta is a cocycle for the conjugation action of a(C) on D.
The group C is locally finite, being an image of A_0. If g lies in
D intersect AB, every factorization g=a(c)b(d) has cd=1, hence

    g=a(c)b(c)^-1=-delta(c).

Conversely every such element lies in D intersect AB. Thus
D intersect AB=-delta(C), and Lemma 2 excludes g and 2g together.

Theorem proof. It suffices to show that any g with <g> subset AB has
finite order. Induct on the derived length of G. If G is abelian,
AB is periodic since commuting finite-order elements have finite-order
product. In the nonabelian case let D be the last nontrivial derived
subgroup. The quotient G/D and the images of A,B satisfy the induction
hypothesis, so g^r lies in D for some positive integer r. Let T be the
torsion subgroup of D. It is characteristic in D and normal in G.
The image D/T is torsion-free abelian and normal in G/T. The images
of A,B are locally finite by Lemma 1. Both g^r T and g^(2r) T lie
in (D/T) intersect (AT/T)(BT/T). Lemma 3 therefore gives g^r in T.
This makes g finite-order and proves the theorem.

Scope: A and B need not permute, and AB need not be a subgroup.
Solubility is used for induction and local finiteness of periodic
subgroups. Nothing here proves the locally soluble version 9.65.
