# Problem 14.97: a finite covering diagnostic

12 September 2026. General problem unresolved. This note rules out a
tempting shortcut; it does not construct the requested infinite group.

A periodic locally soluble group is locally finite: a finitely generated
periodic soluble group has finite abelianization, hence finitely generated
derived subgroup by Schreier's lemma, and induction on derived length
makes the entire group finite. However, one cannot conclude that a finite
subgroup contained in a product of two finite p-subgroups must be a
p-group. There is a small counterexample to that auxiliary assertion.

Work with row vectors over F3. Put

    i = [[0,1],[2,0]],   j = [[1,1],[1,2]].

They generate a quaternion group Q of order eight: both square to -I
and ij=-ji. Every nonidentity element acts fixed-point-freely on F3^2:
the central involution is -I, and the six remaining elements have square
-I, so cannot fix a nonzero vector. Thus Q acts regularly on the eight
nonzero vectors.

Let G=F3^2 semidirect Q, of order 72, using affine maps x -> xA+b.
Let P be the linear complement Q and let P^t=t^{-1}Pt, where t is
translation by v=(1,0). Chronological multiplication of affine pairs is

    (A,a)(B,b) = (AB,aB+b).

For B in Q, an element of P^t has the form (B,v-vB). Consequently
the translations in PP^t are exactly v-vQ. Since vQ is the set of all
nonzero vectors, this is F3^2 with the vector v removed. It contains
the entire order-three translation subgroup

    H = {(I,(0,s)) : s in F3}.

The complements P and P^t intersect trivially, since Q fixes no
nonzero vector. Therefore |PP^t|=64, so PP^t is a proper subset of G.
This distinction is essential: H is contained in the product, but the
ambient group is not factored by the two 2-subgroups.

Exact Python arithmetic checked the eight matrices, the regular orbit,
all 72 affine elements and their products, the 64-element product of
the complements, and containment of all three elements of H. The
calculation exited zero (165dbd); its data are in
`results/14.97-affine-diagnostic.json`. An earlier S4 diagnostic checked

zero (d567fe). Neither computation answers 14.97. The remaining task
would require compatible embeddings and nested p-subgroups producing
an entire locally finite factorization, not just one finite covering.

For the easier finite covering with q=2 and any odd p, choose a
fixed-point-free cyclic p action on a finite vector space over F2.
The same construction makes any nonzero translation difference an
order-two subgroup contained in PP^t. This likewise does not supply
the infinite factorization required in the question.
