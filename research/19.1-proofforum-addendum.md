## Algebraic audit

For a finitely generated group G, the nongenerators for finite-index
generation form a characteristic subgroup lambda(G). Closure under
products follows by removing x then y from <S,x,y> when x,y lie
in lambda(G); inverses and automorphism invariance are immediate.

Every infinite-index subgroup lies in a maximal infinite-index
subgroup: a chain union of finite index would be finitely generated
by Schreier's theorem and hence already occur in the chain.
Zorn's lemma applies. This proves lambda(G)=mu(G), denoted psi(G),
by the witness-subgroup argument in the prior local report.

Take a finite-index RFRS subgroup H and an element g of infinite
order in G. Choose m>0 with x=g^m in H. In a separating RFRS
chain H=H_0>=H_1>=..., choose i with x in H_i but not H_(i+1).
The rational derived subgroup of H_i is contained in H_(i+1),
so x has nonzero image in H_i,ab tensor Q. Finite generation of
H_i supplies a homomorphism chi:H_i -> Z with chi(x) nonzero.
Its kernel L has infinite index in H_i and therefore in G.
But <L,x> has index [chi(H_i):<chi(x)>]<infinity in H_i, so
<L,g> has finite index in G. Thus g is a near generator.

If G is torsion-free, every nonidentity element qualifies, proving
psi(G)=1. Notice that L need not be finitely generated, and H
need not be normal in G. Neither is required in this argument.

## Topological input and exact reading scope

Nontrivial knot exteriors are compact orientable aspherical
3-manifolds with nonempty toroidal boundary. Their fundamental
groups are finitely generated and torsion-free. The unknot gives Z.

Przytycki--Wise, *Graph manifolds with boundary are virtually special*,
[author PDF](https://www.math.mcgill.ca/pprzytyc/jtopology.pdf),
Corollary1.3 on PDF page2 covers graph manifolds with nonempty
boundary. The first two pages, including the discussion of RAAG
embeddings and virtual RFRS, were inspected; the full proof is imported.

Przytycki--Wise, *Mixed 3-manifolds are virtually special*,
[author PDF](https://www.math.mcgill.ca/pprzytyc/JAMS.pdf),
Theorem1.1 on page319 covers compact connected oriented irreducible
mixed manifolds with arbitrary boundary. The introductory statement
and scope were inspected; the full29-page proof is imported.

For an explicit statement covering all boundary cases, including
hyperbolic knot exteriors, Aschenbrenner--Friedl--Wilton,
[*3-manifold groups*, author manuscript](https://friedl.app.uni-regensburg.de/papers/3-manifold-groups-v3.pdf),
Theorem5.25 on page60 says that every compact orientable aspherical
3-manifold with nonempty boundary has virtually special group.
Theorem5.23 through5.26 and their surrounding discussion were read,
and page60 was viewed. These are statements of prior deep results;
the entire book and their original proofs have not been audited.

Agol, *Criteria for virtual fibering*,
[arXiv:0707.4522](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.4522), Section2,
Definition2.1, subgroup inheritance, Theorem2.2 and Corollary2.3
were inspected. Corollary2.3 includes right-angled Artin groups
among virtually RFRS groups; its page4 was viewed. Intersecting
the RFRS chain with a subgroup preserves the defining rational
derived containment and finite indices. Therefore finitely
generated virtually special groups are virtually RFRS.

All hypotheses are met for every knot group. The preprint's
answer therefore covers both parts of19.1 without restrictions
on connected-sum or cable conventions. The older Allenby-based
proof remains useful and unchanged in `19.1-19.3-prior-results.md`.

## A further elementary consequence

The same argument proves that for every finitely generated
virtually RFRS group G, psi(G) is its largest finite normal
subgroup, even if G has torsion. Indeed no element of infinite
order can lie in lambda(G). An RFRS group is torsion-free:
any torsion element belongs to every rational derived kernel
along its RFRS chain, hence to the trivial intersection.
Thus lambda(G) intersects a finite-index RFRS subgroup trivially,
and |lambda(G)| is at most that index. It is a finite normal
subgroup. Conversely, for finite normal K and any k in K,
finite index of <S,k> implies finite index of SK and then of S,
since [SK:S]<=|K|. So K<=lambda(G).

This consequence is recorded with its proof and no novelty claim.
The restriction to finitely generated virtually RFRS groups is
essential to the stated audit; arbitrary residually finite groups
are not substituted for them.
