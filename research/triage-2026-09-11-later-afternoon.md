## 20.19: two commensurated subgroups

A discrete irreducible lattice in a product of two locally compact
tree groups suggests two subgroups obtained by intersecting with
K_1 x G_2 and G_1 x K_2, where the K_i are compact open and small
enough that their product meets the lattice trivially. These can be
infinite, disjoint and commensurated. This does not produce a free
or hyperbolic example without a further argument.

Rattaggi, *Anti-tori in square complex groups*, arXiv:math/0411547,
[primary repository copy](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/814240ed-60b8-4038-9564-26b85cc8dcfc/download),
was inspected in indexed Section 4 text: Lemma 23 and proof,
Proposition 24 and proof, and Problem 25. Its free anti-torus route
is explicitly an open additional condition there. The abstract also
exhibits a tempting quaternion pair that does not generate freely.
No generic-freeness assertion is justified by those passages.

*On the residual and profinite closures of commensurated subgroups*,
[primary repository PDF](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/431925/1/On_the_residual_and_profinite_closures_of_commensurated_subgroupspdf.pdf),
was inspected only in indexed Corollary 21 and proof, its following
paragraph and lattice example, and the beginning of Corollary 22.
The lattice construction does not establish hyperbolicity here.

*Greenberg–Shalom's commensurator hypothesis and applications*,
[primary publication page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S072308692500091X),
was inspected in indexed Proposition 3.1, the Section 4 and 5
introductions, and the beginning of Section 6 and Question 6.1.
Its use of the named commensurator hypothesis is conditional; no
proof of that hypothesis or answer to 20.19 is imported.

## 20.12: unique products

Naive induction from unique twofold products to unique n-fold
products is insufficient: an intermediate product-set element can
have several representations in the preceding original factors.
No theorem removing this obstruction was verified, and no general
conclusion is claimed.

## Other leads with no resulting general resolution

- 19.59: Mycielski's 1998 [primary paper](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/cm/cm75/cm75112.pdf)
  was inspected only in indexed Problems 3–5 on page 153; Problem 5
  is the rational three-dimensional affine free-action question.
  Sato 2002, DOI 10.1016/S0019-3577(02)80017-9, was read at primary
  abstract level only. Cyclic point stabilizers do not mean trivial
  stabilizers; a free sphere action does not directly give a free
  affine action on all rational points.
- 19.54: Konovalova–Monakhov–Sokhor, DOI
  10.1080/00927872.2021.1952213, was read at primary abstract level.
  Its simple examples satisfying the strict 2-maximal condition
  prevent an inference that all such groups are soluble. The Russian
  companion DOI 10.54341/20778708_2021_4_49_95 was inspected only in
  indexed abstract, introduction and definitions; no classification
  was audited.
- 19.44: the 2018 finite-group Engel-sink rank theorem needs a uniform
  rank bound; arXiv:2505.01409v1 was read only at primary abstract level
  and concerns finite cardinality of specified sinks. These do not
  answer the arbitrary rank formulation.
- 19.66: Plotkin 2020, [primary survey](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/8/10/1694),
  was inspected only in indexed Definition 5 and Problem 5 about
  isotypicity. It supplied no Burnside/Tarski-variety resolution.
- 19.79: no group with exactly one irreducible representation in
  every positive dimension was established. Assumptions about unique
  field embeddings or suitable simple subgroups of SL2(Q) were not
  justified.
- 21.61–62: only indexed introductory material in an
  [Oberwolfach report](https://oa.tib.eu/renate/server/api/core/bitstreams/c939347f-d9ef-45ba-9d04-3fc2e633ce7f/content)
  was read; a secondary abstract pointed to a 2023 effective-coherence

- 21.70–71: arXiv:2506.14666 was read at primary abstract level only.
  Finiteness or duality over all fields does not automatically imply

- 21.79–80: embedding countable left-orderable groups into finitely
  generated transformation groups does not show the new generators
  are distorted. No finitely generated all-distorted example follows
  from the construction inspected here.


## 20.75–76 and 20.89

Halasi–Podoski–Pyber–Szabo, arXiv:2305.02037v2, was cached from
https://zhalasi.web.elte.hu/papers/19.HPPSZ_PhiA_rang.pdf . Read:
opening definitions, Theorems 1.1 and 1.4–1.5, Example 1.3, the
Section 3 passage containing Questions 3.1–3.11, Lemma 3.6 and its
proof, and the proof of Theorem 1.9. Question 3.9 is exactly 20.75
and Question 3.11 is exactly 20.76. A single maximal abelian normal
subgroup of small rank does not bound the ranks of all abelian normal
subgroups: Example 1.3 explicitly illustrates the difference. The
main algebra bound and full group-rank proofs were not audited.

Shumyatsky, arXiv:1610.03126v1, was cached. Read: introductory
statements, Section 2 preliminary results, the Section 3 statements
and proofs, and Section 4 including the complete main proof. The
finite-by-hypercentral theorem requires every element to be almost
Engel. The finite-generation theorem requires virtual solubility.
Neither permits concluding that arbitrary almost Engel generators
in an arbitrary linear group generate an almost Engel subgroup.
No such hypothesis is silently dropped in interpreting 20.89.
