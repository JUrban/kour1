# Soluble p-Jordan groups have exponent at most two

12 September 2026. Partial result for 21.121(b), with no new complete
candidate count. The argument is an elementary deduction from finite
group structure. Priority is not established and outside review remains
pending. The optimal exponent-two affine family is in
[the first proof](21.121b-normal-sylow-proof.md).

**Theorem.** Suppose every finite p'-subgroup of Gamma has an abelian
normal subgroup of index at most J. If every finite subgroup of Gamma
is soluble, then Gamma is p-Jordan with exponent at most two. More
precisely, every finite G <= Gamma has a **characteristic abelian
p'-subgroup** B with

    [G:B] <= J^3 |G_(p)|^2.                              (1)

If every finite subgroup instead has a soluble subgroup of index at
most a fixed integer d, the bound d! J^3 |G_(p)|^2 holds with B normal
in G. Thus every virtually soluble p-Jordan group has exponent at
most two, independently of its soluble subgroup's derived length.

When Gamma is already p-Jordan, the hypothesis on p'-subgroups is
automatic from its defining constant. Linearity and finite generation
are not assumptions. Exponent two is attained and sharp, even for the
locally finite metabelian affine group over the algebraic closure of F_p.

## 1. The characteristic abelian subgroup lemma

We give the classical Chermak–Delgado argument in the precise form
needed. If a finite group X has an abelian subgroup of index at most
j, it has a characteristic abelian subgroup of index at most j^2.

For H <= X put m(H)=|H||C_X(H)| and let m be the largest such value.
For any H,K <= X, the product-size formula and centralizer inclusions
give

    m(H)m(K) <= m(H intersect K) m(<H,K>).               (2)

Indeed |H||K|=|HK||H intersect K| <= |<H,K>||H intersect K|,
whereas C_X(H) intersect C_X(K)=C_X(<H,K>) and
C_X(H)C_X(K) is contained in C_X(H intersect K). Multiply these
two inequalities.

If m(H)=m(K)=m, both groups on the right side of (2) also have
measure m. Hence the intersection D of all subgroups of measure m
itself has measure m. It is characteristic in X. Moreover C_X(D)
has measure m, because D <= C_X(C_X(D)) and no measure exceeds m.
The minimality of D among groups of measure m therefore gives
D <= C_X(D). Thus D is abelian.

If A is the given abelian subgroup, then

    |X| |D| >= m(D) >= m(A) >= |A|^2 >= |X|^2/j^2.

It follows that [X:D] <= j^2, as asserted. This construction is
characteristic but need not lie in A. Below X itself is a p'-group,
so D necessarily has p'-order; we never apply the lemma to an
arbitrary mixed-order group to infer a p'-subgroup.

## 2. A finite soluble group with O_(p')=1

Let R be a nontrivial finite soluble group with O_(p')(R)=1, and assume
its p'-subgroups have Jordan constant at most J. Its Fitting subgroup
F=F(R) is a nontrivial p-group. Indeed every Sylow subgroup of the
nilpotent characteristic subgroup F is normal in R, and a nontrivial
Sylow subgroup for a prime other than p would lie in O_(p')(R).
Nontriviality follows from a minimal normal subgroup of soluble R.

We recall why C_R(F) <= F. Set C=C_R(F); this is normal in R and
C intersect F=Z(F). If C/Z(F) were nontrivial, it would contain a
minimal nontrivial R/Z(F)-normal subgroup D/Z(F), which is elementary
abelian since R is soluble. Then D is normal in R, D/Z(F) is abelian,
and Z(F) is central in D because D <= C. Hence D is nilpotent of class
at most two, forcing D <= F, a contradiction to D>Z(F)=C intersect F.
This uses the usual Fitting theorem that the finite product of normal
nilpotent subgroups is nilpotent.

Choose a Hall p'-subgroup H of R. Its existence in a finite soluble
group follows by induction from Schur–Zassenhaus: take an elementary
abelian minimal normal subgroup M and lift a Hall p'-subgroup of R/M.
If M is a p'-group the lift is already p'; if M is a p-group, split
it off using Schur–Zassenhaus. The lifted subgroup has p-power index.

Choose A normal abelian in H with [H:A] <= J. The action of H, and
hence of A, on F is faithful, since C_R(F) <= F and H intersect F=1.
The abelian coprime automorphism lemma from
[Section 1 of the first proof](21.121b-normal-sylow-proof.md) gives

    |A| <= |F/Phi(F)|-1 <= |R_(p)|-1.

Consequently

    |R|=|R_(p)||H| <= J |R_(p)| (|R_(p)|-1).            (3)

No normality of a Sylow p-subgroup of R was assumed. In particular
the argument applies to S4 at p=2.

## 3. Removing the p'-radical

Now let G be any finite soluble subgroup of Gamma and put

    N=O_(p')(G),       R=G/N.

The group N is characteristic in G and has order prime to p. The
group R has O_(p')(R)=1: the preimage of a normal p'-subgroup of R
would be a normal p'-subgroup of G larger than N.

The same Jordan constant J works for p'-subgroups of R. For any such
H, its preimage in G is a p'-group, because N is p'. An abelian normal
subgroup of that preimage of index at most J maps to one in H of
index at most J. Also |R_(p)|=|G_(p)|.

Apply Section 1 to N. The hypothesis on Gamma supplies an abelian
normal subgroup of N of index at most J. Thus N has a characteristic
abelian subgroup B with [N:B] <= J^2. Since N is characteristic in G,
B is characteristic in G. Its order is prime to p.

If R != 1, combine (3) with this bound to get

    [G:B] <= J^3 |G_(p)| (|G_(p)|-1) < J^3 |G_(p)|^2.

If R=1, the bound [G:B] <= J^2 <= J^3 |G_(p)|^2 is immediate,
since J>=1. This proves (1), with its characteristic conclusion.

## 4. A bounded index of solubility

Suppose a finite G <= Gamma has a soluble subgroup S of index at
most d. Its core R=intersection_(g in G) S^g is a soluble normal
subgroup with [G:R] <= d!, by the coset action. Section 3 applied to
R produces B characteristic in R, hence normal in G, with

    [G:B]=[G:R][R:B] <= d! J^3 |R_(p)|^2
                           <= d! J^3 |G_(p)|^2.

The index d is uniform by hypothesis, so this is a p-Jordan bound at
exponent two. If Gamma has a soluble subgroup of finite index d,
intersect it with each finite G to obtain the required hypothesis.

The unrestricted 21.121(b) also permits finite subgroups with no
uniformly bounded soluble index. That case is not addressed here.
The only substantial classical finite-group inputs above are
Schur–Zassenhaus and the Fitting theorem; the characteristic-subgroup,
faithful-action, quotient, and quantitative deductions are supplied.
