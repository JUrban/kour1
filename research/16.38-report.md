# Problem 16.38: completion report

12 September 2026. Complete affirmative candidate, pending independent
review and further priority checking. The candidate says that a subgroup
contained in AB is periodic whenever G is soluble and A,B are periodic.
Such a subgroup is consequently locally finite as well.

The proof reduces by derived-length induction to a torsion-free abelian
normal subgroup D. The two periodic factors give sections over the
intersection of their quotient images. Their difference is a cocycle
of a locally finite group, and D intersect AB is the negative of its
image. On a suitable finite subgroup the cocycle is a coboundary.
An invariant positive inner product excludes both t and 2t from that
image for any nonzero t. Factoring out the torsion of D completes
the induction.

This uses no assumption that AB is a group, that A and B permute,
or that any of the groups are finitely generated. An explicit infinite
direct-sum example shows why an individual element of AB can still have
infinite order. The proof and 15-point internal review are in
`16.38-proof.md` and `16.38-review.md`.

## Exact controls and process evidence

`python3 scripts/check_16_38.py` tests four finite rational matrix
actions (C3,S3,C4,D8), conjugated to nonorthogonal coordinates. For
49 rational vectors per action it checks 6,125 cocycle pairs, 6,125
independently multiplied affine pairs, the averaged inner product, and
the exact translation set in AB. The average cocycle vector is
reconstructed and checked at every group element.

Eight finite truncations of the infinite sign-action example check
87,380 further cocycle/product pairs. A module over F3 deliberately
violates the doubling obstruction, confirming the role of
torsion-freeness. A written Z->Q cocycle illustrates failure without
local finiteness. These checks are finite corroboration, not a substitute
for the proof. The child and outer runner actually exit zero with clean
