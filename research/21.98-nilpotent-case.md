# 21.98: the locally finite-by-nilpotent case

12 September 2026. Partial result; no new complete candidate. The full
question for arbitrary groups and multilinear commutator words remains
unresolved here. No novelty claim is made for the following consequence
of the classical nilpotent embedding theorem.

**Theorem.** Let w be any group word. Suppose every finitely generated
subgroup of G is finite-by-nilpotent. If the w-values in G are contained
in finitely many cyclic subgroups, then w(G) is finite-by-cyclic. If G
is torsion-free and locally nilpotent, then w(G) is cyclic.

Here finite-by-nilpotent means having a finite normal subgroup with
nilpotent quotient. There is no bound on the nilpotency classes of the
finitely generated subgroups. The conclusion allows a finite w(G).

## 1. Finite generation

Write the covering cyclic subgroups as C_1,...,C_m and let S be the set
of w-values. Put B_i=<S intersection C_i>. Each B_i is cyclic and is
generated by a finite subset S_i of S intersection C_i: a generator of
B_i, if nontrivial, is a finite product of elements of that intersection
and their inverses. Thus w(G)=<S_1 union ... union S_m> is finitely
generated. In particular, we can choose a finitely generated subgroup
G_0 of G containing all entries of word tuples producing these finitely
many values. Then w(G_0)=w(G). The finite cyclic coverage is inherited
by G_0, by intersecting each C_i with G_0.

## 2. A polynomial path lemma

Let A be a finite-dimensional associative unital Q-algebra and J a
nilpotent ideal, J^s=0. Suppose L is any subgroup of 1+J and its w-values
are covered by finitely many cyclic subgroups of L. Then all logarithms
of w-values lie in one rational line in J, unless every value is 1.

We use the finite polynomial expressions

    log(1+u) = sum_(k=1)^(s-1) (-1)^(k+1) u^k/k,
    exp(v)   = sum_(k=0)^(s-1) v^k/k!,
    a^t      = exp(t log(a)).

The exp and log expressions are inverse, and a^t agrees with the group
power when t is an integer, including negative integers. These are
identities in the one-variable truncated rational power-series algebra,
so their use requires no commutativity between distinct elements of J.

Choose any two tuples (a_1,...,a_d) and (b_1,...,b_d) in L^d. Define

    X_j(t) = a_j^(1-t) b_j^t,
    F(t)   = log(w(X_1(t),...,X_d(t))).

Every coefficient of F in a fixed Q-basis of J is a rational polynomial
in t. Products preserve this property, and inverses in 1+J are given by
the finite geometric series. For every integer n, X_j(n) belongs to L,
so F(n) is the logarithm of a w-value. If the covering subgroups are
<c_1>,...,<c_m>, then F(n) belongs to the union of the lines
Q log(c_i), allowing the zero subspace when c_i=1.

One line contains F(n) for infinitely many integers n. Every rational
linear functional annihilating that line therefore annihilates F(t)
identically, since a nonzero polynomial has only finitely many roots.
Consequently F(t) lies in that line for all rational t. In particular,

    F(0)=log(w(a_1,...,a_d)),
    F(1)=log(w(b_1,...,b_d))

are linearly dependent. If there is a nonidentity w-value z, fix it as
the first endpoint and vary the second. All logarithms of w-values
then lie in Q log(z). Hence w(L) is a subgroup of

    {exp(q log(z)) : q in Q},

which is isomorphic to the additive group Q. By Section 1, w(L) is
finitely generated, and every finitely generated subgroup of Q is
cyclic. This proves the lemma and the cyclic conclusion for L.

The interpolation does not assert that w is multiplicative in a
variable, or that powers of a w-value are again w-values. It works
for arbitrary words using only powers of the input elements.

## 3. The classical embedding input and the conclusion

For a finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group N, Jennings1955,
Theorem 5.2, gives an integer s such that

    N intersection (1+Delta^s) = {1}

inside Q[N], where Delta is the augmentation ideal. Thus N embeds in
1+J for A=Q[N]/Delta^s and J=Delta/Delta^s. The algebra A is finite
dimensional: differences g_i-1 for finitely many group generators
generate it, their inverses have finite geometric expansions, and only
words of length less than s are needed to span A. This is the exact
classical input; its proof remains imported. The source was already
used and scoped in `research/14.26-sources.md`.

Now take G_0 from Section 1. By hypothesis it has a finite normal
subgroup E with nilpotent quotient G_0/E. A finitely generated nilpotent
group has a finite torsion subgroup and torsion-free quotient. Let M
be its full inverse image in G_0. Then M is finite and normal, and
N=G_0/M is finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent. The images of
the original cyclic covering subgroups still cover the w-values of N.
The embedding and Section 2 show that w(N) is cyclic. Since

    w(N) = w(G_0) M/M,

the subgroup w(G)=w(G_0) has finite normal subgroup w(G_0) intersection M
and cyclic quotient. This proves the first assertion. If G is
torsion-free and locally nilpotent, G_0 itself is torsion-free nilpotent,
so M=1 and w(G) is cyclic.

For clarity, the finite-torsion fact used above is the standard theorem
for finitely generated nilpotent groups; it is also recalled on printed
page 171 of the retained Jennings source. It is not asserted for
arbitrary nilpotent groups without finite generation.

## 4. Relation to the general problem

Cutolo--Nicotera2010, Lemma 1.1, already shows that for an arbitrary
group and word under finite cyclic coverage, w(G) is finitely generated
and contained in the FC-center, and its torsion elements form a finite
subgroup. Removing that subgroup makes w(G) free abelian of finite
rank with finite conjugation action. This is a prior reduction, not a
new answer. The missing general step is to show that rank is at most
one for multilinear commutator words.

Our polynomial path requires the torsion-free nilpotent embedding.
It has not been extended to an arbitrary group with central or abelian
verbal subgroup. In particular, no such embedding is inferred just
from the preceding FC reduction. Cutolo--Nicotera's Theorem C already
gives a stronger conclusion for lower central words in the locally
finite-by-nilpotent class. The theorem here supplies a uniform argument
for every word with the weaker finite-by-cyclic conclusion.

## 5. A concrete obstruction used for controls

For w=[[x_1,x_2],[x_3,x_4]], in UT_6(Z) take
a_j=I+E_(j,j+1) and b_j=I+E_(j+1,j+2), j=1,2,3,4.
Then w(a)=I+E_15 and w(b)=I+E_26. These are independent logarithmic
directions. The polynomial path above connects them, so its integer
values alone require infinitely many cyclic covering subgroups.
This is an explicit example of the obstruction used in the proof,
not a counterexample to 21.98: it fails that problem's finite-cover
hypothesis.

Exact polynomial multiplication gives

    F(t) = (1-t)^4 E_15 + t^4 E_26
           + (t-4t^2+7t^3-6t^4+2t^5) E_16.

For integers t>=2 the ratio of the E_26 and E_15 coefficients is
(t/(t-1))^4, which is strictly decreasing. Thus this single path has
infinitely many distinct rational logarithmic directions. Both native
GAP and independent rational polynomial matrices verify the displayed
identity; the saved Python control also evaluates 100 distinct such
directions. This explicit identity is supplementary to Sections1-3.
