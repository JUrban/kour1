# Internal review of the algorithm for Problem 16.9

Status: complete internal audit; outside review and further priority checks
pending. 11 September 2026. The candidate is affirmative and constructive.

## Statement and algebraic audit

1. The supplied Notebook's printed page 97 was read visually. It fixes a
   free basis, allows inverse letters, and asks for the minimum number of
   palindromes in a group product. Cancellation between factors is allowed.
   The proof treats exactly this definition and all finite ranks.
2. The map tau in the proof is an automorphism, not word reversal or the
   map taking every group element to its inverse. On a reduced word it
   changes every letter's sign while keeping the order. The equation
   tau(p)=p^-1 characterizes palindromes because reduced words are unique.
3. The embedding in the free product of n+1 involutions is an extension,
   not the quotient obtained by imposing x_i^2=1 in the original group.
   The presentation change has an explicit inverse. In particular no
   information about the input element is lost.
4. All nonidentity involutions of this free product are conjugates of
   individual generators, by cyclic reduction. All have odd parity.
   Consequently every reflection is uniquely p t for a palindrome p.
   This surjectivity is needed for the reverse length inequality.
5. The alternating applications of tau in the factorization formula
   cannot be omitted. The final t-power cancels because the number of
   reflections has the parity of the selected endpoint w or wt. Both
   directions of the minimum formula hold, including the empty word.
6. Identity palindromes may be used in the inequalities, but a minimum
   output for a nonidentity input cannot contain one. Its removal would
   contradict the already proved equality of the two minima.

## Pairing and algorithm audit

7. Pairs must have equal involution labels and be noncrossing. Pairing
   opposite signed letters in the original alphabet would compute a
   different quantity. The encoding first converts each free letter to
   two involution letters.
8. The adjacent-cancellation lemma covers all partner cases, including
   two different external partners. Joining those partners preserves
   noncrossing because the deleted positions are adjacent. The lemma
   gives invariance, not just monotonicity, so initially unreduced
   encoded words are permitted.
9. The upper and lower bounds for reflection length are distinct
   arguments. Conjugate words supply pairings. In the opposite direction,
   deleting the unmatched letters leaves a fully paired word equal to
   the identity and supplies explicit conjugate factors. There is no
   appeal to a finite quotient to prove minimality.
10. In the interval recurrence the first position is either unmatched
    or paired with one later equal label. Noncrossing separates the
    interior and following intervals, so the recurrence covers every
    pairing. Its cubic number of operations and quadratic table size
    are bounds in the input word length, uniformly in the rank. Stored
    costs are bounded by 2L+1 and need O(log(L+1)) bits each.
11. For witness recovery, the prefix for the next deletion omits all
    previously deleted letters. The implementation freely reduces this
    prefix in the involution alphabet; conjugation is unchanged. The
    even-word decoding toggles parity at zero letters as well as at
    nonzero letters. The alternating tau correction then gives actual
    factors in the original free group.

## Completed exact controls

Both verification processes were observed to exit with status zero.
The mathematical theorem rests on the proof, with the following controls
checking the implementation and its conventions.

| Control | Exact completed coverage |
|---|---|
| Independent deletion-subset enumeration | All 9,841 words of lengths 0 through 8 on three involution labels, including unreduced words |
| Reduced free-word enumeration | All 13,121 rank-two words through length 8 and all 23,437 rank-three words through length 6 |
| Separate two-palindrome criterion | All 36,558 reduced words, using direct enumeration of Frid's reduced form A P Q A^-1 |
| Known exact families | 20 words of distinct generators, with a sharp mod-2 lower bound; 20 members of Saarela's family with length three |
| Longer witness controls | 100 fixed-seed random words obtained from 40 letters in rank three |
| Independent GAP free-group replay | All 36,698 witnesses, with 310,658 total palindrome letters, in an actual free group of rank 22 |

The Python checks also verify literal palindrome factors, their reduced
forms and product, the mod-2 abelianization lower bound, and the endpoint
reflection parities. The two reflection lengths differ by exactly one:
each endpoint is the other times the reflection t, so their difference
is at most one, while their parities differ. GAP independently checks
the literal normal forms, products and parity lower bounds. It does not
independently prove minimum length above the parity bound. Minimality
for arbitrary inputs follows from the proof, and the separate deletion
and two-palindrome controls test that proof's computational components.

The retained literal witnesses make the GAP replay independent of running
the Python algorithm again. `scripts/summarize_16_9.py` checks completion
markers, counts, exhaustive input coverage and artifact hashes. Its report
is `results/16.9-summary.json`. It does not replace either verification run.

## Literature and reading scope

The reflection deletion characterization is due to Matthew J. Dyer,
*On minimal lengths of expressions of Coxeter group elements as products
of reflections*, Proceedings of the AMS 129 (2001), 2591–2595,
[DOI 10.1090/S0002-9939-01-05876-2](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-01-05876-2).
The AMS full-paper request returned HTTP 403; that proof was not read.
The exact theorem is reproduced as Theorem 1.20 in Marco Lotz,
*Powers of Coxeter elements with unbounded reflection length*,
[arXiv:2406.05218v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05218v1).
Its introductory definitions, Theorems 1.19–1.20, adjacent examples,
the deletion-set definition, and the algorithm discussion after
Remark 2.16 were read. The present proof supplies the entire special
free-product argument and imports no unaudited general Coxeter theorem.
Neither the deletion principle nor interval dynamic programming is
claimed as new.

Anna E. Frid, *Small palindromic lengths in free groups and word equations
with antimorphisms*, [arXiv:2512.10024v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10024v1),
discusses the algorithm question and characterizes the cases of at most
two and three palindromes. The introduction, definitions, both theorem
statements, the initial word-equation lemmas, and the full proof of
Theorem 1 were read. The long proof of Theorem 2 was not audited and
is not used in the controls. Its overbars must not be reconstructed
from text extraction, which loses some of them.

Aleksi Saarela, *Palindromic Length in Free Monoids and Free Groups*,
[author's institutional PDF](https://www.utupub.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/162254/palindromes.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1):
the introduction and definitions were read directly. They distinguish
group factorization from monoid factorization and propose further
algorithmic work. The special family in the controls was checked via
Frid's Example 1; Saarela's full paper was not audited.
