# Problem 19.61: G2 over Z/9 via its congruence kernel

12 September 2026. Initial experiment plan, amended during its proof audit.

The additive subgroups are 0, 3R and R. Derive every carpet condition
from actual integral root matrices modulo nine, and enumerate all valid
assignments. Reduction modulo three gives one of the 217 already checked
prime-field carpets. If every root has unit parameters then the original
carpet is already full; handle that single case directly.

For each other reduced carpet, let H0 be generated by x_r(1) for its
full roots, and let B be its matrix image modulo three. All these B
have order at most 5,832. Enumerate B completely, keeping one lift and
its inverse in H0 for each element. Every edge from a representative
by a root generator yields a discrepancy in the congruence kernel:
the inverse of the selected target representative times the edge lift.

The kernel of GL14(Z/9) -> GL14(F3) consists of I+3X. Multiplication
there is addition of X. Span the edge discrepancies over F3 and check
that this space is invariant under all full-root generators and contains
their third powers. The cosets of this space represented by the chosen
lifts are then closed under multiplication by every generator. Thus
they exhaust H0, proving that the space is exactly its kernel. This
argument also handles the precise orientation of the discrepancies;
it does not assume they are un-conjugated Schreier generators. No
smooth-group or Lie-algebra dimension theorem is needed.

For an arbitrary Z/9 carpet reducing to B, adjoin its x_r(3) generators
and close their kernel vectors under conjugation by H0. This gives
precisely the full congruence kernel W of its generated group H. Indeed
the extra generators lie in the abelian reduction kernel; their normal
closure in H is their H0-invariant linear span together with H0's kernel.

The complete modulo-three enumeration confirms that a unit root
parameter occurs in B exactly for the initial full roots. Consequently
no other root can acquire a unit parameter in H. Each remaining root
acquires 3R exactly when (x_r(3)-I)/3 belongs to W. This determines every
root closure by exact finite linear algebra. Check it against every
original carpet implication. A failure needs a separate certificate
of both positive memberships and the required negative membership.

Matrix products use a small native routine for moduli three and nine,
compared with a pure Python implementation on 128 fixed-seed dense
products and every root group law. The matrix model and both prior
carpet enumerators remain frozen. Store all actual process exits,
complete enumeration counts, kernel bases and source bindings. Any
state cap is a failure, never an accepted bounded result.
