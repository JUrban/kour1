# Characteristic subgroups and preservation of p-Jordan exponents

12 September 2026. Supplemental deductions for 21.121. These are
partial structural results, not another complete Notebook resolution.
Priority and outside review are unestablished.

Fix a prime p. Call a real number e admissible for Gamma if there is
a constant J>=1 such that every finite G<=Gamma has an abelian normal
p'-subgroup A with [G:A]<=J|G_(p)|^e. The arguments also work if, by
convention, admissible exponents are restricted to nonnegative or
positive numbers. We explicitly retain the finite constant needed
for negative exponents below, so that no convention about zero or
negative exponents affects the permanence assertions.

## 1. A characteristic form with the same exponent

**Theorem 1.** If e is admissible with constant J, then every finite
G<=Gamma has a characteristic abelian p'-subgroup D with

    [G:D] <= J^3 |G_(p)|^e.                              (1)

Indeed, set N=O_(p')(G), the largest normal p'-subgroup of G. It is
characteristic. The given A lies in N, so

    [G:N] <= [G:A] <= J |G_(p)|^e.

The group N is itself a finite p'-subgroup of Gamma. Applying the
original bound to N, whose Sylow p-subgroup is trivial, gives an
abelian normal subgroup of N of index at most J. The classical
Chermak--Delgado argument, proved in Section 1 of
[the soluble-case note](21.121b-soluble-proof.md), gives a
characteristic abelian subgroup D of N with [N:D]<=J^2. Since N
is characteristic in G, so is D. Its order is prime to p because
it lies in N. Multiplying the two index bounds proves (1).

Theorem 1 does not assume G soluble. Its only finite-group ingredient
is the characteristic abelian subgroup bound, whose proof is supplied
in the cited note. Although that note later studies soluble groups,
its Section 1 applies to every finite group.

More generally, if J_0 is a fixed bound for abelian normal indices
in finite p'-subgroups, the constant in (1) is J J_0^2. Thus replacing
“normal” by “characteristic” in the definition does not change the
set of admissible exponents or whether its infimum is attained.

Equivalently, under this fixed p'-subgroup bound, put

    r_p(G)=[G:O_(p')(G)],
    m_p(G)=min [G:A] over normal abelian p'-subgroups A.

The same argument gives the uniform comparison

    r_p(G) <= m_p(G) <= J_0^2 r_p(G).                    (2)

Consequently the admissible exponents can be computed from the
growth of r_p(G). A bound on r_p(G) alone, without the uniform
p'-subgroup hypothesis, is not asserted to suffice.

## 2. Extensions whose quotient has bounded finite subgroups

**Theorem 2.** Let Lambda be normal in Gamma. Suppose every finite
subgroup of Gamma/Lambda has order at most B. Then Gamma and Lambda
have exactly the same admissible p-Jordan exponents.

Restriction proves one inclusion. For the other, suppose Lambda has
an admissible exponent e with constant J, and let G<=Gamma be finite.
Put H=G intersect Lambda. Then H is normal in G and [G:H]<=B.
Theorem 1 applied to the finite subgroup H of Lambda supplies a
characteristic abelian p'-subgroup D of H with

    [H:D] <= J^3 |H_(p)|^e.

Characteristic invariance in H implies D is normal in G. Also
|G_(p)|/|H_(p)| is the p-part of [G:H], so it lies between 1 and B.
Therefore

    [G:D] <= B J^3 max(1,B^(-e)) |G_(p)|^e.             (3)

The factor B J^3 suffices for e>=0. In either case the constant is
independent of G. This proves equality of the admissible sets.

**Corollary 2.1.** A subgroup of finite index has the same p-Jordan
exponent as the ambient group, and attainment of the infimum is
preserved. Normality of the finite-index subgroup is unnecessary.

If Lambda has index d, its core C is normal with [Gamma:C]<=d!.
Every bound for Lambda restricts to C and then extends to Gamma by
Theorem 2. Bounds for Gamma restrict to Lambda, giving equality of
the full admissible sets. Equality of their infima and attainment
follow, whenever an infimum is in the allowed range. No assertion
about attaining an infinite extended-real infimum is intended.

## 3. Quotienting by a finite normal p-subgroup

**Theorem 3.** If P is a finite normal p-subgroup of Gamma, then
Gamma and Gamma/P have exactly the same admissible exponents.

First suppose e is admissible for Gamma with constant J. For any
finite subgroup Gbar of Gamma/P, its full preimage G is finite,
with |G_(p)|=|P||Gbar_(p)|. A normal abelian p'-subgroup of G maps
to one in Gbar without increasing its index. Thus the constant
J|P|^e works for Gbar; it may be enlarged to one if necessary.

Conversely, suppose e is admissible for Gamma/P with constant J.
Let G<=Gamma be finite, write P_0=G intersect P, and let Gbar be
its image. Choose Abar normal abelian of p'-order in Gbar with

    [Gbar:Abar] <= J |Gbar_(p)|^e.

Let M be its preimage in G. The group M is normal in G, P_0 is its
normal Sylow p-subgroup, and M/P_0 is abelian of p'-order. By the
[normal-Sylow bound](21.121b-normal-sylow-proof.md), with complement
constant one, M has a normal abelian p'-subgroup of index at most
|P_0|^2 (the trivial P_0 case has index one).

Set D=O_(p')(M). It contains that subgroup, so [M:D]<=|P_0|^2.
Moreover D maps injectively into M/P_0=Abar, since D intersect P_0=1;
therefore D is abelian. It is characteristic in M and hence normal
in G. Using |G_(p)|=|P_0||Gbar_(p)| yields

    [G:D] <= J |P_0|^(2-e) |G_(p)|^e
           <= J max(1,|P|^(2-e)) |G_(p)|^e.             (4)

This proves the reverse inclusion. Equality of exponents and
attainment follow as in Corollary 2.1.

## 4. Why an arbitrary finite kernel cannot replace a p-kernel

Choose an odd prime q different from p. Let U and V be countable
direct sums of F_q, with the ordinary finite-support pairing u.v.
On U x V x F_q define

    (u,v,z)(u',v',z')=(u+u',v+v',z+z'+u.v').

Bilinearity proves associativity. The subgroup Z={(0,0,z)} is
central of order q, and the quotient is the abelian group U x V.
Thus Gamma/Z is Jordan with constant one.

The subgroup E_d using the first d coordinates has order q^(2d+1).
Its commutator pairing on E_d/Z is the nondegenerate alternating
form u.v'-u'.v. The projection of an abelian subgroup is isotropic,
of dimension at most d: an isotropic subspace W lies in its
orthogonal complement, whose dimension is 2d-dim W. Its order is
therefore at most q^(d+1). Every abelian subgroup of E_d has index
at least q^d, which is unbounded. Since all E_d have order prime
to p, Gamma is not even generalized p-Jordan, and hence is not
p-Jordan. The fixed finite central kernel Z therefore cannot be
allowed in Theorem 3 without the p-group restriction.

This is the classical infinite extraspecial construction, included
as a boundary example without any novelty claim.

## 5. Consequences and limits for the Notebook question

One may pass between finite-index groups, or quotient by finite
normal p-subgroups, without changing either the optimal exponent or
its attainment. In particular those operations cannot convert the
nonattainment example for 21.121(a) into an attained infimum.

Theorems 1--3 do not bound the growth in (2) by exponent three for
arbitrary p-Jordan groups. Part (b) remains unresolved. The source
comparison and scope of the finite controls are in the accompanying
[report](21.121-permanence-report.md).
