# Internal review of the unitary cyclic case of 15.65

11 September 2026. No outside reviewer has reviewed this candidate.
The following is an adversarial audit of `15.65-proof.md`.

## Mathematical checks

1. **Exact target.** Cyclic means equality of minimal and characteristic
   polynomials in both the supplied Notebook, printed p.91, and FNP p.1.
   The source's different meaning of separable is explicitly excluded.
2. **Full unitary group.** FNP U(n,q) preserves a Hermitian form over
   F_(q^2). GAP GU(n,q) has the required order. No special-unitary or
   projective quotient is substituted. If q^2 is used as the field-size
   parameter, rationality in q^2 would imply rationality in q, so the
   negative result remains applicable.
3. **Limit input.** Theorem 2.1.9 asserts existence and the exact first
   product used. This is an imported enumeration theorem, not an inference
   from finite examples or an exchange of unproved limits.
4. **Signs and powers.** The first factor is 1-1/[q^d(q^d+1)]; the second
   is 1+1/[q^(2d)(q^(2d)-1)]. Their t-expressions are A(t^d) and B(t^(2d)).
   Both were visually checked on the source page.
5. **The exceptional count matters.** N_1=q+1, and M_1=(q^2-q-2)/2.
   The latter differs from the generic odd-degree formula. All three
   cases of Lemma 1.3.12 are included explicitly and checked visually.
6. **No fictitious fields.** Polynomial count formulas define Laurent
   polynomials in t for analytic continuation. Only their values at
   genuine field sizes are asserted to count polynomials.
7. **Removability at zero.** Count poles have order at most d or 2d;
   the logarithms vanish to order at least 2d or 4d. Hence each summand
   extends holomorphically and vanishes at zero.
8. **Infinite product.** The uniform log-series majorants 729/2368 and
   81/112 are strictly less than one at R=3/4. They yield summable
   bounds by constants times |t|^d and |t|^(2d), respectively.
9. **Nonvanishing, not just convergence.** The remainder H is defined as
   the exponential of a holomorphic sum, so it cannot have a zero or
   a pole at the proposed singularity. No cancellation is hidden in
   an infinite product tending to zero.
10. **Omitted factor.** Only d=1 in the N-product is omitted from H.
    The d=1 term of the M-product stays in H and satisfies its estimate.
11. **Correct location.** alpha=(1-sqrt(5))/2 lies between -3/4 and -1/2.
    The zero of 1+t-t^2 there is simple; 1+t is nonzero.
12. **Correct exponent.** N_1(1/alpha)=1+1/alpha=alpha, not an integer.
    The relation follows from alpha^2=alpha+1.
13. **Continuation branch.** A(t)>0 for alpha<t<0. The real logarithm
    on this interval agrees with the analytic branch continued from
    zero. The remainder's logarithms are also real there.
14. **Variable exponent handled.** Replacing 1+1/t by its value alpha
    in the leading asymptotic is justified by O(x)log(x)->0, not by
    assuming a constant exponent. The ratio in equation (8) has a
    finite, strictly positive limit.
15. **Rational obstruction.** A rational function's local order is an
    integer. Equation (8) has irrational order. No claim concerning
    the rationality of an individual probability is needed or made.
16. **Only prime powers known.** The sequence t=2^(-k) accumulates at
    the interior point zero, where F is holomorphic. A rational
    interpolant is removable there since it tends to one on this
    sequence. The identity theorem therefore applies.
17. **Possible intermediate poles.** A rational interpolant agreeing
    with the germ cannot acquire a pole within the interval where
    the continuation (6) is holomorphic. Its behavior at alpha must
    therefore agree with the displayed asymptotic, giving contradiction.
18. **No extrapolation from coefficients.** Eighty coefficient checks
    support transcription and algebra; they do not prove that the
    coefficients fail every possible recurrence. The proof is analytic.
19. **No independent source proof overclaim.** The standard conjugacy
    classification and centralizer orders underlying FNP are imported.
    The new analytic argument is fully written and internally checked.
20. **Scope of resolution.** This is a complete negative answer for
    C(U;q), one component of 15.65. No classification of the symplectic,
    orthogonal or literal semisimple limits is claimed in this packet.

## Exact computational controls

`check_15_65.py` computes the Taylor coefficients through degree 80 by
logarithmic differentiation and, separately, by root-power recurrences
for A and the identity B(u)=(1+u^3)/(1-u^2). Both use the explicitly
recorded count polynomials. They agree exactly; every coefficient is
an integer. The first thirteen agree with the displayed FNP p.40
series. All eighty-one coefficients, including the constant, also
match the known GL cyclic limit (1-t^5)/(1+t^3) modulo two, as predicted
by the prior Britnell--Fulman result.

`verify_15_65_unitary.g` independently constructs fourteen finite full
unitary groups: q=2 in dimensions 1--4; q=3,4 in dimensions 1--3;
q=5,7 in dimensions 1--2. It sums all conjugacy-class sizes, checks
against the group-order formula, and tests cyclicity in two ways:
the minimal-polynomial degree and the matrix rank of the flattened
powers I,g,...,g^(n-1). All 426 class tests agree. Their proportions
match exact coefficients of the finite generating product in FNP
Theorem 2.1.7, computed separately in Python using rational arithmetic.

The GAP subprocess exited zero, with the completion sentinel and no
errors or syntax warnings. The outer Python process also exited zero
and printed `15_65_CONTROLS_DONE 14 426`. No failed runs occurred in
this control packet. Neither finite-group counts nor the coefficient
checks certify the analytic conclusion; that conclusion is in the proof.
