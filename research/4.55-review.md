# Internal audit of the Problem 4.55 candidate

Date: 11 September 2026. This is an internal audit, not outside review.

## Completed exploratory screen

The character-table screen now covers all 2,750 installed library table
names and 7,573 cases for which a modular decomposition matrix was
available. It found 840 rationality-constraint flags and 265 parity
flags. These are exploratory flags, not additional counterexamples.
The parity screen precedes rationality reduction, and a constraint flag
alone does not prove nonunique decomposition.

The original reduced-row run stopped at index 2679, `Alt(2)`, because
`FactorsInt(1)` returns `[1]`, an invalid modular prime. Its complete
checkpoint through index 2600 is retained. The corrected screen filters
for prime divisors and independently reran indices 2601--2750 to a
completion record. The overlap after the first run's index-2600
checkpoint is excluded from aggregation.
`scripts/summarize_4_55_screen.py` checks the disjoint completed ranges,
completion/error boundaries, counts, and log hashes. The stopped log
is archived as `results/4.55-table-screen-stopped.log.gz`; the final
range and summary are retained separately. Both GAP processes have
exited. An earlier run using unreduced rows was manually stopped and
is not included in this aggregation.

The proof below depends on the independently reconstructed 3.A7 data,
not on completeness of a broad exploratory screen.

## Exact statement and conclusion

Notebook p.14 was inspected visually. The question specifies decomposition
into indecomposable projectives, uniquely up to isomorphism and permutation,
without requiring local endomorphism rings. The proof in `4.55-proof.md`
constructs incompatible finite decompositions over `Z_(5)[3.A7]` and hence
answers both the finite and the unrestricted versions negatively.

The old failure of semiperfectness alone does not establish this conclusion.
The new argument requires four distinct rays of possible projective
multiplicity vectors and actual rational descent on all four rays.

## Main logical checks

1. A projective over RG is finite free over R, because RG is finite free
   over the local PID R. Thus rank descent gives finite indecomposable
   refinements and a nonzero projective has nonzero reduction.
2. Modulo-5 projectivity is lifted using a Sylow subgroup, freeness over
   its modular local group ring, equal R-ranks, and averaging over its
   prime-to-5 index. No unproved lifting of extensions is used.
3. The dense-basis descent lemma only applies after a Q-form of the
   entire generic representation is supplied. Rational basis vectors
   chosen modulo 5M form an integral basis of M; the resulting action
   matrices lie in `Q intersect Z_5 = Z_(5)`.
4. The four Brauer pairs are Frobenius pairs over F5. They are not assumed
   to be arbitrary rational Galois orbits of Brauer irreducibles. A general
   Galois automorphism need not preserve Brauer irreducibility; this was
   an important discarded shortcut in the exploratory work.
5. Ordinary rationality is tested on the actual ordinary Galois orbits.
   The two degree-21 pairs are separate orbits, while all four degree-24
   characters are one orbit. This gives precisely `a+d=b+c` on the
   selected support.
6. Rational values alone do not guarantee a rational representation.
   Multiplication by 168 handles all possible global Schur indices via
   the explicit rational modules `QG e_O`, whose characters have
   coefficient chi(1) on the orbit O. The only degrees involved are
   6,21,24, each dividing 168.
7. After extension to Q_5, equality of ordinary characters identifies
   semisimple Q_5 representations. Different simple Q_5 representations
   have disjoint absolute constituents, so there is no descent ambiguity.
8. Equal modular reductions of finite RG-projectives imply isomorphism:
   lift the modular map, use Nakayama for surjectivity, then equal R-ranks.
9. The L_i need not be indecomposable. Every nonzero summand remains on
   the same ray, because its modular multiplicities are nonnegative,
   supported on the same two coordinates, and satisfy the rationality
   equation. Distinct rays have no common nonzero vector. Thus *any*
   indecomposable refinements of the two sides of the displayed
   isomorphism are incompatible. This avoids computing minimal Schur
   indices, minimal ranks, or endomorphism rings of the L_i.

The common module has R-rank 55,440. This is an explicit upper bound for
the construction, not a minimality claim.

## Independent computational checks

`scripts/group_4_55.g` contains two explicit permutations on 45 points.
They were initially extracted from PerfectGroup(7560,1); the final check
constructs their group directly, obtaining order 7560, centre order 3,
perfectness, and an isomorphism from the central quotient to A7.

`scripts/verify_4_55.g` computes the ordinary character table of this
actual group and checks equivalence to CTblLib. For modular data it starts
with its 45-dimensional permutation module over F25. Its composition
factor degrees are 1,1,13,15,15. It takes a 15-dimensional factor and
decomposes its tensor square, of dimension 225. The resulting absolutely
irreducible factors and their fifth-power Frobenius twists give all
twelve faithful Brauer characters of degrees

`3,3,6,6,15,15,15,15,18,18,21,21`.

Brauer traces are evaluated on representatives of every 5-regular class,
using words in the actual permutation generators: 240 values in total.
The twelve characters have rank twelve. All fourteen faithful ordinary
restrictions are then decomposed in these constructed characters, with
nonnegative integral coefficients. They agree with the library matrix.
The nonfaithful ordinary characters cannot have faithful modular
constituents, by the central subgroup of order 3. Thus all eight columns
used in the proof are independently accounted for. The library Brauer
table is a comparison target, never an input to the modular construction.

The GAP verifier also checks all four ordinary Galois orbits, the
Frobenius pairs, rational values for all four ray characters, the factor
168, and the equality of the two sums. Actual modular matrices are
retained in `results/4.55-modular-matrices.g` for inspection.

`scripts/verify_4_55.py` uses its own field arithmetic for
`F5[z]/(z^2-z+2)` and exported 3-by-3 matrices. It checks 15,625 field
triples, enumerates the matrix group (order 7560) and its three scalar
central elements, and finds a nine-dimensional matrix algebra, certifying
absolute irreducibility independently of the MeatAxe. It also checks
28,561 multiplicity vectors (1,468 nonzero solutions), their ray
decompositions, the four atoms in this rationality monoid, and the common
rank. These finite controls supplement the proof; they do not prove the
infinite descent assertion by enumeration.

Both final controls pass. The first Python attempt rejected a GAP export
containing line-continuation backslashes, before any matrix checks ran.
Disabling GAP stream formatting produced genuine JSON; no field or
mathematical assertion needed changing.

## Source and novelty audit

- **Notebook:** supplied September-2026 edition, p.14, visually read.
- **Modular Atlas / CTblLib:** the [group information page](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Thomas.Breuer/ctbllib/ctbltoc/data/3.A7.html)
  supplies the group order and sources. Blocks 9 and 10 in the
  [5-modular decomposition PDF](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~MOC/decomposition/tex/A7/A7mod5.pdf)
  were visually checked on p.1. Its character numbering across covering
  groups differs from the standalone GAP table; the proof explicitly
  separates these conventions. The data themselves are prior work.
- **Johnston--Rumynin:** [arXiv:2507.21316v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21316),
  October 2025; published in Journal of Algebra 687 (2026), 776–791,
  DOI 10.1016/j.jalgebra.2025.09.014. The introduction explicitly separates
  semiperfectness from the finite and unrestricted uniqueness questions
  and says the paper addresses the former. Its introduction and relevant
  lifting section were read; the broader audit in `21.60-review.md` is
  retained. This paper is not claimed to solve the present uniqueness
  question.
- **Woods:** [Some results on semi-perfect group rings](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1974-013-x),
  Canadian J. Math. 26 (1974), 121–129. The introduction and Section 6
  were read from the primary PDF. Its localized example uses C3 at 7 to
  show failure of semiperfectness, a different conclusion. Other sections
  were not re-proved for this audit.
- **Chernousov--Merkurjev:** [Motivic decomposition and the Krull--Schmidt theorem](https://www.math.ualberta.ca/~chernous/ChernousovPublications/Krull-Schmidt.pdf),
  introduction and Section 7, especially Remark 7.4, were read. The
  Beneish example there concerns summands of permutation lattices. Its
  module L has rank phi(p-1)<p for `C_p semidirect C_(p-1)` and is not
  projective over the localized group ring, since projective ranks are
  divisible by p. It cannot simply be imported as an answer to 4.55.
- **Jacobinski:** the classical genera/decomposition paper was located,
  but the primary full PDF could not be retrieved during this turn.
  Search excerpts and a secondary text mirror were not used as a theorem
  in this proof. Its relation to novelty remains a broader audit item.
- **Atlas matrices:** an optional download of a 3-dimensional matrix
  representation timed out. No Atlas matrix file is used in the checks;
  all matrices were constructed from the permutation module as above.

Targeted searches for the exact group with projective decomposition and
for the Roggenkamp uniqueness problem found no matching prior answer.
This is limited negative search evidence. No claim of established
priority or external validation is made.
