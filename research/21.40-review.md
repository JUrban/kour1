# Internal review of the candidate solution to 21.40

Reviewed internally on 11 September 2026. This is not independent outside
review. Status: complete candidate affirmative proof; novelty remains
unconfirmed.

## Exact question and claimed conclusion

The supplied Notebook page 173 was inspected visually. Problem 21.40,
attributed to A. Dantas and E. de Melo, asks whether a subgroup of GL(n,Q)
with finitely many automorphism orbits is virtually soluble. It does not
assume finite generation. The proof establishes a normal torsion-free
nilpotent subgroup U of class at most n-1 and index at most (2n+1)^(n^2).

The main proof is `research/21.40-proof.md`. The reduction from the orbit
hypothesis to deep roots, the algebraic-number finiteness argument, and
the trace-algebra argument are all given in full. No structural
classification of infinite linear groups is imported.

## Audit of the argument

1. The pigeonhole step uses the sequence g^(2^i), not an assumption
   about conjugacy of rational matrices. An abstract automorphism alpha
   sends h to h^r. Iterating and then applying alpha^(-k) gives an actual
   matrix X_k in the original group with X_k^(r^k)=h. This does not
   require alpha to act linearly on the matrix algebra.
2. Spectral mapping works for matrices with nontrivial Jordan blocks.
   Each chosen eigenvalue mu_k has degree at most n over Q. Its powers
   have all Q-conjugates among the fixed spectrum of h, because the
   characteristic polynomial of h is rational. These facts give both
   the degree bound and the uniform archimedean bound.
3. A fixed integer D clearing h's entry denominators makes D lambda
   integral. The power of D mu_k is integral, so D mu_k is integral by
   transitivity. A bound on the minimal-polynomial coefficients then
   gives finiteness directly. The proof does not confuse bounded degree
   with bounded height, and does not assume the mu_k themselves integral.
4. Repetition of mu_k at two different root exponents forces mu_k, and
   hence lambda, to be a root of unity. Eigenvalues of the original g
   are roots of unity because a positive power of each is such a lambda.
5. Every trace is a rational algebraic integer. This integrality is
   essential: bounded rational traces alone would not give a finite set.
   All products gg_i must be covered, not just chosen generators.
6. The trace radical is explicitly a two-sided ideal in the span of G.
   Vanishing power traces and Newton identities prove that every element
   of this ideal is nilpotent. A subspace merely spanned by nilpotent
   matrices would not suffice here.
7. The finite-generator module argument proves R^n=0. It uses generators
   for a finite-dimensional A-module, not generators for G. The equation
   W=RW expands with coefficients in R because R is an ideal. Inverting
   1-a_s eliminates one generator, so a nonzero stable term is impossible.
8. The filtration of 1+R proves its nilpotency class bound. Characteristic
   zero rules out nonidentity finite-order unipotent matrices. The
   quotient map to (A/R)^* gives normality. Trace tuples are equal
   exactly on U-cosets, proving the finite-index bound.

For n=1, the argument gives R=0 and a finite G, so the stated class-zero
convention is consistent. The normal subgroup is defined through the
given representation; no characteristic-subgroup assertion is used.

## Exact controls

Run from the repository root:

    python3 scripts/verify_21_40.py
    bin/gap scripts/verify_21_40.g

The Python script uses only exact rational arithmetic from the standard
library. It passes 72 root-tower identities for full unipotent Jordan
blocks of sizes 2 through 5, with exponents r^k for r=2,3,5 and 1<=k<=6.
The largest denominator encountered is 59,604,644,775,390,625. This tests
precisely the case where matrix-entry denominators cannot be bounded.
It also verifies 24 roots of a rational order-three rotation, showing
why the eigenvalues need not all be 1.

For upper triangular rational algebras of sizes 1 through 6, the exact
trace Gram matrices have the expected strict-upper-triangular radical.
The radical powers vanish at n, and the full Jordan nilpotent has
nonzero (n-1)-st power when n>1. These are algebra controls; we make no
claim that every associated infinite triangular unit group has finitely
many automorphism orbits.

A further example meets the actual group hypothesis and prevents an
overstrong conclusion. Embed

    A5 x (Q,+) -> GL(7,Q),
    (p,q) -> diag(P_p, [[1,q],[0,1]]),

where P_p is the five-point permutation matrix. Conjugation by S5 has
four orbits on A5, of sizes 1,15,20,24. Scaling Q by Q^* has two orbits,
so their product automorphisms give at most eight orbits on the group.
The group is infinite and nonsoluble, but its normal Q-factor is abelian
of index 60. Its rational span has dimension 18 and trace radical exactly
Q E_(6,7), of dimension one. Python checks 300 matrices, five rational
coordinates for each of the 60 permutations, with exactly 60 trace tuples.

GAP independently constructs the rational triangular algebras and this
A5 example, computes actual algebra bases and trace nullspaces, and
computes Aut(A5) and its four orbits. It passes 546 trace-pairing checks,
25 additional Jordan-root identities, the 24 finite-order root identities,
and the 60-coset calculation.

Both scripts include the negative control U(1), L(1) in SL(2,Z): each
generator is unipotent but their product has trace 3. One cannot check
only a generating set for the finite-trace property. Both initial runs
completed successfully. Results are in `results/21.40-python-controls.json`
and `results/21.40-gap-controls.log`. None of these finite computations
substitutes for the arbitrary-group proof.

## Prior literature checked and limits of the check

The following primary sources were inspected on 11 September 2026.
No exact prior answer to 21.40 was found in this limited search. That is
not a claim of exhaustive novelty verification.

- Bastos--Dantas--de Melo, [*Soluble Groups with few orbits under
  automorphisms*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.01375v1), Geometriae Dedicata
  209 (2020), 119--123, DOI 10.1007/s10711-020-00525-7. Primary abstract
  read. Its structural theorem assumes solubility and finite rank; it
  does not establish solubility from rational linearity. Full proof
  unaudited here.
- Bastos--Dantas--de Melo, [*Virtually nilpotent groups with finitely many
  orbits under automorphisms*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10800v1),
  Archiv der Mathematik 116 (2021), 261--270, DOI
  10.1007/s00013-020-01566-w. Primary abstract read. It assumes virtual
  nilpotence and proves a decomposition involving a torsion-free
  radicable nilpotent characteristic subgroup. That theorem does not
  supply the implication from rational linearity proved here. Full
  proof unaudited here.
- De Melo--Kato, [*Automorphism Orbits of the Group of Unitriangular
  Matrices*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09353v1), version 1, 10 October
  2025. Introduction and Theorems 1.1--1.2 on pages 1--3 read in the
  downloaded primary PDF. It discusses the above structural results
  and motivates classification of linear groups with finite orbit
  count. Its theorems concern the unitriangular family. The full proof
  and its extensive appendix were not audited; no theorem from it is
  required here.
- Dantas--de Melo--Kato, [*On soluble groups of finite rank with finitely
  many automorphism orbits*](https://doi.org/10.1080/00927872.2025.2578695),
  Communications in Algebra 54(6) (2026), 2341--2347. Primary publisher
  abstract read; full proof unaudited. The stated classification concerns
  soluble groups of finite rank with four or five orbits, retaining the
  solubility hypothesis.
- Dantas--Sousa, [*Some families of locally graded groups with finitely
  many orbits under automorphisms*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02090v1),
  version 1 submitted 3 May 2026. Primary abstract, introduction,
  Theorems A--C and the full proof of Theorem A read. Theorems A and B
  concern residually finite groups and finitely generated locally graded
  groups, respectively. Neither directly covers an arbitrary rational
  linear group: finite-generation residual-finiteness theorems cannot
  be applied to the whole infinitely generated group, and the additive
  Q is already a counterexample to that unjustified inference. Theorem C
  concerns particular Mal'cev completions. Remaining proofs unaudited.

Searches also used the exact problem number and combinations of rational
linear groups, finite automorphism orbits, deep roots and roots of unity.
A secondary database labels the problem open, but that label is not
used as proof of current status or of novelty. The theorem is retained
as a complete candidate for independent mathematical and literature review.
