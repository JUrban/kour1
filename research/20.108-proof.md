# 20.108(a),(b): an order605 centreless counterexample

Status: complete candidate affirmative answers to(a) and(b); internal
proof audit and independent GAP/Python controls pass. Novelty and outside
review remain pending. Part(c) is already affirmative by Caranti--Tsang2023;
see `20.108c-known-consequence.md`.
The statement on printed page163 was visually checked.

We construct a finite centreless group G such that
T(G)=NHol(G)/Hol(G) contains an element of order four. In particular,
T(G) is not an elementary abelian2-group. We do not need to determine
the whole multiple holomorph.

## The group and its full automorphism group

Let lambda=3 in F_11; it has order5. On

    G = F_11^2 x Z/5Z

define

    (x,y,k)(x',y',k') =
      (x+lambda^k x', y+lambda^(2k)y', k+k').

Thus G=(C_11 x C_11) semidirect C_5, where a generator of C_5 acts
by diag(3,9). This is a group of order605. Its centre is trivial:
commuting with both coordinate translations forces k=0, and commuting
with (0,0,1) then forces x=y=0. In GAP this is SmallGroup(605,5).

The normal subgroup V=F_11^2 is the unique Sylow11-subgroup and hence
characteristic. An automorphism induces a matrix M in GL_2(11) on V
and multiplication by u in (Z/5Z)^* on G/V. Compatibility with the
conjugation action gives

    M diag(lambda,lambda^2) = diag(lambda^u,lambda^(2u)) M.

The eigenvalue sets must agree, so {u,2u}={1,2} in Z/5Z. Checking
u=1,2,3,4 shows u=1. The matrix M is therefore diagonal, with nonzero
entries a,d. The image of (0,0,1) can have any vector component:
the sum of its first five conjugate vector components is zero because
lambda and lambda^2 are nontrivial fifth roots of unity.

It follows, including the converse, that the full automorphism group
consists of the maps

    (x,y,k) -> (a x+b(1-lambda^k),
               d y+e(1-lambda^(2k)), k),

where a,d are arbitrary nonzero elements of F_11 and b,e are arbitrary
elements. These maps are bijective and respect the displayed group law.
Their number is 10^2*11^2=12,100.

## The full holomorph as a concrete permutation group

For any group, the normalizer of its left regular subgroup consists
exactly of left translations followed by automorphisms. Indeed, after
composing a normalizing permutation with a left translation to make
it fix the identity, its conjugation of left translations shows that
it is a group automorphism.

Using the preceding classification, Hol(G) therefore consists exactly
of the following permutations of the605 triples:

    h(x,y,k) = (A x+B+C lambda^k,
                D y+E+F lambda^(2k), k+j),                 (1)

where A,D are nonzero in F_11, B,C,E,F are arbitrary in F_11, and
j is arbitrary in Z/5Z. Every parameter choice occurs: the translation
coordinates make B,C and E,F independently adjustable. The parameters
are unique, since 1 and lambda^k (respectively 1 and lambda^(2k))
are independent functions of k. Thus |Hol(G)|=5*11^4*10^2=7,320,500.

## An element of order four in the quotient

Define a permutation

    theta(x,y,k) = (y, lambda^(-k)x, 2k).                  (2)

It is bijective, with inverse

    theta^(-1)(X,Y,K) = (lambda^(3K)Y, X, 3K).

For h as in(1), direct composition yields

    (theta h theta^(-1))(X,Y,K) =
      (D X+E+F lambda^K,
       lambda^(-j)A Y+lambda^(-j)C
                         +lambda^(-j)B lambda^(2K),
       K+2j).                                           (3)

This is again of the form(1), so theta normalizes Hol(G). Hence
theta belongs to NHol(G).

On the other hand,

    theta^2(x,y,k) = (lambda^(-k)x,lambda^(-2k)y,-k),
    theta^4(x,y,k) = (x,y,k).

Every element of Hol(G) acts on the third coordinate as k->k+j,
whereas theta^2 acts as k->-k. These cannot be equal functions on
Z/5Z. Thus theta^2 does not belong to Hol(G), and theta's coset
in T(G) has order exactly four. This proves(a) and(b). QED.

## A family with unbounded element orders

The same mechanism gives more than the single example. Let p,q be
primes with q odd and q dividing p-1, let lambda in F_p have order q,
and let r in F_q^* have order2d. Put s_i=r^i for 0<=i<d, and take

    G_(p,q,r) = F_p^d semidirect C_q,

with the generator acting diagonally by lambda^(s_0),...,lambda^(s_(d-1)).
The group is centreless by the same argument. Every automorphism
induces the identity on C_q. In fact, a possible multiplier u would
have to preserve S={1,r,...,r^(d-1)}. Since u*1 lies in S, u=r^j
for some 0<=j<d. Translating this proper consecutive half of the
cyclic exponent set Z/(2d)Z preserves it only for j=0. Distinct
eigenvalues then force a diagonal matrix on F_p^d.

Consequently the full holomorph consists of maps

    x_i -> a_i x_i+b_i+c_i lambda^(s_i k),    k -> k+j,

with independent coefficients and every a_i nonzero. The permutation

    Theta(x_0,...,x_(d-1),k) =
      (x_1,...,x_(d-1),lambda^(-k)x_0,rk)

normalizes this holomorph. For coordinates i<d-1, conjugation simply
moves the parameters of coordinate i+1 into coordinate i. In the last
coordinate it replaces (a_0,b_0,c_0) by

    (lambda^(-j)a_0, lambda^(-j)c_0, lambda^(-j)b_0),

and the quotient shift becomes rj. The exponents work because
s_(i+1)=r s_i and -r^(-1)=r^(d-1) in F_q.

After d applications,

    Theta^d(x_0,...,x_(d-1),k)
      = (lambda^(-s_0 k)x_0,...,lambda^(-s_(d-1)k)x_(d-1),-k).

Thus Theta^(2d)=1, while its action on C_q has order2d. Since a
power of Theta fixes the identity, it can lie in the holomorph only
if it acts trivially on C_q. Its coset therefore has order exactly2d.

These orders are unbounded even without a prime-progression theorem.
For any odd prime q, choose r primitive in F_q^*, so 2d=q-1. Any
prime divisor p of 1+q+...+q^(q-1) satisfies p!=q and the residue
of q has order q modulo p: order1 would force p=q. Therefore
q divides p-1. Such p supplies the required example, and odd primes
q are unbounded. The order605 example takes p=11,q=5,r=2,d=2.

## Independent checks and literature scope

`scripts/verify_20_108.g` constructs the regular group on all605
points and independently identifies it as SmallGroup(605,5). It checks
the centre, regularity, all generators of the asserted holomorph,
and its order against GAP's automorphism group of the library group.
It then checks all seven conjugated holomorph generators, the fourth
power of theta, nonmembership of theta^2, and index4 of the generated
extension of the holomorph. All checks pass.

`scripts/verify_20_108.py` uses only integer arithmetic and explicit
permutations. It checks all13,200 invertible matrices against all
four possible quotient automorphisms, obtaining exactly100 diagonal
solutions for u=1 and none otherwise. The normalizer identity is
checked on all605 points for each of the seven holomorph generators
and for8,000 parameter choices. A deliberately incorrect permutation
without the multiplier lambda^(-k) fails a normalizer check.

`scripts/verify_20_108_family.py` checks the general formulas over four
parameter choices with coset orders4,6,12,16. It checks all quotient
coordinates on the zero vector and every coordinate basis vector for
every holomorph generator:5,939 composition checks pass. These are
finite affine-basis controls; the general theorem rests on the proof.

Cindy Tsang's [The multiple holomorph of centerless groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.13690v2),
v2 of26 November2024, published in J. Pure Appl. Algebra229(2025),
107843, states the exponent-two conjecture. Theorem4.5 leaves the
specific groups SmallGroup(605,5) and SmallGroup(1210,11), as well as
order1536, as exceptions to its test. The discussion explicitly allows
the two relevant characteristic kernels to be exchanged. Our choice
of group was prompted by this exceptional case. The introduction,
normalizer conventions and the theorem with its following discussion
were read; the full general classification argument was not audited.

The limited subsequent searches found no earlier explicit order-four
certificate for this group. This does not establish novelty. The proof
above is elementary and does not assume Tsang's main theorem. No
assertion that all of T(G) is cyclic of order four is needed or made.
