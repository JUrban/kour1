# 11.116: a finite coordinate bound for the subgroup lattice

Status: candidate affirmative solution. The argument is complete; independent
review and final literature checks are pending. The supplied Notebook page56
was visually checked: dimension means order dimension, namely the least number
of linear orders whose intersection is the subgroup-inclusion order.

**Theorem.** Suppose G has a normal subgroup

    D = C_(p1^infinity) x ... x C_(pr^infinity),

where the primes p1,...,pr are distinct, r>=1, and [G:D]=m is finite.
Then the order dimension of the entire subgroup lattice L(G) is at most2rm.
The extension need not split, and its finite quotient need not be abelian.

A Chernikov group has a normal subgroup of finite index which is a finite
direct product of quasicyclic groups. Under the hypothesis in11.116 no prime
can occur twice in this product. The theorem therefore answers the question
affirmatively when G is infinite. A finite group has a finite subgroup
lattice, whose dimension is automatically finite (also at most |G|, by
embedding its subgroups into the membership-coordinate cube {0,1}^G).

## 1. Cosets in one quasicyclic group are intervals after a bijection

Write P=C_(p^infinity) additively as Z[1/p]/Z. Each x in P has a unique
representative in [0,1) and a unique terminating base-p expansion

    x = sum_(i>=1) d_i p^(-i),       0<=d_i<p,

where all but finitely many digits are zero. Define a bijection

    b_p : P -> N_0,       b_p(x) = sum_(i>=1) d_i p^(i-1).

This reverses the place values, not the order of a chosen finite string:
appending zero digits to an expansion does not change b_p(x). Its inverse
uses the finite base-p expansion of an integer, so bijectivity is explicit.
No homomorphism property is asserted or needed.

Let P_n be the unique subgroup of order p^n, with P_0={0}. Two elements
belong to the same coset of P_n precisely when the digits with i>n agree.
Indeed their difference lies in p^(-n)Z/Z exactly when their fractional
tails beyond position n agree. Consequently

    b_p(x+P_n) = [a p^n, (a+1)p^n-1] intersect N_0,
    a = floor(b_p(x)/p^n).

The remaining subgroup of P is P itself, whose unique coset maps to N_0.
Thus every nonempty coset of every subgroup of P maps to an integer interval,
possibly the whole N_0. Associate its left and right endpoints (L,R), using
(0,+infinity) for the whole group. For any two such cosets C,E,

    C subset E  iff  L(C)>=L(E) and R(C)<=R(E).                 (1)

This is simply inclusion of intervals after a fixed bijection. The use of
terminating expansions, the trivial subgroup, and the entire quasicyclic
group are all included.

## 2. Cosets in D are boxes

Every subgroup A of D is the product of its primary components A_i<=P_i.
For completeness, each primary component of an element a of finite order
is an integer multiple of a, by the Chinese remainder theorem. Hence all
components of each a in A belong to A. Since r is finite, their products
give exactly A=product A_i. Each A_i is a finite cyclic subgroup or P_i.
Distinctness of the primes is essential here.

Under the product bijection b=product b_(p_i), a coset d+A is therefore
the box product_i b_(p_i)(d_i+A_i). Each factor is a nonempty interval.
Inclusion of two nonempty boxes is equivalent to inclusion in every
coordinate, and so is determined by the2r endpoint inequalities (1).

Include the empty set as well. Give it L_i=+infinity and R_i=-infinity
for every i. Its coordinates make it a subset of every box; no nonempty
box has its coordinate vector below the empty set. Consequently the poset
of cosets of subgroups of D, together with the empty set, embeds in a
product of2r chains: order each L coordinate in reverse order and each
R coordinate in its usual order. Equality of all coordinates determines
the box or empty set, so this embedding is injective.

## 3. Encode every subgroup by its finitely many coset fibers

Choose one representative t_q for each q in G/D. For H<=G put

    S_q(H) = {d in D : d t_q in H}.

If this set is nonempty and d_0 belongs to it, then

    S_q(H) = (H intersect D) d_0.                             (2)

To check (2), for d,d_0 in the fiber the product
(d t_q)(d_0 t_q)^(-1)=d d_0^(-1) lies in H intersect D. Conversely
(a d_0)t_q is in H for every a in H intersect D. Thus each S_q(H) is
empty or a coset of a subgroup of D, to which Section2 applies.

The right cosets D t_q partition G, and translation by t_q is a bijection
on each fiber. It follows that

    H<=K  iff  S_q(H) subset S_q(K) for every q in G/D.         (3)

Combine all2r endpoint coordinates from each of the m fibers. Equations
(1)--(3) give an injective order embedding

    L(G) -> product of2rm chains.

This construction uses neither a homomorphic transversal nor a complement
to D. Infinite subgroups H are encoded just as finite ones are.

## 4. From coordinates to linear orders

Here is the standard elementary implication, included to make the argument
independent of terminology. Suppose a poset P order-embeds in a product of
d chains, with d finite and positive. Let <_0 be the lexicographic order
of the complete coordinate vectors; it is a linear extension of P because
the embedding is injective. For each coordinate j, linearly order P first
by that coordinate and break ties by <_0. These d orders
all extend P. If x is not <=y in P, some coordinate of x is strictly larger
than the corresponding coordinate of y; its linear order places y before x.
The intersection of the d orders is therefore exactly the order on P.

Applying this with d=2rm proves the theorem. No order-extension theorem
is needed. The underlying poset is countable as well: Section3 embeds it
in finitely many countable sets of cosets.

## 5. Prior context and verification scope

L. N. Shevrin, *Semigroups with certain finiteness conditions and Chernikov
groups*, Algebra and Discrete Mathematics13(2) (2012),299--306,
[journal page](https://admjournal.luguniv.edu.ua/index.php/adm/article/view/706),
states the converse implication: a locally finite group with finite subgroup
lattice dimension has the structure assumed in the theorem. The same source
poses the sufficiency statement as a still-open conjecture and identifies
it with11.116. That necessity theorem is prior work and is not reproved or
claimed as new here. The proof above establishes sufficiency directly.

The exact-arithmetic and actual finite-group controls accompanying this
proof test its interval and fiber encodings. They are not substitutes for
the proof for infinite groups. Literature reading scope, completed counts,
and remaining review limitations are recorded in11.116-review.md.
