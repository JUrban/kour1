# Problem 12.40: no finite bound in terms of the Sylow order

11 September 2026. Complete negative candidate; independent review
and further priority checks remain pending.

## Statement

For every integer k>=3 there are a positive odd integer a and an
irreducible 2-Brauer character phi of G=PSL_6(5^a) such that

    |G|_2 = 8192,       phi(1)_2 = 2^k.

Consequently there is no finite-valued function f with
phi(1)_p <= f(|G|_p) for all finite groups and irreducible p-Brauer
characters. This already fails with p fixed at two, even for simple
groups. It answers the exact inequality in the supplied Notebook,
Problem 12.40, page 60. No optimal finite bound remains to determine.

## The irreducible degree

We use the following established representation-theoretic input.
For n>=3, p dividing n, and a prime power q congruent to 1 modulo p,
the projective-point permutation module of PSL_n(q) in characteristic
p has an absolutely irreducible heart of dimension

    (q^n-1)/(q-1) - 2.

This is included in John N. Bray, *A new family of modules with
2-dimensional 1-cohomology*, Main Theorem, author version dated
27 July 2007, page 2; the irreducibility argument is on page 3:
https://webspace.maths.qmul.ac.uk/j.n.bray/Papers/BigCoho/Dim2H1.pdf .
Only this representation assertion is needed, not the cohomology
calculation. The cited hypotheses include p=2, n=6, q=5^a.

For clarity, the argument specializes as follows. Over algebraically
closed characteristic two, let P be the projective permutation module,
U its constant line, W its augmentation kernel, and M=W/U. Perfectness
of SL_6(q) excludes a trivial submodule of M: its preimage would split
as two trivial lines in P. Self-duality excludes a trivial quotient.
Every nontrivial simple factor has dimension at least q^5-1: restrict
to the elementary abelian unipotent subgroup E=F_q^5, which normally
generates SL_6(q); its nontrivial weights form one orbit under the
Levi subgroup SL_5(q). Since 2(q^5-1)>dim M, only one nontrivial
factor occurs. Absence of trivial submodules and quotients then forces
M to be simple. Central scalars act trivially on projective points.

Thus, writing q=5^a, the required irreducible degree is the positive
integer

    F(q) = q^5+q^4+q^3+q^2+q-1.                       (1)

## The group-order calculation

For a positive odd integer a,

    q=5^a == 5 (mod 8),      q == -1 (mod 3).

In particular gcd(6,q-1)=2 and v_2(q-1)=2. The order formula gives

    |PSL_6(q)| = (q^15/2) product_{i=2}^6 (q^i-1).

The five factors have 2-adic valuations, in order,

    3, 2, 4, 2, 3.

Indeed q^2-1=(q-1)(q+1) has valuation three; an odd geometric
sum has odd value, giving the valuations for i=3 and i=5.
The factors q^2+1 and q^4+q^2+1 give the i=4 and i=6 cases.
Their sum is fourteen, and division by two leaves

    v_2(|G|)=13.                                      (2)

This is an exact constant, independent of a.

## Elementary lifting in the exponent

First, for each integer j>=0,

    v_2(5^(2^j)-1)=j+2.                              (3)

For j=0 this is v_2(4)=2. Squaring multiplies the preceding
difference by 5^(2^j)+1, which has valuation one, so induction proves
(3). Consequently, for every k>=3,

    5^(2^(k-2)) == 1+2^k (mod 2^(k+1)).               (4)

For every odd x the derivative

    F'(x)=5x^4+4x^3+3x^2+2x+1

is odd. The binomial theorem, without any analytic assumptions, yields

    F(x+2^k) == F(x)+2^k (mod 2^(k+1)).               (5)

Suppose a is positive and odd and 2^k divides F(5^a). Replacing a
by a+2^(k-2) preserves positivity and oddness, and (4) changes 5^a
by 2^k modulo 2^(k+1). By (5), exactly one of these two exponents
makes F(5^a) divisible by 2^(k+1). The other makes its valuation
exactly k.

Start with a_3=1, since F(5)=3904=2^6*61. Recursively choose

    a_(k+1) = a_k                  if 2^(k+1) divides F(5^a_k),
              a_k+2^(k-2)         otherwise.         (6)

Induction proves that a_k is positive and odd and 2^k divides
F(5^a_k). The unused exponent at step k, denoted b_k, satisfies

    v_2(F(5^b_k))=k.                                 (7)

Use q=5^b_k in (1) and (2) to obtain the theorem. All q are actual
finite prime powers. There is no need to realize arbitrary integers
as field orders, invoke primes in progressions, or take a limiting
group. For any proposed finite f(8192), choose k with 2^k>f(8192).

## Examples and the role of computation

The successive exponents b_k for k=3,...,16 are

    3,5,9,1,17,49,241,369,113,1649,2673,4721,8817,625.

In particular PSL_6(5^625) has the degree (1) with 2-part 65536,
eight times its group-order 2-part. The finite checks verify these
examples and many more lifts; they do not prove an infinite statement
by extrapolation. The argument (3)--(7) proves that statement.
