# Problem 19.62: reducing the remaining inclusions to rank four

12 September 2026. Plan following the audited G2 certificate. No full
solution is claimed yet.

Each square-expansion target uses either two or three commutator
summands. If its destination is p and one summand has source roots
r,s with i r+j s equal to p or -p, those two roots lie in the span
of p and r. Thus all the variables' root labels in one target lie in
a subspace of rank at most four. Intersect this span with the ambient
root system. This is a reduced crystallographic root subsystem, since
reflection in any of its roots preserves the span. Every source root
belongs to the irreducible component containing p: a nontrivial root
decomposition cannot cross two orthogonal components.

Restriction of A to that subsystem is a carpet. A derived summand
formed inside the subsystem is also a derived summand in the ambient
system. Therefore it suffices to certify each target in the derived
carpet of this subsystem, even though restriction need not commute with
taking the full derived carpet.

The irreducible possibilities have rank two, three or four. It is
enough to treat A4, B4, C4, D4, F4 and G2: lower-rank A, B and C
systems sit as coordinate root subsystems of their rank-four versions.
For a lower-rank certificate, retain only rules in that subsystem;
do not extend a carpet by zero in an arbitrary nonclosed subsystem.
Alternatively run the lower ranks separately, which is cheap and
avoids that extension issue entirely.

Export and audit integral Chevalley data for the remaining types.
Generate every rule and target, obtain explicit arithmetic derivations,
and independently check them as in the G2 packet. Exact polynomial
matrix identities must verify all structure constants. No Weyl-orbit
reduction is assumed without an explicit equivalence audit.

paper suffices outside the remaining symplectic cases. His 2026 article
