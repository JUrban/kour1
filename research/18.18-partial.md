# 18.18: the cofinite theory is not computably enumerable

Status: a proof of the first of the two requested nonenumerability
assertions. The assertion about its complement remains unresolved here.
No novelty claim is made for this elementary reduction.

Interpret “almost all finite groups” as all but finitely many isomorphism
types, equivalently all groups of sufficiently large finite order. This
note does not treat an interpretation involving asymptotic density.

Let T_fin be the set of group-language sentences true in every finite
group, and T_cof the set true in almost all finite groups. The Notebook
recalls that T_fin is not computably enumerable.

For any sentence phi, effectively construct a sentence Psi_phi as follows.
Take new variables a,b, rename bound variables to avoid capture, and
relativize every quantifier in phi to the formula

    C(x;a,b) := (xa=ax and xb=bx).

Universal quantifiers become `forall x (C(x;a,b) implies ...)`;
existential ones become `exists x (C(x;a,b) and ...)`. Atomic formulas,
Boolean connectives, group operations, and the identity are unchanged.
Let phi^C(a,b) be the resulting formula and put

    Psi_phi := forall a forall b phi^C(a,b).

The common centralizer C_G(a,b) is a subgroup, so induction on formulas
proves

    G satisfies phi^C(a,b) iff C_G(a,b) satisfies phi.

Consequently phi in T_fin implies Psi_phi in T_fin, hence in T_cof.

Conversely suppose a finite group H fails phi. For each n>=3, take
G_n=H x S_n, with

    a_n=(1,(1 2 ... n)),  b_n=(1,(1 2)).

These two permutations generate S_n: conjugating the transposition by
powers of the cycle supplies the adjacent transpositions. The center
of S_n is trivial for n>=3. Therefore

    C_{G_n}(a_n,b_n)=H x Z(S_n)=H x 1.

Thus every G_n fails Psi_phi. Their orders |H| n! are unbounded,
so Psi_phi is not in T_cof. We have established the computable reduction

    phi in T_fin iff Psi_phi in T_cof.

If T_cof were computably enumerable, its computable preimage T_fin
would be computably enumerable, a contradiction.

## The remaining obstruction

Negating Psi_phi does not give the second requested reduction. When H
fails phi, it only supplies infinitely many groups satisfying the
negation, and does not make that negation cofinite. Confusing these
quantifiers would give an invalid proof about the complement of T_cof.
No such claim is made.

The standard upper bound is T_cof in Sigma^0_2: effectively enumerate
finite multiplication tables up to isomorphism and write
`exists N forall i>=N (G_i satisfies phi)`. Its complement is in Pi^0_2.
This hierarchy placement alone proves neither desired nonenumerability.
