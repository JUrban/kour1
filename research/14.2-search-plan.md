# Problem 14.2: restricted exact search in integral centres

Status: exploratory search; not a proof of the general conjecture.
Started 11 September 2026.

The target is a nonmonomial automorphism of Z(ZG), in its integral basis
of conjugacy-class sums. We first test a narrow, fully explicit family:
swap two rational primitive central idempotents and fix all the others.
This is a rational algebra automorphism, regardless of whether the other
simple components of the rational centre are rational fields.

Let chi and psi be rational-valued irreducible characters of degrees d,e,
let n=|G|, and index conjugacy classes by j with representatives g_j and
sizes s_j. The character values are integers and

    lambda_chi(j) = s_j chi(g_j)/d

is an integer, since it is both rational and an algebraic integer. The
coefficient of the class sum K_k in e_chi-e_psi is

    N_k/n = (d chi(g_k)-e psi(g_k))/n.

Complex conjugation does not change the rational values, so no inverse
class permutation is needed in this formula. Swapping the two
idempotents sends

    K_j -> K_j + (lambda_psi(j)-lambda_chi(j)) (e_chi-e_psi).

Thus this algebra involution preserves the integral centre exactly when
n divides D_j N_k for all j,k, with D_j the displayed difference of
central characters. Equivalently,

    n divides gcd_j(D_j) * gcd_k(N_k).

The equivalence follows prime by prime, or from the equality of ideals
generated by all pairwise products with the product of the two generated
ideals. When the divisibility holds, the map and its inverse are the same
integral map, hence it is an automorphism of the integral centre.

A preliminary invariant is the least positive integer c_chi such that
c_chi e_chi lies in the integral centre. It equals

    c_chi = n / gcd(n, d chi(g_1), ..., d chi(g_k)).

An integral automorphism preserves this invariant, so pairs with unequal
values may be discarded. The implemented search constructs the full
integral matrix for every passing pair, verifies its square is the
identity, and tests whether every row has exactly one nonzero entry,
equal to plus or minus one. An integral invertible matrix with that row
property also has one such entry in each column.

Coverage is limited to pairs of rational characters in the 2,750 named
ordinary library tables. A negative result excludes only these individual
transpositions. It does not exclude longer cycles, permutations that
become integral only when combined, or automorphisms involving nonrational
components. Table names may duplicate group isomorphism types.

Hertweck's
[*Isomorphisms between centers of integral group rings*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0612436)
gives a prior monomiality result for nilpotent groups. The abstract,
introduction, Lemma 1, Lemmas 4--5 and Section 3 through Remark 8 have
now been read, including the proof of Theorem 7 (class-sum correspondence).
The nilpotent proof has not yet been audited. The screen retains known
positive cases as controls and makes no novelty assertion from a finite
absence of hits.

## Completed transposition screen and independent controls

All 2,750 table names completed, with 148,739 rational rows and
16,146,897 unordered pairs. Of these, 465,670 have equal conductors and
682 give integral transpositions. Every integral transposition is
monomial. The GAP process exited with status zero. The reproducible
Python audit compares the complete ordered table-name manifest and
every per-table arithmetic total; source, script, and raw-log hashes
are retained in `results/14.2-summary.json`.

The independent check uses the entire central-character matrix, including
nonrational columns, and exact matrix inversion. On ten actual groups,
all 76 rational pairs agree with the divisibility criterion: 19 are
integral and 57 are not. For each integral map, all products of class
sums are checked using 33,572 actual element multiplications in total,
giving 503 multiplicativity checks. This does not rely on the same
idempotent coefficient formula to construct the comparison matrix.

## Extension to longer permutations

For a block of rational idempotents with equal conductor, let pi be a
permutation of its indices. Fix all other primitive idempotents and map
e_i to e_pi(i). The class-sum matrix is

    M[j,k] = delta[j,k] + (1/n) sum_i lambda_i(j)
                                  (N_pi(i)(k)-N_i(k)),

where N_i(k)=chi_i(1)chi_i(g_k). In particular the coefficient at the
identity has numerator sum_i lambda_i(j)(chi_pi(i)(1)^2-chi_i(1)^2).
If this is nonintegral for any j, the permutation is discarded before
the full matrix is formed. Integral matrices are checked to have finite
order dividing the permutation order and determinant plus or minus one.
The inverse is therefore integral as well.

The extension considers each mixed-degree conductor block of size at
most six, in tables with at most 100 classes. Only degree-changing
permutations need screening, by the prior class-sum correspondence
theorem. This is a **single-block** search: simultaneous changes to
different blocks might cancel denominators and are not excluded.

Independent full-matrix controls enumerate every permutation of each
eligible block on S3 wreath C2, S4, and S3 times C4, including the
degree-preserving permutations omitted by the search. All 174 cases
agree exactly: nine integral and 165 nonintegral. In particular, all
120 permutations of the mixed-degree size-five block of the order-72
wreath product were covered. Only its identity permutation is integral.
The control process exited with status zero. The full block screen also
completed and exited with status zero: all 2,750 table-name records,
249 mixed-degree blocks, and 21,646 degree-changing permutations.
No tested permutation was integral. Exact name coverage, per-table
counts and the completion totals pass the retained audit. This leaves
the general conjecture unresolved.
