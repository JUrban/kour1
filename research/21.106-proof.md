# A parameter-free finite definable set generating an infinite subgroup

**Problem:** Kourovka Notebook 21.106, printed page 183 of the supplied September 2026 PDF.

**Status:** candidate complete negative solution, discovered 2026-09-10. The proof below is self-contained. It has not undergone external review or formal verification. Literature checks so far found the question still open in the relevant 2025 and May 2026 papers; novelty remains subject to a wider check.

## Statement addressed

A first-order formula with one free variable is called concise in a class of groups if, in every group in that class, a finite set defined by the formula generates a finite subgroup. Problem 21.106 asks whether all such formulae are concise in residually finite groups.

We give a parameter-free formula whose truth set in the integral Heisenberg group is precisely the two generators of its infinite cyclic centre. All quantification below is over group elements; there is no quantification over integers, subgroups, or generating sets.

## The formula

Use the group-language abbreviation

\[
[u,v]=u^{-1}v^{-1}uv.
\]

Define the following finite first-order formulae as syntactic abbreviations:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cent}(z)&:=\forall t\;([z,t]=1),\\
P(u)&:=\forall z\;\bigl(\operatorname{Cent}(z)\Rightarrow
                    \exists t\;([u,t]=z)\bigr),\\
Q(u,v)&:=\forall g\;\exists h\;\exists k\;
          \bigl(g=hk\ \wedge\ [h,u]=1\ \wedge\ [k,v]=1\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

Finally set

\[
\boxed{\varphi(x):=\exists u\;\exists v\;
\bigl(x=[u,v]\ \wedge\ P(u)\ \wedge\ P(v)\ \wedge\ Q(u,v)\bigr).}
\]

Bound variables in the abbreviations can always be renamed before substitution. Expanding them gives a parameter-free first-order formula in multiplication, inversion, equality, and the identity constant, with exactly one free variable, namely x. Informally, P(u) says that every central element is a commutator with u; Q(u,v) says that the set product of the two centralizers is the whole group.

## The group

Let

\[
H=\{(a,b,c):a,b,c\in\mathbb Z\},\qquad
(a,b,c)(d,e,f)=(a+d,b+e,c+f+ae).
\]

This is the group of upper triangular integral 3 by 3 matrices with ones on the diagonal, where a, b, c are the entries in positions (1,2), (2,3), (1,3). Matrix multiplication proves associativity. The identity is (0,0,0), and

\[
(a,b,c)^{-1}=(-a,-b,ab-c).
\]

A direct multiplication gives

\[
[(a,b,c),(d,e,f)]=(0,0,ae-db). \tag{1}
\]

Consequently

\[
Z(H)=\{(0,0,c):c\in\mathbb Z\}=\langle z_0\rangle,
\quad z_0=(0,0,1).
\]

Indeed, commuting with (1,0,0) forces b=0, and commuting with (0,1,0) forces a=0. Conversely all such elements are central by (1). The central element z_0 has infinite order.

Reduction of the three coordinates modulo any integer m >= 2 is a homomorphism onto the finite group H(Z/mZ), of order m cubed. Every nonidentity element has a nonzero coordinate, and reduction modulo an integer larger than the absolute value of that coordinate keeps it nonzero. Thus H is residually finite. In fact, powers of any fixed prime also separate its elements.

The group is generated by A=(1,0,0) and B=(0,1,0), since [A,B]=z_0 and

\[
(a,b,c)=A^aB^b z_0^{c-ab}.
\]

It is torsion-free: if a power with nonzero exponent is the identity, the first two coordinates first force a=b=0, and the third then forces c=0. Equation (1) shows that its nilpotency class is exactly 2.

## Interpreting the formula

Write pi:H -> Z^2 for the homomorphism pi(a,b,c)=(a,b).

**Lemma 1.** For u=(a,b,c), P(u) holds in H if and only if gcd(a,b)=1, where gcd is nonnegative and gcd(0,0)=0.

**Proof.** By (1), as t ranges over H, the third coordinates of [u,t] range over aZ+bZ. This is all of Z exactly when gcd(a,b)=1, by Bezout's identity. The first two coordinates of every such commutator are zero, so this is exactly the required surjectivity onto Z(H). In particular, P(u) excludes pi(u)=0. QED.

**Lemma 2.** If P(u) holds and pi(u)=(a,b), then

\[
\pi(C_H(u))=\mathbb Z(a,b).
\]

**Proof.** By (1), an element with first two coordinates (r,s) centralizes u exactly when as-br=0. Since gcd(a,b)=1, choose alpha,beta in Z with alpha a+beta b=1. Put k=alpha r+beta s. The relation as=br gives

\[
ka=\alpha ar+\beta as=(\alpha a+\beta b)r=r,
\qquad
kb=\alpha br+\beta bs=(\alpha a+\beta b)s=s.
\]

Thus (r,s)=k(a,b). Conversely, every such vector has zero determinant with (a,b), and any third coordinate is allowed. QED.

**Lemma 3.** Suppose P(u) and P(v) hold, and pi(u)=(a,b), pi(v)=(d,e). Then Q(u,v) holds if and only if ae-db is 1 or -1.

**Proof.** Both centralizers contain Z(H), and each is the full inverse image under pi of its projected subgroup. Therefore their set product is all of H if and only if

\[
\pi(C_H(u))+\pi(C_H(v))=\mathbb Z^2.
\]

For completeness, if the sum is Z^2, factor the image of any g as pi(h)+pi(k) with h in C_H(u), k in C_H(v). Then (hk)^(-1)g is central; absorb it into k to obtain the required factorization of g. The reverse implication follows immediately by applying pi.

By Lemma 2 the sum in question is Z(a,b)+Z(d,e). These two column vectors generate Z^2 exactly when their determinant ae-db is a unit of Z. One direction follows from the integral inverse matrix when the determinant is +/-1. For the other direction, preimages of the two standard basis vectors give an integral right inverse to the column matrix, so multiplicativity of determinant forces its determinant to be +/-1. QED.

## Conclusion

If H satisfies phi(x), its witnessing u,v satisfy P(u), P(v), and Q(u,v). Lemma 3 gives ae-db=+/-1, so (1) gives x=z_0 or z_0^(-1).

Conversely, u=A, v=B witness phi(z_0), and u=B, v=A witness phi(z_0^(-1)). Lemma 1 gives P for these elements, while Lemma 3 gives Q. Hence

\[
H_\varphi=\{z_0,z_0^{-1}\},\qquad
\langle H_\varphi\rangle=Z(H)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

Thus phi is not concise in the class of residually finite groups. The counterexample is already a two-generated torsion-free nilpotent group of class 2, and is residually a finite p-group for every prime p. This answers the stated Problem 21.106 negatively.

## Scope and checks

- This is a formula with alternating quantifiers, not a group word or an existential formula. It does not resolve Problem 21.105, and does not contradict the known conciseness of existential formulae in torsion-free groups of class 2.
- Parameters A, B, and z_0 are used only in the proof to describe witnesses and values. They do not occur as constants in phi.
- Formula interpretation is in the infinite discrete group H. No assertion that arbitrary first-order formulae commute with passage to finite quotients is used.
- The proof handles zero coordinates and negative determinants; P excludes only the zero vector and nonprimitive vectors.
- The original statement was visually checked on PDF page 183 on 2026-09-10.
