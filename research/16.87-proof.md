# A candidate negative answer to both parts of Problem 16.87

Date: 2026-09-10. Status: complete self-contained candidate proof;
independent review and further novelty checking remain outstanding.

## Statement being answered

Problem 16.87 in the supplied September 2026 Kourovka Notebook, printed
page 103 (also PDF page 103), asks whether a variety whose relatively free
group of every finite rank r has test rank r must be abelian. Part (b)
asks whether a nonperiodic such variety must be the variety of all abelian
groups. A test set is fixed **pointwise** by an endomorphism only when
that endomorphism is an automorphism. The entry was checked visually;
it has no star and imposes no torsion-free condition on the free groups.

## The counterexample

Fix a prime p. Let M_p be the variety defined by the two identities

    [[x,y],z] = 1,       [x,y]^p = 1.

This variety is nonperiodic, since it contains every abelian group,
including the infinite cyclic group. It is nonabelian: the group of
upper unitriangular 3 by 3 matrices over F_p belongs to M_p and is
nonabelian. This applies also to p=2, when that group is dihedral of
order 8.

We prove that the relatively free r-generator group G_r in M_p has test
rank exactly r, for every positive integer r. Thus this single variety
gives negative answers to both questions.

## A central endomorphism observation

Let G be any group, f:G -> Z a homomorphism, and c in Z(G). Then

    phi(g) = g c^{f(g)}

is an endomorphism. Indeed, centrality of c and additivity of f give

    phi(g)phi(h) = gh c^{f(g)+f(h)} = phi(gh).

It fixes every element of ker f, and

    f(phi(g)) = (1+f(c)) f(g).

Consequently, if f is onto and 1+f(c)>1, phi is not onto: its image under
f is contained in the proper subgroup (1+f(c))Z of Z. In particular phi
is not an automorphism. There is no requirement that phi fix c.

## Proof of the test-rank assertion

Write x_1,...,x_r for the free generators of G_r. Its abelianization is
Z^r, with the images of these generators as its standard basis. One way
to see this without assuming a presentation is to use that Z^r belongs
to M_p: the map x_i -> e_i extends to a homomorphism G_r -> Z^r.
Together with the natural epimorphism Z^r -> G_r/G_r', this gives inverse
maps. Let v:G_r -> Z^r denote the resulting exponent-sum map.

In every group in M_p, the commutator identities for a group of class
at most two give

    [x_i^p,g] = [x_i,g]^p = 1.

Thus each x_i^p is central in G_r.

Let S={s_1,...,s_k} be an arbitrary set of k<r elements of G_r.
There is a nonzero integer row vector w=(w_1,...,w_r) such that

    w v(s_j) = 0       (1 <= j <= k).

To obtain it, take a nonzero rational solution of these k homogeneous
linear equations in r unknowns and clear denominators. Divide all its
entries by their greatest common divisor, so that w is primitive.
Then f(g)=w v(g) is an epimorphism G_r -> Z and S is contained in ker f.

Choose an index i with w_i nonzero, put epsilon=sign(w_i), and set

    c = x_i^{p epsilon}.

This element is central and f(c)=p|w_i|>0. The preceding observation
therefore produces an endomorphism phi fixing S pointwise which is not
an automorphism. Hence no set of fewer than r elements is a test set.

Conversely, the set of all r free generators is a test set: an
endomorphism fixing every generator is the identity. Therefore

    test_rank(G_r) = r.

For r=1 the same argument works with S empty and phi(x_1)=x_1^{p+1};
the empty set is not a test set and {x_1} is one. This covers the
quantifier over every r>=1 and completes both negative answers.

## More explicit form and a broader sufficient condition

On generators the endomorphism above is

    x_j |-> x_j x_i^{p epsilon w_j}.

Its matrix on abelianization is I+p epsilon e_i w and has determinant
1+p|w_i|>1, giving another check of noninvertibility. Its action on every
commutator is the identity, since every generator was multiplied by a
central element. Arbitrary commutator components in S are therefore
fixed exactly, not just modulo a subgroup.

The argument in fact only needs G to have r generators, G_ab=Z^r, and
the image of Z(G) in G_ab to have full rank. Any nonzero homomorphism
to Z is then nonzero on a central element; replacing that element by
its inverse if necessary supplies c with f(c)>0. Thus such a group
has test rank r. In M_p the central powers supply the full-rank image
pZ^r. This general observation is not needed as a separate theorem
from the literature.

## Exact model for computational checks

The relatively free group can also be modeled on

    Z^r x F_p^{binom(r,2)}

with multiplication

    (a,c)(b,d) = (a+b, (c_ij+d_ij+a_i b_j)_{i<j} mod p).

Bilinearity of the extra term proves associativity. The coordinate
generators x_i=(e_i,0) generate: their commutators provide each central
basis vector. Collecting a word in reverse generator order gives a
unique expression using these generators and commutators. Conversely,
in any group satisfying the defining identities the usual class-two
collection gives this same expression, so the model is relatively free.
The polynomial multiplication also makes the centrality of x_i^p
visible directly.

The checker `scripts/verify_16_87.py` uses exact integer coordinates
and modular central coordinates. It compares actual substitution of
generator images against the closed formula for phi, including negative
exponents and arbitrary commutator factors. It is a sanity certificate;
the universal statement for all ranks and all finite sets is proved above.
