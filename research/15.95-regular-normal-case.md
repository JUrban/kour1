# Problem 15.95: the case of a regular normal subgroup

Proved restricted case, 2026-09-11. The general Mann--Praeger question
remains unresolved here. This elementary deduction is not counted as a
new complete Notebook solution, and its priority has not been established.

## Proposition

Let P be a finite p-group acting faithfully and transitively on a set
Omega with more than one point. Suppose P has a regular normal subgroup
N. If every derangement in P has order p, then P has exponent p.

## Proof

Choose a point alpha and write H=P_alpha. Regularity of N gives
P=N semidirect H and identifies Omega with N; H acts by conjugation.
Every nonidentity element of N is a derangement, so N has exponent p.

Fix h in H. Its order is a power of p. The cyclic p-group generated by h
acts on the underlying set of N. Its fixed-point count is divisible by p,
because |N| is divisible by p and every nontrivial orbit has size divisible
by p. Identity is fixed, so |C_N(h)| is at least p.

An element of the coset Nh fixes a point exactly when it is conjugate to h
by an element of N. Indeed, the stabilizers are the N-conjugates of H,
and the quotient map P -> P/N=H identifies the element of H in such a
conjugate uniquely as h. There are exactly |N:C_N(h)| such elements in Nh.
This is less than |N|, so Nh contains a derangement d.

The projection of d into P/N is h. By hypothesis d has order p, hence
the order of h divides p. This holds for every h in H.

Finally any element of P either is a derangement, of order p, or fixes
a point and belongs to a conjugate of H, of exponent dividing p.
Thus all elements of P have order dividing p, as required. QED.

## Audit and scope

- N need not be abelian; the proof covers all regular normal subgroups.
- Faithful transitive actions split over N because N is regular, without
  a general complement theorem for p-groups.
- The count of fixed elements in a coset uses the actual conjugation
  orbit of h, not an average fixed-point assumption.
- The p-orbit congruence applies to every h, including h=1.
- The argument does not show that a general transitive p-group satisfying
  the derangement condition has a regular normal subgroup. That would
  require a separate theorem.
- Degree one is excluded above. A faithful action of that degree has
  trivial group and exponent dividing p, but not exponent exactly p.

The Notebook statement on PDF page 95 credits Mann and Praeger and records
the known p=2,3 cases and an exponent bound in terms of p. The same general
conjecture is stated on
[Tong-Viet's author page](https://people.math.binghamton.edu/tongviet/Permutation_Group.html).
Only that page's statement was inspected; it supplies context, not a proof
premise. No claim is made that its update date establishes current novelty.
