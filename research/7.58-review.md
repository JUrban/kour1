# Internal review of the 7.58 prior-consequence packet

11 September 2026. No outside review and no new priority claim.

1. **Exact question.** Printed p.25 asks for an image criterion in a
   verbal wreath product. The page was rendered and inspected. The
   result uses its particular canonical embedding, not all embeddings.
2. **Actual base.** The verbal product of free V-factors is free in V
   on the disjoint union of their bases. The intersection with the
   Cartesian subgroup in the general definition changes nothing here.
3. **Action.** Left shift on edge origins preserves the right Cayley
   graph. The multiplication and inverse path conventions agree.
4. **Multigraph.** Positive edges with reversed endpoints remain
   distinct; a formal inverse edge is not another positive edge. Loops
   and repeated quotient-generator images are allowed.
5. **Tree.** Each tree path is finite, including for infinite Q and X.
   The basis automorphism and its inverse fix all tree letters, making
   their compositions visibly the identity on every generator.
6. **Retraction.** Tree letters map to 1, fundamental cycles are fixed,
   and the retraction descends through every verbal subgroup. No
   Schreier property for arbitrary relatively free subgroups is used.
7. **Kernel.** R maps isomorphically onto the based-loop free factor.
   Applying the retraction gives C intersect V(K)=V(C), so the kernel
   of the embedding is exactly V(R).
8. **Image.** The fibre over q is C_bar t_q. Fixed points of an
   idempotent endomorphism are exactly its image, proving both
   directions of the criterion.
9. **Witness.** Replacing each edge of b by its Schreier loop gives
   rho(b); appending the tree representative of q gives the desired
   preimage when the equality holds.
10. **Effectiveness.** Only finitely many substitutions are needed for
    a finite word. No unconditional solution of either word problem
    is asserted. This is the material limitation in the general question.
11. **Source convention.** The explicit relabelling
    a(q,x) -> u_x(bar(x)^-1 q^-1) is equivariant and sends our generator
    image to bar(x) u_x(1). Both source pages were visually checked.
12. **Abelian reduction.** The boundary is injective on the finite
    tree-chain span by leaf elimination, so its kernel is the cycle
    subgroup. The usual flow criterion follows.
13. **Prior work.** McCool1987 discusses exactly 7.58 on pp.139–140
    and proves the conditional algorithm in Corollary5. The splitting
    used here is in Theorem1(4). This packet makes that existing method
    explicit; it is excluded from the new-candidate count.
14. **Source scope.** All ten pages of McCool's mathematical text and
    only the relevant two thesis pages were read. McCool p.140 and the
    thesis pages were visually inspected. The unavailable 1999 survey
    has no role as a proof premise.
15. **Free-group controls.** All reduced edge words of length at most
    three are checked for five quotient/generator configurations,
    including loops, parallel edges, reversed tree paths, and S3.
    Direct path-continuity membership is compared with the retraction
    equality in 82,080 cases. The basis maps are checked both ways.
16. **Nonabelian finite control.** The base UT(3,3) is generated by its
    two elementary transvections and is free of class two and exponent
    three on those generators: collection bounds its universal group
    by 27, and the matrices realize all 27 elements. Swapping the two
    generators acts as (a,b,c) -> (b,a,ab-c). The entire 54-element
    semidirect product is checked against direct generation of the
    cyclic image of order six. All 19,683 base associativity triples
    and all 1,458 action/retraction product checks pass.

The controls validate formulas and conventions in the stated ranges;
the unrestricted theorem rests on the written free-factor argument.
