# Problem21.102: recent source and unresolved scope

12 September2026. Alexander Olshanskii, *Finite groups and rings
generating varieties of rapid growth*, arXiv:2606.04577v3,
31 July2026: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04577v3 .

Theorem1 proves that a finite group G has maximal growth base |G|
exactly when it has a unique minimal normal subgroup and that
subgroup is nonabelian. In that case log|F_n| is bounded above and
below by positive constant multiples of |G|^n, so the required
limit exists and is integral. Lemma3 handles finite joins of
varieties by taking the maximum. Proposition1 bounds the upper
growth base by [G:M] for abelian normal M. The paper includes
nilpotent, dihedral and Miller--Moreno cases.

Its Questions1 and2 on p.6 are precisely the general integrality
and existence issues in21.102. They remain open in this source.
An upper bound by an integer is not an integrality proof.

Reading scope: all group-theoretic argument in Sections1–3 was
read, along with the group examples and all questions in Section5.
Theorem1 and the questions were checked against images of pp.1
and6. Section4's ring proof was only scanned and is not imported.
The primary PDF and extracted text are archived. The lower bound
for nonabelian monolithic groups uses the source's two elementary
subdirect-product lemmas and generating-tuple count; that proof
was inspected. The cited older classifications and free-order
formula for Miller--Moreno groups remain imported.

The adjacent `21.102-nilpotent-normal-bound.md` extends the
coordinate-partition argument from abelian to nilpotent normal
subgroups and records a further positive case. It does not settle
the general question and its priority has not been established.
