# Problem 16.45: normal-intersection obstruction

11 September 2026. Exploratory plan, no solution claimed.

The intended maximum includes nonfaithful permutation representations.
Cameron's original and later papers explicitly say this, and it is
also forced by the Notebook's equivalent normal-bottom formulation.
Thus the extraspecial-group comparison with faithful bases alone is
not a counterexample: its elementary abelian quotient supplies the
missing base size.

Primary sources inspected for scope:

- P. J. Cameron, *Some measures of finite groups related to permutation
  bases*, https://webspace.maths.qmul.ac.uk/p.j.cameron/odds/baseabs.pdf,
  source extraction around Corollary 3.3.
- P. J. Cameron, *Independence and bases: theme and variations*,
  https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/27617/1/pjc_final.pdf,
  sections on the three maxima, Proposition 2 and the question following
  it (PDF pp.14--16). Those extracted sections were read, not the full
  paper. No proof is being imported beyond the definitions.

For a finite p-group P, let r=max d(S), over all S<=P. Burnside's
basis theorem gives mu'(P)=r: an independent set is a minimal
generating set of the subgroup it generates, and all such sets in a
p-group have size d(S).

Two sufficient certificates for b(P)=r are cheap to search:

1. An abelian subgroup S of rank r. Express S as a direct product of
   r nontrivial cyclic groups and omit one factor at a time. The r
   resulting subgroups have trivial intersection, irredundantly, so
   their coset actions in P give a minimal base of size r.
2. A subgroup S with d(S)=r and Phi(S) normal in P. Lift the r
   coordinate hyperplanes of S/Phi(S). Their intersection is Phi(S),
   irredundantly, and normality makes it the kernel of the resulting
   coset action of P. Faithfulness is not required.

Enumerate conjugacy classes of subgroups to determine r and check
these sufficient conditions. Every conjugate has the same rank and
the normality property is invariant under conjugacy. Any failure of
both tests is only unresolved, never a counterexample. Retain subgroup
counts, all d(S) frequencies and the number of witnesses of each kind.

Start with all p-groups of orders 2,3,4,5,7,8,9,16,25,27,32,49,64.
Use one GAP process under the existing 4-GiB ceiling; the six 14-GiB
n=7 workers remain untouched. Require actual exit zero, a clean log,
a sentinel, and hashes. If unresolved cases appear, export their full
subgroup lattices and independently enumerate irredundant intersections.
If not, consider a bounded extension or a structural proof, while
keeping the unrestricted problem open.
