# Omitting one opposite-root pair: a consequence of the carpet candidates

12 September 2026. A partial structural consequence, not a solution of
15.46. Its dependencies are the audited adjoining lemma for 19.61 and
the square inclusion for derived carpets proved in 19.62.

Let A be an elementary carpet, B its derived carpet, and fix a root p.
Put

    H = E(Phi,A),
    H_p = <x_p(A_p), x_-p(A_-p)>,
    N_p = <x_s(A_s) : s different from p and -p>.

Then N_p is normal in H, H=N_p H_p, and

    N_p intersect X_p = x_p(B_p),
    N_p intersect X_-p = x_-p(B_-p).                              (1)

For normality it suffices to conjugate a generator x_s(u) of N_p by
x_p(t) or x_-p(t), with their original carpet parameters. Every
commutator factor has a root different from p and -p: a positive
combination i p+j s cannot equal either p or -p when s is not parallel
to p. Its parameter belongs to the corresponding A subgroup by the
carpet conditions. Thus H_p normalizes N_p. The asserted factorization
follows directly from the generating set of H.

To see x_p(B_p) is contained in N_p, take a defining derived generator
c u^i v^j at p, with source roots r,s and i r+j s=p. Neither r nor s
equals p or -p, since that would make the other source parallel to p.
The commutator of x_r(u) and x_s(v) is therefore in N_p. Every factor
other than the factor at p also has root different from p and -p,
and hence is separately in N_p. Multiplying their inverses on the
appropriate sides isolates x_p(c u^i v^j). The argument allows any
factor order and does not assume that these factors commute. Addition
and inverses give all of x_p(B_p). The opposite-root argument is the
same.

Define a new carpet F by F_p=B_p, F_-p=B_-p and F_s=A_s otherwise.
It is a carpet: its inputs are original A parameters, so every
commutator parameter belongs to the original B at its destination;
that subgroup is contained in the designated F subgroup. The preceding
paragraph shows E(Phi,F)=N_p.

Apply the explicit square completion from the 19.61 proof to F. At
each stage the subgroups at p and -p remain exactly B_p and B_-p,
because B_p^2 B_-p is contained in B_p and its opposite analogue
holds by 19.62. This assertion uses the root-pairwise form of the
completion: an update at p uses only the current groups at p and -p.
The completed family C is a carpet and satisfies the known square
closedness criterion, so it is closed. Since N_p=E(Phi,F) is contained
in E(Phi,C), it has no root elements at p or -p outside those in (1).
This proves the reverse inclusions and completes the lemma.

The missing step for 15.46 remains substantial. If x_p(t) belongs to
H=N_p H_p, write it as n h. Then n lies in N_p intersect the full
rank-one group generated by X_p and X_-p. Equation (1) identifies its
individual root intersections, but does not identify this full
rank-one intersection or place n in H_p. Consequently the lemma does
not yet show x_p(t) belongs to H_p, even when the initial rank-one
subcarpet is closed.
