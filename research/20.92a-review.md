## Logical audit

1. **Exact question.** The page-161 image was visually read. The product
   has a minus sign, p-1 summands indexed 0 through p-2, additive scalar
   multiples 2^i a, and weights 2^(-i). All are preserved. Part (b) is
   starred and attributed to Trappeniers; this packet concerns part (a).
2. **Quantifiers.** Fix dimension k first. The sufficient prime bound is
   max(k,2^((k-1)k^(2k-2))) for k>=2, uniform over all its braces.
   No optimal threshold is asserted.
3. **Affine group.** The ordinary brace identity gives additive lambda
   and lambda_(a circle b)=lambda_a lambda_b. Gamma has exactly p^k
   elements, not the generally smaller order of lambda(A). Keeping the
   translation column is essential for regularity.
4. **Triangularity.** The fixed-vector orbit-count argument applies on
   every nonzero successive quotient. It gives a complete flag without
   requiring right nilpotence or a nonzero brace socle.
5. **Lazard applicability.** UT_(k+1) has class k<p. Every matrix series
   uses degrees at most k. Applying the subgroup theorem in the ambient
   matrix group gives the ordinary matrix Lie operations. The primary
   source asserts subgroup correspondence, not merely object correspondence.
6. **Linearity.** Linearity of log Gamma comes from that subgroup theorem,
   not from exp/log being bijections. A Lie subring of the elementary
   abelian additive group J is F_p-linear.
7. **Projection.** Zero translation in a logarithm implies zero translation
   in its exponential, which regularity makes identity. Equal dimensions
   then give an isomorphism. The exponential projection is not assumed linear.
8. **Pre-Lie sign.** The top block of [X_x,X_y] is [L_x,L_y]; its last
   column is L_x y-L_y x. This is exactly the left-symmetric identity.
9. **W and Omega.** The j-th exponential translation term is
   L_x^(j-1)x/j!. Consequently W has degree <=k and linear term x.
   The correct lambda formula uses Omega=W^(-1), not W.
10. **Formal BCH closure.** Finite-field pointwise equality does not
    imply polynomial equality. Instead universal truncated Lie BCH and
    scalar extension prove X_C=log(exp X exp Y) formally. This remains
    valid when substituted polynomials have degrees exceeding p.
11. **BCH degree.** A length-l associative monomial in X_x,X_y has
    variable degree l and lies in J^l. Lengths above k vanish. Thus
    C has degree <=k despite a larger naive composition bound.
12. **Flag.** Only L_x(V_i) subset V_(i+1), for arbitrary x, is used.
    The strong condition L_(V_i)(V_j) subset V_(i+j) is not assumed.
13. **Correction.** If a-W(x)=r in V_i, right multiplication by exp X_r
    changes the residual to r-exp(L_x)W(r), in V_(i+1). This calculation
    holds over F_p[a_1,...,a_k], so k corrections give a formal identity.
14. **Inverse and degrees.** A polynomial right inverse suffices since W
    is already bijective on the finite set. x_1=a and each later degree
    is at most k^2 times its previous bound. No polynomial automorphism
    theorem or inverse-degree theorem is concealed here.
15. **Linear term.** All corrections have zero constant term. Comparing
    degree one in W(Omega(a))=a gives Omega_1(a)=a, hence P_1(a)=L_a.
16. **Average.** Nonlinear degrees j disappear when 2^(j-1)!=1. The
    sufficient condition ord_p(2)>D(k) guarantees this. The degree-one
    sum is p-1=-1 and the leading minus cancels it. Repeated scalar
    values are allowed, so 2 need not be a primitive root.
17. **Prime bound.** If p>2^D and ord_p(2)<=D, then p divides a
    positive integer 2^ord-1<p, a contradiction. No conjecture about
    the distribution of primitive roots is required.
18. **Addition.** The extracted matrix is L_a in original additive
    coordinates. Omega proves polynomiality; it does not transport addition.
19. **Boundary cases.** k=1 follows from GL_1(F_p) having no nontrivial
    p-subgroup. p=2 is excluded from the averaging formula. Small-prime
    failures below are compatible with the eventual-in-p quantifier.
20. **Status.** The proof is complete relative to classical Lazard. No
    unresolved mathematical lemma remains in this route. External
    review and comprehensive novelty checking are still pending.

## Exact controls and limits

`scripts/verify_20_92a.py` implements exact sparse polynomial arithmetic
over Q and reads no GAP data. It checks the pre-Lie basis identity,
formal BCH closure at every correction, the residual flag, the identity
W(Omega(a))=a, and the linear part of lambda-I. It checks the weighted
formula by homogeneous coefficients and independent literal evaluations.

PSNZ Example 7.2 supplies two non-right-nilpotent pre-Lie examples,
with (j,k,y)=(0,0,1) and (2,3,2). The computed lambda degrees are 5
and 7; the correction degrees are [1,9,15,5] and [1,9,15,7]. The third
example is the associative ideal (t) in Q[t]/(t^5), with linear lambda-I.
Each is reduced at 5,7,11,17,31,127: 23,328 literal matrix-entry
comparisons in total. All comparisons required by the sufficient order
condition pass. At p=127, 2 has order 7 instead of p-1; both non-right-
nilpotent examples still pass. At p=31 the degree-7 example retains
nonlinear terms. Discrepancy means inequality with the recovered product;
we do not claim every discrepant operation violates the pre-Lie identity.

`scripts/verify_20_92a.g` independently constructs the actual affine
matrix groups from PSNZ Section 5, with i=k=0,y=1. It reads no Python
data. At p=5,7,11 their orders are 625,2401,14641. Every translation
occurs once, all logarithms form the claimed linear graph, all exp/log
inverses pass, and the recovered basis products satisfy pre-Lie. The
iterative inverse passes on 101 inputs per field. The average is checked
as a full matrix for every input a; the disagreement counts with the
recovered product are 400,2058,0. In each actual brace the cycle

    S -> S*Q=-P -> (-P)*R=-S -> (-S)*Q=P -> P*R=S

is verified. Repeating it proves non-right-nilpotence, so these controls
exercise the additional case. The published brace and pre-Lie examples
use their own coordinates; their structure constants are not asserted equal.

The initial Python run detected an index error in the *associative
