# 16.14: arbitrary-exponent class-two proof and exact controls

September 12, 2026. This extends the earlier exponent-four partial result
to every finite 2-group of class at most two. The original inequality in
all classes already follows from Sambale, as recorded in the earlier
`16.14-prior-result.md`. The new elementary proof and its stronger bound
add no complete candidate; the count remains 44. There are no outside
reviews, and priority for the refinement is unestablished.

The statement on printed/physical page 98 was viewed again. Its rank
of G/G^2 is the minimal generator number d(G), since G^2=Phi(G).
The hypothesis means every involution is central. No stronger condition
on elements of order four is substituted.

## Mathematical result and audit

Write Z=Z(G), Phi=Phi(G), r=rank(Z), and
c=dim_F2 Z/(Z intersection Phi). The new proof gives

    d(G)+c <= 2r.

Squaring defines q:(G/Z)[2] -> (Z intersection Phi)/Z^2. Its only
zero is zero: a central square root of x^2 would turn x into an
involution in the same coset of Z. Class two makes the source an
elementary abelian group and makes q quadratic. A parity count bounds
its source dimension by twice its target dimension. The exact sequence
for G/Phi accounts for the c term.

The proof explicitly checks the two points that the exponent-four
argument did not address: extracting a square root in the full centre,
and comparing the rank of (G/Z)[2] with the rank of (G/Z)/(G/Z)^2.
Neither assumes that G/Z, G', or Z itself has exponent two. Products
of Q8 and cyclic 2-groups attain the stronger bound, at arbitrarily
large exponent. The same theorem applied to subgroups bounds every
section's generator number by 2 rank(Z(G)).

The class assumption cannot simply be removed from this proof: for
Q16 the quotient by the centre is nonabelian, so its involutions are
not the vector space used here. This is a scope control, not a
counterexample to the original question.

## Finite models and independent verification

The native GAP models are the trivial group, C64, C2 x C4 x C8,
D8, Q8, Q8 x C8, Q8 x Q8, D8 x C4, and two coordinate groups.
The latter have elements (a,b,c) in Z/A x Z/A x Z/C, for
(A,C)=(4,2),(8,4), with multiplication

    (a,b,c)(u,v,w)=(a+u,b+v,c+w+a*v).

Here C divides A, so the bilinear extra term is well-defined and
gives an associative multiplication. The identity is (0,0,0) and
the inverse is (-a,-b,-c+a*b). Right translations by the coordinate
generators supply actual GAP permutation groups. The order-256
model has exponent eight and derived exponent four; all its
involutions are central. It directly tests a case outside any
assumption that the original commutators have order two.

GAP exports ten literal multiplication tables. The independent
stdlib Python checker reconstructs centres, square subgroups,
central quotients, both F2 coordinate spaces, and the entire map q.
It verifies 84,097 table entries and 291,200 associativity identities
with the third entry a specified generator. This is sufficient:
each left translation commutes with every generating right
translation, and reachability from the identity extends this to
every right translation. Thus associativity holds for all triples.
The table has an identity and all generator right translations are
permutations, giving a finite group. Inverses are also checked.

There are 401 representative-independence checks and 4,483 complete
polarization triples. D8 and D8 x C4 have three zeros of q and
noncentral involutions, as predicted by the proved equivalence.
Five deliberately corrupted tables or expected invariants are rejected.

A separate enumeration checks every quadratic map F2^n -> F2^s
with no constant term for 1<=n<=4, s=1,2, and n=5,s=1:
1,086,606 maps in total. Every case with n>2s has an even number
of zeros. A cubic anisotropic map F2^3 -> F2 is rejected by the
quadratic reconstruction, checking that the degree restriction is
being used. None of these finite ranges substitutes for the proof
at arbitrary dimensions.
