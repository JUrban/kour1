# A central-product construction for Problem 21.114

2026-09-12. **Partial result:** weakly ab-maximal finite groups need not be
metabelian. A certified construction has order8192 and derived length3.
The general bounded-derived-length question remains unresolved; no new
complete candidate is counted. Priority of this partial result is unestablished.

Write a(X)=|X/X'| for a finite group X. It is weakly ab-maximal if
a(H)<=a(X) for every subgroup H. This is the exact notion in21.114.

## A general construction with a cyclic central kernel

**Theorem.** Let P be a finite p-group, let Z<=Z(P) be cyclic of order
m=p^k with k>=1, and suppose Q=P/Z is weakly ab-maximal. For every r>=1
there is a weakly ab-maximal group G containing P such that

    |G|=|P|m^(2r),      a(G)=a(Q)m^(2r),
    derived length(G)=max(derived length(P),2).

In particular a cyclic central extension that raises derived length can
be embedded in a weakly ab-maximal group of the same derived length.

**Construction.** Put R=Z/mZ. Let E consist of triples (u,v,c) with
u,v in R^r and c in R, with multiplication

    (u,v,c)(u',v',c')=(u+u',v+v',c+c'+u dot v').

Bilinearity of the dot product proves associativity directly. The inverse
of (u,v,c) is (-u,-v,-c+u dot v), and its commutator with (u',v',c') is
(0,0,u dot v'-u' dot v). It follows that

    E'=Z(E)={(0,0,c):c in R},   E/Z(E)=R^(2r).

For the centre assertion, commuting with the coordinate unit vectors
forces every coordinate of u and v to vanish. A commutator of two such
unit vectors gives (0,0,1), proving the derived-subgroup assertion.

Fix an isomorphism i:Z->Z(E), and define

    G=(P x E)/{(z,i(z)^(-1)):z in Z}.

The two factors embed, because the displayed diagonal subgroup meets
each factor trivially. Identify the common image of Z with Z(E) and
still call it Z. Then

    G/Z = Q x V,       V=R^(2r).

Since E'=Z, we have Z<=G'. Consequently a(G)=a(Q)|V|, and the asserted
order formula follows immediately. The derived length formula follows
because G is a quotient of P x E and contains embedded copies of both.

**Weak ab-maximality.** Let H<=G. Since Z is central,
(HZ)'=H', and hence a(H)<=a(HZ). Thus it suffices to take H containing Z.
Put W=H/Z, viewed as a subgroup of Q x V, let U=W intersect V, and let
T be the image of W in Q. The exact sequence from W onto T gives

    a(W)<=|U|a(T)<=|U|a(Q).                         (1)

Indeed W' maps onto T', so |W'|>=|T'|; the order of W is |U||T|.
Write |Z/(H' intersect Z)|=p^t, where 0<=t<=k. Since the derived
subgroup of H/Z is H'Z/Z,

    a(H)=p^t a(W).                                 (2)

The lifts of U lie in H intersect E, and their commutators lie in
H' intersect Z=p^t Z. Thus the image L of U under reduction

    V -> V_t=(Z/p^t Z)^(2r)

is isotropic for the standard alternating form. For t>0 the pairing
exp(2 pi i omega(x,y)/p^t) is nondegenerate. Character orthogonality
therefore gives |L||L^perp|=|V_t|: this follows also by summing the
pairing over all pairs in L x V_t in either order. Since L<=L^perp,

    |L|<=p^(rt).

The kernel of reduction has order p^(2r(k-t)), so

    |U|<=p^(2rk-rt).                               (3)

For t=0 the same inequality is simply |U|<=|V|. Combining(1)--(3),

    a(H)<=p^(2rk-(r-1)t)a(Q)<=p^(2rk)a(Q)=a(G).

This proves the theorem. The isotropic subgroup L need not be free as
a module; the character proof covers all additive subgroups. The proof
also covers p=2, where E can have exponent greater than m.

## The certified derived-length-three instance

For each label j in {854,860}, the files

    results/21.114-central-j-p.json
    results/21.114-central-j-q.json
    results/21.114-central-j-map.json

define literal finite permutation models P_j, Q_j and a homomorphism
P_j->Q_j. The certificates and independent checker establish:

| Property | j=854 | j=860 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Order of P_j | 512 | 512 |
| Orders in its derived series | 512,32,2,1 | 512,32,2,1 |
| Cyclic central kernel order | 4 | 4 |
| Order of Q_j | 128 | 128 |
| a(Q_j) | 16 | 16 |
| Subgroups of Q_j checked | 380 | 396 |
| Largest a(H) for H<=Q_j | 16 | 16 |

Use either P_j and its kernel as P,Z in the theorem with p=2, k=2,
r=1. Then E is the upper unitriangular group of degree3 over Z/4Z,
and the resulting G has order512*4^2=8192, a(G)=16*4^2=256, and
derived length3. Thus a possible universal derived-length bound in
21.114 must be at least3.

The models were discovered using Schur covers of SmallGroup(128,854)
and SmallGroup(128,860). Those catalogue names and the Schur-cover
algorithm are not needed to validate the final finite hypotheses:
`scripts/verify_21_114_central.py` reconstructs groups directly from
permutations, verifies the entire homomorphism multiplication table,
enumerates all subgroups of each Q_j, and computes commutator subgroups
directly. Five corrupted inputs are rejected. The two constructions
are not asserted to be nonisomorphic.

## Why this does not give an unbounded family by iteration

**Proposition.** In the theorem, suppose d>=2 and P^(d-1)<=Z, where
superscripts denote derived-series terms. Then every central extension
X of the constructed G has derived length at most d.

Let K be the central kernel of X->G, and let A,B be the preimages
of the embedded P,E. Then X=AB and [A,B]<=K. Since this latter subgroup
is central, commutation with a fixed member of B gives a homomorphism
from A into K; it kills A'. Consequently [A',B]=1, and similarly
[B',A]=1. The full preimage of Z is B'K because E'=Z. Therefore

    A^(d-1)<=B'K<=C_X(A),

and A^d=1. Also B has nilpotency class at most3, being a central
extension of E, and hence B''=1. The identity
X'=A'B'[A,B], with commuting factors and a central last factor, now
gives X^d=A^d=1 for d>=2. This proves the proposition.

In the certified cases P_j'' has order2 and lies in the common cyclic
kernel of order4. Thus all central extensions of these constructed
groups still have derived length at most3. Repeatedly taking central
covers of these outputs cannot prove that derived lengths are unbounded.
