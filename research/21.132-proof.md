# Candidate solution to Problem 21.132

Developed 2026-09-10. Status: complete candidate argument, under internal audit;
novelty and outside review pending. The construction meets the definition of a
Golod group in Notebook 9.76. No claim of a Golod–Shafarevich *group presentation*
for the final group is made or required by that definition.

## Statement

For every prime p there is an infinite, at most four-generated, residually finite
p-group G with Z(G) isomorphic to C_p. Moreover, G is generated by 1 plus algebra
generators of a finitely generated, non-nilpotent nil algebra over F_p, as required
by the definition in 9.76. In fact G is residually a finite p-group.

All algebras below are associative and nonunital unless a unitization is specified.
An algebra is nil if each of its elements is nilpotent; this does not mean that
the algebra is nilpotent. The centre of an algebra means its commuting centre.

## 1. The graded Golod starting algebra

The graded Golod–Shafarevich construction provides a two-generated,
infinite-dimensional, positively graded nil algebra A over F_p, generated in
degree one, with finite-dimensional homogeneous components. Here is the standard
construction to make the grading requirement explicit.

Enumerate the nonzero elements f_i of the free nonunital algebra F_p<x,y>_+.
Put tau=3/4 and let d_i be the largest degree of f_i. Choose N_i >= 2 so large
that

    d_i N_i tau^(N_i) < 2^(-i-2).

Impose as homogeneous relations every nonzero homogeneous component of f_i^N_i.
Their degrees range from N_i to d_i N_i, so their total contribution to the
relation series at tau is at most d_i N_i tau^N_i. If R is their union, then

    1 - 2 tau + H_R(tau) < -1/2 + 1/4 < 0.

The graded Golod–Shafarevich inequality shows that the resulting unital algebra
is infinite-dimensional, and hence so is its positive-degree ideal A. Every
element of A is the image of some f_i and has zero N_i-th power. Thus A is nil.
The relations are homogeneous, so A has the claimed grading and finite-dimensional
homogeneous components. Since A is generated by two elements, nilpotence of A
would imply finite dimension; therefore A is not nilpotent.

This step is the classical Golod construction. A reference with the graded
inequality and the construction is M. Ershov, *Golod–Shafarevich groups: a survey*,
Sections 2.1–2.2, https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.0490 . The rest of the construction
and all properties used below are proved here.

## 2. Remove the elements annihilated by a sufficiently high power

For n >= 1 define

    J_n = {a in A : A^n a = 0},       J = union_{n>=1} J_n.

Each J_n is a two-sided ideal. Indeed, for a in J_n and c in A,
A^n(ac)=(A^n a)c=0, while A^n(ca) is contained in A^(n+1)a=0, since
A^(n+1) is contained in A^n. The J_n are increasing. They are homogeneous:
testing multiplication by homogeneous elements of A^n shows that the homogeneous
components of any member of J_n also lie in J_n. Thus J is a homogeneous ideal.

J is proper. If J=A, its two degree-one generators x,y would belong to some
common J_N. Then A^N x=A^N y=0, which kills every generator word of length at
least N+1. This would make A nilpotent, a contradiction.

Put B=A/J. Then B is nonzero, nil, positively graded, generated in degree one
by the images b_1,b_2 of x,y, and locally finite-dimensional as a graded algebra.
Its common right annihilator is zero:

    {b in B : B b = 0} = 0.                         (1)

To prove this, suppose B(a+J)=0. Then xa and ya belong to J, so there is an N
with A^N xa=A^N ya=0. Every generator word of length at least N+1 ends with x
or y after a prefix of length at least N. Consequently A^(N+1)a=0, so a is in J.

Since B is nonzero and satisfies (1), it cannot be nilpotent: the last nonzero
power of a nonzero nilpotent algebra is annihilated on the left by the algebra.
The grading is locally finite-dimensional and B is generated in degree one,
so a finite-dimensional B would have only finitely many nonzero degrees and
would be nilpotent. Hence B is infinite-dimensional.

## 3. A finitely generated graded nil algebra with zero centre

Let B^1=F_p 1 direct-sum B be the unitization. On the vector space

    T = B direct-sum B^1

define multiplication by

    (a,m)(b,n) = (ab, a n).

Equivalently these are upper triangular matrices [[a,m],[0,0]] over B^1;
in particular the multiplication is associative. T is generated as an algebra by

    s_1=(b_1,0),  s_2=(b_2,0),  e=(0,1).

Products of s_1,s_2 generate B in the first coordinate, and multiplication of
those products by e generates B in the second coordinate; e supplies its scalar
part. T is infinite-dimensional because it contains B in the first coordinate.

For k >= 1,

    (a,m)^k = (a^k, a^(k-1)m),

where a^0=1 in B^1. Since a is nilpotent, so is (a,m); thus T is nil.
Give T the positive grading

    T_n = B_n direct-sum (B^1)_(n-1),

where (B^1)_0=F_p and (B^1)_j=B_j for j>=1. This is a locally finite-dimensional
algebra grading; s_1,s_2,e all have degree one.

We claim Z(T)=0. A central (a,m) commutes with e, so (0,a)=(0,0) and a=0.
Commuting (0,m) with (b,0), for all b in B, then gives Bm=0. Write m=lambda 1+c
with c in B. If lambda is nonzero, m is a unit of B^1, since c is nilpotent;
Bm=0 would imply B=0. If lambda=0, equation (1) gives c=0. Thus m=0 in either
case, proving the claim.

## 4. The centreless Golod group is residually a finite p-group

In the adjoint group 1+T, take

    H = <1+s_1, 1+s_2, 1+e>.

Every element 1+t of 1+T has p-power order: for sufficiently large r,
(1+t)^(p^r)=1+t^(p^r)=1. Thus H is a p-group.

It is infinite. The F_p-linear span of H inside the unital algebra T^1 is a
unital subalgebra, contains s_1,s_2,e by subtracting 1, and hence contains T.
If H were finite, that span would be finite-dimensional, contradicting the
infinite dimension of T.

It has trivial centre. If 1+t is central in H, commuting it with each of its
three generators gives ts_i=s_it and te=et. Since these algebraically generate
T, t commutes with all of T. The equality Z(T)=0 gives t=0.

For residual finiteness, use the homogeneous ideals

    T_(>=N) = direct-sum_{n>=N} T_n.

Their intersection is zero. Each quotient T/T_(>=N) is finite-dimensional over
F_p and nilpotent, so 1+(T/T_(>=N)) is a finite group of order a power of p.
The induced homomorphisms from H into these finite groups separate its elements.
This proves residual p-finiteness directly; it does not rely on arbitrary
quotients of residually finite groups being residually finite.

## 5. Obtain the prescribed nontrivial finite centre

Let E=F_p z be the one-dimensional zero-multiplication algebra, and put S=T direct-sum E
with componentwise multiplication. It is again a finitely generated, positively
graded, locally finite-dimensional nil algebra; give z degree one. It is not
nilpotent, since it contains the infinite-dimensional degree-one-generated
algebra T.

In 1+S let

    G = <1+s_1, 1+s_2, 1+e, 1+z>.

The two direct factors commute and intersect trivially, so G is exactly H x C_p.
Therefore G is infinite, at most four-generated, a p-group, residually a finite
p-group, and

    Z(G) = Z(H) x C_p = C_p.

Finally S is a quotient of the free nonunital algebra on the four displayed
generators. Its nilness and non-nilpotence show that G has exactly the form of a
Golod group stipulated in 9.76. This supplies the requested construction for
every prime p.

## Scope and audit points

- The source statement imposes no indecomposability or fixed generator number.
  The final direct factor C_p is therefore permitted as written. Even the stronger
  requirement of being a Golod group under 9.76 is explicitly verified above.
- The use of a homogeneous ideal J is essential to the residual-finiteness proof.
  We do not assume residual finiteness for an arbitrary quotient algebra or group.
- Finite truncations cannot validate the zero-centre assertion: finite nontrivial
  p-groups always have nontrivial centre. The infinite centre calculation is
  an algebraic proof.
- The predecessor centreless construction of Sereda–Sozutov (2006), cited in
  Archive 11.101, was compared after this proof was written. Their Theorem 1
  quotients by the Levitzki radical to obtain a centreless Golod group. It does
  not state a residual-finiteness conclusion for that quotient. Our argument
  uses a homogeneous annihilator ideal and a triangular algebra extension;
  residual p-finiteness is proved from the surviving grading. This comparison
  does not establish that our construction or its consequences are new.
