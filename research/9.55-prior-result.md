# Problem 9.55: negative by p-adic permutation-lattice rigidity

2026-09-11. Complete negative deduction from Weiss' published
rigidity criterion, with the earlier Roggenkamp--Scott group-basis
result explicitly credited. Excluded from the possible-new count;
outside review remains pending.

## Statement and imported input

Let R be the ring of p-adic integers, let G be a finite p-group,
and let phi be an augmentation-preserving R-algebra automorphism of
RG. Then there are v in the unit group of RG and alpha in Aut(G)
such that, writing alpha also for its R-linear extension,

    phi(x) = v^(-1) alpha(x) v   for every x in RG.

Thus the automorphism requested in 9.55 does not exist. This
statement requires neither that phi fixes the centre pointwise
nor that it comes from an automorphism of ZG.

The non-elementary input is the following special case of Weiss'
permutation-lattice criterion. If P is a finite p-group, N is a
normal subgroup, and M is a finitely generated RP-module such that
its restriction to RN is free and M^N is a permutation R[P/N]-module,
then M is a permutation RP-module. A permutation module here has
an R-basis permuted by the indicated group.

Source: A. Weiss, *Rigidity of p-adic p-torsion*, Annals of
Mathematics 127 (1988), 317--332, Theorem 2,
https://doi.org/10.2307/2007056 . The exact statement, including the
case R=Z_p and theorem number, was read and visually checked in
R. Boltje, R. Kessar and M. Linckelmann, *On Picard groups of blocks
of finite groups*, Journal of Algebra 558 (2020), 70--101,
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2019.02.045 , Remark 1.8 on page 6
of the accepted manuscript dated 8 February 2019:
https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/21526/8/endoPic.pdf .

Weiss' original proof has not been independently read here. The
criterion is imported, not claimed reproved. We use it only over
Z_p, so no extension of coefficient rings or perfect-residue-field
variant is needed. The setup and Remark 1.8 of the accepted paper
were read, as well as selected Section 4 passages illustrating the
related bimodule argument; no full-paper reading is claimed.

## 1. The double action

Write P=G x G and N=G x {1}. On M=RG define

    (g,h) . m = g m phi(h)^(-1).

The two actions commute, and the inverses on the right ensure that
this is a left action of the direct product. Both G and P are finite
p-groups. As an RN-module M is the ordinary left regular module,
free of rank one.

Set s=sum(g : g in G). Comparing the coefficients of a vector fixed
by every left multiplication gives

    M^N = R s.

For every a in RG we have s a = epsilon(a) s, where epsilon is
augmentation. Since epsilon(phi(h))=1, the second factor G acts
trivially on R s. Thus M^N is the rank-one trivial permutation
R[P/N]-module. All hypotheses of Weiss' criterion hold, so M has
an R-basis B permuted by P.

## 2. The left G-action on the permutation basis

For any nonidentity g in G, its left action on RG has trace zero,
as is seen on the original basis G. On the new basis B the same
trace is the number of fixed basis vectors, viewed as an integer
inside R. Since R has characteristic zero, that number is zero.
Consequently every stabilizer for the action of N on B is trivial.

There are exactly |G| basis vectors, since M has R-rank |G|. Every
N-orbit has size |G|, so there is exactly one orbit. Choose v in B.
Then

    B = {g v : g in G},

and these elements form an R-basis. In particular right multiplication
by v on RG is a bijective R-linear map.

This makes v a unit in RG itself. Indeed surjectivity supplies a
with a v=1. Then (v a-1)v=0, and injectivity of right multiplication
by v gives v a=1. This step does not merely produce a unit in Q_p G.

## 3. Recovering the group automorphism

The right multiplication action of phi(G) permutes B, because B is
P-invariant. For each h in G, therefore, there is a unique alpha(h)
in G such that

    v phi(h) = alpha(h) v.

For h,k in G,

    alpha(hk) v
      = v phi(hk)
      = alpha(h) v phi(k)
      = alpha(h) alpha(k) v.

Cancellation by the unit v shows that alpha is a homomorphism.
If alpha(h)=1 then phi(h)=1, hence h=1. Since G is finite, alpha
is an automorphism. The displayed equation rearranges to

    phi(h) = v^(-1) alpha(h) v.

Both sides are R-linear, so this identity holds for every element
of RG. This proves the statement.

## 4. Return to the exact question

An augmented automorphism phi_0 of ZG extends, by scalar extension
to R, to an augmentation-preserving R-algebra automorphism phi of
RG. Its inverse extends in the same way. The result above applies.

The printed wording places unit conjugation before the group
automorphism. Either order is equivalent, and this can be checked
without a convention ambiguity. Define c_w(x)=w^(-1)xw. The proven
identity is

    phi = c_v composed with alpha
        = alpha composed with c_(alpha^(-1)(v)).

The conjugating element alpha^(-1)(v) is again a unit in RG.
This is precisely conjugation by a p-adic integral unit followed
by an automorphism induced from G. The additional centrality
assumption in 9.55 imposes no difficulty. Notebook page 35 was
visually checked in `references/cache/notebook-page35-947-955.png`.

## 5. Prior work, reading scope, and audit

The group-basis conclusion is classical: K. W. Roggenkamp and
L. L. Scott, *Isomorphisms of p-adic group rings*, Annals of
Mathematics 126 (1987), 593--647,
https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1987/126-3/p08 . The official
metadata was checked. The original article was not obtained;
public PDF attempts returned access errors. A same-title 1976
article by E. Spiegel is a different paper and is not substituted.

Scott's primary follow-up, *Defect groups and the isomorphism
problem*, Asterisque 181--182 (1990), 257--262,
https://www.numdam.org/item/AST_1990__181-182__257_0.pdf , was read
in full. It explicitly credits the p-group defect-group conjugacy
case to Roggenkamp--Scott on page 259. Its separate cyclic-defect
theorem is not needed here. The proof above instead isolates the
precisely stated Weiss criterion and derives the needed conclusion
directly.

Internal checks:

1. The ring is Z_p, not F_p. Trace zero implies no fixed basis
   vectors because the coefficient ring has characteristic zero.
2. P and its normal subgroup N satisfy the finite p-group
   hypotheses, and M is free over RN, not just projective.
3. The fixed module is R times the sum of all group elements.
   Augmentation preservation supplies its trivial quotient action.
4. Weiss supplies an actual permuted integral basis, not only an
   equality of ordinary characters.
5. Character comparison is used only after this basis exists, to
   prove that its left G-action is free and transitive.
6. The chosen basis vector is a unit in RG by a two-sided inverse
   argument. Rational conjugacy alone would not answer 9.55.
7. The resulting alpha is proved a group automorphism, and both
   orders of composition are displayed explicitly.
8. No centrality assumption enters the proof; the stronger
   augmentation-preserving statement gives the required negative.
9. No finite GAP search could verify the existence of the integral
   permutation basis for all p-groups. This packet is a theorem
   deduction and does not claim a computational proof.
10. The exact result is prior and contributes no new candidate.
    Weiss' input and the written deduction await outside review.
