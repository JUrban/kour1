# Problem 19.62: affirmative candidate in every type and characteristic

12 September 2026. Computer-assisted proof with explicit integer
derivations. This is a complete candidate, subject to independent review
and priority checking.

**Theorem.** Every derived elementary carpet over a commutative ring
with identity is closed, for every reduced irreducible crystallographic
root system of rank at least two.

Let A be the original carpet and B its derived carpet. We prove

    B_p^2 B_-p is contained in B_p for every root p.                  (1)

The already known fact that B is a carpet is part of the Notebook
statement; its source is Nuzhin's 2011 paper on factorization of carpet
subgroups. Applying the square closedness criterion then proves the
theorem. The criterion is Nuzhin's 2026 Theorem 2, and a direct proof
is also given in `19.62-closedness-criterion.md`, including an integral
symplectic-matrix argument that works in characteristic two and over
rings with zero divisors. No invertibility assumption on 2 or 3 occurs
in either (1) or that argument.

## Why finitely many arithmetic implications suffice

Use the standard Chevalley commutator convention with factors ordered
by increasing (i+j,i,j). For a nonopposite source pair r,s, put
c=|C_ij,rs|. The defining summands of B_p are

    c a^i b^j, with a in A_r, b in A_s and i r+j s=p.                (2)

Signs have no effect on the additive subgroup they generate. Expanding
the square of a finite sum of summands (2), then multiplying by a
finite sum at -p, reduces (1) to:

- the square of one summand at p times a summand at -p;
- twice the product of two independently chosen summands at p and
  a summand at -p.

Equal summand types in the second case still use independent variables.
These are respectively four-variable and six-variable monomials, with
integer coefficients determined by two or three commutator constants.
The expansion is an identity over Z, so it remains valid over every
commutative ring. The two families include all finite sums and signs.

## Reduction to root subsystems of rank at most four

Consider one target monomial. Write its two or three root decompositions
as i_j r_j+k_j s_j=epsilon_j p, where epsilon_j is 1 or -1 and both
integer coefficients are positive. Every s_j lies in the real span of
p and r_j. Therefore all its source roots lie in a subspace U spanned
by p and at most three additional roots. Its dimension is at most four.

Let Psi=Phi intersect U. Reflection in a root alpha of Psi preserves U,
because it subtracts a scalar multiple of alpha. Thus Psi is a reduced
crystallographic root system of rank at most four. Every source root
lies in the irreducible component Psi_0 containing p: a root is contained
in one component, so a positive linear combination of roots in different
orthogonal components cannot itself be a root in one component. The
source pair is nonopposite and contributes a nontrivial commutator term,
so Psi_0 has rank at least two.

Restriction of A to Psi_0 is a carpet. Every root string between its
roots stays in U, hence every applicable ambient root-string rule is
also a rule of Psi_0, with the same absolute Chevalley constants. The
restricted Chevalley root vectors give the standard normalization up to
signs, which do not change additive subgroups. Every derived summand
formed within Psi_0 is also a derived summand in the original system.
Consequently, certifying this target in the derived carpet of A|Psi_0
proves its membership in B_p. We do not assume that restriction
commutes with taking the full derived carpet.

The classification of reduced irreducible crystallographic root systems
now leaves exactly

    A2, A3, A4, B2, B3, B4, C3, C4, D4, F4, G2,

where B2 and C2 are isomorphic. Every one of these eleven systems is
checked separately. In particular, no extension by zero in a possibly
nonclosed root subsystem is used, and no unverified Weyl-orbit sampling
is involved. This reduction is the reason the finite certificate applies
to arbitrary rank, including E6, E7, E8 and the infinite classical families.

## The elementary logic of a certificate

For one target, assign independent commuting variables X_d to their
specified original subgroups A_(r_d). A node records a root r, a
monomial M and a positive integer a, with assertion aM in A_r.
Initial nodes are the variables themselves with coefficient one.

If preceding nodes give aM in A_r and bN in A_s, a carpet rule gives

    c a^i b^j M^i N^j in B_(ir+js), and hence in A_(ir+js).          (3)

If two applications give the same destination monomial with coefficients
u and v, integer Bezout coefficients give gcd(u,v) times that monomial
in B. This uses addition and integer multiplication, including negatives,
inside an additive subgroup; it never divides an element of the ring.

Every noninitial certificate node lists two applications of (3) and
claims their exact integer gcd. The checker validates roots, monomial
exponents, parent availability, integer powers, products and gcd. Its
last node is noninitial, so it certifies membership in the derived
subgroup, not merely in the original subgroup. The final coefficient
must divide the requested target coefficient. Induction on the saved
nodes therefore proves the target over every commutative ring.

The target generator renames variables by sorting (assigned root,
exponent) pairs, retaining separate variables when pairs coincide.
For identical targets it requests their coefficient gcd, which suffices
for every original coefficient. A separate target-coverage checker
enumerates all ordered pairs in the off-diagonal square expansion and
compares the entire resulting dictionary. Thus neither deduplication
nor the searcher's enumeration order is a logical assumption.

## Mathematical and computational inputs

The standard Chevalley construction and the finite classification of
root systems are mathematical inputs. The fact that the derived family
is a carpet is the known assertion supplied in 19.62. The new work is
the universal square inclusion, the rank-four reduction, and its finite
integer derivations. The accompanying direct proof of the known
closedness criterion makes the last group-theoretic step reviewable.

GAP exports integral Chevalley root operators. Python checks their
integral divided powers, all basis Jacobi identities, Cartan weights,
and polynomial root-group laws. Every commutator constant is also
checked in an exact polynomial matrix identity over Z[t,u]. The final
auditor verifies the expected Cartan matrices and reconstructs every
root set by reflections in its simple roots. Identification by a file
label alone is not accepted.

The C++ optimizer only discovers proofs. Independent Python checks the
saved integer derivations; its validation does not use the optimizer's
coefficient-valuation search or trust an unsuccessful search as evidence.
Forty-four deliberately corrupted certificates are rejected. The direct
symplectic argument has 2,592 integral matrix controls at ranks 1–8,
including centralizer equations eliminated entirely with unit pivots.
Those controls support the uniform written argument rather than replace
it by a bound on the rank.

The earlier G2 packet is frozen separately. The generic optimizer
reproduces its complete certificate byte for byte before generating the
other ten types. All 75 new search intervals and their controller exit
zero. Their coverage is disjoint and complete. Sources, raw outputs,
individual interval proofs, merged proofs, failures and actual exit
observations are retained in the local Git repository.
