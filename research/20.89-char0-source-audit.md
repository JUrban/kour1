## Imported statements and audit depth

* J. Tits, *Free Subgroups in Linear Groups*, Journal of Algebra 20
  (1972), 250--270, DOI 10.1016/0021-8693(72)90058-0.
  [Original paper, page 250](https://perso.univ-rennes1.fr/serge.cantat/Documents/Tits-Alternative.pdf).
  The primary PDF's indexed first page explicitly states Theorem 1
  for arbitrary linear groups over characteristic-zero fields: the
  absence of a nonabelian free subgroup implies virtual solubility.
  There is no finite-generation assumption in that statement. The
  adjacent Theorem 2 explains the different positive-characteristic
  case. The statement was checked against this primary indexed page;
  the full proof has not been newly audited. Direct downloads from
  both the historical and current Rennes domains returned HTTP 403,
  and the browsing tool could not open the full PDF. No cached PDF
  or visual inspection of that inaccessible file is claimed.

  These supply a faithful finite-dimensional representation for an

## What is proved here and what remains uncertain

The elementary torsion-free lifting lemma and the assembly of these
inputs are written out in the proof. The argument first shows the
generated subgroup locally finite-by-nilpotent, then proves virtual
solubility of that subgroup in characteristic zero, and only then
uses almost Engel closure in its virtually abelian algebraic quotient.
The finite lifted kernel is invariant under the whole ambient group.
This is the step that supplies the original uniform finite-set
quantifier.

Searches included the exact phrase "almost Engel elements" with
"characteristic zero", "subgroup", "virtually soluble", and the
problem identifiers 19.83 and 20.89. They did not identify an explicit
prior statement of this characteristic-zero closure theorem. This
limited search does not establish novelty. The constituent structure
theorems and virtually abelian consequence are prior work and are
credited. Results about almost *right* Engel elements or a different
meaning of "almost Engel" were not substituted for this question.

Positive characteristic still lacks both the unrestricted Tits step
and the torsion-free unipotent kernel. The proof does not solve that
case. No outside referee has assessed the present deduction, and
nothing has been sent to an author, forum, or repository host.
