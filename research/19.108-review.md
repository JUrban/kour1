# Internal audit of the 19.108 partial theorem

This is an internal review, not outside verification or a novelty claim.
The exact question was visually checked on Notebook page 145.

1. The orbit argument uses only a p-subgroup modulo p; every such
   subgroup of GL(2,F_p) is conjugate into the order-p unitriangular
   subgroup. No assumption that H itself has order p^4 is needed.
2. The stabilizer of a row vector (a,b) with a!=0 under that
   unitriangular subgroup is trivial. This justifies identifying
   the stabilizer modulo p with the congruence kernel H_0.
3. The fibers of an equivariant map of transitive H-sets have constant
   size. A single full fiber modulo p^2 therefore suffices for the
   contradictory p^5 count.
4. The powering congruence requires p odd. At p=2 the quadratic term
   can survive, and the actual order-16 controls correctly give
   failures. No assertion for p=2 is made.
5. An abelian normal A permits induction of its linear characters
   even in a nonsplit extension. Frobenius reciprocity bounds all
   irreducible degrees by |P:A|; equality forces irreducible induction
   and a regular orbit. No extension of lambda to a complement is used.
6. A nonfaithful putative counterexample reduces to a smaller group;
   the cyclic intersection with the kernel controls the original
   element order. Thus Wilde's faithful intersection lemma applies.
7. The direct-product conclusion A=C_(p^3)^2 uses normality to put
   both conjugate cyclic subgroups in A, and commutativity to multiply
   their orders. It would not follow from disjointness alone in P.
8. The dual action is faithful because its kernel fixes lambda. The
   primal versus contragredient convention does not change the orbit
   lemma: it covers every p-subgroup of GL(2,Z/p^3Z).
9. Translation invariance by p^2 in a coordinate kills the sum on
   every vector of order p^3, with an exact nontrivial p-th-root factor.
   A numerical Fourier tolerance is never used.
10. The numerical coverage is bounded. The final partial theorem is
    proved for every odd prime, but the full Notebook problem remains
    open here and the count of complete candidates stays unchanged.

Primary-source reading scope: Wilde's introductory statements,
Lemma 2.7 with proof, and Section 4's Theorem C proof were read.
Its external character-five input was not independently audited.
