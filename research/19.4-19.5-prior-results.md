# 19.4(a), 19.4(b), and 19.5: an established root-amalgam counterexample

Status: complete negative deductions from Allenby 2000, excluded from
the possible-new-priority count. Printed page 132 was visually checked.
The free groups in 19.4(a) are allowed to have rank one.

## Construction and exact answers

Let

\[
 G=\langle z,a_2,a_3,a_4,\ldots\mid a_n^n=z\ (n\ge2)\rangle,
 \qquad H=\langle z\rangle.
\]

This is the free product of the infinite cyclic groups
A_n=\langle a_n\rangle with their infinite cyclic subgroups
\langle a_n^n\rangle amalgamated. The normal form theorem for
amalgamated free products, applied to finite subfamilies and then
their increasing union, embeds every A_n and the common H in G.
Alternatively z's infinite order already follows from the map
G -> (Q,+) sending z to 1 and a_n to 1/n.

The element z is central: it is a power of each generator a_n.
Moreover,

\[
 G/H\cong *_{n\ge2} C_n.
\]

The quotient is nonabelian, so this is a nonabelian example even
though each factor A_n is cyclic. Every factor is both a finitely
generated free group and a finitely generated free abelian group.

We have

\[
 \lambda(G)=\mu(G)=\psi(G)=H\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

Consequently both assertions in 19.4 are false. In 19.5, the torsion
subgroup of H is trivial whereas psi(G)=H is not; its assertion is
also false.

## Prior theorem and source attribution

R. B. J. T. Allenby, *The existence and location of the near Frattini
subgroup in certain generalized free products*, Houston Journal of
Mathematics 26 (2000), no. 3, 463–468,
[primary journal PDF](https://www.math.uh.edu/~hjm/restricted/pdf26(3)/03ALLENBY.pdf),
Theorem 4, proves more generally that the same construction with
positive root degrees n_1,n_2,... has lambda(G)=mu(G)=H exactly
when the sequence of degrees is unbounded.

Our choice 2,3,4,... is a direct instance. Allenby's Section 5 also
credits C. Y. Tang's 1972 prime-root construction in connection with
the ordinary Frattini subgroup. No novelty is asserted for the group
or for the near Frattini calculation.

## A direct check of the essential lower bound

Here is a separate proof that z is a non-near generator, using the
exact definition on Notebook page 132. Let S be any subgroup such
that K=\langle S,z\rangle has finite index d in G. Because z is
central, S is normal in K and K/S is cyclic, generated by zS.

If S had infinite index in G, then K/S would be infinite cyclic.
There would be a homomorphism f:K -> Z with f(z)=1. The action of
G on its d cosets of K shows that g^{d!} belongs to K for every g
in G; normality of K is not required. In particular, a_n^{d!} is in K
for every n. Its defining relation gives

\[
 n f(a_n^{d!})=f(z^{d!})=d!.
\]

Taking n>d! is impossible. Thus S has finite index in G, proving
z in lambda(G), and hence H <= lambda(G).

For the upper bound one can apply Allenby Theorem 2 after regrouping
the factors as A_2 *_H B, where B is the amalgam of the factors
with n>=3. Both inclusions of H are proper, and H remains infinite
cyclic and central. The theorem gives mu(G) <= core_G(H)=H.
Together with lambda(G) <= mu(G), this establishes the displayed
equality. This route imports the same published upper-bound theorem;
the direct lower-bound proof is not presented as a new proof of all
its dependencies.

The infinite family is essential. No finite prefix of the presentation
is claimed to be a counterexample. In a finite prefix one can clear
the finitely many denominators and map its central generator
nontrivially into Z; that is exactly what the infinite root family
prevents even in arbitrary finite-index subgroups.
