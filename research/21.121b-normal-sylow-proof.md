# A sharp exponent-two partial result for 21.121(b)

12 September 2026. The general question remains unresolved here.
This elementary partial deduction and its sharpness example have no
established priority and await outside review. They add no complete
candidate to the count.

Fix a prime p. Suppose an abstract group Gamma satisfies:

1. Every finite subgroup has a normal Sylow p-subgroup.
2. There is a constant J such that every finite p'-subgroup H of Gamma
   contains an abelian normal subgroup A with [H:A] <= J.

**Theorem.** For every finite G <= Gamma with Sylow p-subgroup P != 1,
there is an abelian normal p'-subgroup B of G such that

    [G:B] <= J |P| (|P/Phi(P)|-1) < J |P|^2.             (1)

For P=1 the bound [G:B] <= J holds. Consequently Gamma is p-Jordan
with exponent at most two. For every prime p, exponent two is optimal
within this class.

In particular (1) applies if Gamma is already p-Jordan and satisfies
condition 1: its defining bound restricted to p'-subgroups gives
condition 2, with the same J and no use of its original exponent.

## 1. Abelian coprime automorphisms of a finite p-group

Let P != 1 be a finite p-group, and let C be an abelian p'-subgroup of
Aut(P). Put |P/Phi(P)|=p^d. Then

    |C| <= p^d-1.                                       (2)

Here Phi(P) is the intersection of its maximal subgroups. Every maximal
subgroup of a finite p-group is normal of index p: this follows, for
example, by induction using a nontrivial central subgroup of order p,
according as it is contained in the maximal subgroup or not. Thus
P/Phi(P) is elementary abelian. A tuple lifting a basis of that quotient
generates P; otherwise a maximal subgroup containing the tuple would
contain all of P/Phi(P), a contradiction.

Let K be the kernel of Aut(P) -> Aut(P/Phi(P)). Fix a basis of the
quotient. K acts on the tuples of its lifts, of which there are
|Phi(P)|^d. The action is free, since each lift tuple generates P and
an automorphism fixing its entries is the identity. Hence |K| divides
|Phi(P)|^d and is a power of p. Therefore C embeds faithfully in
GL_d(F_p).

For clarity, the needed bound on such a linear abelian p'-group is
also elementary. Its module V=F_p^d is completely reducible: averaging
a projection over C gives an invariant complement, since |C| is
invertible in F_p. Write V as a sum of simple modules. For a simple
constituent W, the commutative algebra generated by C on W is a finite
field: a simple module over the commutative algebra F_p[C] is a quotient
by a maximal ideal. If dim(W)=d_W, this field has p^(d_W) elements,
and the image of C acts by its nonzero scalars.

Faithfulness therefore embeds C in a product of groups of orders
p^(d_W)-1, with one factor for each constituent. The constituent
dimensions sum to d. Since (u-1)(v-1) <= uv-1 for integers u,v>=2,

    |C| <= product_W (p^(d_W)-1) <= p^d-1.

This proves (2), including the trivial automorphism group C. No
faithfulness of an original action on P is assumed when applying the
lemma: first divide its acting group by the centralizer of P.

## 2. A normal abelian subgroup of the whole finite group

Let G be finite with normal Sylow p-subgroup P. The Schur–Zassenhaus
theorem supplies a Hall p'-complement H, so G=P semidirect H. Existence
is all that is used; no choice of a characteristic complement or a
soluble complement is assumed.

Choose A normal abelian in H with [H:A] <= J, and set

    B=C_A(P).

Because H normalizes both A and P, it normalizes B. Every element of
P centralizes B by its definition. Thus B is normal in G=PH. It is
abelian and has order prime to p, being a subgroup of A.

The faithful action of A/B on P is an abelian coprime action. If
P != 1, (2) gives

    [G:B] = |P| [H:A] [A:B]
           <= J |P| (|P/Phi(P)|-1),

as required. If P=1, take B=A in G=H. This proves the theorem's bound.
The normality requirement on A inside H is essential to this proof;
an arbitrary large abelian subgroup would not justify normality of B.

## 3. Optimal exponent two

Let k be an algebraic closure of F_p, and take the affine group

    Gamma = {x -> ax+b : a in k^*, b in k}.

This group is locally finite. A finite collection of its coefficients
lies in a finite subfield, and its generated subgroup lies in the
finite affine group of that subfield.

Its translation group V is elementary abelian of exponent p, while
Gamma/V is k^*, all of whose finite subgroups have order prime to p.
For any finite G <= Gamma, its intersection P=G intersect V is
therefore its normal Sylow p-subgroup. If G is a p'-group, projection
to k^* is injective, so G is abelian (in fact cyclic). Hence the first
two hypotheses hold with J=1. Section 2 gives a p-Jordan bound at
exponent two.

For q=p^n, Gamma contains G_q=AGL_1(F_q), of order q(q-1), with normal
translation subgroup P_q of order q. Every normal p'-subgroup N of
G_q commutes with P_q: [N,P_q] is contained in both normal subgroups
and hence in their trivial intersection. But C_(G_q)(P_q)=P_q, since
a scalar commuting with all translations has scalar coefficient one.
Thus N=1. The least index of a normal abelian p'-subgroup is exactly
q(q-1).

For every e<2 the ratio

    q(q-1)/q^e = q^(2-e)(1-1/q)

is unbounded as n tends to infinity. No p-Jordan bound at exponent
e<2 can hold. Consequently e(Gamma)=2, and it is attained. This
affine example works for every prime p and needs no conjecture about
primes in arithmetic progressions. It does not answer 21.121(a),
whose separate nonattainment example remains distinct.

## 4. Boundary of the partial result

Finite subgroups of an arbitrary p-Jordan group need not have normal
Sylow p-subgroups. Nothing here reduces that general case to Section 2.
In particular this does not prove the upper bound three requested in
21.121(b). The finite computations accompanying the proof check its
subgroup construction and sharpness formulas in bounded models; the
proof, not those computations, supplies the arbitrary-size assertion.
