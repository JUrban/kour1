# Problem 5.25: an explicit prior negative answer

11 September 2026. Excluded from the new-candidate count.

Bludov--Kopytov--Rhemtulla, *Normal relatively convex subgroups of
solvable orderable groups*, Algebra and Logic 48 (2009), 163--172,
Example 3.1, explicitly answers Problem 5.25 negatively. Its constructed
group H is totally orderable, soluble of derived length three, and has
abelianization of exponent two. The paper distinguishes its auxiliary
right-orderable group from the final totally orderable group, so this
is not merely a left-orderable counterexample.
[Primary paper](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10469-009-9050-4.pdf).

The construction lifts a metabelian group generated by an integer-indexed
sequence of successive inverting elements into an extension by a free
abelian module. A normal series with conjugation-invariant orders on
its abelian factors establishes total orderability. Relations express
the additional generators as commutators and the squares of the lifted
generators as products of commutators.

Reading scope: introduction, notation, and Example 3.1 with Lemma 3.2,
formulas (6)--(17), and Remark 3.3, pp.168--171, were read. Pages 170
and 171 were visually checked. The other sections were searched but
their proofs were not audited. The omitted consistency calculations
for the module action and the exact claimed factor bases have not
been independently reconstructed. This is therefore an identified
prior resolution, not a claim of a fully independent proof.

## Local sign audit

There is an apparent sign misprint in displayed formula (17), under
the commutator convention [u,v]=u^-1 v^-1 u v used by the accompanying
formula b_(i,j)=[b_(i,j+1),h_(i+1)]. At f=1 and epsilon=1, formula (11)
gives, directly,

    b_(i,1)^(c_(i+1)) = b_(i,1) a_i a_i^(c_(i+1)).

The abelian module implies that conjugating its elements by h_(i+1)
and by c_(i+1) agrees. Therefore

    [b_(i,1),h_(i+1)] = a_i a_i^(c_(i+1)).

The printed (17) instead has negative exponents on both a factors.
The positive expression is consistent with the subsequent square
formula: if t=a_i a_i^(c_(i+1)), displayed (16) gives
h_i h_i^(h_(i+1))=t, and t commutes with h_i. Consequently

    [h_(i+1),h_i] = (h_i^(h_(i+1)))^-1 h_i = t^-1 h_i^2,
    h_i^2 = [h_(i+1),h_i] [b_(i,1),h_(i+1)].

For the conclusion that the abelianization has exponent two, even
replacing t by its inverse would not matter: either belongs to H'.
This local correction does not replace the unaudited module checks.

## Consequence for the earlier exploration

The metabelian positive argument in `5.25-exploration.md` remains valid.
The suggested extension lemma there must not be assumed. Indeed,
accepting the published example, choose a finitely generated subgroup
K of H of derived length three, by taking the finitely many elements
in a nontrivial second-derived commutator. Then K intersect H' is
metabelian and has finite index in K, because K/(K intersect H') is
a finitely generated group of exponent two. But K is bi-orderable
and is not metabelian. Thus even the proposed finite-extension lemma
fails at derived length two. This explains why that induction stalls.
