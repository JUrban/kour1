## 17.48: stability of a free product

The supplied Notebook asks whether a free product of two groups with
stable first-order theories again has stable theory.

Z. Sela, *Diophantine Geometry over Groups X: The Elementary Theory
of Free Products of Groups*, [arXiv:1012.0044v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1012.0044v1),
Theorem 8.1, states precisely the affirmative answer. This theorem
allows arbitrary groups A and B. The introduction identifies Jaligot's
question as the motivation for the work.

Reading scope: introduction, Theorem 6.3 and the initial part of its
proof, Section 8's introductory discussion and Theorems 8.1–8.2,
the final parameter-removal step of the proof of Theorem 8.1, and
Theorem 8.7 with its proof. The intervening resolution and stability
arguments have not been audited. The full paper is cached locally.

Theorem 6.3 gives existence of a uniform finite reduction of sentences
to the factors. The associated resolutions are explicitly noncanonical.
No effective construction was established in this reading. In
particular, this note does **not** claim an answer to the decidability
question 17.117.

## 18.56: finite core-2 2-groups

The hypothesis is that G is a finite 2-group of order greater than two
and every subgroup H satisfies |H:core_G(H)| <= 2. The question asks
for an abelian subgroup of index four.

B. Wilkens, *More on core-2 2-groups*, Journal of Group Theory 20
(2017), 193–225, [DOI:10.1515/jgth-2016-0035](https://doi.org/10.1515/jgth-2016-0035),
states that G has an abelian subgroup containing Phi(G), of index
at most four. The publisher's abstract explicitly identifies this as
an affirmative answer to Kourovka Problem 18.56.

“At most four” gives “four” here: if the obtained abelian subgroup A
has index 1 or 2, then |G| >= 4 and the finite abelian 2-group A has
a subgroup of the necessary order |G|/4. Its index in G is four.
Thus the order assumption in the printed problem removes any small
exception to the exact-index wording.

Reading scope: publisher-indexed abstract, introduction, main theorem,
and introductory corollary. The 33-page proof is not audited or used
as independently checked evidence. No GAP search was launched, since
the exact question is already answered by the primary source.
