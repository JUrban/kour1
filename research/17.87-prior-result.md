# Problem 17.87: intermediate growth with trivial Schur multiplier

Status: complete consequence of prior results, excluded from the count
of candidates with possible new priority. Recorded 11 September 2026.
The supplied question was visually checked on printed page 113.

There is a finitely generated group U of intermediate growth with

    H_1(U,Z) = H_2(U,Z) = 0.

In particular its Schur multiplier is finitely generated: it is trivial.
Take the universal central extension of an infinite finitely generated
simple group of intermediate growth constructed by Nekrashevych.
The checks below do not require the central kernel to be finitely
generated or finite.

## 1. Fixing a published group

Let B be the explicitly defined group of Cantor-set homeomorphisms in
Section 8.1 of Volodymyr Nekrashevych, *Palindromic subshifts and simple
periodic groups of intermediate growth*, Annals of Mathematics 187
(2018), 667–719,
[publisher PDF](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v187-n3-p02-p.pdf),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2018.187.3.2).
Its twelve displayed generators are a_i,b_i,c_i,d_i for i=0,1,2.
Theorem 8.1 gives intermediate growth and says that P=[B,B] is simple
and of finite index in B. The construction produces an infinite P.

Thus P is finitely generated, infinite, nonabelian, and perfect. It has
intermediate growth: finite-index subgroups have the same growth type.
One could equivalently use the simple groups supplied by Theorem 1.2.
The construction and growth theorem are imported, not reproved here.

Choose any finite generating tuple p_1,...,p_d of P, let F be the free
group on x_1,...,x_d, and let R be the kernel of x_i -> p_i. Define

    U = [F,F] / [F,R].

This specifies the group up to canonical isomorphism over P,
independently of the chosen free presentation. No finite presentation
of P or U is assumed.

## 2. Perfectness, centrality and finite generation

Set E=F/[F,R] and C=R/[F,R]. The subgroup C is central in E, and
E/C=P. Since P is perfect, E=E'C. Hence

    E' = [E'C,E'C] = [E',E'].

Thus U=E' is perfect. The restriction U -> P is onto, with central
kernel C intersect U. These arguments also show [F,R] is contained
in [F,F], as required for the displayed construction.

Choose lifts u_i in U of the p_i and put H=<u_1,...,u_d>. If K is
the kernel of U -> P, then U=HK, with K central. Consequently

    U = U' = [HK,HK] = H' <= H.

Therefore the d lifts generate U. In particular finite generation
does not require any assumption about the size or rank of K.

## 3. Universality and the Schur multiplier

Given a central extension q:X -> P, lift the free generators of F to
X. Every element of R maps into ker(q), so [F,R] maps to 1.
Restriction gives a homomorphism U -> X over P. If two such maps
exist, their pointwise quotient is a homomorphism from the perfect
group U into the abelian central kernel of q. It is trivial. This
proves the universal central extension property.

We recall the standard Hopf formula, for any free presentation J=L/S:

    H_2(J,Z) = (S intersect [L,L]) / [L,S].

It identifies the kernel of the above universal central extension of
a perfect group with that group's Schur multiplier. This is the sole
standard homology formula used here. See, for example, Section 2 of
Bardakov–Lavrenov–Neshchadim,
[arXiv:1710.02330](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02330),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.4310/HHA.2019.v21.n1.a12), already consulted
for Problem 19.9.

Here is the group-theoretic proof that the multiplier of U itself
vanishes. Form its universal central extension q:V -> U by the same
free-presentation construction. Both U and V are perfect. Let
p:U -> P be the extension already constructed.

The composite pq:V -> P is central. Indeed, for z in its kernel,
q(z) is central in U, so [z,v] belongs to the central subgroup ker(q)
for every v in V. With z fixed, this commutator map is a homomorphism
V -> ker(q), since its values are central. Perfectness of V makes it
trivial. Thus z is central in V.

Universality of U -> P supplies s:U -> V over P. The composite qs
and the identity of U are maps U -> U over P, so uniqueness gives
qs=id_U. Thus V=s(U) ker(q), with trivial intersection and central
kernel. Since V is perfect,

    V = V' = s(U)' = s(U).

It follows that ker(q)=1. The Hopf formula now gives H_2(U,Z)=0,
and perfectness gives H_1(U,Z)=0.

## 4. A complete central-extension growth argument

The following is a special case of Zheng's stronger FC-central
extension lemma. A direct block-counting proof is included to check
that an infinitely generated central kernel causes no problem.

**Lemma.** If a finitely generated group E maps onto a group Q with
central kernel, and Q has subexponential growth, then E has
subexponential growth.

**Proof.** Fix a finite symmetric generating set S of E and use its
image to measure word length in Q. Write B_E(r) and B_Q(r) for balls
of radius r, including the identity, and choose a set-theoretic section
t:Q -> E with t(1)=1. For each fixed positive integer r, put

    D_r = {u t(pi(u))^(-1) : u in B_E(r)}.

This is a finite subset of the central kernel. Let k_r=|D_r|. A word
of length at most mr can be split into m consecutive blocks of length
at most r, padding with empty blocks. If the block values are u_i,
write u_i=z_i t(q_i), where z_i lies in D_r and q_i in B_Q(r).
Centrality permits the exact rearrangement

    u_1 ... u_m = (z_1 ... z_m) t(q_1) ... t(q_m).

There are at most |B_Q(r)|^m choices for the ordered tuple (q_i).
Because the z_i commute, their product is determined by the number
of times each element of D_r occurs. There are at most (m+1)^k_r
such multiplicity vectors. Hence

    |B_E(mr)| <= (m+1)^k_r |B_Q(r)|^m.

For arbitrary n, use m=ceiling(n/r) and monotonicity of balls. For
this fixed r, divide the logarithm by n and let n tend to infinity:

    limsup_n log|B_E(n)| / n <= log|B_Q(r)| / r.

The right side tends to zero as r tends to infinity, proving the
claim. The exponent k_r is finite for each fixed r; it need not be
uniformly bounded. No control on the lengths of section values is
needed. This completes the proof.

Apply the lemma to U -> P, using Section 2 for finite generation.
It proves that U has subexponential growth. On the other hand, the
quotient map sends balls of U onto balls of P for the corresponding
generating sets. Polynomial growth of U would imply polynomial
growth of P, a contradiction. Therefore U has intermediate growth,
as required.

## 5. Attribution and reading limits

Tianyi Zheng, *On FC-central extensions of groups of intermediate
growth*, [arXiv:2001.07814v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07814v1),
Lemma 3.1 and its full proof, including Claim 3.2, were read, as were
the initial definitions and Example 3.4. The source imposes no finite
generation assumption on the kernel. The proof above treats only
central kernels, and requires neither the FC-group structure theorem
nor a symmetric section of the quotient map. The rest of Zheng's
paper was not audited.

For Nekrashevych, the introduction, Theorems 1.1–1.2, the complete
definition in Section 8.1 and Theorem 8.1, and the displayed proof of
Proposition 8.8 were read. Portions of the final simplicity argument
were also read. The full fragmentation, periodicity and growth proofs
were not independently audited; Theorem 8.1 is used as a published
input. The precise reading scope is also recorded in the audit JSON.

The standard universal-extension argument is written in full above.
Neither that argument nor preservation of subexponential growth is
claimed as new. These prior ingredients give the requested group by
a short deduction, so this entry is excluded from the count of
candidates with possible new priority. The limited searches did not
find an explicit earlier statement of the 17.87 answer; this does not
establish novelty.
