## Explicit construction

Let `Omega=F_3^2`, with origin `o=(0,0)`. Let `H` be the group of eight
signed coordinate permutations

\[
(x,y)\longmapsto(\varepsilon_1 x,\varepsilon_2 y)
\quad\text{or}\quad
(x,y)\longmapsto(\varepsilon_1 y,\varepsilon_2 x),
\qquad \varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2\in\{1,-1\}.
\]

It is the dihedral group of order eight, generated by
`d(x,y)=(-x,y)` and `s(x,y)=(y,x)`. Let

\[
Q=\mathbb F_3^2\rtimes H
\]

act affinely on `Omega`. This is a faithful transitive group of order 72,
and its stabilizer at the origin is `H`.

Let `E=C_2^Omega` be the elementary abelian group of order 512, with
basis elements `z_w`, one for each `w in Omega`. Form the permutation
wreath product

\[
G=E\rtimes Q.
\]

Concretely, `G` acts on the eighteen points `Omega x F_2`: a base element
flips selected two-point fibers, and `Q` permutes their nine indices.
Every element has a unique description

\[
(w,e)\longmapsto(q(w),e+v_w),
\qquad q\in Q,\quad v\in\mathbb F_2^{\Omega}.
\]

Thus `|G|=512*72=36,864`. Put

\[
A=\langle z_o\rangle\times H,
\qquad B=C=G_{(o,0)}.
\]

The direct product in `A` is valid because `H` fixes the origin.
The point stabilizer has the exact form

\[
B=\langle z_w:w\ne o\rangle\rtimes H.
\]

Therefore `|A|=16` and `|B|=|C|=2^8*8=2,048`. All three are 2-groups,
and hence nilpotent. No requirement in the problem prohibits `B=C`.

## The small affine stabilizers

For every nonzero vector `w in F_3^2`, its stabilizer `H_w` has order two.
This can be checked directly from the eight displayed maps:

- On the x-axis the stabilizer is generated by `(x,y)->(x,-y)`.
- On the y-axis it is generated by `(x,y)->(-x,y)`.
- On the line `y=x` it is generated by `(x,y)->(y,x)`.
- On the line `y=-x` it is generated by `(x,y)->(-y,-x)`.

These are the four one-dimensional subspaces of `F_3^2`. Write `R_L`
for the corresponding subgroup of order two. Each such line has exactly
two nonzero vectors. The four stabilizers are distinct, and

\[
H_u\cap H_v=
\begin{cases}
R_L,&u,v\in L\setminus\{o\},\\
1,&u,v\ne o\text{ lie on different lines}.
\end{cases}
\]

In particular, every intersection `H intersect Q_w` is nontrivial:
it is `H` when `w=o`, and `R_<w>` otherwise.

## The entire conjugacy family of B

For each `w in Omega`, the stabilizers in `G` of `(w,0)` and `(w,1)`
are equal. Indeed, fixing either point requires exactly `q(w)=w` and
`v_w=0` in the displayed description of an element. Denote this subgroup
by `B_w`; explicitly,

\[
B_w=\langle z_t:t\ne w\rangle\rtimes Q_w.
\]

Because `G` acts transitively on the eighteen points, conjugating the
point stabilizer `B` gives exactly these nine subgroups. They are
distinct because `z_w` is absent from `B_w` and present in every `B_t`
with `t!=w`. Thus independent choices of `x,y in G` in the original
question give exactly all pairs `B_u,B_v`, for `u,v in Omega`.
This also accounts for conjugation by every base-group element, not
just by the affine quotient.

## Exact triple intersections and both generated minima

Put `Z=<z_o>`. For `u,v in Omega`, the intersection
`D_(u,v)=A intersect B_u intersect B_v` is given by the following table
of cases:

| Indices | Intersection | Order | Number of ordered index pairs |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `u=v=o` | `H` | 8 | 1 |
| Exactly one index is `o`, the other lies on `L` | `R_L` | 2 | 16 |
| Both nonzero, on the same line `L` | `Z x R_L` | 4 | 16 |
| Both nonzero, on different lines | `Z` | 2 | 48 |

To verify the table, membership in `B_o` kills the `Z` factor of `A`;
membership in any `B_w` with `w!=o` leaves it intact. The remaining
condition on an element of `H` is to fix each of the indicated indices.
The preceding explicit stabilizer calculation proves every entry.

Consequently the complete family of distinct subgroups is

\[
\{H,Z\}\ \cup\ \{R_L:L\le\mathbb F_3^2,\ \dim L=1\}
\ \cup\ \{Z\times R_L:L\le\mathbb F_3^2,\ \dim L=1\}.
\]

There are ten distinct members. None is trivial. The five subgroups
`Z` and the four `R_L` have minimum order two. They are exactly the
inclusion-minimal members: each larger member contains one of them.
The four `R_L` generate `H`, since they include `<d>` and `<s>`.
Hence

\[
\boxed{\min_G(A,B,B)=\operatorname{Min}_G(A,B,B)=A.}
\]

An explicit outside witness will be the involution `d`. In the table,
take `u=o` and `v=(0,1)`. Then

\[
A\cap B_o\cap B_{(0,1)}=\langle d\rangle.
\]

If desired, `B_(0,1)=B^{t}` for the lifted translation
`t(x,y)=(x,y+1)`. Thus the displayed subgroup is an actual intersection
of the printed form, with one conjugating element equal to the identity.

## Solubility and the exact Fitting subgroup

The group `G` is soluble: it is an extension of the elementary abelian
2-group `E` by an extension of the elementary abelian 3-group
`T=F_3^2` by the 2-group `H`.

First `O_2(Q)=1`. Any normal 2-subgroup of `Q` commutes with the normal
3-subgroup `T`, because the commutator of two normal subgroups is
contained in their intersection. The centralizer `C_Q(T)` is `T`:
an affine map commutes with all translations precisely when its linear
part is the identity, as follows by applying it to `x+a`. Thus the
normal 2-subgroup must be trivial.

Next `C_G(E)=E`. Conjugation permutes the nine independent base flips
according to the action of the affine part `q` on `Omega`. Commuting
with every flip forces `q` to fix all nine indices; faithfulness then
gives `q=1`.

Now let `N` be any normal nilpotent subgroup of `G`. Each Sylow subgroup
of the finite nilpotent group `N` is characteristic in `N`, so is normal
in `G`. Any odd-order Sylow subgroup commutes with the normal 2-group
`E`, by the same commutator argument, and consequently lies in
`C_G(E)=E`; it is therefore trivial. Thus `N` is a 2-group. Its image in
`Q` is a normal 2-subgroup, so is trivial. This proves `N<=E`.
Conversely, `E` is abelian and normal. Hence

\[
\boxed{F(G)=E.}
\]

The element `d` has nonidentity affine part, so it does not lie in `E`.
We have proved

\[
\min_G(A,B,B)=\operatorname{Min}_G(A,B,B)=A\not\le F(G)
\]

in a finite soluble group with nilpotent inputs. The answers to (a),
(b), and both questions in (c) are therefore **no**.
