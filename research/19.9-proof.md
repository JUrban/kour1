# 19.9: negative in general, affirmative for braid groups

Date: 2026-09-11. Complete derived resolutions, subject to outside review.
Both are consequences of existing results; no new-priority claim is made.
The exact statement was checked visually on supplied PDF page133.

All tensor squares below are the non-abelian tensor squares for conjugation
actions, and all homology has trivial integral coefficients.

## 1. Standard tensor-square input

For a free presentation G=F/R, write T(G)=G tensor G and W(G)=G wedge G.
The standard Brown--Loday and Hopf descriptions give

    W(G) = F'/[F,R],
    1 -> H_2(G) -> W(G) -> G' -> 1,
    Gamma(G_ab) -> T(G) -> W(G) -> 1.

The kernel of the last map is the central diagonal subgroup generated
by g tensor g. The first map sends the quadratic generator associated
to gG' to g tensor g. In particular Gamma(0)=0 and Gamma(Z)=Z.
These precise facts are recorded in Section2 of [BLN].

If G is perfect, T(G)=W(G) is its universal central extension. Here is
the group-theoretic part of this assertion. Since F=F'R,
F'=[F'R,F'R]=F'' modulo [F,R], so U=F'/[F,R] is perfect.
Given any central extension E -> G, choose lifts of the free generators
to E. The resulting map F -> E sends R into the central kernel, hence
kills [F,R]. Its restriction gives U -> E over G. Two such maps differ
by a homomorphism from the perfect group U into the abelian central
kernel, so coincide. This proves universality.

## 2. Part(a): the symplectic counterexample

Let

    G = Sp(10,Z) <= GL_10(Q).

This group is finitely generated and perfect. Finite generation follows
from the classical elementary symplectic generators ([FM], Theorem6.1);
perfectness is also recorded in the stable-rank H_1 table of [BCRR].
Its topological-cover
pullback fits into a central extension

    1 -> <z> ~= Z -> E -> Sp(10,Z) -> 1,

where E is the inverse image of Sp(10,Z) in the universal covering
group of Sp(10,R). For this rank, E is the abstract universal central
extension of G; see [BCRR], page4071 and Lemma6.11. The choice of
rank5 avoids the exceptional rank3 homology summand.

Deligne's theorem says that every finite-index subgroup of E contains
z^2; see the first-page statement of [D]. Since z has infinite order,
E is not residually finite. Section1 therefore identifies

    G tensor G ~= E.

We must check finite generation before applying residual finiteness
of finitely generated linear groups. In any perfect central extension
E -> G of a finitely generated group, lifts of a finite generating set
generate E: if H is the subgroup of these lifts and C is the central
kernel, then E=HC and E=E'=(HC)'=H' <= H. Thus E is finitely generated.

Malcev's residual-finiteness theorem now excludes a faithful
finite-dimensional representation of E over every field. For clarity,
the standard argument works in all characteristics: put all entries of
finitely many generating matrices and their inverses into a finitely
generated commutative domain A. To detect a nonzero entry a of M-I,
take a maximal ideal of A[1/a]; its residue field is finite by the
standard finite-type field lemma over Z. Reduction detects M in a
finite general linear group.

Consequently Sp(10,Z) is a finitely generated linear group with a
nonlinear non-abelian tensor square. This answers19.9(a) negatively.

This argument identifies E with the tensor square itself. It never
infers non-residual finiteness merely from a quotient. Also <z> denotes
the displayed covering kernel, not necessarily the entire centre of E.

## 3. Splitting the diagonal when G_ab is infinite cyclic

Suppose e:G -> Z is the abelianization, and choose s with e(s)=1.
The induced map on tensor squares is a homomorphism

    epsilon:T(G) -> Z tensor_Z Z = Z,
    epsilon(g tensor h)=e(g)e(h).

Let d=s tensor s. By the Gamma(Z) description in Section1, the diagonal
subgroup is generated by d; epsilon(d)=1. Thus d has infinite order,
and epsilon restricts to an isomorphism of that subgroup with Z.
Since the diagonal subgroup is central, every t has a unique expression
d^m u with u in ker(epsilon), and the exterior projection restricts to
an isomorphism ker(epsilon) -> W(G). Hence

    T(G) ~= Z x W(G).

No vanishing condition on H_3(G), and no assumed splitting of an
arbitrary central extension, is being used.

## 4. Part(b): an explicit faithful embedding for all n>=4

Let G=B_n, with its usual Artin generators s_1,...,s_(n-1).
It has abelianization Z and H_2(G)=C_2 for every n>=4; see [AKS],
Section2.2. Let F be free on x_1,...,x_(n-1), and R the normal
closure of the Artin relations.

We construct a linear representation of F in which every element of
R is scalar, while the Hopf kernel H_2(G) is detected. This explicitly
provides the additional representation needed beside the braid-group
representation.

Let A_n be the rational Clifford algebra on e_1,...,e_n with

    e_i^2=1/2,   e_i e_j=-e_j e_i for i!=j.

It has Q-basis e_I for subsets I of {1,...,n}, with factors ordered
increasingly, and dimension2^n. Indeed the relations span by these
monomials; independence follows by representing e_i on an exterior
algebra as exterior multiplication plus one-half contraction and
applying the ordered monomials to1. Put u_i=e_i-e_(i+1). Then

    u_i^2=1,
    u_i u_(i+1)+u_(i+1)u_i=-1,
    u_i u_j=-u_j u_i when |i-j|>=2.

The middle identity implies

    u_i u_(i+1) u_i = -u_i-u_(i+1)
                         = u_(i+1) u_i u_(i+1).

Thus phi:F -> A_n^x, x_i |-> u_i, sends every braid relator to1,
and every distant commuting relator to -1. In particular phi(R)
is contained in the central group {1,-1}, so phi kills [F,R].
Its restriction descends to

    bar(phi): W(G)=F'/[F,R] -> A_n^x.

For n>=4, r=[x_1,x_3] belongs to R intersect F' and phi(r)=-1.
Therefore bar(phi) is nontrivial on H_2(G). As H_2(G) has order2,
its restriction to this kernel is injective. If kappa:W(G)->G'
is the natural projection, we have proved that

    (kappa,bar(phi)): W(G) -> G' x A_n^x

is injective: an element of its kernel lies in H_2(G), where the
second coordinate is injective.

Left multiplication embeds A_n^x in GL_(2^n)(Q), since a left
multiplication operator determines its value at1. The braid group
has a faithful Lawrence--Krammer representation of dimension
d=n(n-1)/2 over Q(q,t), by [K]. Combining this representation with
the Clifford representation and Section3 gives the explicit bound

    B_n tensor B_n embeds in GL_(1+d+2^n)(Q(q,t)).

For an element v in the tensor square, the three diagonal blocks are
2^epsilon(v), the faithful braid representation of its commutator
image, and left multiplication by bar(phi) of its exterior image.
If all three blocks are identity, the exterior image is identity by
the kernel argument above, and the diagonal component is identity
by epsilon. Hence this is a faithful representation. This answers
19.9(b) affirmatively for every n>3.

There is also a conceptual description: the Schur cover C_n is the
pullback of a finite double cover of S_n, as established in [AKS],
Proposition2.3. Its derived subgroup is W(B_n). Thus the same conclusion
follows from C_n <= B_n x (2.S_n). The Clifford proof above supplies
the needed kernel detection without assuming that arbitrary finite
central extensions of linear groups remain linear.

## 5. Provenance and scope

These implications are written here; the cited papers are not claimed
to state the exact tensor-square answers. Since the essential ingredients
are prior results and the deductions are short, the result is retained
as a complete prior-theorem consequence and excluded from the working
count of candidates with possible new priority. Absence of an exact
answer in the searches is not evidence of novelty. Independent review
must check both the standard identifications and their use here.
