# Internal audit of the candidate affirmative answer to 16.20

Date: 2026-09-11. This is an internal audit, not independent external
review. The candidate's novelty is not established.

## Statement and conventions

The supplied PDF page 98 was visually inspected. The question fixes
H<=A in a quasivariety M and asks whether the set of dominions as N
ranges over subquasivarieties of M can be modular and nondistributive.
There is no printed restriction that A belong to every N. The entry
is unstarred.

Shakhova (2005), p.239, explicitly says that the definition is extended
to A not in the target class, then defines exactly the lattice in the
question. Budkin (2008), pp.542--543, repeats this convention. Thus
the unrestricted construction is responsive to the intended definition.
The seven-factor construction additionally covers the stronger convention
that A must belong to every admitted N, with nontrivial H of order two.

## Potential failure points checked

| Point | Audit |
|---|---|
| Generator targets versus generated quasivariety | Finiteness of A gives a finite quasiidentity encoding two multiplication tables and agreement on H. This proves exact transfer to q(C), including its infinite members. No claim that all its members are finite is used. |
| Cross-factor homomorphisms | A nontrivial image of U_i has order at least |S_i| and order divisible by |S_i|. Pairwise simple-order nondivisibility excludes every map U_i -> S_j for i!=j, including nonsurjective maps. |
| Perfect-cover normal subgroups | A normal subgroup not contained in the centre projects onto S_i. Then U_i=R Z(U_i), and perfectness forces U_i<=R. This also rules out a proper subgroup surjecting onto S_i when multiplied by the centre. |
| Centre after quotient | W/K is central, and its quotient is a product of centreless simple groups; consequently it is exactly the centre. The preimage of one surviving simple factor is its perfect cover times this centre. |
| Maps into quotients | The needed assertion is that every A -> A/R kills R. Mere invariance under endomorphisms would be insufficient. The proof treats arbitrary quotient targets explicitly. |
| Central directions | Every nonzero central vector is the image of some z_i. Each vector in K is killed by examining the corresponding factor map. A basis alone would not suffice for arbitrary K; all nonzero vectors are included deliberately. |
| Normality of dominions | Equalizers need not be normal. The proof uses precomposition by inner automorphisms, which fix central H. For H=1, a separate direct intersection-of-kernels argument is available. |
| Full lattice, not a sublattice only | Every admissible dominion is normal and contains H, and every such normal subgroup is attained by q(A/R). Thus the entire family is [H,A], whose modularity is known. The diamond then proves nondistributivity. |
| Restricted convention | Targets containing A force the dominion inside Z(A). Endomorphisms act as zero or identity on the whole centre, and nonzero H distinguishes these actions. The classes q(A,A/K) realize every intermediate central K. |
| Ambient class | A belongs to M since R=1 occurs. A finite product of the finite quotient targets generates M. Its finite-exponent identity makes M proper as well. |
| Lattice anti-homomorphism | The natural map from subquasivarieties to dominions is order reversing, but the proof does not assert that it preserves both lattice operations. Budkin's conditional semidistributivity theorem therefore does not prohibit this example. |
| Numerical size | The seven-factor group has order 599766788342131458048000 and is handled structurally. The smaller three-factor group has order 44029440 and was actually constructed in GAP. Neither log purports to enumerate all homomorphisms. |

## Exact verification

Commands, from the repository root:

    bin/gap scripts/verify_16_20.g > results/16.20-factor-verification.log 2>&1
    python3 scripts/verify_16_20.py
    bin/gap scripts/verify_16_20_small.g > results/16.20-small-verification.log 2>&1

All logs and the JSON certificate pass. GAP checks factor orders,
centres, perfectness, simple quotients, and complete factor normal lists.
The independent Python code enumerates the binary Hamming code and
the finite central linear conditions for maps into every structurally
classified normal quotient. It checks 199 normal labels, 2,609 allowed
factor supports, and 584,430 modular identities in the 154-element
interval. It finds precisely five central dominions above H.

The smaller GAP check constructs the quotient group itself, independently
enumerates all 15 normal subgroups, and checks its lattice operations.
There are 1,305 applicable modular identities, all passing. Its three
central involution subgroups give the explicit nondistributive diamond.

These calculations verify finite ingredients and a concrete lattice.
The arguments covering every target quasivariety and all homomorphisms
remain mathematical proofs, not exhaustive computational claims.

## Literature audit and reading scope

1. S. A. Shakhova, *Lattices of Dominions in Quasivarieties of Abelian
   Groups*, Algebra Logika 44:2 (2005), 238--251; Algebra and Logic
   44:2, 132--139. https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/al108 ;
   DOI 10.1007/s10469-005-0014-z. Read the introduction and definitions
   on pp.238--240. The explicit A-not-in-M convention is on p.239.
   The paper concerns abelian target classes; the full classification
   proof was not audited.
2. A. I. Budkin, *Lattices of dominions of universal algebras*, Algebra
   Logika 46:1 (2007), 26--45; Algebra and Logic 46:1, 16--27.
   https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/al7 ; DOI 10.1007/s10469-007-0002-6.
   Read the introduction and definitions, Section 3 (Theorems 5--7,
   their proofs, and the associated remarks), and Section 4's Lemma 8
   and Theorem 8 construction. Page 40 explicitly states that whether
   M_3 embeds in a dominion lattice was unknown. Theorem 6 requires
   the natural map to be a lattice anti-homomorphism; no such condition
   is imposed by 16.20 or claimed in our proof. Theorem 5 has further
   distributivity and fiber hypotheses. None of the results read gives
   the present construction or rules it out.
3. A. I. Budkin, *Dominions of universal algebras and projective
   properties*, Algebra Logika 47:5 (2008), 541--557; Algebra and Logic
   47:5, 304--313. https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/al374 ;
   DOI 10.1007/s10469-008-9029-6. Read the abstract and pp.541--543,
   and screened the rest of the extracted text for lattice terminology.
   The paper introduces projective closure properties and a result for
   divisible abelian subgroups in torsion-free class-two groups. The
   full proofs were not audited. Its definitions confirm the convention.
4. Budkin, *Dominions in Quasivarieties of Universal Algebras*, Studia
   Logica 78:1--2 (2004), 107--127, DOI 10.1007/s11225-005-7127-1.
   Only its abstract was located and read. It contains related
   transfer theory; our finite-domain Horn argument is explicit and
   does not depend on an unaudited general version of that result.
5. Searches on 11 September 2026 combined the exact problem number,
   Kourovka, dominion lattices, modular and nondistributive, M_3,
   central products, and the corresponding Russian terms. Budkin's
   author index and accessible citation lists were also screened.
   No prior resolution or matching finite perfect-group example was
   found. This limited search does not establish priority. Later
   papers on solvable and metabelian groups were located but not
   audited in full.

The three downloaded Russian originals are cached with descriptive
filenames under the ignored `references/cache/` directory. Public
links and exact reading scope are retained here.

## Current conclusion

The argument supplies a complete candidate affirmative answer, with
two explicit finite groups and no unresolved proof step identified in
this internal audit. Mathematical outside review and a broader novelty
audit remain necessary before describing it as a verified new solution.
