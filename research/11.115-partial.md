# Problem 11.115: two relation-module sufficient conditions

11 September 2026. Partial theorem only; the arbitrary normal-subgroup
question is unresolved here. No novelty claim or complete-candidate count.

Let F be a noncyclic free group, 1 != N normal in F, and
N <= T < F. Then [T,N] is properly contained in [F,N] if either

1. N has finite index in F; or
2. N contains a primitive element of F (an element in some free basis).

No assumption that N is not maximal is needed in these two cases.
In particular, case 2 applies whenever F has infinite rank and F/N
is finitely generated. These statements do not settle all the
hypotheses printed in the Notebook.

## Relation-module criterion

Put Q=F/N, P=T/N and M=N/[N,N], written additively as a right
ZQ-module by conjugation. Since N <= T, both [T,N] and [F,N] contain
[N,N]. Their images in M are respectively

    M I_P = span_Z { m(p-1) : m in M, p in P },
    M I_Q = span_Z { m(q-1) : m in M, q in Q }.

For example [n,t]=n^-1 n^t gives exactly the first type of difference.
Thus equality of the two commutator subgroups would imply equality
of these subgroups of M. Tensoring with the rational field k gives

    V I_P = V I_Q,       V = M tensor_Z k.                 (1)

Suppose V has a direct summand U isomorphic to the regular right
kQ-module, with a kQ-linear projection V -> U. Projecting (1) would
give U I_P=U I_Q. But U/U I_P has a basis indexed by the left
cosets qP, whereas U/U I_Q has dimension one. Since P<Q there
are at least two left cosets, so these two subspaces are distinct.
This contradicts (1). The criterion holds for infinite Q as well:
the coset basis is then interpreted in the ordinary free vector space,
with finite-support vectors.

## Case 1: finite Q

First suppose F has finite rank d>=2. The cellular chain sequence of
the regular Q-cover of a rose gives an exact sequence of right
kQ-modules

    0 -> V -> (kQ)^d -> I_Q -> 0.

Here V is the first homology of that covering graph, naturally
N_ab tensor k, and I_Q is the kernel of the augmentation kQ -> k.
Maschke's theorem splits the sequence. Since kQ is semisimple and
kQ is isomorphic to k direct_sum I_Q, cancellation of the finite
semisimple summands gives

    V is isomorphic to k direct_sum (kQ)^(d-1).

This is the usual rational relation-module form of Gaschuetz's
theorem. It has a regular direct summand, and the criterion applies.

If F has infinite rank, choose a finite subset S_0 of a free basis
whose images generate the finite group Q, and choose another basis
element x. There is a word w in S_0 with the same image as x in Q.
Replacing x by x w^-1 is an elementary free-basis change: its inverse
replaces that element by its product with w, fixing all other basis
elements. Hence x w^-1 is primitive and belongs to N. Case 2 below
applies. This argument also works when Q is any finitely generated
group.

## Case 2: a primitive element in N

Choose a free basis containing x in N. In the Q-covering graph, the
edges labelled x are loops, one at each vertex q in Q. Their span U
is a regular kQ-module and has zero cellular boundary. Splitting the
edge space into the x-labelled edges and all other edges gives

    ker(boundary) = U direct_sum ker(boundary on other edges).

This decomposition is Q-equivariant, and graphs have no 2-chains.
Thus V has precisely the regular direct summand required by the
criterion. This proves case 2 and finishes the partial theorem.

## Limits and source comparison

The finite-index relation-module decomposition was also used in
Tursunbaev's [September 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06755v1)
on equal commutator subgroups, whose full proof and source packet are
recorded in `11.124-prior-result.md`. The elementary application here
has different commutator subgroups and does not follow just by
substituting into that paper's main theorem. No claim that these
special cases were previously unknown is made.

The direct-summand condition is sufficient. Faithfulness of the
action of Q on V alone would not establish it or imply unequal
coinvariant kernels. For an arbitrary N of infinite index, this
argument gives no such summand and no answer to the full problem.
