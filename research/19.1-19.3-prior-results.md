## Two elementary facts

If f:G -> Q is onto, then f(mu(G))<=mu(Q). Indeed the preimage
of each nearly maximal subgroup of Q is nearly maximal in G:
it contains ker(f), so all its overgroups correspond to overgroups
in Q and the indices agree. Intersect these preimages. The analogous
inclusion for lambda follows directly by lifting a witness subgroup.
In particular mu(Z)=1, since its trivial subgroup is nearly maximal.

Every finite normal subgroup K of a group L lies in lambda(L).
For S<=L, if <S,k> has finite index for k in K, then SK has finite
index and [SK:S]<=|K|. Thus S has finite index. No finite-generation
or normality assumption on S is used.

For completeness, the standard equality lambda(G)=mu(G) for finitely
generated G follows by Zorn's lemma. The union of a chain of
infinite-index subgroups cannot have finite index: otherwise it is
finitely generated by Schreier's theorem, and some chain member
contains all its generators. Thus every infinite-index subgroup
lies in a nearly maximal one. If g is a near generator, a witness
S of infinite index with <S,g> of finite index extends to such a
subgroup M; necessarily g is outside M. This proves mu<=lambda.
The fact is credited to Riles 1969, Proposition 1, also quoted by
the Allenby papers; the argument here makes the deduction explicit.

## 19.3(a),(b),(c),(f)

Write K=core_G(H).

For (a), Allenby 1999 Theorem 3 gives lambda=mu=K=1.

For (b), K is finite and normal in both A and B, so the elementary
finite-normal-subgroup fact gives K<=lambda(A) intersect lambda(B).
Either stated intersection condition therefore forces K=1, and (a)
applies.

For (c), Allenby 2000 Theorem 6 gives mu(G)<=H. Since mu(G) is
normal, it is contained in K=1. Thus lambda=mu=psi=1.

For (f), choose retractions A -> H and B -> H. They agree on H
and induce a retraction r:G -> H. The cyclic-amalgam theorem gives
mu(G)<=H, whereas r(mu(G))<=mu(H)=1. Because r is the identity
on H, mu(G)=1. Again lambda=mu=psi=1. Neither A nor B needs to
be finitely generated.

## 19.3(e), including arbitrary ranks of the free factors

Suppose [A:H] is infinite; interchange A and B if needed. We claim
there is c in A with H intersect H^c=1. If A has infinite rank,
the finitely many generators of H use only finitely many letters
of a free basis. An unused basis letter c freely conjugates the
free factor containing H off itself, proving the claim.

If A has finite rank, Marshall Hall's theorem gives a finite-index
subgroup L=H*C of A. Here C is nontrivial, because otherwise H
would have finite index. Any nonidentity c in C has H intersect
H^c=1 by free-product normal form. The claim is also immediate if
H=1. Allenby 2005 Corollary (b) now gives mu(G)=1, and hence psi(G)=1.

Hall's finite-index free-factor theorem is classical. A graph proof
is in Kapovich–Myasnikov, *Stallings foldings and the subgroup structure
of free groups*, [arXiv:math/0202285v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0202285v1),
Lemma 8.10 and Theorem 8.11. Those statements and proofs, together
with Proposition 8.3's finite-index criterion, were read. The whole
paper was not audited. The additional excluded element in its
strengthened theorem is not needed here.

One can see the precise free-factor assertion directly: take the
finite connected folded core graph for H. For each basis label,
pair vertices missing an outgoing edge with vertices missing an
incoming edge and insert the corresponding edges. The two sets have
equal size. This completes the graph on the same vertices to a
finite cover of the basis rose. The original connected graph stays
a subgraph, so extending its spanning tree shows its fundamental
group H is a free factor of the cover group L. At least one edge
was added because H has infinite index; hence the complementary
free group C is nontrivial.

## 19.1(a): products of knots

For a nontrivial connected sum, the group is A *_H B, where A and
B are the constituent knot groups and H is the infinite cyclic
meridian subgroup. A knot group's abelianization is Z generated
by the meridian. Sending an element to the corresponding power of
its meridian therefore retracts each factor onto H. Apply the
argument for 19.3(f). Iteration treats a product with any finite
number of nontrivial factors. This also follows from Azarian 1994
Theorem 3.11 and the finitely generated equality proved above.

## 19.1(b): cable knots

Use the standard decomposition in Allenby–Tang–Tang 1980 Section 5:
G=A *_H B, with B infinite cyclic, H a proper cyclic subgroup of B,
and G_ab=G/G' infinite cyclic. The cyclic-amalgam theorem gives
mu(G)<=H, and the abelianization map gives mu(G)<=G'.

The elementary intersection identity needed here is

    G' intersect B = B' (A' intersect H).

To check it, let alpha:H -> A_ab and beta:H -> B_ab be the natural
maps. Abelianization of the amalgam is the abelian pushout

    (A_ab direct-sum B_ab) / {(alpha(h),-beta(h)):h in H}.

The displayed set is a subgroup, since alpha and beta are
homomorphisms into abelian groups. The kernel of B_ab -> G_ab
is exactly beta(ker(alpha)); taking the preimage in B proves the
identity. This is Lemma 5.4 in the 1980 paper, with a direct check.

If mu(G) were nontrivial, B intersect G' would contain a nonzero
subgroup of the infinite cyclic B. The image of B in G_ab would
then be finite. Since G_ab is torsion-free, that image is trivial,
so B<=G'. But B'=1 and the intersection identity would imply
B=A' intersect H<=H, contradicting H<B. Therefore mu(G)=1 and
psi(G)=1. This also recovers Azarian's stated lower-bound result.

## Exact limits and other parts

The published amalgam theorems concern proper amalgams H<A,B.
Suppressing this convention can change an assertion: taking H=A
collapses G to the arbitrary group B. We do not use such collapsed
presentations as purported new counterexamples.

The knot deductions use the usual nontrivial connected-sum and cable
constructions, as the original Section 5 does. If one allows insertion
of an unknot to call every knot a product, or a winding-one trivial
cabling to call every knot a cable, the displayed decompositions can
collapse. The arguments here are not asserted to prove the theorem
for every knot group under those enlarged conventions. The unknot
itself has group Z and psi=1.

19.3(d) remains unresolved here without additional hypotheses. The
known implication lambda(G) intersect H=1 => lambda(G)=1 does not
by itself prove mu(G)=1. When G is finitely generated, the equality
above does give the requested implication, using the published lower
theorem, but the question does not assume finite generation.

The hypothesis of 19.3(g) is impossible with the printed definition:
if H is finite normal, [HN:N]<=|H|, so [G:HN]<infinity forces
[G:N]<infinity. The same observation and H<=lambda(G) make both
sides of 19.2 false whenever psi(G) exists. Existence of psi(G) in
arbitrary groups is a separate issue. These are formulation findings,
not additional new resolutions.
