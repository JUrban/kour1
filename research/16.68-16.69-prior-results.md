# Problems 16.68 and 16.69(b): compact almost laws

Status: complete deductions from Thom's published theorem, excluded
from new-candidate priority. Recorded 11 September 2026. Both supplied
questions were visually checked on printed page 102.

The SO(3,R) part of 16.68 and part (b) of 16.69 have negative answers.
The two PSL(2) cases of 16.68 and part (a) of 16.69 remain unresolved
in this project.

## Published input

Andreas Thom, *Convergent sequences in discrete groups*, Canadian
Mathematical Bulletin 56 (2013), 424–433,
[arXiv:1003.4093v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.4093v3),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.4153/CMB-2011-155-3), Corollary 3.3:
for every positive integer n and every epsilon>0 there is a nonidentity
word w in the two-generator free group for which

    ||I - w(g,h)|| <= epsilon     for all g,h in U(n).

Here the norm is the operator norm, as defined before Lemma 2.1.
The word is nontrivial as a free-group element; choose its reduced
representative to meet the Notebook's wording. No quantitative bound
on its word length is needed below.

## The SO(3,R) case

Apply the theorem with n=3 and epsilon=1/2. Real special orthogonal
matrices are unitary, so the same estimate holds on SO(3,R). But the
matrix diag(-1,-1,1) belongs to SO(3,R) and has operator-norm distance
2 from the identity. It is omitted by w. This disproves surjectivity.

## All nonzero quaternions, not only unit quaternions

Apply the theorem with n=2 and epsilon=1/2. Let e_x and e_y be the
exponent sums of the two generators in w. Substituting
g=exp(i theta)I and h=I gives w(g,h)=exp(i e_x theta)I. If e_x were
nonzero, theta=pi/e_x would give -I, contradicting the norm estimate.
Thus e_x=0. The same argument gives e_y=0.

Every nonzero quaternion has a unique expression r u with r>0 real
and u of norm one. Positive real quaternions are central. Therefore
for all such r,s,u,v,

    w(r u,s v) = r^(e_x) s^(e_y) w(u,v) = w(u,v).

For completeness, writing a quaternion as z+t j with z,t complex,
the map

    rho(z+t j) = [[z,t],[-conjugate(t),conjugate(z)]]

is an injective real-algebra homomorphism into M_2(C), and
rho(q)^*rho(q)=|q|^2 I. Consequently its operator norm is |q|,
and norm-one quaternions map to SU(2). The theorem now gives

    |1 - w(u,v)| <= 1/2.

The quaternion w(u,v) has norm one, so

    |1 - w(u,v)|^2 = 2 - 2 Re(w(u,v)).

It follows that Re(w(r u,s v)) >= 7/8 for every pair of nonzero
quaternions. In particular -5/27 is omitted. This proves the negative
answer to 16.69(b) with precisely its unrestricted nonzero inputs.

The vanishing exponent sums are essential to the extension from unit
quaternions. A norm estimate merely on SU(2), without that step, would
not by itself address the printed question.

## Scope and source audit

Thom's operator-norm definitions, full Lemma 2.1 proof, full Proposition
3.1 proof with Lemma 3.2, Corollaries 3.3–3.4 and Remarks 3.5–3.6 were
read. The entire paper was not audited. The proof of Proposition 3.1
constructs nontrivial free words and establishes the uniform smallness
used here. The quantitative assertion in Remark 3.6 is unnecessary
and is not imported.

Gordeev, Kunyavskii and Plotkin, *Geometry of word equations in simple
algebraic groups over special fields*,
[arXiv:1808.02303v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.02303v1),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1070/RM9838), Theorem 2.2(i) and its attribution
paragraph independently identify the compact-group consequence as
Thom's result. Their Proposition 2.6 and the displayed proofs of
Lemmas 2.7–2.8 were also read. A statement about semisimple classes
in PSL(2,R) does not automatically supply a prescribed signed trace
in GL(2,R); no such deduction is claimed for 16.69(a).

The 2012 Elkasapy–Thom primary abstract
[arXiv:1207.5596](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.5596) and the 2021
Jezernik–Sánchez primary publisher abstract
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2021.08.014) were read only
as leads. They concern restricted word classes or ranks and do not
settle the remaining cases here. Their full proofs were not audited.

No finite experiment is used to infer a universal norm bound. These
are prior-theorem consequences, not two additional new candidates.
