# Every finite p-group occurs as such a subdirect quotient

This is an additional self-contained construction developed during the
13.19 investigation. Its novelty is not established. Problem 13.19 itself
is excluded from our new-solution count because of the prior implication
documented in `13.19-review.md`.

**Proposition.** For every finite p-group K there are finite p-groups
Q, H, G_1,...,G_n with H normal in Q, Q/H isomorphic to K, and a common
embedding Q <= G_1 x ... x G_n under which both Q and H project onto
every G_i.

The trivial K can be handled by Q=H=G_1=C_p. Assume K is nontrivial and
choose generators g_1,...,g_d.

## The nilpotent algebra used in the construction

Let I be the augmentation ideal of F_p[K]. We first recall with proof
that I^|K|=0. Induct on |K|. Choose a central element z of order p.
The natural algebra map to F_p[K/<z>] has kernel J=(z-1)F_p[K].
Indeed, on each coset of <z> its kernel consists of linear combinations
with coefficient sum zero, all generated by differences z^i-1.
By induction, I^(|K|/p) is contained in J. Since z is central and
(z-1)^p=z^p-1=0, we have J^p=0. Consequently I^|K|=0.
For the base group of order 1 its augmentation ideal is zero.

Put m=|K|. Let A be the nonunital free associative F_p-algebra on the
md symbols x_ij (1<=i<=m, 1<=j<=d), truncated by setting every monomial
of length m equal to zero. A has the monomials of lengths 1,...,m-1
as an F_p-basis, so it is finite dimensional and A^m=0.

The set 1+A is a finite group under multiplication in the unitization:
the inverse of 1+a is the finite geometric series. Its cardinality is
p^(dim A), so it is a finite p-group. Define

    Q = <1+x_ij : 1<=i<=m, 1<=j<=d> <= 1+A.

The substitutions x_ij -> g_j-1 define an algebra homomorphism A -> I,
because I^m=0. On Q its unitized map has image exactly
<g_1,...,g_d>=K inside the unit group of F_p[K]. Denote this surjection
by phi and put H=ker(phi).

## A faithful family of projections

For each i, let A_i be the truncated free algebra on the symbols from
all blocks except block i. Deleting block i defines an algebra map

    delta_i:A -> A_i,   x_ij -> 0,

which fixes the symbols in all other blocks. It induces a group map on
1+A. Let G_i=delta_i(Q), so Q projects onto G_i by definition.

The combined map Q -> product_i G_i is injective. To see this, suppose
q=1+a maps to 1 in every factor. Then delta_i(a)=0 for every i. Each
monomial in a has length at most m-1 and therefore omits at least one
of the m blocks. Choose an omitted block i. That monomial survives
delta_i unchanged, and no different surviving monomial maps to it.
Its coefficient in a must therefore be zero. This holds for every
monomial, so a=0 and q=1.

Finally H projects onto each G_i. For any other block k and each j,

    h=(1+x_kj)(1+x_ij)^(-1)

lies in H since phi sends both factors before inversion to g_j, and
delta_i(h)=1+x_kj. These elements generate G_i. Hence delta_i(H)=G_i.
After the faithful embedding, Q,H and their factors satisfy every
assertion of the proposition. In particular, the quotient condition
imposes no restriction at all on the isomorphism type of a finite
p-group if the number and sizes of the factors may vary.

The construction is intentionally an existence proof. Its finite groups
are far larger than needed for D_8; the order-256 example in
`13.19-proof.md` is the practical certificate for that case.
