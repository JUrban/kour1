# Problem 9.4: no finite generator for a nontrivial group-isotope variety

Date: 11 September 2026. Complete negative candidate; priority and
independent review pending.

## Statement and conventions

Use ordinary quasigroups, with three binary operations (*, \\, /).
Thus a\\c is the unique b such that a*b=c, and c/b is the unique a
such that a*b=c. All variables and operation values belong to the same
underlying set. A variety generated by Q has its ordinary universal
algebra meaning, equivalently the algebras satisfying every identity
of Q. Corresponding quasivariety and pseudovariety generation also
preserve every identity of Q.

Let G be a nontrivial finite group, and let M be the variety generated
by G. Let IM consist of all quasigroups isotopic to groups in M.
Then IM cannot be generated by a finite quasigroup, or even by a
finite collection of finite quasigroups. The same assertion holds
when M and IM are interpreted as quasivarieties or pseudovarieties.

In particular, G=C2 gives a negative answer to Problem 9.4 on page 32
of the supplied Notebook. The one-element group is an exception:
its isotope class consists of the one-element quasigroup.

## 1. A separating identity for every finite generator

For a positive integer N, define the two-variable terms recursively by

    t_0(x,y)=y,       t_{j+1}(x,y)=x*t_j(x,y).

Suppose Q is a quasigroup of size n>=1. For every a in Q the left
translation L_a:b -> a*b is a permutation of n elements. Each of its
cycle lengths is at most n and divides n!, so L_a^(n!) is the identity.
Consequently Q satisfies the ordinary identity

    t_(n!)(x,y)=y.                                      (1)

This is an identity in the multiplication operation alone; neither
a distinguished zero nor a constant for a is present in it. Its
variables x and y are universally quantified.

Every algebra in the variety generated by Q satisfies (1).
One can see this directly: equations are preserved under direct
products, subalgebras, and homomorphic images. The same applies to a
quasivariety, since an identity is a quasi-identity with empty premise;
or use preservation under subalgebras, products, and ultraproducts.
For a pseudovariety, preservation under finite products, subalgebras,
and homomorphic images suffices. Thus the finite witnesses below
exclude generation in all three senses.

For finitely many finite Q_i, take n=max_i |Q_i|. Each cycle length
in each Q_i divides n!, so the same single identity (1) holds in all
the generators and throughout the class they generate.

## 2. Finite isotopes with an unbounded translation order

First take G=C2. Given N>=1, put m=N+1 and let

    A_m = (F2)^m,

with basis e_0,...,e_(m-1). Define the coordinate-cycle automorphism
T by T(e_i)=e_(i+1 mod m). On the underlying set of A_m put

    a*b = a+T(b).                                      (2)

This is a quasigroup: its two divisions are explicitly

    a\c = T^(-1)(c-a),       c/b = c-T(b).             (3)

Substituting (3) in (2) proves all four quasigroup axioms. Moreover
the three bijections (id,T,id) give an isotopy from this quasigroup
to the additive group A_m, since a+T(b)=a*b. The group A_m is a
finite direct power of C2, so it belongs to the variety, quasivariety,
and pseudovariety generated by C2. Therefore the finite quasigroup
Q_m given by (2) belongs to IM in each interpretation.

At the particular assignment x=0,y=e_0, however,

    t_N(0,e_0)=T^N(e_0)=e_(N mod m)=e_N != e_0.         (4)

Here m=N+1 and N>=1. The use of 0 in this assignment does not add
a constant to the language; any element is allowed in a universally
quantified variable. Equations (1) and (4), with N=n!, show that no
finite quasigroup Q of size n generates IM. This proves the claimed
negative answer already for C2.

The dimension N+1 is chosen for simplicity, not minimality. One may
instead use any m that does not divide N, in particular any prime
m>n when N=n!. The witness always has finite size 2^m.

## 3. Every nontrivial finite generating group

Choose an element g!=1 of a nontrivial finite G. If p is a prime
divisor of |g|, then g^(|g|/p) generates a subgroup C_p of G. Hence
each finite direct power (C_p)^m belongs to every one of the three
classes generated by G: only subgroups and finite direct products
are used here.

Repeat (2)--(4) over F_p. The displayed divisions with subtraction
remain valid, the coordinate shift still has an orbit of length m,
and t_N(0,e_0)!=e_0. Thus the very same separating identity excludes
every proposed finite generator, or finite family of finite generators.
No assertion about quotient closure of a quasivariety is needed.

## 4. Independent local-finiteness obstruction

For the variety M generated by C2, let

    A = direct sum over i in Z of F2 e_i,
    T(e_i)=e_(i+1),       a*b=a+T(b).

The additive group A belongs to M: it is a subgroup of the direct
product of countably many copies of C2. The shift is bijective on
finite-support vectors, so (3) again defines a quasigroup in IM.
This quasigroup is generated, using its three operations, by e_0.
Indeed e_0\\e_0=0, and from 0 and e_i one obtains

    0*e_i=e_(i+1),       0\e_i=e_(i-1).

Thus the generated subquasigroup contains every basis vector.
It also contains sums of any of its elements, because

    a+b = a*(0\b).

All vectors of A have finite support, proving that the generated
subquasigroup is A itself. In particular it is infinite.

For completeness, a variety generated by a finite algebra Q is
locally finite. On k generators, identify terms when they induce
the same function Q^k -> Q. There are at most |Q|^(|Q|^k) such
functions, and this term algebra modulo equality of term functions
is the relatively free algebra in the variety generated by Q.
Every k-generated algebra in that variety is its homomorphic image,
so is finite. The infinite monogenic quasigroup above contradicts
this property for IM. Section 2 gives the stronger finite-witness
argument, which also handles pseudovarieties without invoking an
infinite member.

## 5. Terminology and prior-literature boundary

Gvaramiya's original paper, *Quasivarieties of automata. Connections
with quasigroups*, Siberian Mathematical Journal 26 (1985), 315--331,
Section 3, distinguishes ordinary quasigroups from three-sorted
reversible automata. Theorem 8 on p.328 proves the ordinary-variety
assertion appearing in the premise of 9.4; Theorem 10 treats ordinary
quasivarieties. Theorem 11 separately treats reversible automata.
The relevant section, pp.326--330, was read; pp.326 and 328 were
visually checked. Earlier sections were not audited.
[Primary paper](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/BF00968618.pdf).

Krapez--Marinkovic, *Isotopy invariant quasigroup identities*,
Commentationes Mathematicae Universitatis Carolinae 57 (2016),
537--547, uses the ordinary three-operation signature on pp.537--538
and identifies the class of Boolean group isotopes as a quasigroup
variety. Those pages were read and visually checked. Its discussion
of three-sorted objects was also inspected in the extracted text;
the remainder of the paper was not audited.
[Primary paper](https://dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/145946/CommentatMathUnivCarolRetro_57-2016-4_7.pdf).

These sources support the ordinary interpretation; they are not
credited with the obstruction proved here. In a three-sorted algebra,
the iterated term x*(x*y) is not generally well typed, so this proof
does not assert a result for that different generation operation.
Nor does it assert that IM fails to have a finite generator when
generation is specially defined to include closure under isotopy.
Neither qualification occurs in the supplied problem statement.

Searches for the problem number, finite generation of isotope
varieties, local finiteness, and Russian variants did not locate an
explicit earlier answer to 9.4 in the material checked. This limited
search is not proof of novelty. Falconer's 1970 primary PDF returned
HTTP 403 and was not read. Drapal's 2005 paper on free abelian-group
isotopes was downloaded and searched, but its proof was not audited
and no result from it is used. Given the elementary obstruction,
priority and the intended convention particularly need outside review.

## 6. Computational scope

The accompanying standard-library controls exhaust all Latin squares
of orders 1 through 4 and verify their factorial translation identity.
Separately they construct coordinate-shift quasigroups, check the
division axioms, compare every left-translation permutation with a
separate coordinate implementation, and verify concrete identity
violations and one-generator closures. These are checks of formulas
and conventions, not a replacement for the proof for arbitrary n.
