# 20.90: a two-generator infinite profinite group with abelian centralizers

Status: complete candidate affirmative solution; internal checks pass. The
matrix centralizer fact is classical. The application to the exact profinite
question and its priority require further literature and outside review.

## Exact question and construction

The supplied Notebook, printed page160 (visually checked), asks for a finitely
generated infinite profinite group whose nonidentity centralizers are
pronilpotent and which is not prosoluble. Finite generation here is topological,
as usual for profinite groups and in the source paper.

Let F4=F2(omega), where omega^2+omega+1=0, and put

    R = F4[[t]],     K = F4((t)),
    X = [ 1  1 ],   Y = [ 0      1     ].
        [ 0  1 ]       [ 1  omega + t ]

Both matrices have determinant1, because the characteristic is2. Define

    G = closure of <X,Y> in SL2(R)

using the t-adic topology. We prove that G is infinite, topologically
two-generated, not prosoluble, and has abelian centralizers of all nonidentity
elements. In particular, G answers20.90 affirmatively.

## 1. Profinite topology and finite generation

The ring R is the inverse limit of the finite discrete rings R/(t^n).
It is compact, Hausdorff and totally disconnected. The determinant-one locus
in R^4 is closed, with continuous multiplication and inversion. The congruence
kernels modulo t^n are open normal subgroups of SL2(R), have finite index,
and intersect trivially. Thus SL2(R) is profinite, as is its closed subgroup G.
By its definition, X and Y topologically generate G.

We do not assert that these two elements generate all of SL2(R).
Indeed, their image modulo t^2 has order240, whereas SL2(R/(t^2)) has
order60*4^3=3840. Surjectivity modulo t alone suffices below.

## 2. Infinitude

Write z=omega+t, and define polynomials over F4 by

    P_0(z)=0,  P_1(z)=1,  P_(n+1)(z)=z P_n(z)+P_(n-1)(z).

Direct induction by right multiplication gives, for n>=1,

    Y^n = [ P_(n-1)(z)   P_n(z)     ].
          [ P_n(z)       P_(n+1)(z) ]

For every n>=1, P_(n+1)(z) is monic of degree n: multiplication by z
raises the preceding leading degree, and the other summand has smaller
degree. Substitution z=omega+t preserves that leading degree. Consequently
the lower-right entry of Y^n is not1 for any positive n. Thus Y has infinite
order and G is infinite. This is an argument in the polynomial subring of
R; no assertion about limits of element orders is needed.

## 3. A nonsoluble finite continuous quotient

Reduction modulo t sends X,Y to

    x = [ 1  1 ],   y = [ 0    1   ]  in SL2(F4).
        [ 0  1 ]       [ 1  omega ]

Here is an elementary proof that they generate SL2(F4) and that this group
is not soluble; identifying it with A5 is not needed for the proof.

Put U(a)=[[1,a],[0,1]] and L(a)=[[1,0],[a,1]]. Direct multiplication gives

    y x y^(-1) = L(1),
    d := y^(-1) x y^(-2) = diag(omega,omega^2).

Furthermore,

    d U(a) d^(-1) = U(omega^2 a),
    d L(a) d^(-1) = L(omega a).

Since U(a)U(b)=U(a+b), and similarly for L, the pairs
{U(1),U(omega^2)} and {L(1),L(omega)} give every U(a) and L(a) over F4.
These elementary matrices generate SL2 over a field: row additions reduce
a determinant-one matrix to a diagonal one, allowing a row interchange
by W=U(1)L(1)U(1); and

    U(a)L(a^(-1))U(a)W = diag(a,a^(-1))  (a != 0)

in characteristic2. Hence <x,y>=SL2(F4).

The derived subgroup contains

    d U(a) d^(-1) U(a)^(-1) = U(omega a),
    d L(a) d^(-1) L(a)^(-1) = L(omega^2 a).

As a ranges over F4 these are all the elementary generators. Thus SL2(F4)
is nontrivial and perfect, so it is not soluble. Its order is60
((4^2-1)*4), and it is also the familiar simple group A5.

The continuous reduction map on G is therefore onto this nonsoluble finite
group. Every finite continuous quotient of a prosoluble group is soluble.
It follows that G is not prosoluble.

## 4. Abelian centralizers

We use a field-level lemma, not a statement about congruence quotients.

**Lemma.** If A is a nonscalar two-by-two matrix over any field k, then
its centralizer in M2(k) is the commutative algebra k[A].

**Proof.** There is a vector v such that v,Av are independent. Otherwise
every line would be A-invariant: taking a basis e1,e2 gives eigenvalues
a,b, and invariance of the line through e1+e2 forces a=b, making A scalar.
If B commutes with A, write Bv=c v+d Av. Then B and cI+dA agree on v,
and commutation with A makes them agree on Av too. They agree on a basis,
so B=cI+dA. The converse is immediate. This algebra is commutative. QED.

Now regard G as a subgroup of SL2(K). A scalar aI in SL2(K) has a^2=1.
Since K is a field of characteristic2, (a-1)^2=0 implies a=1. Therefore
every nonidentity g in G is nonscalar. By the lemma, C_G(g) is contained
in the group of units of the commutative algebra K[g], so it is abelian.

Finally, C_G(g) is closed (commutation is a closed condition in a Hausdorff
topological group), and hence is an abelian profinite group. Its finite
continuous quotients are abelian and thus nilpotent. It is pronilpotent,
as required. QED.

## 5. A family of simple finite sections

The same idea gives, for every q=2^m>=4, a topologically finitely generated
infinite profinite CA-group mapping onto SL2(F_q)=PSL2(F_q). Namely, in
SL2(F_q[[t]]) take the closed subgroup generated by the finite constant
subgroup SL2(F_q) and

    D(t)=diag(1+t,(1+t)^(-1)).

The generating set is finite. The first diagonal entry of D(t)^n is the
nonconstant polynomial (1+t)^n for every n>0, so D(t) has infinite order.
The reduction is onto the constant subgroup. The field centralizer proof
above applies without change. For completeness, SL2(F_q) is perfect when
q>=4 by the same elementary-generator commutator argument, choosing a
nonidentity a in F_q^* so that a^2-1 is nonzero. These quotients are the
standard nonabelian simple groups PSL2(2^m).

This supplies an affirmative existence answer to Question2 of the2019 CA
paper as well, and realizes all these simple sections; it does not classify
all possible simple sections. The explicit two-generator example in
Sections1--4 gives the requested stronger finite-generation bound for q=4.

## Verification and limitations

- `scripts/verify_20_90.py` implements F4, polynomial arithmetic, matrices,
  and finite subgroup generation directly. It enumerates all60 matrices,
  verifies the two generator words and the commutator formulas, checks
  all59 nontrivial normal closures, and checks the algebra centralizer
  lemma on all252 nonscalar elements of M2(F4). It independently checks
  256 polynomial powers and their exact leading terms.
- `scripts/verify_20_90.g` independently constructs the field and finite
  group in GAP, checks A5 identification and perfectness, every one of
  the59 nonidentity centralizers, and128 polynomial powers.
- The Python image modulo t^2 has order240 and contains the nonidentity
  scalar (1+t)I. Its centralizer is the entire image, which still maps
  onto A5 and is not nilpotent. Thus this finite quotient is not CN.
  This deliberate control guards against the incorrect assertion that
  the construction is an inverse limit of finite CN-groups.
- The infinite claims follow from the proofs above, not from these finite
  tests. The computation of order240 is not needed for the theorem.

Outputs are retained in `results/20.90-python-controls.log` and
`results/20.90-gap-controls.log`. See `20.90-review.md` for the internal audit.
