# Problem 19.48: a counterexample over a quadratic extension

**Independent characteristic-three counterexample; a prior negative answer
was located, so this does not increase the new-candidate count.**
The prior September 2026 preprint and its audited characteristic-zero
construction are documented in `19.48-source-audit.md`.
The counterexample has all its levels free of rank two over F3[t] and
works in every order n>=3.

Let F=F3[j], with j^2=-1=2, and let t be transcendental over F. Put

    R=F3[t],       k=F3(t),       K=F(t),       J=tF[t],       beta=1+j.

Then R is a principal ideal domain with identity, k is its fraction
field, and K/k is algebraic of degree two. Indeed, 1,j are linearly
independent over F3(t): clearing denominators in a putative relation
A(t)+jB(t)=0 and comparing coefficients over F3 gives A=B=0.
Every element of K is in the field k(j), so the extension degree is two.

For a fixed n>=3 define the off-diagonal levels

    sigma_12 = F3+J,       sigma_21 = beta*F3+J,
    sigma_ij = J           for every other i!=j.                    (1)

These are R-modules. More explicitly, their R-bases can be chosen as

    sigma_12: (1,jt),      sigma_21: (beta,t),      J: (t,jt).

For the second equality use jt=beta*t-t. Independence of each displayed
pair follows from independence of 1,j over k and t!=0. Thus the levels
are in fact finitely generated free R-modules of rank two. Every one
contains the nonzero element t, proving irreducibility.

For pairwise distinct i,r,j, at least one of the positions (i,r),(r,j)
is outside {(1,2),(2,1)}. Its level is J, and the other is contained
in F[t]. Since J is an ideal of F[t],

    sigma_ir sigma_rj is contained in J, hence in sigma_ij.

Therefore (1) is an elementary net satisfying every hypothesis of 19.48.

It remains to show that it is not closed. Work first in SL2(F) with

    U=[[1,1],[0,1]],       V=[[1,0],[1+j,1]].

Straight matrix multiplication in characteristic three, using j^2=2,
gives the following nine-factor identity:

    V U V U^-1 V U V^-1 U^-1 V^-1 = [[1,2+j],[0,1]].                (2)

All ten prefix matrices, including the identity, are printed in
`19.48-word-table.md`, so (2) can be checked without any group enumeration.
Two independent calculations also verify it: 4-by-4 matrices over F3
and GAP's native 2-by-2 matrices over F9. No classification of finite
groups is used.

Embed these matrices in the upper-left 2-by-2 block of SL_n(K).
We have U=t_12(1) in E(sigma) and V=t_21(beta) in E(sigma), by (1).
Their inverses are also allowed since the levels are additive groups.
Identity (2) therefore proves t_12(2+j) in E(sigma).

However, 2+j does not belong to sigma_12=F3+tF[t]. Its constant
coefficient is 2+j, which is not in F3. This is an additional elementary
transvection, proving that sigma is not closed. QED.

The distinction between R-modules and k-vector spaces is essential:
the levels above are not k-vector spaces. Likewise K is a proper
algebraic extension of the fraction field k, rather than k itself.
Known affirmative theorems with either stronger hypothesis do not apply.
The proof does not depend on the complete candidates 19.61, 19.62 or
21.76; the eight-parameter finite search for 21.76 suggested (2), but
only this explicit identity is required here.
