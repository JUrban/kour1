## Why these certificates suffice

For a formation F and M<K, the condition K^F<=M is equivalent to
K/Core_K(M) in F: the residual is normal and hence, when contained
in M, is contained in its core. Conversely a quotient in F has kernel
containing the residual. Therefore a maximal chain all of whose core
quotients have Fitting height at most three is an N^3-subnormal chain.
This uses the exact definition on p.2 of the cited source.

For each exported group, a breadth-first traversal assigns an actual
permutation to every Cayley-graph vertex. Every generator edge is checked
by direct permutation composition, and all assigned permutations are
distinct. Thus the n vertices form an n-element subgroup of a symmetric
group. Their multiplication table is reconstructed from the traversal
words. Associativity follows from permutation composition; no sampled
associativity test is substituted for it.

All subsequent computations use that table. Generator closure gives
each subgroup. A Sylow certificate has exactly the full prime-power
part of the group order. Testing conjugates of its generators against
every group element gives its complete normalizer. For an edge M<K,
iteratively intersecting conjugates of M by generators of K produces
the exact core. Maximality is checked by verifying <M,x>=K for a
representative of every nontrivial right coset xM. Every element in
that coset generates the same overgroup together with M, so the check
exhausts all possible proper intermediate subgroups.

The certificate's tower Core_K(M)=T0<...<Tr=K has r<=3. Every Ti is
checked normal in K. For each Ti/T(i-1), the checker starts at T(i-1)
and repeatedly takes the full preimage of the center of Ti modulo the
current subgroup, using commutators with generators of Ti. Every step
must increase strictly until Ti is reached. This constructs a central
series and proves that the factor is nilpotent. It does not call any
GAP nilpotence or Fitting routine.

Changing a Cayley edge, deleting the normalizer generators, replacing
a nontrivial core by1, collapsing a nonnilpotent quotient's tower to
one factor, or deleting a chain edge is rejected. These controls check
five distinct logical obligations; they do not prove the verifier
itself free of defects. The printed algorithm above makes its argument
reviewable without treating successful execution as a general theorem.

## A simple lifting case and the remaining obstruction

For any hereditary formation F, suppose N is normal in G and contained
in every Sylow normalizer. If G/N belongs to w*F and pi(G)<=pi(F), then
G belongs to w*F. Indeed, for P a Sylow subgroup, normalizer lifting gives
N_(G/N)(PN/N)=N_G(P)N/N=N_G(P)/N. Its F-subnormal chain lifts under
the quotient map, preserving maximality and the residual condition.
Thus every Sylow normalizer in G is F-subnormal.

In particular, a central Frattini subgroup cannot give a saturation
counterexample. The prime sets of G and G/Phi(G) agree: a Sylow subgroup
contained in Phi(G) would be a normal Sylow subgroup, have a complement
by Schur--Zassenhaus, and hence contradict the nongenerator property
of Phi(G). Here we import Schur--Zassenhaus and the classical nilpotence
of the finite Frattini subgroup. The original screen reports Phi of
order2 for the six cases1296,3081--3086, so these also fall under this
elementary lifting case. No novelty is claimed for the observation.

The unresolved situation has a Frattini kernel not contained in a
Sylow normalizer. Passing from N_G(P)N to N_G(P) still requires an
argument. Neither these finite checks nor the central-kernel observation
establish it for arbitrary hereditary saturated formations.
