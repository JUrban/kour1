# Internal audit of the candidates for 18.92(a),(b)

Date: 2026-09-11. No outside review or confirmed priority claim.

## Exact definition and scope

The supplied printed page 128 was visually checked. Its definition
requires closure under intersections and a largest element. It does
not require closure under ambient joins, or require the inclusion of
all subformations of the largest member. The symbols in the compactness
definition refer to joins in the complete lattice under discussion.

This interpretation is explicit in a primary source from formation
theory: Tsarev--Kukharev, Section 2.2, defines the join using the
intersection of all members of the chosen family containing the given
classes. The examples therefore use the stated convention, with no
new interpretation of the word “formation.”

Both examples even contain the formation of all finite groups as top.
This handles the empty intersection whether it is understood relative
to that top or relative to the ambient universe of finite groups.
Neither example needs the empty class to count as a formation. If a
convention additionally requires the empty class to belong to the
family, one can adjoin it below the present bottom: the nonmodular
interval persists, and no element above the trivial-group formation
becomes compact. Thus the two failures persist under that convention.

## Mathematical audit

- For each S, groups in F_S have orders involving only primes in S.
  Quotients and subgroups cannot introduce new primes. A finite
  subdirect product embeds in the direct product of its factors;
  hence F_S has the required formation closure. Finite generation,
  solvability, and saturation of a formation are not required.
- Cyclic groups of prime order detect every inclusion and strict
  comparison. Thus mapping S to F_S is an order embedding and
  preserves arbitrary intersections, without needing a classification
  of formations or a theorem about their ambient joins.
- In part (a), an intersection containing a finite prime set is finite;
  otherwise it is P. An infinite union of allowed prime sets has no
  finite allowed upper bound, so its only allowed upper bound is P.
  The claimed join formula includes empty families and families that
  already contain P.
- For every non-bottom F_T, choose a prime q in T, including when
  T=P. The chain formed by exhausting all other primes has join F_P.
  Every finite subfamily is bounded by one member of that chain and
  omits C_q. This directly contradicts compactness for F_T. The chain
  is directed; there is no dependence on an unconventional definition
  allowing only directed witnesses. The bottom is compact and is the
  only compact member, proving nonalgebraicity.
- In part (b), all five formations are distinct and intersections give
  exactly N5. The join of the 2-group and 5-group formations inside
  this family is the top. The modular witness uses A<=B, as required
  by the modular law, and gives A on one side and B on the other.
  C_3 separates them.

The special role of internal joins is a substantive limitation of
the claim: these examples do not assert that the full lattice of
formations fails either property. They answer the existential
questions with the definition provided in the problem.

## Independent finite control

`python3 scripts/verify_18_92.py` passes. The five formation classes
are represented by their membership signatures on group orders from
1 through 210. Since membership depends only on order, every order
has a cyclic-group witness and the five signatures are distinct.
The signature sizes are 1,8,25,4,210. All 32 subfamilies have their
meet and join independently recovered as greatest lower and least
upper bounds, agreeing with the pentagon order.

Of the 65 triples satisfying the order premise of modularity, exactly
the displayed triple (A,C,B) fails, with sides A and B. The same
modular calculation passes all 1,296 applicable triples in a Boolean
lattice on four generators, providing a positive control.

This finite control supports only the five-element witness. It does
not test infinite nonalgebraicity. Finite truncations of the lattice
in (a) would themselves be algebraic and cannot verify that assertion.
Its proof is the explicit infinite chain for each nonzero element.

## Primary references and novelty scope

Tsarev--Kukharev, *On the lattice of all tau-closed totally composition
formations of finite groups*, institutional preprint:
https://elib.sfu-kras.ru/bitstream/handle/2311/142250/preprint-3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1 .
Read the abstract, Definition 1.1, and Section 2.2's definition of a
complete lattice of formations and its internal joins. The remaining
proof about the particular natural lattice of composition formations
was not audited or used. PDF/text cached under
`references/cache/tsarev-kukharev-formations`.

Tsarev's 2018 paper *Inductive lattices of totally composition
formations*, DOI 10.15446/recolma.v52n2.77156, was located in a
search excerpt with the same convention. Its publisher DOI link did
not open through the web tool. The primary institutional preprint
above independently supplies the definition used here.

English and Russian searches used the exact problem number,
non-algebraic and non-modular lattices of formations, prime sets,
the pentagon lattice, and the cofinite topology. They located the
question and papers about specified natural families, but no earlier
exact answer. This limited negative search does not establish novelty.
The underlying set-lattice constructions are elementary and standard;
the claimed candidate contribution is their application to the exact
definition in 18.92. Older formation monographs and unpublished
clarifications from the problem author remain unaudited.
