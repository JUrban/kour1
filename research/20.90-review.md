# Internal review of20.90

The two proofs answer one Notebook question affirmatively. The two-generator
characteristic-two example has abelian centralizers. The four-generator
2-adic example has centralizers which are abelian or pro-2. Both are
infinite and admit a nonsoluble finite continuous quotient. Novelty and
outside review remain pending.

## Exact scope and topology

- Page160 was visually checked against the supplied PDF. The question
  concerns centralizers of nonidentity elements and imposes no further
  restrictions on the characteristic or on the congruence quotients.
- Finite generation is topological; an infinite profinite group is not
  being asserted to be abstractly generated by finitely many elements.
- Both constructions use closed subgroups or quotients of compact matrix
  groups. Finite congruence quotients separate points. There is no use of
  an abstract subgroup equipped with an unproved profinite topology.
- The first construction deliberately takes the closure of two explicit
  generators. Surjectivity modulo t does not establish equality with
  SL2(F4[[t]]), nor is equality required. Its order240 image modulo t^2
  independently confirms that equality would be false.

## Infinite elements and nonsoluble quotients

- In characteristic two, the lower-right entry of Y^n is a monic
  polynomial of degree n for every n>0. The induction covers even n
  too; cancellation of lower terms cannot remove the leading term.
- The general q family uses D(t); (1+t)^n is nonconstant even when n is
  a power of2. In the 2-adic example U(n) cannot become scalar for any
  positive integer n. These give infinite elements in all constructions.
- The two mod-t generator words for L(1) and diag(omega,omega^2) were
  checked directly and independently in Python and GAP. The resulting
  root subgroups generate the entire SL2(F4).
- Nonsolubility is proved by perfectness using explicit commutators.
  It does not depend on an unverified finite group identification or a
  maximal-subgroup classification. A5 identification and all59 normal
  closures are independent finite controls.
- The finite quotient maps are continuous and onto. This is the needed
  obstruction to prosolubility; merely containing a finite nonsoluble
  abstract subgroup would need its own topological justification.

## Centralizers

- The characteristic-two proof works in the field F4((t)), where the
  only scalar of determinant1 is I. It does not apply this statement
  to a ring with nilpotents.
- The nonscalar two-dimensional centralizer is proved to be exactly
  the commutative algebra generated by the matrix. No diagonalizability,
  separability of eigenvalues, or algebraic closure is assumed.
- Closed abelian centralizers are profinite and have nilpotent finite
  quotients, so they are pronilpotent in the exact required sense.
- The finite quotient modulo t^2 actually contains the nonidentity scalar
  (1+t)I and is not CN. This is compatible with the theorem: centralizers
  in a quotient can acquire elements whose lifts do not commute.
- In the 2-adic example a projective centralizer may lift to matrices
  conjugating g to -g. This possibility is retained. Trace excludes it
  only in the nonzero-trace case.
- For trace-zero g, the argument excluding reduction I uses det(g)=1
  as well as trace(g)=0. Its conclusion does not require an unproved
  lifting claim about finite centralizers.
- The strict centralizer is pro-2 because its reduction and kernel are
  pro-2; the sign extension and the final central quotient remain pro-2.

## Reproducible controls and corrections

Python independently enumerates all60 determinant-one F4 matrices,
all59 nontrivial normal closures, all252 nonscalar algebra centralizers,
256 polynomial powers, and the240-element image modulo t^2. GAP checks
the finite group, all59 centralizers, perfectness, the two generator

The companion 2-adic check enumerates all65,536 matrices over O/4O,

An initial GAP polynomial check stopped at an assertion because its

explicit identity matrix. The complete corrected run passes. Earlier
interactive launcher/matrix syntax probes were also excluded; they
did not supply mathematical evidence.

## Literature and claim discipline

The CA property of SL2 in characteristic2 is an explicit old theorem of
Fine--Gaglione--Rosenberger--Spellman2015/2016 and is credited. The
2019 CA and2020 CN papers still posed the relevant profinite existence
questions. Their finite-generation and centralizer conventions were
checked. The2024 compact p-adic analytic paper answers other questions;
its full consequences have not been exhaustively audited.

The shortness of the characteristic-two application makes priority
verification a significant outstanding issue. No earlier explicit
profinite application was found in the limited searches, which is not
proof of novelty. The documents are candidate solutions for outside
review, with no external submission or priority claim made.
