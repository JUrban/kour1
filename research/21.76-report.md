# Problem 21.76: report and review guide

The candidate affirmative answer covers every n>=3 and every odd
characteristic. For any field k, take K=k(x,y) and put J=y*k[x,y].
Set the two levels at (1,2),(2,1) equal to kx+J and every other level
equal to J. The proof of closedness reduces modulo y and uses a direct
degree argument for alternating 2-by-2 matrix words. Completion would
force x^3 into kx+J, which reduction modulo y excludes.

The alternate characteristic-three example over F9(t) has exceptional
constant levels F3 and jF3, j^2=-1, and common ideal tF9[t]. Its seed
group has order 120; its two root intersections have order three.
This gives a construction with transcendence degree one.

Start with `research/21.76-proof.md`. The primary statement and source
scope are in `research/21.76-source-audit.md`. The polynomial-pair lemma
was proved by Koibaev in 2011; it is credited and reproved for our special
case. The proposed extension to irreducible nets, and both constructions'
priority, await outside review. They count as one problem resolution.

- Polynomial controls check 36,432 alternating words in characteristics

  coefficients, determinant one and the completion-degree witness.
  These are finite controls; the written proof covers arbitrary length.

## Mathematical review points

1. The elementary-net condition uses three distinct indices; both
   exceptional positions cannot appear in such a two-step path.
2. Every level contains a nonzero ideal, satisfying the exact irreducible
   definition; no field-linearity condition occurs in 21.76.
3. Any elementary transvection in the generated group has its parameter
   in the polynomial ring before the evaluation homomorphism is applied.
4. The alternating-word argument needs only existence of a reduced
   expression. Its nonconstant diagonal proves that no additional root
   element appears, without assuming a free-product presentation.
5. In an odd-length word, only omission of the final factor contributes
   to the relevant top-degree diagonal entry. Omitting an interior
   factor produces adjacent equal matrix units and hence zero.
6. The noncompletion argument uses necessary full-net multiplication
   rules only, not a quoted sufficiency criterion.
7. Both reductions work for all n>=3, and the first works in every
   characteristic. The F9 construction requires only its explicit finite
   matrix certificate; no finite-group classification enters.
8. Neither construction imports 19.61, 19.62 or a general assertion
   about closedness being detected by opposite pairs.
