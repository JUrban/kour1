# Problem 6.47: exact finite pilot and its limits

11 September 2026. No complete general solution or novelty claim.

## Exact scope and result

For each of the following groups, the only binary word functions that
give group operations are its multiplication and its reverse:

| SmallGroup ID | Normalized functions | Associative functions | Triples checked |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| [6,1] | 27 | 2 | 5,832 |
| [8,3] | 2 | 2 | 1,024 |
| [12,3] | 256 | 2 | 442,368 |
| [16,6] | 2 | 2 | 8,192 |
| [32,2] | 2 | 2 | 65,536 |

The totals are 289 normalized functions and 522,952 associativity
triples. Thus each of these five individual groups satisfies the
conclusion of 6.47 for every word in the question. Reversing the
multiplication gives an isomorphic group via inversion.

SmallGroup(24,3) had 8,192 normalized functions, exceeding the cap of
4,096. That case was omitted, with an explicit SKIP_CAP result. No
inference about its possible operations follows from this omission.
The separate extension to higher nilpotency classes is not part of
this frozen pilot packet.

## Exhaustiveness argument

Let e=exp(G), and let F be the subgroup of G^(G x G) generated by the
coordinate functions x and y. It consists exactly of the binary word
functions. Aut(G)-equivariance of words means that their values on
one representative of each orbit of ordered pairs determine all their
values. The exported orbit transporters specify that reconstruction.

Every permissible group operation has identity 1, since v(1,1)=1 is
an idempotent. Its exponent sums a,b satisfy a=b=1 modulo e, by the
two unit laws. The abelianization of F is C_e x C_e: its two generators
have order dividing e, while restriction to the two coordinate axes
maps F onto C_e x C_e and kills F'. The orders of the coordinate power
functions are exactly e. Thus the normalized operations are precisely
the coset xy F'. Conversely every member of this coset has the unit
laws, the original inverses, and the original powers. Associativity
is the sole remaining group axiom to test.

The GAP exporter constructs this direct product, generated subgroup,
derived subgroup and entire coset. Its enumeration and SmallGrp data
are trusted computational inputs, with source retained. Python expands
the coordinates and checks every triple for each exported operation.
It also checks uniqueness, the coset count, every transporter as an
automorphism, its action on its pair, unit and inverse laws, and powers
through e. The original and opposite operations must both be present.
A first failed triple is retained whenever any operation fails.
The auditor replays these finite checks from the frozen exports;
it is not a separate implementation of GAP's group enumeration.

## Provenance and failures

All six actual GAP processes completed with exit 0, clean logs and
completion sentinels. The outer pilot process also completed with
actual exit 0. The initial Python attempt failed with exit 1 because
NumPy was absent from the default import path; it started no GAP jobs.
Its original script and log are retained. The successful runner uses
the already installed NumPy 2.5.3 in software/coset-python. The stored
requirements file records that dependency; no new package was installed.

This is a serial search, using the existing bin/gap launcher with a
4 GiB workspace ceiling and 180-second timeout for each group.

## Literature and mathematical boundary

Płonka, *Associative words in the symmetric group of degree three*,
Algebra and Discrete Mathematics 15(1) (2013), pp. 83–95, already
classifies all associative words in S3. Lemma 3.4 covers the normalized
case and gives precisely xy and yx. Our S3 result is a computational
rediscovery. All thirteen pages of mathematical text were inspected;
we do not claim an independent audit of every source calculation.

Primary PDF:
https://admjournal.luguniv.edu.ua/index.php/adm/article/download/736/267

The original conjecture and the older classification through class
three appear in Cooper (1974); see the source audit and self-contained
class-two proof in research/6.47-class-two.md. Neither that special
case nor this finite pilot increases the complete-candidate count.
For larger searches, a nonisomorphic group operation is insufficient:
an identity obstruction or a positive variety-membership certificate
is still necessary. Every finite abelian output of the same exponent
belongs to var(G), since G has cyclic subgroups of every element order,
their direct product has exponent e, and the abelian variety of
exponent dividing e is generated by C_e.
