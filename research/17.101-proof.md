# Problem17.101: candidate homogeneous extension of every representation

Status: complete candidate affirmative answer, with an internal audit and
passing exact Python and GAP controls. Novelty and outside review remain
pending. This is the eleventh complete candidate in the portfolio.

Fix a commutative ring k with identity (in particular, any field). A
representation is a left kG-module V together with its group G. A
morphism (f,theta):(U,A)->(V,G) consists of a k-linear map f and a group
homomorphism theta satisfying f(a u)=theta(a)f(u). An embedding is
injective on both sorts. Homogeneity has the meaning in the Notebook:
every isomorphism between finitely generated subrepresentations extends
to an automorphism of the whole representation.

## Extension of any set of partial isomorphisms

Suppose (V,G) is given, with any set of isomorphisms

    (f_i,theta_i):(U_i,A_i) -> (U_i',B_i)

between subrepresentations. Neither U_i nor U_i' need be a kG-submodule;
they are invariant only under A_i and B_i, respectively. Form the
multiple HNN extension

    H=<G,t_i | t_i a t_i^(-1)=theta_i(a), a in A_i, all i>.

The HNN embedding theorem identifies G with a subgroup of H. Define
left kH-modules

    M=kH tensor_(kG) V,
    E=direct_sum_i kH tensor_(kA_i) U_i.

Define a kH-linear boundary map delta:E->M on the i-th summand by

    delta_i(h tensor u)=h tensor u - h t_i^(-1) tensor f_i(u).

This is well-defined: for a in A_i, the HNN relation gives

    h a t_i^(-1)=h t_i^(-1) theta_i(a),

and equivariance gives f_i(a u)=theta_i(a)f_i(u). Thus both terms
respect the tensor relation h a tensor u=h tensor a u. Put

    W=M / image(delta).

The crucial assertion is that V->W, v->class(1 tensor v), is injective.

## Why no vector is lost

Use the Bass--Serre tree T of the multiple HNN extension. Its vertices
are the cosets hG, and its oriented edges of type i are the cosets
hA_i, with endpoints hG and h t_i^(-1)G. The coset endpoints are
well-defined by the displayed HNN relation. This graph is connected;
a non-backtracking closed path would give a reduced HNN word in G,
contrary to Britton's lemma. Hence it is a tree.

As k-modules, induction decomposes M as a direct sum of copies of V,
one at each vertex. Similarly E is a direct sum of copies of U_i,
one on each corresponding edge. At its two endpoints an edge module
maps injectively to the vertex modules: up to invertible group actions,
the maps are U_i->V and f_i:U_i->U_i'->V.

Suppose delta(z) is supported at just one vertex v_0, with z in E.
Only finitely many edge components of z are nonzero. If this support
is nonempty, it is a finite forest with an edge, and has a leaf v
other than v_0. Exactly one nonzero edge component contributes at v.
Its endpoint map is injective, so the component of delta(z) at v is
nonzero, a contradiction. Therefore z=0, and delta(z)=0.

It follows that image(delta) intersects every single vertex module
trivially. In particular V embeds in W. This proof uses no semisimplicity,
finite-dimensionality, purity of submodules, or division by group orders;
it is valid over any fixed commutative coefficient ring k.

In W the relation delta_i(1 tensor u)=0 says

    t_i (1 tensor u)=1 tensor f_i(u).

Thus the automorphism of the representation (W,H) given by

    w -> t_i w,       h -> t_i h t_i^(-1)

extends the prescribed isomorphism (f_i,theta_i).

## Iteration and homogeneity

Starting with (V_0,G_0)=(V,G), let (V_(n+1),G_(n+1)) be the extension
just constructed, using the set of all isomorphisms between finitely
generated subrepresentations of (V_n,G_n). The embeddings let us form

    V_infinity=union_n V_n,      G_infinity=union_n G_n.

The actions are compatible, so this is a representation containing the
original one. Every finitely generated subrepresentation of this union
is contained in one stage: choose its finitely many group generators
and module generators, all of which occur at a common finite stage.
That stage is closed under the group action and all k-linear operations.
Consequently the domain and range of any isomorphism between two such
subrepresentations lie together in some (V_n,G_n). A stable letter in
G_(n+1) realizes that isomorphism. Acting by this same stable letter
and conjugating by it gives an automorphism of the final union, so
the extension persists through all later stages.

This proves the affirmative answer to17.101 under the usual fixed-ring
category of representations. In fact all required automorphisms can be
chosen in the form induced by an element of the final acting group.

## An example showing why enlargement matters

Over k=F_p, let G=C_p act on V=k e_1+k e_2+k e_3 by
g e_1=e_1, g e_2=e_2+e_1, and g e_3=e_3. The subrepresentations
(k e_1,G) and (k e_3,G) are isomorphic by e_1->e_3 and the identity
on G. This isomorphism cannot extend to an automorphism of (V,G)
with group component the identity: any commuting linear map preserves
image(g-1)=k e_1, so cannot send e_1 to e_3.

An explicit extension is W=k[z,z^(-1)]^2, regarded as a k-vector
space, with H=C_p x <t>. Let g act by the same two-dimensional
unipotent matrix and t by multiplication by z. Embed the original
basis as e_1->(1,0), e_2->(0,1), e_3->(z,0). These three vectors
are k-linearly independent and give the prescribed G-action. The
element t sends the first to the third and centralizes G, as required.
This example also makes clear that we enlarge the representation
space, while keeping the original coefficient field fixed.

## Size control

Put kappa=max(aleph_0,|k|,|G|,|V|). Finitely generated subrepresentations
and their isomorphisms are determined by finite tuples of elements and
images, so at each stage there are at most kappa of them. Each HNN
group, induced module, quotient, and countable union has cardinality
at most kappa. Thus the construction does not require enlarging this
infinite cardinal bound. Over a countable ring, a countable input has
a countable homogeneous extension.

## Sources and verification

The category is the fixed-ring, two-sorted category Rep-k used in
Gvaramia--Plotkin, *Action-type axiomatizable classes of group
representations*, Section1.1, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0406544 . Their
right actions can be converted to our left actions by g v=v circle g^(-1).

Aladova--Gvaramia--Plotkin, *Logic in representations of groups*,
Algebra Logika51:1 (2012),3–40, Definition5.8 and Problem5.11,
https://alglog.org/51-60/51/51N1-1.pdf , formulates exactly this notion
and embedding question. Their discussion notes that ordinary induction
after enlarging G does not automatically extend the prescribed module
map. The quotient by the edge relations above supplies the missing
compatibility, and the tree proves it preserves V.

The group-theoretic inputs are the multiple HNN embedding theorem and
Britton's lemma, equivalently the Bass--Serre tree of a graph of groups.
See Higman--Neumann--Neumann, *Embedding Theorems for Groups* (1949),
https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-24.4.247 , and the descriptions of
TheoremII, Britton's lemma, and the tree theorem in Bridson--Nyberg-Brodda,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10800 . The module argument above is explicit.
