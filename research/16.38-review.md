# Problem 16.38: internal review

12 September 2026. Same-agent review, not independent acceptance.

1. A subgroup K contained in AB has every power of each element in AB.
   The argument uses this, not merely g in AB. Products of periodic
   subgroups can contain individual elements of infinite order.
2. A finitely generated periodic soluble group is finite: its quotient
   by the last derived subgroup is finite by induction; that subgroup
   is then finitely generated by Schreier and is periodic abelian.
3. Local finiteness passes to subgroups and homomorphic images. Thus
   the intersection of the two images used in Lemma 3 is locally finite.
4. The canonical map D->Q tensor D is injective precisely because D is
   torsion-free. The action and cocycle extend to this rational module.
5. Only two selected elements of the locally finite group are needed.
   They generate a finite group H, so the cocycle can be averaged there.
   A global average or a global coboundary on C is not asserted.
6. Averaging gives delta(h)=v-hv with the displayed left-action
   convention. The finite orbit span is invariant and finite-dimensional.
7. A positive definite inner product averaged over H is invariant and
   still positive definite. Equality of the norms of v,v-t,v-2t forces
   t=0 by the two explicit quadratic equations.
8. In Lemma 3, A_0 and B_0 meet D trivially because they are periodic.
   They therefore give isomorphic sections over exactly the same group C.
   The full preimage of C is D A_0=D B_0 even though AB need not be a group.
9. Write b(c)=delta(c)a(c). Multiplication gives the cocycle identity.
   A factorization of a point in D has inverse scalar coordinates, so
   a(c)b(c^-1)=-delta(c). This proves the exact set equality
   D intersect AB=-delta(C), with no permutability assumption.
10. The quotient G/D has smaller derived length. The image of the
    entire cyclic subgroup, not a chosen finite set of powers, satisfies
    the induction hypothesis. Hence some positive power g^r lies in D.
11. T=tor(D) is characteristic in the abelian characteristic subgroup D,
    so is normal in G. The group D/T is torsion-free: if a positive
    multiple of d is torsion, then d itself is torsion.
12. Both g^r and g^(2r) remain in the required product after quotienting
    by T. Their images in D/T are forced trivial by Lemma 3. Thus g^r
    is torsion, which makes g torsion. There is no lost finite-order case.
13. The trivial, abelian, finite, and intersecting-factor cases are all
    included. No countability or finite-rank condition is used.
14. The infinite direct-sum example demonstrates that the cocycle need
    not be globally principal in the finite-support rational module.
    It also gives nonperiodic elements of AB without violating the theorem.
15. The exact controls deliberately use nonorthogonal coordinates,
    reconstruct AB separately, and retain a finite-torsion mutation in
    which the doubling obstruction fails. They corroborate calculations;
    the lemmas establish the infinite assertion.
