# A counterexample to the stronger endomorphism inequality

Status: verified finite counterexample to the stronger question in Cameron's
2025 paper. **This is not a solution to the equality question in Notebook
19.20.** No prior example was located in the targeted searches, but novelty
and outside review remain outstanding.

Let E be the central product of two dihedral groups of order 8, identifying
their central involutions, and put G=E x C_2. Then |G|=64 and

    |End(G)|  = 6,074,368,
    |PIso(G)| = 3,277,312.

In particular, |End(G)| > |PIso(G)|. This disproves the strict inequality
in the concluding question of Peter J. Cameron, *Endomorphisms and partial
isomorphisms*, Semigroup Forum 111 (2025), 538--539,
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00233-025-10514-5 . His reported search through
order 63 found the opposite inequality throughout. The supplied Notebook
asks only whether equality characterizes abelian groups; the example here
does not give equality.

## Explicit group and endomorphism count

Use binary coordinates (v,z,c) with v=(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4) in F_2^4 and
z,c in F_2. Multiplication is

    (v,z,c)(w,t,d) = (v+w, z+t+v_1 w_2+v_3 w_4, c+d).

This is an associative group law because the central correction is bilinear.
The central coordinate z is the shared involution of the two dihedral
factors, and c is the independent central involution. The four standard
v-basis elements x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2 and c are involutions. Their only
nontrivial basic commutators are [x_1,y_1]=[x_2,y_2]=z.

On V=F_2^4 the square map is

    q(v)=v_1 v_2+v_3 v_4,

and the commutator form is b(v,w)=q(v+w)+q(v)+q(w). An endomorphism
sends z to either 1 or z, since z is a commutator and G'=<z>.

If z maps to 1, the images of the five generators are commuting involutions.
Their v-coordinates span a totally singular subspace of V. There are one,
nine, and six such subspaces of dimensions 0,1,2, respectively; there are
none of larger dimension. These small counts can be obtained directly
from q, or by the independent enumeration below. The number of linear
surjections from F_2^5 onto a fixed k-space is the product of (32-2^i)
for 0<=i<k. Each of the five chosen vector images has four independent
choices of the two central coordinates. This gives

    4^5 (1 + 9*31 + 6*31*30) = 6,000,640.

If z maps to z, the first four vector images form an isometry of the
nonsingular quadratic form q. There are 72 such isometries: choose the
first nonzero singular vector in 9 ways, its singular partner with pairing
1 in 4 ways, and a hyperbolic basis of the remaining plane in 2 ways.
The four images have 4^4 central-coordinate choices. The image of c must
centralize their full vector span, and hence can be any of the four
elements of Z(G). This gives 72*4^4*4=73,728 maps. Every counted choice
satisfies the displayed presentation, so there are no additional constraints
or duplicate maps. Summing the two cases proves the endomorphism count.

## Partial isomorphisms and a complete finite certificate

For each isomorphism type T let s_T count the actual subgroups of G of
that type. Then

    |PIso(G)| = sum_T s_T^2 |Aut(T)|.

The table below gives all types. Its first three columns are respectively
the subgroup order, centre order, and number of elements satisfying x^2=1
(including the identity). In this particular group these three invariants
distinguish the 17 types; the checker also verifies the necessary quadratic
isometries, rather than assuming the invariants are sufficient.

The counts sum to 593 subgroups, and the weighted squares give 3,277,312.
Here is why the enumeration and its isomorphism checks are complete.

1. Subgroups containing z correspond exactly to subspaces of G/<z>=F_2^5.
   There are 374 of those subspaces. The checker generates them all by
   adjoining every vector outside each previously generated subspace.
2. A subgroup avoiding z is elementary abelian: its squares and commutators
   lie in both that subgroup and <z>, and hence are trivial. Its image U
   in F_2^5 is totally singular. The full inverse image of U is elementary
   abelian and has exactly 2^dim(U) complements to <z>. The checker obtains
   them by independently choosing the z-coordinate of each lifted basis
   vector. This supplies the other 219 subgroups, with exact deduplication.
3. Abelian subgroups are elementary abelian or C_4 x C_2^r, since all
   squares belong to <z>. Their automorphism orders are |GL(k,2)| and
   2^(2r+1)|GL(r,2)|, respectively. The latter formula chooses the C_4
   generator's image, the invertible map on the elementary quotient, and
   the independent central order-two adjustments.
4. For any nonabelian subgroup L, L'=<z> is characteristic. Isomorphisms
   between two such groups correspond to quadratic isometries on L/<z>,
   together with the independent choices of lifts of a quotient basis.
   The group has the presentation specified by those basis squares and
   commutators, so every isometry lifts and gives exactly 2^dim(L/<z>)
   automorphisms. The checker counts all isometries by enumerating linearly
   independent basis images with the prescribed q-values and pairings.

The independent certificate is `scripts/verify_19_20_extraspecial.py`, whose
complete output is `results/19.20-extraspecial-independent.json`. It uses
only binary arithmetic and the explicit multiplication law, with no GAP,
SmallGrp data, or subgroup/automorphism library. It also checks all 91,277
ordered products internal to the enumerated subgroups.

A separate GAP certificate constructs E as the central product D_8*D_8,
forms G=E x C_2, and counts by subgroup/quotient types:
`scripts/verify_19_20_extraspecial.g` and
`results/19.20-extraspecial-gap.log`. It identifies G as SmallGroup(64,264)
and obtains exactly the same two totals by a different method.

The broader GAP search also finds SmallGroup(64,263) with End=3,276,800
and PIso=1,970,176. That second example has not received the independent
coordinate audit above and is not needed for the counterexample.
