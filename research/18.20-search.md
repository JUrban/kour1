# 18.20: semiproportional-character screen

Status: complete bounded search, no counterexample. No general solution
or novelty claim is made.

Two distinct irreducible characters phi,psi are semiproportional when
their restrictions to each part of some partition of the conjugacy
classes are proportional. To test this, consider the column vectors
(phi(g),psi(g)) in C^2. Ignore common zeros. The nonzero vectors must
lie on exactly two lines. The identity fixes the first line, with
ratio phi(1)/psi(1); the first column off that line fixes the second.
All later columns must lie on one of them.
The printed statement on page119 was visually checked against the PDF.

The criterion uses cross products, so no division or numerical
approximation is involved. It also admits horizontal and vertical
lines, making it at least as broad as a convention requiring nonzero
proportionality constants. A negative result therefore cannot be
caused by excluding a zero constant.

`scripts/search_18_20.g` completed over all 2,750 ordinary CTblLib
tables with none unavailable. It checked 33,810,661 unequal-degree
row pairs, finding no semiproportional pair. The 1,647,090 equal-degree
pairs were counted and omitted from the counterexample search.
Output: `results/18.20-tables.log`. Table entries may describe
isomorphic groups; this is library coverage, not a count of distinct
isomorphism types.

## Independent finite controls

`scripts/verify_18_20.g` constructs ten actual groups and computes
their character tables. For all 129 unordered pairs of distinct rows,
it enumerates every partition of the conjugacy classes, fixing the
identity in the first part, and tests the ranks of both restrictions.
This exhaustive definition-level test agrees with the sequential
criterion on all pairs: 16 positive and 113 negative controls.
Every positive pair has equal degrees. This includes common zeros and
nonrational cyclotomic entries. All controls PASS; see
`results/18.20-controls.log`.

## Orthogonality supplies a further necessary identity

For completeness, suppose a semiproportional pair exists and set
a=phi(1)/psi(1)>0. A vertical second line is impossible: then psi
vanishes on the second part, and <phi,psi>=a<psi,psi>=a, contrary to
orthogonality. A horizontal second line is likewise impossible,
because the identity part contributes strictly positively to the
inner product and the other part contributes zero.

Thus write phi=a psi on the identity part and phi=b psi on its
complement, where b is finite and nonzero. Let

    x = |G|^(-1) sum_{g in the first part} |psi(g)|^2.

Both x and 1-x are positive. Orthogonality and the two unit norms give

    a x + b(1-x)=0,
    a^2 x + |b|^2(1-x)=1.

Hence b is negative real and b=-1/a. It follows pointwise that

    phi^2 - psi^2 = (a-1/a) phi psi.

Writing phi(1)=g d and psi(1)=g e, with gcd(d,e)=1, shows that de
divides every irreducible multiplicity in phi psi: multiply the
identity by de and use gcd(de,d^2-e^2)=1. This necessary condition is
not asserted to force equal degrees; it is not a proof of the
conjecture. None of the screen counts depends on assuming it.

## Primary-source check

Belonogov,
[*On the semiproportional character conjecture*](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=smj&option_lang=eng&paperid=965&wshow=paper),
Siberian Math. J.46 (2005), 233--245, states the same conjecture and
reports partial results, including the 2-decomposable case and closure
under direct products. Its primary abstract and metadata were read;
the full proof was not audited. Later work on symplectic and
alternating groups was located during limited searches, but no full
resolution was found. No novelty claim is made for the orthogonality
observations or the finite computation.
