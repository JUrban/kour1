# Problem 17.34: candidate positive answer

Status: complete candidate positive resolution. Exact finite controls passed;
the internal mathematical and first literature audits are complete. Outside
review and broader novelty checking remain. The class-at-most-two case was
already proved by Shakhova (2015) and is credited below.
Recorded 2026-09-11. The printed statement on PDF page 108 was visually checked.

## Exact scope

Let N_c be the quasivariety of all torsion-free nilpotent groups of class
at most c. If H is a divisible subgroup of G in N_c, is its dominion in
G relative to N_c equal to H? The answer proposed here is yes, for every
positive integer c and without any finite generation assumption.

The claim concerns this full quasivariety. It does not assert the same
result for every subquasivariety, which can impose additional identities
or quasi-identities on the separating target.

Recall that the dominion consists of the elements g of G for which every
pair of homomorphisms from G to any group in N_c agreeing on H must also
agree on g. We construct one pair whose equalizer is exactly H.

## A bounded-class Lie algebra separation lemma

**Lemma.** Let L be a Lie algebra over Q, nilpotent of class at most c,
and let h be any Lie subalgebra. There is a Lie algebra P over Q of
class at most c and two injective Lie homomorphisms f,g:L -> P such
that f(x)=g(x) if and only if x is in h.

Write U=U(L) for the unital universal enveloping algebra and I for its
augmentation ideal. Let J=U h be the left ideal generated by h, and put

    M = I / (J + I^(c+1)).

Both ideals in the denominator are contained in I and are left ideals,
so M is a left U-module. Define

    d:L -> M,  d(x)=x + J + I^(c+1).

The enveloping relation gives

    d([x,y]) = x d(y) - y d(x),

so d is a Lie algebra 1-cocycle for the induced action on M.

We first prove that ker d=h, taking care that the truncation has not
killed any additional elements of L. Write L_i=gamma_i(L), with
L_(c+1)=0. Choose a basis of L adapted both to this finite filtration
and to h: the basis elements of filtration weight at least i form a
basis of L_i, and a subset forms a basis of h. Such a choice is obtained
successively down the filtration, extending bases of h intersect L_i
and then bases of L_i. Give a basis vector its largest filtration index
as weight, an integer between 1 and c.

Order the basis with all vectors outside the chosen basis of h before
all vectors in that basis. The order need not respect the weights.
The PBW theorem supplies the ordered monomial basis of U. For each n,
I^n is spanned by precisely the PBW monomials of total weight at least n.
Indeed a vector of weight w belongs to L_w, hence to I^w, giving one
inclusion. For the other, reordering a product of at least n elements
of I uses xy=yx+[x,y]. The relation [L_i,L_j] is contained in L_(i+j),
so no term produced by reordering decreases total weight. Equivalently,
the span of PBW monomials of weight at least n is the corresponding
multiplicative filtration, whose degree-one term is I.

Also J is exactly the span of ordered PBW monomials containing at least
one h-basis factor. Such a monomial ends in an h-factor and is in J.
Conversely, multiplying an ordered monomial on the right by an element
of h only requires reordering its final h-block. Since h is a Lie
subalgebra, the resulting terms still have a nonempty h-block.

Consequently M has as a basis all nonempty ordered monomials on the
complementary basis vectors having total weight at most c. Each
complementary basis vector survives as its own single-letter monomial,
and those images are independent. The h-basis vectors vanish. This
proves ker d=h, including when L has infinite dimension.

Form the semidirect Lie algebra P=L semidirect M, making M abelian:

    [(x,m),(y,n)] = ([x,y], x n - y m).

Set f(x)=(x,0) and g(x)=(x,d(x)). The cocycle identity makes g a Lie
homomorphism, and both maps are injective by their first coordinate.
Their equalizer is ker d=h.

It remains essential to preserve the bound c. For every r>=1,

    gamma_r(P) is contained in gamma_r(L) + I^(r-1) M.

Induction proves this because gamma_r(L) is contained in I^r,
[gamma_r(L),M] is contained in I^r M, and
[L,I^(r-1)M] is contained in I^r M. But I^c M=0: the module starts
with I and is truncated modulo I^(c+1). Thus gamma_(c+1)(P)=0,
as required. Using U instead of I as the numerator would generally
give class c+1 and would not suffice for the Notebook problem.

## Passage to torsion-free nilpotent groups

Use the standard rational Malcev completion and characteristic-zero
Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff correspondence. Every torsion-free nilpotent
group G of class at most c embeds in a uniquely divisible nilpotent
group D of the same class bound. The BCH correspondence identifies D
with a nilpotent Lie algebra L over Q of class at most c. Uniquely
divisible subgroups correspond to Q-Lie subalgebras, and homomorphisms
correspond to Q-Lie homomorphisms. These facts hold for arbitrary
dimension and arbitrary torsion-free nilpotent groups, by the rational
completion construction (or its directed union over finitely generated
subgroups).

Since roots are unique in a torsion-free nilpotent group, a divisible
subgroup H of G is uniquely divisible. Its image in D therefore
corresponds to a Q-Lie subalgebra h of L.

Apply the lemma. The BCH group B associated to P is uniquely divisible,
torsion-free, and nilpotent of class at most c; hence B belongs to N_c.
The two induced group homomorphisms from D to B, restricted to G,
agree exactly on H. Every element outside H is thus excluded from the
dominion. The reverse inclusion follows directly from the definition.
Therefore

    dom_(N_c)^G(H) = H.

For c=1 the construction reduces to M=L/h with the trivial action,
providing the elementary abelian-group control. The trivial group and
the cases H=1 or H=G are also covered.

## Imported facts and provenance

The only imported mathematical results are PBW and the rational Malcev/BCH
correspondence. A modern explicit statement of the latter is Theorem 2.8
of Bahturin--Olshanskii, *Nilpotent algebras, implicit function theorem,
and polynomial quasigroups*, [arXiv:2208.09527v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09527v1).
The theorem preserves the nilpotency class and describes recovery of the
Lie operations from group operations and rational powers. Their Lemma 3.1
supplies the divisible hull for every torsion-free nilpotent group. Both
statements and the surrounding discussion were read in the
[deposited manuscript](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10438489), pages 13--15.
Suciu, *Formality and finiteness in rational homotopy theory*,
[DOI 10.4171/EMSS/74](https://doi.org/10.4171/EMSS/74), Section 6.2,
also explicitly states rationalization for arbitrary nilpotent groups,
its universal property, and its torsion kernel.

In particular, a divisible subgroup is closed under the rational powers
in the ambient group, because roots are unique there. The inverse BCH
operations are finite expressions in products, inverses, and these
powers (with formulas depending only on c). This gives the required
subalgebra statement directly, without a dimension assumption. These
same formulas show functoriality. The finite-dimensional construction
and its directed union also preserve the bound c.

The proof of the PBW filtration and the class bound is given above rather
than imported as a dimension-subgroup theorem. Finite exact controls
using a tensor presentation are recorded in `17.34-review.md`.

Bergman's [unpublished pre-1973 manuscript](https://math.berkeley.edu/~gbergman/papers/unpub/LieEpi.pdf)
on Lie algebra epimorphisms uses PBW and an enveloping-algebra construction
to show trivial dominions in the category of all Lie algebras (Theorem 2.1).
The bounded nilpotency class is an additional issue in the construction here.

Shakhova, [*Absolutely Closed Groups in the Class of 2-Step Nilpotent
Torsion-Free Groups*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/mzm10471), Math. Notes
97 (2015), 946--950, Theorem 1, proves the c<=2 case. The Russian original
explicitly identifies this as a partial resolution of 17.34 on page 937.
Budkin, [*On dominions of the rationals in nilpotent groups*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/smj3008),
Siberian Math. J. 59 (2018), 598--609, concerns the two-additional-generator
case for Q in arbitrary subquasivarieties of class at most three. Our
target need only belong to the full N_c and does not establish that
stronger subquasivariety assertion. A first search has found no prior
all-c resolution, but it cannot establish novelty.
