# Problem 11.5: the arbitrary coefficient ring is an obstruction

2026-09-11. Complete elementary negative answer to the literal printed
formulation; retained as a coefficient qualification, not a new-candidate
increment. The supplied Notebook p.46 was rendered and visually checked:
k is described as a commutative ring, without further hypotheses.

Let R=Z[t]/(t^2+5), let I=(2,1+t) in R, and take the infinite cyclic
group G=<z>. Thus G is torsion-free and polycyclic, and its group ring
over R is S=R[z,z^{-1}]. Define

    P = S direct-sum (I tensor_R S).

Then P is a finitely generated projective S-module of rank two and
contains two S-linearly independent elements, but P is not free.

## Projectivity, explicitly

Work in the fraction field of R. Set u=2, v=1+t,
a=(t-1)/2, b=-(1+t)/2. Direct calculation gives

    au=t-1, av=-3, bu=-(1+t), bv=2-t, au+bv=1.

In particular aI and bI lie in R. The surjection R^2 -> I given by
(c,d) -> uc+vd has an R-linear section x -> (ax,bx). Hence I is a
projective direct summand of R^2. The corresponding idempotent is

    E = [ t-1     -3  ] ,    E^2=E.
        [ -1-t   2-t  ]

Tensoring this split presentation with S preserves the splitting,
so P is finitely generated projective. Since R and S are domains,
the elements (1,0) and (0,2) of P are S-linearly independent.

## Nonfreeness

First, I is not principal. The homomorphism R -> F_2 sending t to1
has kernel I, so the additive index [R:I] is2. Multiplication by
alpha=c+dt on the free abelian group R, in basis1,t, has matrix

    [ c   -5d ],
    [ d    c  ]

of determinant c^2+5d^2. Thus I=(alpha) would imply c^2+5d^2=2,
which is impossible for integers c,d: if d is nonzero the left side
is at least5, and if d=0 an integer square cannot equal2.

The projective module I has constant rank one: localizing its split
presentation at any prime of R gives a free module over a local ring,
and its fraction-field dimension is one. Consequently its second
exterior power is zero and

    exterior^2_R(R direct-sum I) is isomorphic to I.

If P were free, its fraction-field rank would force P=S^2. Apply
the ring homomorphism S -> R that sends z to1. Tensoring the purported
isomorphism would give R direct-sum I isomorphic to R^2, whose second
exterior power is R. This would imply I isomorphic to R. An R-module
isomorphism R -> I has principal image, contrary to the preceding
norm calculation. Therefore P is not free.

The variant S^2 direct-sum (I tensor_R S) is nonfree by the same
determinant argument and even contains a free rank-two direct summand.
Thus strengthening independence to a split free summand would not
remove the coefficient obstruction.

## Reading limits and interpretation

The argument is self-contained apart from elementary localization and
exterior-power facts for finite projective modules. It does not assume
the full ideal-class classification or a group-ring freeness theorem.
The nonprincipal ideal used here is a classical example.

For context, the primary abstract and introductory excerpt of
Artamonov's *Projective nonfree modules over group rings of solvable
groups*, Russian1981 / English1983, were consulted:
https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/sm2459 . They concern stably free rank-one
examples and do not settle the higher-rank question after restricting
the coefficient ring. A primary excerpt from his later survey likewise
distinguishes principal-ideal coefficients in the free-abelian case:
https://www.mathnet.ru/links/4c9e660e5f9c852ea2db48968ad88801/rm56_eng.pdf .
Neither entire source was read, and no precise source theorem is needed
for the construction above. Nothing here settles a corrected version
with k required to be a field, Z, or another prescribed ring.
