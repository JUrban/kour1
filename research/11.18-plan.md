# Problem11.18: mutually Engel periodic presentations

Started 2026-09-11, approximately18:17 UTC. Exploration only, not counted.

The exact target is G(a,b)=<x,y | x=[x,_a y], y=[y,_b x]>.
Any nontrivial solvable quotient is impossible, since the two generators
are commutators. The Notebook notes G(1,b)=G(2,2)=1 and singles out
G(2,3) as unknown. One infinite example would answer the general
finiteness question negatively; an additional finite quotient would not.

## Routes and stopping conditions

1. Run a bounded coset enumeration for the first few presentations.
   Retain actual exits, limits, failures and successful tables. A timeout
   or failure is inconclusive. Any surprising collapse needs a separate
   algebraic or independently replayable proof certificate.
2. Investigate characteristic-zero two-by-two representations using
   Heineken's canonical matrix pair. A periodic auxiliary coordinate
   alone is insufficient: the multiplicative update of the other
   coordinate must also return to one.
3. In particular, his period-two auxiliary construction has multiplier
   lambda^2. Its eigenvalue need not be a root of unity in characteristic
   zero. Finite-field periodicity therefore does not directly give a
   complex mep-pair or an infinite image.
4. If a finite quotient is found, a subgroup with positive-rank
   abelianization would prove infinitude; otherwise record the quotient
   only. A full finite multiplication table does not prove that no
   larger presented group maps onto it.

Source novelty checks are bounded; no universal finiteness result or
infinite example has been established here.
