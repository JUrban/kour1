# Problem18.120: normal closure and bounded factorization search

12 September2026. General question unresolved; no new candidate.

Let G=AB, with A abelian and B of class at most two, and put
K=<A,B'>. If the normal closure C of B' in K is abelian, then
K intersect B is abelian. Indeed K=AC and C is generated by the
A-conjugates of B'. If x=ac belongs to K intersect B, it centralizes
B'; since C is abelian, a centralizes B', and since A is abelian,
a centralizes every A-conjugate of B', hence C. Two such elements
ac,a'c' now commute. Finally the factorization G=AB gives
K=A(K intersect B). Thus B0=K intersect B works, without assuming
A intersect B=1.

This condition holds whenever G has nilpotency class at most four.
For b,c in B', we have [b,c]=1, and b^a=b u, c^d=c v where
u,v belong to gamma_3(G). Since B' lies in gamma_2(G), all cross
commutators belong to gamma_5(G) or deeper and vanish. Hence all
A-conjugates of B' commute. This is a complete elementary partial;
its priority has not been determined.
