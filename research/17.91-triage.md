The question asks for an absolute bound on d(G)-d(M), where G is finite
soluble and M is maximal. If M is normal, d(G)<=d(M)+1 because G/M is
cyclic of prime order. If M has trivial core, the standard primitive
soluble structure gives G=V semidirect M with V elementary abelian,
again giving d(G)<=d(M)+1. Neither argument cancels the contribution
of a nontrivial core in the general case.

Medina Capilla--Sabatini, [On the maximal subgroups of almost simple and
primitive perfect groups](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004126102175),
published online 29 July 2026, concerns stabilization of the derived
series in maximal subgroups of almost simple and primitive perfect
groups. The introduction and nearby generalised-derived-length
statements were read in the publisher HTML. The paper was not read in
full or visually audited, and no theorem from it is imported here.
Its direction and hypotheses do not solve the present soluble-group
question. The notebook's formulation was read in the local index;
