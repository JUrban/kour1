# A four-regular counterexample to Problem15.89

Status: complete candidate negative resolution; internal audit and exact
controls pass. Outside review and further novelty checks remain pending.
11 September 2026.

## Statement

Problem15.89, printed page94 of the supplied Notebook, asks whether
every complex number is an eigenvalue of the adjacency operator of
every infinite connected vertex-transitive simple graph of finite
valency, acting on all complex-valued vertex functions. The page was
visually checked, including the unrestricted function space.

**Theorem.** There is an infinite connected vertex-transitive simple
graph of valency4 whose adjacency operator is invertible on the vector
space of all complex-valued vertex functions. In particular, zero is
not an eigenvalue, so the answer to15.89 is negative.

## The graph

Let

    G = C3 * C2 = <a,b | a^3=b^2=1>

and take the connection set

    S = {aba, aba^2, a^2ba, a^2ba^2}.

Define the undirected graph Gamma to have vertex set G, with g adjacent
to gs for every s in S. The free-product normal form shows that the
four displayed words are distinct and nonidentity. Inversion interchanges
aba with a^2ba^2 and fixes each of aba^2 and a^2ba. Thus S=S^-1,
and Gamma has no loops or multiple edges and has valency4.

For s1=aba and s2=a^2ba we have

    s1 s2^-1 = a^2,       (a^2) s1 (a^2) = b.

Consequently S generates both a and b, so Gamma is connected. The
words (ab)^n have distinct reduced normal forms for n>=0, so its
vertex set is infinite. Left multiplication by G preserves all edges
and is transitive on vertices. This checks all graph hypotheses.

## An inverse on all functions

For g in G define the translation operator R_g on C^G by

    (R_g f)(x) = f(xg).

These operators satisfy R_g R_h=R_(gh). Put

    U = R_a + R_(a^2),       B = R_b.

Since a^3=1 and b^2=1,

    U^2 = U + 2I,       U^-1 = (U-I)/2,       B^2=I.

Expanding the product of the three finite sums gives exactly the four
distinct neighbours in the connection set:

    A_Gamma = U B U.

Therefore

    A_Gamma^-1 = (U-I) B (U-I)/4.                 (1)

Multiplication in either order gives I, using U(U-I)=(U-I)U=2I
and B^2=I. Explicitly, the inverse sends f to the function whose
value at x is

    1/4 [ f(xaba) + f(xaba^2) + f(xa^2ba) + f(xa^2ba^2)
          - f(xab) - f(xa^2b) - f(xba) - f(xba^2) + f(xb) ].

Every coordinate in this formula is a finite sum. Hence (1) is an
identity on the entire space C^G, including unbounded functions;
there is no convergence, Hilbert-space or support hypothesis. If
A_Gamma f=0, applying (1) gives f=0. This proves the theorem.

The same proof works over every field of characteristic different
from2. Only the complex case is needed for the Notebook question.

## Verification and prior construction

The proof is finite and algebraic. Independent controls use free-product
normal forms in Python and projective integer matrices in GAP. Both
check the two-sided inverse and the graph connection set. GAP also
constructs actual finite quotient Cayley graphs of orders12,60 and168,
checking their full adjacency matrices and63,936 identity-matrix entries.
All processes exit zero. The scope and hashes are in
`results/15.89-free-product-summary.json`; the logical audit is in
`15.89-review.md`.

The factorization U B U is the familiar zig-zag pattern: move within
a triangle, cross a matching edge, and move within the next triangle.
The general graph product is due to Reingold--Vadhan--Wigderson,
*Entropy waves, the zig-zag graph product, and new constant-degree
expanders*, Annals of Mathematics155 (2002),157--187,
[DOI10.2307/3062153](https://doi.org/10.2307/3062153).
No novelty is asserted for that graph-product operation. The deduction
above supplies the particular infinite simple vertex-transitive graph
and the explicit inverse required here, without importing an expansion
theorem. Further priority checking for this consequence remains needed.
The related finite-matrix observation in Cavaleri--D'Angeli--Donno2019
is also credited, with precise reading scope, in `15.89-review.md`.

The earlier positive result for all finite-valency Cayley graphs of the
infinite dihedral group remains valid; see `15.89-dihedral.md`. It does
not apply to C3*C2. The present construction supplies a negative answer
to the full question rather than a further instance of that subclass.
