## Logical audit

1. **Exact topology.** Notebook page35 says E-topology. Protasov's
   original1986 opening paragraph identifies its upper subbasis
   with containment in an arbitrary open set, and its lower
   subbasis with meeting an arbitrary open set. Both were visually
   checked. Replacing this with Chabauty would invalidate the answer.
2. **Ambient scope.** G is an arbitrary locally compact Hausdorff
   group. Every subgroup under discussion is closed. No commutativity,
   discreteness, countable weight, or compact generation is assumed
   in the negative direction.
3. **Hausdorff subgroup space.** For H different from K, a relatively
   compact neighborhood of an element in H minus K can be chosen
   with closure disjoint from K. Its lower hit set and the upper
   avoidance set separate H and K. Finite point exclusions are
   consequently legitimate later.
4. **Containment sets.** D_1(H) is closed because H is closed. It is
   not assumed open in the whole subgroup space. Compactness of the
   chosen family and noncompactness of the particular subgroup
   supply the needed local conclusion.
5. **Full neighborhood proof.** The packet reproduces the needed
   compact-family case of Komarov--Protasov1981/Protasov1986. It
   now has no unproved specialized subgroup-space lemma.
6. **Non-relative-compactness.** For a neighborhood W, if the
   elements outside H in its offending subgroups had compact
   closure C, choose h in H outside C union C^(-1)C. A sufficiently
   small V makes both Vh and C Vh miss C. The element zx in the
   proof is outside H because x is in H and z is outside it. No
   normality of H or commutativity of G is hidden in this step.
7. **Inductive selection.** The forbidden elements at stage i lie
   in a finite union x_j U U^(-1) of compact sets. The previous
   assertion supplies x_i outside that union, and its witnessing
   subgroup also avoids all earlier V_j.
8. **Closed discrete elements.** Pairwise disjoint x_i U ensure
   that sufficiently small translates contain at most one x_i.
   Removing that possible point at any other point of G proves
   closedness, not merely absence of convergent sequences.
9. **Closed discrete subgroup family.** Lower neighborhoods meeting
   only finitely many K_i give discreteness. An alleged additional
   closure point must avoid every x_i; its upper neighborhood
   avoiding the closed set of all x_i then misses every K_i.
   Thus closedness is in the entire L(G), not only in the sequence.
10. **Compactness contradiction.** The constructed infinite closed
    discrete family lies in compact F and would be compact itself.
    This is impossible. No sequential compactness of F is assumed.
11. **Neighborhood versus openness.** To show U_H is open at a
    subgroup K contained in H, apply the lemma at K, not just at H.
    This is where every member of the image family being noncompact
    is used. Its local U_K lies inside U_H.
12. **Selector identities.** Reflexivity, antisymmetry, and
    transitivity follow directly from subgroup inclusion. Pullback
    along a homeomorphism preserves all three conditions and
    clopenness.
13. **Almost-disjoint family.** The binary encoding of each function
    f gives a distinct branch. Its set of initial-segment codes
    is infinite, and distinct branches intersect in only finitely
    many codes. This defines all A_f simultaneously in ZFC.
14. **Local topology.** Row tails in B_f are compact clopen sets
    in individual rows. The neighborhoods C_(f,N) yield a Hausdorff
    space because intersections A_f intersect A_g are finite.
15. **Compact neighborhoods.** In a cover of C_(f,N), a member
    containing a_f covers all but finitely many rows of that set.
    The remainder is covered by finitely many compact row tails.
    The neighborhood itself is therefore compact.
16. **Adding infinity.** A is closed discrete in Z, so Z is
    noncompact and its one-point compactification is available.
    Compact subsets of Z meet A finitely. This justifies the
    cofinite condition at infinity, even though Z has uncountably
    many points a_f.
17. **Scatteredness.** The four derived levels are checked directly.
    The displayed topology ensures no extra isolated or nonisolated
    points appear at any level. The height is four under the
    convention X^(4)=empty and X^(3) nonempty.
18. **All functions are present.** The thresholds t(n) depend on
    an assumed selector, but the space was already built with a_f
    for every f in omega^omega. Therefore each f_k=t+k is available;
    the space is not being modified after seeing the selector.
19. **Witness points.** For n in a cofinite part of A_f, the point
    v_(t(n)+1,n) lies in U_(u_n) and hence U_(a_f), while it lies
    outside B_f because t(n)+1<=f(n). The strict tail inequalities
    are consistent at every step.
20. **No unwanted accumulation.** D_f has one point in each used
    row, avoids a neighborhood of a_f, and meets the rows relevant
    to every different a_g only finitely. Thus it is closed
    discrete in Z. This is stronger and more explicit than merely
    saying it can accumulate only at infinity.
21. **Final contradiction.** Closedness of U_(a_f) forces infinity
    into it for every f>t. Some one of the infinitely many a_(f_k)
    also lies in U_infinity. These are distinct points mutually
    in each other's selector sets, contradicting antisymmetry.
22. **Positive characterization.** In the discrete abelian group,
    H_x contains e_y precisely when x is outside U_y. The complement
    reverses the naive mistaken assignment and gives the required
    monotonicity H_z contained in H_x whenever z is in U_x.
23. **Both subbases.** Lower continuity follows from finite support
    and clopen coordinate-membership sets. Upper continuity follows
    from the entire neighborhood U_x mapping to subgroups of H_x.
    Checking coordinate membership alone would prove only the
    weaker product/Chabauty continuity.
24. **Noncompact positive models.** Every H_x contains an infinite
    cyclic direct summand in a discrete group and is therefore
    noncompact. All such subgroups are closed, and the ambient
    discrete group is locally compact regardless of its cardinality.
25. **Topological embedding.** The selector's antisymmetry proves
    injectivity. Compactness of X and Hausdorffness of the target
    promote the continuous injection to a homeomorphism onto its
    image, as required by the question.
26. **Source corrections.** Dow--Watson's condition(1) is understood
    for distinct points. Their Lemma4/Theorem3 identification with
    canonically good Boolean algebras is not used: the2021 paper
    warns that it involves a misquotation. Only the explicit
    selector and the independently written counterexample enter.
27. **Status.** The mathematical argument is complete and uses only
    elementary topology after the source arguments are expanded.
    Protasov's lemma, the counterexample, and Skula terminology
    are all prior and credited. Newness of this application to the
    still-printed9.47 is unresolved; outside review remains pending.
