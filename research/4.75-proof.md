# Problem 4.75: a negative candidate with Sylow 2-subgroups of order two

Date: 11 September 2026. Complete candidate; independent review and
priority remain pending.

## Statement and input

Problem 4.75 of the supplied Notebook, printed/physical page 14, asks
whether an involution of a periodic group with locally cyclic or
generalized quaternion Sylow 2-subgroups must have central image modulo
the largest normal 2-prime subgroup.

**Claim.** For every sufficiently large odd prime p there is an infinite
three-generated perfect group G of exponent 2p whose Sylow 2-subgroups
all have order two, such that

    i O_{2'}(G) does not belong to Z(G/O_{2'}(G))

for every involution i of G. In particular the answer to 4.75 is negative.

The existence input is the precise result proved in
[`10.62-proof.md`](10.62-proof.md), with its
[`internal audit`](10.62-review.md): for every sufficiently large odd
prime p there exists an infinite group G such that

1. G is generated by three involutions;
2. G has exponent 2p and has no subgroup of order four;
3. all involutions of G form one conjugacy class;
4. G is perfect.

The geometric input to that proof is Marco Amelio,
[*Non-split sharply 2-transitive groups of bounded exponent*,
arXiv:2509.11958v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11958v2),
Proposition 6.1, Theorem 6.7 with its inductive construction, and
Remark 6.8. The application starts with C2*C2*C2 acting on its Cayley
tree, uses the permitted family of all loxodromic elements, and selects
the primitive word abc in the first quotient family, imposing
(abc)^p=1. Tameness at every stage and finite multiplication-table
lifting exclude order-four subgroups in the limit. The odd pair-product
orders force all involutions to be conjugate, and the selected odd
relation kills their common image in the abelianization.

These existence arguments are part of the linked proof, not additional
conclusions attributed to the headline statement of Amelio's theorem.
The new packet preserves the earlier packet unchanged and checks its
hashes and retained controls. What follows is an elementary deduction
from the four listed properties.

## Sylow 2-subgroups

Let P be any 2-subgroup of G. Each element has order a power of two and
an order dividing 2p. Hence every element of P has square one. For
x,y in P, the identity (xy)^2=1 gives xy=(xy)^(-1)=yx. Thus P is abelian.
If P contained two distinct nonidentity elements, they would generate
a subgroup C2 x C2 of order four, which is excluded. Consequently
|P|<=2.

There are involutions in G, so the maximal 2-subgroups are exactly the
groups generated by individual involutions. Each is C2 and in
particular is locally cyclic. This argument does not invoke a Sylow
theorem for infinite groups.

## The odd-order radical

For a periodic group, a 2-prime subgroup means one all of whose elements
have odd order; it need not be a finite group of odd cardinality. The
largest normal such subgroup exists. To see this, let A and B be normal
2-prime subgroups. In AB/A, every element has odd order because this
quotient is an image of B. An involution of AB must therefore lie in A,
which has none. Since AB is periodic, absence of involutions implies
that all its elements have odd order. Thus AB is again a normal
2-prime subgroup. Finite products have the same property, and their
directed union is the subgroup generated by all normal 2-prime
subgroups. This is O=O_{2'}(G).

In particular, an involution i never lies in O, and iO is a nonidentity
element of order two in G/O.

## The central-image obstruction

We use a general observation. Suppose a perfect group H is normally
generated by t, and N is a normal subgroup such that tN is central in
H/N. All conjugates of t then have the same image tN, so normal
generation implies H/N=<tN>. This quotient is cyclic. Every quotient
of a perfect group is perfect, whereas a cyclic perfect group is
trivial. Therefore t belongs to N.

Apply this observation to H=G and t=i. The conjugacy class of i is the
entire set of involutions, which contains a generating set of G.
Consequently i normally generates G. Taking N=O, the conclusion
i in O contradicts the preceding paragraph. Hence iO is not central.
The argument applies to every involution, proving the claim.

## Scope

No determination of O_{2'}(G), simplicity of G, or description of
involution centralizers is needed. In particular this does not settle
the locally cyclic centralizer condition of Problem 15.54. The group
is infinite and finitely generated, so it is not locally finite. The
existence proof supplies no explicit numerical value for the
sufficiently large prime p.

Durakov's 2019
[*On some groups of 2-rank 1*](https://journal.imm.uran.ru/node/562)
provides an affirmative result with an extra assumption that the
involution generates a finite subgroup with every element whose order
is not divisible by four. That assumption is not among the four
properties of G above. The source is prior context, not an input to
this proof. This deduction from our 10.62 application is a separate
Notebook candidate, not a separate geometric construction.
