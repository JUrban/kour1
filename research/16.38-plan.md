Aim: if G is soluble and A,B are periodic subgroups, every subgroup
contained in the set product AB is periodic. It suffices to treat
cyclic subgroups <g> contained in AB.

1. Induct on derived length using the last nontrivial derived subgroup
   D, which is abelian. The quotient sends g to a torsion element;
   replace g by a nonzero power lying in D.
2. Factor out the torsion subgroup of D. A and B intersect the resulting
   torsion-free abelian normal group trivially, since they are periodic.
3. Let C be the intersection of the images of A and B in G/D. Restrict
   A and B to its preimages. They give two sections of an extension
   of C by the torsion-free abelian group D.
4. Their difference is a cocycle delta:C->D. All powers of g lying
   in AB force 0,g,2g into its image (up to a harmless sign/action).
5. C is locally finite, since it is a periodic soluble group. Restrict
   to the finite group generated by the two needed cocycle inputs.
   Rational averaging makes the cocycle a coboundary. An invariant
   positive inner product on the finite orbit span excludes three
   distinct collinear points in one orbit.
6. Check the exact product/cocycle signs, quotient kernels, and whether
   additional torsion in D can affect the final conclusion.
7. Search earlier literature, run finite exact affine controls and
   mutation examples, and freeze a complete proof only after review.

Main proposed new lemma: a cocycle of a locally finite group into a
rational module cannot have both v and 2v in its image for v nonzero.
Its orbit proof uses only a finite subgroup and a finite-dimensional
invariant subspace, so infinite-dimensional modules are allowed.
