# Problem21.102: a bound using any nilpotent normal subgroup

12 September2026. Partial result only; priority unconfirmed.
The coordinate partition is adapted from Olshanskii2026,
Proposition1. The additional nilpotent estimate is proved below.

Let G be a finite group and N a nontrivial nilpotent normal
subgroup, of class c and exponent e. Put q=[G:N], and let F_n(G)
be the n-generator free group in var(G). There is a constant A,
depending on G,N but not on n, such that for every n>=1,

    log |F_n(G)| <= A n^c q^n.                         (1)

Consequently limsup_n (log|F_n(G)|)^(1/n) <= q. Taking N=F(G)
gives this bound whenever the Fitting subgroup is nontrivial.
The trivial-N bound with q=|G| is just Birkhoff's elementary
word-function estimate.

## Proof

Realize F_n(G) as the subgroup of G^{G^n} generated by the n
coordinate projection functions. A word is trivial in this
subgroup exactly when it is an identity of G in these variables,
which proves the claimed realization directly.

Partition the coordinate set G^n by its image in (G/N)^n. There
are q^n classes J. Write S_J for the projection of F_n(G) to G^J.
Modulo N^J the n projected generators have constant coordinates,
so the image of S_J in (G/N)^J lies in a diagonal copy of G/N.
In particular that image has order at most q.

From the n given generators choose k whose images generate this
finite image, with k<=floor(log_2 q). This can be done greedily:
each added generator at least doubles the generated subgroup's
order. Let K be the subgroup of S_J generated by these k lifts.
For d=floor(log_2 q) set C=|G|^{|G|^d}. Since K belongs to var(G)
and has at most d generators, the same word-function realization
gives |K|<=C, a constant independent of n,J. This also covers
q=1: the selected subgroup is trivial.

Multiply each unselected generator by a suitable word in the
selected generators so that it lies in N^J. Denote the resulting
at most n elements by y_1,...,y_s. Thus S_J=<K,y_1,...,y_s>.
Let L be generated by all K-conjugates of the y_i and by
K intersection N^J. This generating set is invariant under K;
therefore S_J=LK and L is normal in S_J. Every generator of L
lies in N^J, so L is nilpotent of class at most c and exponent
dividing e. The finite subgroup K intersection N^J has at most
floor(log_2 C) generators, by the same doubling argument. Hence
L has a generating set of size at most

    D(n)=Cn+floor(log_2 C).

A nilpotent group of class at most c and exponent dividing e,
generated by D elements, has order at most

    e^{D+D^2+...+D^c}.                                (2)

Indeed its i-th lower-central factor is abelian, has exponent
dividing e, and is generated by at most D^i iterated commutators
in the original generators. This last assertion follows by
expanding commutators modulo the next lower-central subgroup,
inductively using multiplicativity in each variable there.
The product of these factor-order bounds proves(2).

We have proved, uniformly over all J,

    log |S_J| <= log C + (log e) sum_{i=1}^c D(n)^i
              <= A n^c

after increasing a constant A. Since F_n(G) embeds in the
product of the S_J over all q^n classes, inequality(1) follows.
Taking nth roots proves the stated upper bound. No complement
to N, faithful conjugation action, or solubility of G was assumed.

## Positive case obtained from the bound

If in addition G/N is monolithic with a nonabelian monolith,
then the limit in21.102 exists and is exactly q=[G:N].
For Olshanskii's Theorem1 gives

    log |F_n(G/N)| >= a q^n

for some a>0 and all sufficiently large n. Because var(G/N)
is a subvariety of var(G), F_n(G) maps onto F_n(G/N). Combining
this lower bound with(1) and taking nth roots gives the limit q.
The nonabelian monolithic quotient theorem is explicitly prior.

When N=G is nontrivial nilpotent, the upper bound gives limsup<=1.
The abelianization G/G' is nontrivial, with a quotient C_p for
some prime p. Thus F_n(G) maps onto C_p^n, giving liminf>=1.
This recovers the known nilpotent case.

## Internal checks and limitations

The projection S_J need not project onto G in each coordinate;
only the diagonal quotient bound is used. The subgroup K is
bounded by its number of generators in var(G), not by claiming
that it is a complement. Including K intersection N^J in L is
essential to avoid an omitted kernel. Products N^J preserve the
fixed nilpotency class and exponent; the generating bound for L
therefore applies even when J is large. Finally, the lower bound
in the positive case comes from a quotient free group, not a
claim that the whole variety has a product decomposition.

This proves an upper bound and the specified positive class.
Neither existence nor integrality for arbitrary G follows.
No finite computation is used as evidence for the asymptotic
statement. Further novelty review is needed; the complete
candidate count remains41.
