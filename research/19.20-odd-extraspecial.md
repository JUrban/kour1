# Odd-order extraspecial counts related to Problem 19.20

Date: 2026-09-10. This is a structural extension of the counterexample to
Cameron's stronger inequality, not a complete resolution of Problem 19.20.
The latter asks about equality and remains open in this work.

## Family and the first odd-order reverse inequality found

Let p be an odd prime and E_(p,m) an extraspecial group of order
p^(2m+1) and exponent p. Put G=E_(p,m) x C_p^r, where m>=1 and r>=0.
The formulas proved below give, for (p,m,r)=(3,2,2),

    |G| = 2187,
    |End(G)| = 8,241,952,876,767,369,
    |PIso(G)| = 2,607,970,224,105,603.

Their ratio is 21,273,921 / 6,731,627 > 1. Thus reverse inequalities
also occur at odd order. No minimality among odd-order groups is claimed.
At (3,3,1), of order 6561, the ratio is
144,219,400,425 / 141,132,548,729, again greater than one.

The 60 evaluated parameter triples p=3,5,7; m=1,2,3,4; r=0,1,2,3,4
give no equality. This is a bounded statement, not an exclusion of
equality in the whole family.

## Linear model and notation

Work over F_p. Let V be a symplectic space of dimension 2m, with
alternating nondegenerate form omega, and let R have dimension r.
Use the vector space L=V direct_sum F_p z direct_sum R with bracket

    [(v,a,b),(w,c,d)] = omega(v,w) z.

Its group operation is X*Y=X+Y+(1/2)[X,Y]. Associativity follows
from bilinearity and the centrality of the bracket. The resulting
group is the stated G: its derived subgroup is <z>, its centre is
F_p z direct_sum R, and it has exponent p. A symplectic basis of V
together with a basis of R generates G; there are k=2m+r such
generators. Their powers and commutators give a complete presentation.

Every subgroup is an F_p-subspace closed under this bracket. Indeed,
it is closed under scalars by powering, and X+Y is obtained from X*Y
by multiplying by the appropriate power of the commutator. Conversely,
a bracket-closed subspace is a subgroup under the displayed operation.

Define (empty products are one)

    GL(n) = product_{i=0}^{n-1} (p^n-p^i),
    Sp(m) = p^(m^2) product_{i=1}^m (p^(2i)-1),
    Gauss(n,d) = product_{i=0}^{d-1}(p^n-p^i) / GL(d),
    Iso(m,d) = product_{i=0}^{d-1}(p^(2(m-i))-1)
               / product_{i=0}^{d-1}(p^(d-i)-1).

Here GL(n)=|GL_n(p)|, Sp(m)=|Sp_(2m)(p)|, Gauss counts d-subspaces
of F_p^n, and Iso counts totally isotropic d-subspaces of V. For the
last formula, count ordered isotropic bases successively: after an
i-dimensional isotropic space has been chosen, the next vector has
p^(2m-i)-p^i possibilities. Divide their product by GL(d), and cancel
the powers of p. Put Iso(m,d)=0 outside 0<=d<=m.

## Endomorphisms

Every endomorphism maps z to a z for some a in F_p, because <z> is the
derived subgroup. The two possibilities for a are disjoint.

If a=0, all images of the k named generators commute. Their V-components
are therefore an arbitrary ordered k-tuple whose span is a totally
isotropic subspace. For span dimension t there are Iso(m,t) choices of
the subspace and product_{i=0}^{t-1}(p^k-p^i) spanning tuples in it.
Each of the k images independently has p^(r+1) central components.
These choices preserve all presentation relations, so every choice
gives an endomorphism and all endomorphisms with a=0 arise this way.

If a is nonzero, the first 2m V-components form a symplectic similitude
with multiplier a. Consequently they span V. The r remaining generator
images have zero V-component, since they commute with that spanning
set. There are (p-1)Sp(m) similitudes, allowing all nonzero multipliers,
and again p^((r+1)k) arbitrary central components. This proves

    |End(G)| = p^((r+1)k) [ (p-1)Sp(m)
                  + sum_{t=0}^m Iso(m,t)
                      product_{i=0}^{t-1}(p^k-p^i) ].              (1)

In the a=0 case, the images of the central R generators need only
centralize the image of the endomorphism. Requiring them to lie in
Z(G) would incorrectly undercount; the isotropic tuple formula permits
all of their possible V-components.

## Subgroup multiplicities

Project L to V direct_sum R with kernel <z>. First take a subgroup H
containing z. It is exactly the full inverse image of a subspace U
of V direct_sum R. Put

    a = dim(U intersection R),
    W = projection of U to V,    d = dim W,
    2t = rank(omega restricted to W),    b = d-2t.

The radical of W is a b-dimensional totally isotropic subspace D of V.
Then W/D is a nondegenerate 2t-subspace of D^perp/D, a symplectic space
of dimension 2(m-b). Conversely these data determine W, since W is
the full inverse image of W/D. The number of such W is therefore

    N(d,t) = Iso(m,b) Sp(m-b) / [Sp(t)Sp(m-b-t)],      b=d-2t,     (2)

when 0<=b<=m and 0<=t<=m-b, and zero otherwise. The quotient in (2)
counts nondegenerate subspaces by the transitive symplectic action:
their stabilizer is the product of the symplectic groups on the
subspace and its orthogonal complement. As a check, summing N(d,t)
over t gives Gauss(2m,d).

For each W there are Gauss(r,a) choices of U intersection R. The
remaining data are a linear map W -> R/(U intersection R), so there
are p^(d(r-a)) possibilities. Thus the number for parameters (d,t,a) is

    B(d,t,a) = N(d,t) Gauss(r,a) p^(d(r-a)).                     (3)

If t=0, these groups H are elementary abelian of rank d+a+1.
If t>0, their type is E_(p,t) x C_p^(d+a-2t). This classification follows
by choosing a symplectic basis for a complement to the radical of the
restricted form; the radical supplies the additional central factors.

Now take H not containing z. Its intersection with <z> is zero, so H
is abelian and its image U in V direct_sum R is isotropic (t=0).
For each such U, H is the graph of one of the p^(d+a) linear maps
U -> <z>. Its type is elementary abelian of rank d+a. Thus (3) contributes

- B(d,0,a) groups of elementary abelian rank d+a+1;
- B(d,0,a) p^(d+a) groups of elementary abelian rank d+a;
- B(d,t,a) groups of type E_(p,t) x C_p^(d+a-2t), for t>0.

Sum over 0<=d<=2m, 0<=a<=r, and all allowable t. This counts actual
subgroups, not just conjugacy classes. The cases containing z and
avoiding z are disjoint and exhaustive.

## Automorphism orders and partial isomorphisms

An elementary abelian group of rank h has GL(h) automorphisms.
For t>=1, the automorphism order of E_(p,t) x C_p^s is

    A(t,s) = p^(2t+s+2ts) GL(s) (p-1) Sp(t).                   (4)

An automorphism preserves the derived line and centre. Its induced
map on the noncentral quotient is a symplectic similitude, and on the
centre modulo the derived line is an arbitrary GL_s map. Independently
one can add central components in C_p^s to each of the 2t noncentral
basis vectors, add derived-line components to each of those vectors,
and add derived-line components to each of the s extra central basis
vectors. These give p^(2ts+2t+s) choices. Every choice is invertible and
preserves the bracket, proving (4).

If n_T is the subgroup multiplicity of an isomorphism type T and a_T
its automorphism order, then

    |PIso(G)| = sum_T n_T^2 a_T.                              (5)

For each ordered pair of subgroups of type T, its set of isomorphisms
has a_T elements. Equations (2)--(5) therefore give an exact finite
formula for the partial-isomorphism count.

## Verification and current computational status

`scripts/count_19_20_odd_extraspecial.py` implements the integer formulas;
every division is checked exact. Its 60 evaluated cases are retained in
`results/19.20-odd-extraspecial-formulas.json`.

An independent GAP construction uses upper unitriangular matrices, then
counts conjugacy classes of subgroups with their actual multiplicities,
normal quotients, and GAP automorphism groups. For the five cases
(p,m,r)=(3,1,0),(3,1,1),(3,1,2),(3,2,0),(5,1,0), both final counts and
all 29 subgroup-type multiplicities and automorphism orders agree.
The comparison is in `results/19.20-odd-extraspecial-validation.json`.

A full GAP check of the 2187-element example completed successfully
as `scripts/verify_19_20_odd_2187.g`. It avoids the SmallGrp identification
limit by classifying exponent-three groups with derived order at most
three using their orders and centre orders, as justified above. It
computes automorphism groups independently of formula (4). All 66,734 conjugacy classes (134,414 actual subgroups) were counted.
The two totals and all 13 subgroup multiplicities and automorphism orders
agree exactly with the formulas. The log contains PASS and DONE and no
errors; runtime was 506,246 ms. The independently computed normal-quotient
multiplicities also reconstruct the same endomorphism count. See
`results/19.20-odd-2187-validation.json`.

This note extends our already certified binary example related to
[Cameron's stronger question](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00233-025-10514-5).
It makes no separate claim of a new complete Notebook resolution.
