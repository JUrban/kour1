## Mathematical result

For every odd prime p and d>=1, the proof constructs a supersoluble
metabelian matrix group of order 2p^(2d+1). It has trivial center and
hypercenter, but exactly d+1 system normalizers are needed to give a
trivial intersection. This refutes the uniform bound in Problem 17.39.
The same family has Frattini subgroup of order p, so fixing p=3 even
keeps the Frattini subgroup's order fixed while the minimum grows.

The core argument identifies the p-parts of the system normalizers
with graphs of all linear functionals from F_p^d to F_p. Intersecting
k such graphs imposes at most k-1 independent equations. Standard
basis functionals attain the lower bound. The proof also checks the
elements outside the normal Sylow p-subgroup; eliminating only the
p-part would not by itself suffice for the upper bound.

The 2018 article cited by the Notebook and the relevant 2019 thesis
chapter retain a trivial-Frattini assumption. Their hypotheses were
checked directly. The current family lies outside that positive case.
The source audit records the limited novelty search without claiming
priority or treating the abstract of the prior article as a full answer.

GAP constructs native matrix groups and verifies their orders,
solubility, normal Sylow subgroup, centralizer/normalizer equality,
trivial center and core, and Frattini order. It enumerates all conjugates
of the system normalizer. With the first conjugate fixed to D, it
deduplicates every possible intersection at each depth until the
trivial subgroup appears. This exhaustive state search proves the
finite minima, rather than merely finding witnesses of those lengths.

Python imports no GAP tables, matrices, or normalizer data. It builds
integer normal forms and enumerates all elements whose square is the
identity. Every nonidentity such element generates a Sylow 2-subgroup,
whose normalizer is its centralizer. Literal commutation with the fixed
D then supplies all pair intersections for a separate state search.
The two implementations agree on every depth's distribution of
intersection sizes, as well as the explicit standard-basis chain.

The independent checks examine all 51,466 group elements, both inverse
identities, the center, and 185,048 conjugation identities on the basis
of U. They also verify all 157,464 associativity triples of the order-54
model. The infinite statement rests on the written proof, not on an
extrapolation from these finite cases.
