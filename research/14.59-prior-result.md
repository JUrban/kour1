# Problem 14.59: a negative consequence of Tent's construction

Status: complete deduction from a published theorem, excluded from new
resolution priority. Recorded 11 September 2026. The supplied question
was visually checked on printed page 78. Stabilizers of two or three
distinct points are pointwise stabilizers.

The answer is negative, even for a countable group acting on a countable
set and with torsion-free two-point stabilizers.

## Imported construction

Katrin Tent, *Sharply 3-transitive groups*, Advances in Mathematics 286
(2016), 722–728,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2015.09.018),
[author-hosted publisher PDF](https://ivv5hpp.uni-muenster.de/u/tent/sharp3_advances.pdf),
Theorem 2.1 and Remark 2.2 give a sharply 3-transitive action for

    G = ((C3 × F(U)) *_{C3} S3 *_{C2} (C2 × F(S))) * F(R),

where R, S, U are countably infinite sets. Start with the natural
three-point action of S3, with a a 3-cycle and t a transposition.
The constructed action preserves the facts that a has no fixed point
and t has exactly one fixed point. The construction uses countably
many extensions of countable sets, so its final set X is countable.

## Checking the precise hypotheses and excluding PGL2

First, every finite subgroup of G is conjugate into a vertex group
C3 × F(U), S3, or C2 × F(S). This is the elementary finite-subgroup
property of amalgamated products and free products: the associated
tree action has no inversions; the convex hull of a finite orbit is a
finite invariant tree; its centre supplies a fixed vertex. Equivalently
one can apply this argument successively at the two amalgams and the
last free product. A finite subgroup of Cp × F projects trivially to
the torsion-free free group, and is therefore contained in Cp.

Consequently every involution of G is conjugate to t, and every element
of order three is conjugate to a. There are no other nontrivial
finite-order elements. The number of fixed points is invariant under
conjugation. Thus involutions each fix exactly one point, and elements
of order three fix none. Every pointwise two-point stabilizer is
torsion-free. Sharp 3-transitivity gives both ordinary triple
transitivity and trivial pointwise three-point stabilizers, exactly as
required in 14.59.

The same finite-subgroup argument proves that G has no subgroup C2 × C2:
none of its vertex groups contains one. On the other hand, for a field
K of characteristic two with more than two elements, choose
u in K outside F2. The translations by 1 and u in PGL2(K) generate

    { z ↦ z+b : b in {0, 1, u, 1+u} } ≅ C2 × C2.

They are four distinct projective transformations, so G cannot even
be abstractly isomorphic to PGL2(K). The remaining field K=F2 gives
the finite group PGL2(F2) ≅ S3. It is also excluded, since the free
factor F(R) embeds in G and is infinite. Hence no proposed permutation
similarity can exist.

The action is faithful because it is 3-sharp on a set with at least
three points. Therefore X is infinite, as G is infinite. Its two-point
stabilizers act regularly on X minus those two points and are infinite.
In particular they are not periodic, consistently with the positive
periodic-stabilizer case printed in the Notebook.
