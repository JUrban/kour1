# 19.108: a positive subclass at order p^10

Status: complete partial argument, with exact independent controls.
This does not resolve 19.108 and is not counted as a complete candidate
resolution. Novelty has not been established.

## Statement

Let p be odd. If P has order p^10 and has an abelian normal subgroup
of order at least p^6, then, for every irreducible complex character chi
of P and every x with chi(x) != 0,

    |x| divides |P|/chi(1)^2.

The argument includes nonsplit extensions. Its new working ingredient
is the following elementary orbit lemma. The completed computation at
p=3 led to this lemma, but the proof applies to every odd prime.

## Orbit lemma

Put R=Z/p^3Z. Let H be any p-subgroup of GL(2,R). If an H-orbit O
on the row vectors R^2 has size p^4, then O is invariant under addition
of p^2 R^2.

Proof. Let O_i be the image of O modulo p^i for i=1,2. An equivariant
map between transitive finite H-sets has equally sized fibers. All
fibers from R^2 to (Z/p^2Z)^2 have size p^2.

After a change of coordinates, the image of H modulo p lies in
U={[[1,t],[0,1]]:t in F_p}. Thus |O_1| is either 1 or p.
If |O_1|=1, then |O_2|<=p^2. If |O_1|=p, then |O_2|<=p^3.

Suppose |O_2|=p^3. Choose v in O. Its first coordinate modulo p is
nonzero: otherwise U fixes v modulo p. Therefore the stabilizer in H
of v modulo p is exactly H_0, the kernel of reduction H -> GL(2,F_p).
The orbit of v modulo p^2 under H_0 has size p^2, because it is the
fiber over v modulo p in O_2.

Every h in H_0 has the form I+pM. Hence this orbit is obtained by
adding p(vM) modulo p^2. Its size p^2 supplies h_1=I+pM_1 and
h_2=I+pM_2 for which vM_1 and vM_2 are linearly independent modulo p.
For odd p the binomial identity gives

    (I+pM_i)^p = I+p^2 M_i  (mod p^3).

Indeed, every binomial term of degree at least two is divisible by
p^3; this also holds when p=3. Products of these two p-th powers
therefore move v through all p^2 vectors v+p^2 w, w in F_p^2, while
fixing it modulo p^2. Consequently the fibers O -> O_2 have size p^2,
and |O|=p^3 p^2=p^5, a contradiction.

We have proved |O_2|<=p^2 in every case. Since |O|=p^4 and a fiber
has size at most p^2, necessarily |O_2|=p^2 and every fiber is full.
This is exactly the claimed invariance. QED.

## Character argument

We use two prior results of Tom Wilde,
[Supports of irreducible characters of p-groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.5780):

- Theorem C: the required divisibility holds if |P|<=p^9, or if
  chi(1)<=p^3.
- Lemma 2.7: if chi is faithful and the divisibility fails at x,
  then <x> and some conjugate <x>^g intersect trivially.

Let A be the stated abelian normal subgroup. Every irreducible chi
is a constituent of an induced linear character lambda^P, with
lambda in Irr(A), by Frobenius reciprocity. Thus chi(1)<=|P:A|.
If |A|>p^6, this is at most p^3, and Wilde's result applies.

It remains to consider |A|=p^6. Assume there is a violating chi,x.
The same degree bound and Wilde's theorem force chi(1)=p^4.
The character lambda^P has this degree and contains chi, so it equals
chi. In particular chi vanishes outside A, and the conjugation orbit
of lambda has size p^4. For the latter assertion, the standard
restriction formula gives

    <lambda^P,lambda^P>_P
      = <lambda,Res_A(lambda^P)>_A = |I_P(lambda):A|;

irreducibility makes this number one.

The character chi must be faithful. Otherwise, with K=ker(chi)>1,
Wilde's order bound in P/K (of order at most p^9) gives

    |xK| divides (|P|/|K|)/chi(1)^2.

Since |<x> intersect K| divides |K|, multiplying yields the desired
divisibility for |x| in P as well, a contradiction.

Now x belongs to A and has order at least p^3, since |P|/chi(1)^2=p^2.
By Wilde's intersection lemma there is g with <x> intersect <x>^g=1.
Both cyclic groups lie in the abelian normal subgroup A. Their direct
product has order at least p^6, so the order of A forces

    A = <x> x <x>^g  isomorphic to C_(p^3) x C_(p^3).

The action of P/A on the dual group of A is faithful: an element in
its kernel fixes lambda and hence belongs to I_P(lambda)/A=1.
Apply the orbit lemma to its orbit O of lambda, of size p^4.

Choose dual coordinates with lambda_y(a)=zeta^(y dot a), where zeta
is a primitive p^3-th root. Then

    chi(a) = sum_(y in O) zeta^(y dot a).

If a has order p^3, one coordinate a_i is not divisible by p. The
orbit lemma makes translation by p^2 e_i a permutation of O, so

    chi(a) = zeta^(p^2 a_i) chi(a).

The multiplier differs from one; therefore chi(a)=0. In particular
chi(x)=0, the final contradiction. QED.

## Independent controls and their limits

The exact p=3 search covered all 571 conjugacy classes of order-81
subgroups in the Sylow subgroup of GL(2,Z/27Z), and all 1,596 regular
dual orbits. Every orbit was invariant under 9A.

The Python replay independently enumerated all 19,683 Sylow matrices,
reconstructed 571 distinct order-81 matrix subgroups from the saved
generators, and recovered every one of the 1,596 regular orbits. It
also evaluated 174,312 exact cyclotomic sums: every order-27 vector
on one regular orbit from each of the 269 subgroup classes that has
such an orbit. These sums all vanish. Conjugacy-class exhaustiveness
still relies on GAP's documented subgroup enumerator.

Separate GAP computations construct nine actual affine permutation
groups, verify that the selected induced characters are irreducible,
and compare 197 exact character values with their orbit sums. Eight
known violating elements in two groups of order 16 provide positive
controls; the odd-prime controls satisfy the divisibility.

The general theorem above rests on the orbit proof and the stated
prior character theorems, not on extrapolating the p=3 calculation.
Groups of order p^10 whose abelian normal subgroups all have order
at most p^5, and groups of greater order in general, remain uncovered.
