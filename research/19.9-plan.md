# 19.9: tensor squares and central extensions

2026-09-11. The printed statement on PDF page133 was visually checked.
Part(a) asks whether the non-abelian tensor square of every finitely
generated linear group is linear; part(b) asks this for every B_n, n>3.

Two routes now have complete mathematical outlines:

1. For G=Sp(10,Z), identify G tensor G with its universal central
   extension. The latter is the pullback of the topological universal
   cover of Sp(10,R), and Deligne's theorem makes it non-residually
   finite. Prove finite generation before using Malcev's theorem.
2. For braids, split off the diagonal infinite cyclic subgroup of the
   tensor square. Identify the exterior square with the derived
   subgroup of the Schur cover. The cover is pulled back from a finite
   double cover of S_n, hence is linear. A Clifford algebra construction
   can check the crucial nontrivial commuting-lift obstruction directly.

The main failure modes to audit are: confusing the topological cover
with the universal abstract central extension in small symplectic rank;
inferring non-residual finiteness from an arbitrary quotient; assuming
arbitrary finite central extensions of linear groups are linear; and
silently splitting the diagonal subgroup without checking its generator.

The original Bardakov--Lavrenov--Neshchadim2019 paper explicitly poses
both questions. Its preliminaries and relevant question statements have
been read. Deligne1978's original first-page theorem and the precise
universal-cover identification in Benson--Campagnolo--Ranicki--Rovi2018
were checked. Akita--Kawasaki--Satoh2024, Sections1--2, gives the braid
Schur-cover pullback. No explicit earlier tensor-square resolution was
found in the limited search. Nevertheless both routes are short
consequences of existing theorems, so keep them separate from claims
of new mathematical priority, consistently with the earlier rediscoveries.
