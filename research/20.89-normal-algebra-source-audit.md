# Source and scope audit: the unit-parameter argument

September 12, 2026. This is partial coverage of the unstarred
19.83 / 20.89 question. The exact source statements remain in
`results/20.89-statements.json`, previously checked against the two
Notebook page images. The arbitrary linear-subgroup question remains
unresolved in positive characteristic. Count stays 46.

The two-parameter identity and the determinant argument are written
out completely. They require no classification of finite simple groups,
Tits alternative, algebraic-group quotient theorem, or characteristic-zero
lifting result. The normality hypothesis supplies the required action
on the original ambient group, and therefore on its minimal sink.

The only imported group-theoretic inputs are:

* Minimal finite sinks consist of the periodic points of the commutator
  map and are permuted by that map. This is stated in the introduction
  of B.A.F. Wehrfritz, *Weak Engel Conditions on Linear Groups*,
  [AGTA 7 (2019), 143--157](https://www.advgrouptheory.com/journal/Volumes/7/Wehrfritz%20-%20Weak%20Engel%20conditions%20on%20linear%20group.pdf).
  The introduction was reread. The same characterization follows
  directly by decomposing eventual orbits in a finite set into cycles.
* Gruenberg's theorem identifies the Engel elements of a linear group
  with its Hirsch--Plotkin radical. The exact form used is recorded
  in the preliminaries, page 4, of P. Shumyatsky,
  [*Almost Engel linear groups*, arXiv:1610.03126v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03126v1).
  Those preliminaries were reread. The original 1966 theorem is an
  imported classical result, not re-proved by these calculations.

Both primary PDF/text pairs are retained from the earlier audits and
are bound in the packet. Native finite-group checks additionally use
the familiar identification of finite Engel elements with the Fitting
subgroup as a consistency control. The independent verifier computes
the sinks directly and does not use GAP's Fitting subgroup.

Searches for "almost Engel" with "unit group", "infinite field",
"algebra", "normal subgroup", and "GL" did not identify an explicit
prior statement of the present theorem or finite-field inequality.
This is not evidence sufficient for a novelty claim. Results on
almost Engel *varieties of algebras* use a different meaning of
"almost" and were not treated as answers to the group-sink question.

The complete finite sink lists, rather than only their sizes, are
compared in the packet audit. The examples also expose two limits:
small finite fields can have nonscalar Engel matrices, and an arbitrary
finite matrix subgroup need not be normalized by the units 1+t*a.
The explicit S3 control disproves the omitted-normality version.

No outside reviewer has assessed this deduction. No author was
contacted, and no submission or push was made.
