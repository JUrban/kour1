# 18.92(a),(b): complete lattices of formations from prime sets

Status: complete internally checked candidate affirmative answers to
both parts. Novelty and outside review remain pending.

The exact definition in the supplied PDF, printed page 128, requires
a nonempty family of formations closed under intersections and having
a largest member. Joins are least upper bounds inside that family.
They need not be the joins in the lattice of all formations.

## 1. A supply of formations

Let P be the set of all primes. For S contained in P, define

    F_S = {finite groups G : every prime dividing |G| belongs to S}.

Each F_S is a formation: it contains the trivial group, is closed
under isomorphisms and quotients, and is closed under finite subdirect
products. Indeed, any such product is a subgroup of the product of
its factors, so its order has no new prime divisor. In fact these
classes are closed under all subgroups and finite direct products.

We have

    F_empty = {trivial groups},     F_P = {all finite groups},
    F_S contained in F_T  if and only if  S contained in T,
    intersection_i F_(S_i) = F_(intersection_i S_i).

The reverse implication in the inclusion assertion is witnessed by
the cyclic group C_p for each p in S minus T. The intersection formula
also holds for the empty family with ambient top F_P.

Consequently every intersection-closed family C of subsets of P
containing P yields a complete lattice of formations

    Theta_C = {F_S : S in C},

isomorphic, as an ordered set and hence as a complete lattice, to C.
For a family of members of C its join is the intersection of all
members of C containing their union. This is a statement about the
chosen family, not a claim about ambient joins of formations.

## 2. Part (a): a non-algebraic complete lattice

Take

    C_a = {finite subsets of P} union {P},
    Theta_a = {F_S : S finite} union {F_P}.

This family is closed under arbitrary intersections: if an intersection
has a finite member, it is finite; if all members are P, or there are
no members, it is P. Thus Theta_a is a complete lattice of formations.
For S_i in C_a, its joins are

    join_i F_(S_i) = F_(union_i S_i)  if that union is finite;
                    F_P             otherwise.                (1)

Every non-bottom element fails to be compact. Let F_T be such an
element and choose q in T. Enumerate P minus {q} as p1,p2,..., and put

    S_n={p1,...,pn},       X_n=F_(S_n),       n>=1.

The X_n form an increasing chain, and their join in Theta_a is F_P
by (1). In particular F_T is below that join. But the join of any
finite nonempty subfamily is X_m for its largest index m, and C_q
does not belong to X_m although it belongs to F_T. The empty subfamily
has join F_empty and also cannot dominate F_T. Therefore F_T is not
compact.

The bottom F_empty is compact, since it is below the empty join.
It is the only compact element. A join of compact elements can thus
only be F_empty; for example F_{2} cannot be such a join. Hence
Theta_a is not algebraic, answering (a) affirmatively.

The infinite join used here really depends on the chosen family.
F_(P minus {q}) is not in Theta_a. It would contain the entire chain
and omit C_q if it were allowed, but the only upper bound in Theta_a
is F_P. This explains precisely why the compactness witness works.

## 3. Part (b): a non-modular five-element lattice

Take the five formations

    Theta_b = {O, A, B, C, U},
    O=F_empty,  A=F_{2},  B=F_{2,3},  C=F_{5},  U=F_P.

They have exactly these strict comparisons:

    O < A < B < U,       O < C < U.

C is incomparable with A and B. Their pairwise intersections are in
the family: A intersect B=A, A intersect C=B intersect C=O, with
the evident intersections involving O,U. Finite pairwise closure
and the presence of U give closure under all intersections, including
the empty one. Thus Theta_b is a complete lattice of formations.

The unique upper bound of both A and C in Theta_b is U, so their
join is U. Since A is below B, the modular identity would imply

    A join (C meet B) = (A join C) meet B.

Its left side is A join O=A; its right side is U meet B=B. They are
distinct because C_3 belongs to B and not to A. Thus Theta_b is not
modular. It is the usual pentagon lattice N5, answering (b)
affirmatively.

## 4. Convention check

Both examples have the formation of all finite groups as their top,
so even interpreting the empty intersection in that ambient universe
causes no difficulty. No formation in either family is empty. The
definition does not require the family to contain every subformation
of its members, or to preserve the joins from the ambient formation
lattice.

The use of internal joins is also the explicit convention of
Tsarev--Kukharev, *On the lattice of all tau-closed totally composition
formations of finite groups*, Section 2.2, in the institutional
preprint at
https://elib.sfu-kras.ru/bitstream/handle/2311/142250/preprint-3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1 .
That section defines Theta-form X as intersection of the members of
Theta containing X and uses it to define the join in Theta. Its
Definition 1.1 states the usual quotient and subdirect-product
definition of a formation. No result about composition formations is
needed here.

These proofs concern the definition actually printed in 18.92. They
assert neither nonmodularity nor nonalgebraicity of the full lattice
of all formations, nor of any of its separately specified natural
families. See `18.92-review.md` for the literature and review scope.
