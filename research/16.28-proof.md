# Candidate negative answers to both parts of Problem 16.28

Date: 2026-09-10 UTC. Status: complete elementary candidate proof for
(a); the negative answer to (b) also follows from prior class-product
results. Independent review and further literature/formulation checks
remain. Only (a) is treated as a candidate for new coverage.

## Statement and field convention

Problem 16.28 (E. P. Vdovin), on printed and physical PDF page 99,
asks the following for a connected reductive linear algebraic group G
over a field of positive characteristic and a closed subset X of G:

- (a) Must X^c be closed for some integer c>1?
- (b) If X is a conjugacy class and X^2 contains an open subset of G,
  must X^2=G?

Here X^c is the set of products of exactly c elements of X. The
printed page was checked visually. Part (a) does not require X to
be irreducible or to contain the identity, and the field is not
restricted to an algebraic extension of a finite field.

Both answers are negative as stated. The example for (b) works
over every algebraically closed field of characteristic five,
including the algebraic closure of F_5. The example for (a) uses
K=an algebraic closure of F_5(t), with t transcendental, so that
K contains an element of infinite multiplicative order. This
field distinction is material.

All closed and open sets below refer to the Zariski topology on
the algebraic group over the stated algebraically closed field.

## A closed conjugacy class with nonclosed square

Let K be algebraically closed of characteristic five, let
H=SL_2(K), and put

    Y = {A in H : tr(A)=1}.

This is a closed subset of H. Every member has characteristic
polynomial x^2-x+1, whose discriminant -3=2 is nonzero in K.
The two roots are distinct, so every member is diagonalizable
with the same pair of eigenvalues. Thus Y is one GL_2(K)
conjugacy class. It is also one SL_2(K) conjugacy class:
given a conjugating matrix P, multiply P by a scalar c with
c^2 det(P)=1, which exists because K is algebraically closed.
This changes neither its conjugation action nor the two matrices
it conjugates, and makes its determinant one.

The central element -I does not belong to Y^2. Indeed, AB=-I
with A in Y would force B=-A^{-1}. For a two by two matrix of
determinant one, tr(A^{-1})=tr(A), so tr(B)=-1, which is not
1 in characteristic five.

On the other hand, Y^2 contains the nonempty open subset

    O = {C in H : tr(C)^2 != 4}.                            (1)

To prove this explicitly, first take s in K^* with s!=-1 and set

    u = s/(1+s),        w = u(1-u)-1,

    A_s = [ u  1 ],     D_s = diag(s,s^{-1}),
          [ w 1-u]

    B_s = A_s^{-1} D_s.

The definition of w gives det(A_s)=1, and tr(A_s)=1. Also
det(B_s)=1 and

    tr(B_s) = (1-u)s + u s^{-1}
            = s/(1+s) + 1/(1+s) = 1.

Consequently A_s,B_s belong to Y and D_s belongs to Y^2.
Every C in O has distinct eigenvalues s,s^{-1}, with s!=1,-1,
and is conjugate in SL_2(K) to D_s by the same scalar-adjustment
argument. Since Y^2 is conjugacy-invariant, (1) follows. The set
O is nonempty, for example diag(2,3) belongs to it in
characteristic five. It is open because it is the nonvanishing
locus of the regular function tr(C)^2-4.

This already disproves (b): Y is a closed conjugacy class in
the connected reductive group SL_2(K), its square contains O,
and its square omits -I.

We will also need that Y^2 is not closed. One may use that O
is dense in the irreducible variety SL_2. Alternatively, there
is a direct curve argument: s -> D_s is a morphism K^* -> H.
For every s!=-1 its value lies in Y^2, but its value at -1
is -I. Since K^* minus {-1} is dense in K^*, -I lies in the
closure of Y^2. It is not in Y^2, proving nonclosedness.

## A closed set for which no power greater than one is closed

Now take K to be an algebraic closure of F_5(t), and use the
same H and Y over K. Let

    G = H times G_m(K),
    z = (I,t),
    X = (Y times {1}) union {z}.

The group G is a connected reductive linear algebraic group:
it is the product of SL_2 and the one-dimensional torus, with
the faithful block-diagonal embedding into GL_3. The subset X
is closed, as a union of two closed subsets. It is in fact a
union of two closed conjugacy classes of G.

For any n>=1, centrality of z gives the exact set identity

    X^n = union_{k=0}^n (Y^k times {t^{n-k}}),               (2)

where Y^0={I}. Each word of length n has k factors from
Y times {1} and n-k factors equal to z. Moving the central
z factors to the end proves one inclusion in (2), and
concatenating the prescribed factors proves the other.

The powers t^j are all distinct: t is transcendental over F_5,
so it satisfies no equation t^m=1 with m>0. For every n>=2,
intersecting (2) with the closed fiber

    F_n = H times {t^{n-2}}

therefore gives precisely

    X^n intersect F_n = Y^2 times {t^{n-2}}.                (3)

The fiber F_n is isomorphic as a variety to H. The right-hand
side of (3) is not closed in this fiber, by the preceding
argument for Y^2. If X^n were closed in G, its intersection
with the closed fiber would be closed in the fiber, a
contradiction. Thus X^n is not closed for **every** n>=2.
This disproves (a), completing both negative answers.

An explicit missing limit point is (-I,t^{n-2}). It lies in
the closure of X^n by the curve s -> (D_s,t^{n-2}), but not
in X^n because (3) would require -I in Y^2.

If desired, the same X can be placed in the simple algebraic
group SL_4(K), using the closed embedding

    (A,v) |-> diag(A,v,v^{-1}).

Closedness and nonclosedness of the relevant subsets are
unchanged inside this closed subgroup. Thus a nontrivial
central torus in the ambient reductive group is not essential.

## Scope and verification

The argument uses neither a characteristic-zero field nor a
nonreductive ambient group. It does use a transcendental
element in (a), and it does use a reducible closed subset X.
It makes no claim about versions of (a) restricted to fields
algebraic over finite fields, irreducible X, or X containing
the identity. None of these restrictions occurs on the
supplied page. Further historical formulation checking remains
part of the novelty audit.

The rational-function matrix identities and characteristic-five
boundary cases are checked independently in GAP and Python:
`scripts/verify_16_28.g` and `scripts/verify_16_28.py`.
These controls verify the calculations. The Zariski-topological
claims and the quantifier over every power n are proved above,
not inferred from any finite-field search.

For the literature distinction, see `research/16.28-review.md`:
Lemma 2.3(b) of Guralnick--Malle--Tiep (2013) gives a prior
route to the open-subset assertion for (b). The proposed step
for (a) is the infinite-order scalar coordinate preserving a
nonclosed square in a separate fiber of every higher power.
