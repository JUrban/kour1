# Problem 7.27: an affirmative maximal-rank consequence

2026-09-11. Complete deduction from the published Fulman--Guralnick
bounded-rank theorem. Excluded from the possible-new-candidate count;
outside review remains pending.

For each fixed integer n>=2 there is Q(n) such that, for every prime
power q>Q(n), SL_n(q) has a diagonal element contained in no proper
irreducible subgroup other than block-monomial subgroups. In fact the
proportion of satisfactory elements in the determinant-one diagonal
torus tends to one as q tends to infinity with n fixed.

Here block-monomial means that the group permutes the summands in a
nontrivial direct-sum decomposition into equal-dimensional subspaces.
The proof gives blocks spanned by subsets of the original coordinate
basis, so it also addresses the stronger coordinate-block reading.
No explicit Q(n), uniformity in n, or answer for other Chevalley types
is asserted. The case n=1 is vacuous.

## 1. Exact imported theorem

Let X=SL_n over an algebraic closure of F_p, let F be the q-power
Frobenius, and put G=X^F. Fulman--Guralnick's Theorem 3.2, with the
four subgroup families immediately preceding it, gives the following.
For fixed n, the union B(q) of the maximal subgroups outside family 4
has |B(q)|/|G| tending to zero as q tends to infinity. Every maximal
subgroup in family 4 has the form Y^F for a proper F-stable closed
algebraic subgroup Y of X with rank(Y^0)=n-1.

The source explicitly permits simply connected X, so this applies
directly to SL_n(q), including its centre. For sufficiently large q
this group is perfect and its central quotient is simple. In
particular a proper subgroup cannot project onto that quotient:
if H Z(G)=G, then G=[G,G]=[H,H]<=H. There is no missing maximal
subgroup caused by passing between linear and projective forms.

Source: J. Fulman and R. Guralnick, *Derangements in simple and
primitive groups*, in *Groups, Combinatorics and Geometry: Durham
2001* (2003), pp.99--121. We use the author's manuscript, Section 3,
pp.9--12, including its complete proof of Theorem 3.2:
https://dornsife.usc.edu/jason-fulman/wp-content/uploads/sites/224/2023/06/durham.pdf
The initial arXiv version is https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0208022.

The classification and the subsidiary cited results inside that
theorem are imported, not claimed to have been reproved here. The
later 2012 Theorem 7.3 about finite maximal tori is useful supporting
context, but the argument below uses the more precise algebraic
family-4 formulation above.

## 2. A positive-density set of split elements

Write T(q) for the determinant-one diagonal matrices and set b=n!.
Let A(q) consist of t=diag(lambda_1,...,lambda_n) in T(q) such that
the n numbers lambda_i^b are pairwise distinct. Thus t and t^b are
regular semisimple, and both have the full algebraic diagonal torus
as their centralizer in X.

Put m=q-1. There are m^(n-1) choices of t. Express lambda_n as the
inverse of the product of the first n-1 entries. For i<j<n, the
condition lambda_i^b=lambda_j^b permits at most b choices of lambda_i
after the other free variables have been fixed. For i<n and j=n,
it permits at most 2b choices, because the resulting equation has
degree 2b in lambda_i. This also covers n=2. Hence

    |T(q) \ A(q)| <= 2b binomial(n,2) m^(n-2).

This bound is valid in every characteristic, including p dividing
b; a polynomial has no more distinct roots than its degree. Thus
|A(q)|/|T(q)| tends to one.

Let U(q) be the union of the G-conjugates of A(q). Each element of
U(q) lies in exactly one split maximal torus, its centralizer. Also
|N_G(T(q)):T(q)|=n! once q is sufficiently large. All permutations of
the eigenlines are realized in SL_n(q), since a diagonal factor can
adjust a permutation matrix's determinant. Consequently

    |U(q)|/|G| = |A(q)| / (n! |T(q)|) -> 1/n!.

Since B(q) is conjugation invariant and has density tending to zero,
U(q) is not contained in B(q) for sufficiently large q. Conjugating
an element of U(q)\B(q), choose d in A(q)\B(q).

More precisely, the same torus-counting identity applied to
A(q) intersect B(q) gives

    |A(q) intersect B(q)|/|T(q)| <= n! |B(q)|/|G| -> 0.

Thus almost every element of T(q) lies in A(q)\B(q).

## 3. The component group is killed by n!

Let Y<=X be a closed subgroup of maximal rank, and put L=Y^0.
Choose a maximal torus S of L; it is also maximal in X. Conjugacy
of maximal tori in the connected group L gives Y=N_Y(S)L. Therefore

    Y/L is isomorphic to N_Y(S)/N_L(S).

Both normalizers contain S, and N_Y(S)/S is a subgroup of the Weyl
group N_X(S)/S, which is S_n. The order of Y/L consequently divides
n!. Every y in Y satisfies y^(n!) in L. This argument does not assume
that the characteristic is prime to n!.

For the selected d in Y^F, d^b belongs to L. If L is reductive, its
semisimple element d^b belongs to a maximal torus S_1 of L. That
torus has dimension n-1 and centralizes d^b. Since d^b has distinct
eigenvalues, C_X(d^b) is precisely the diagonal torus T. It follows
that S_1=T and T<=L. This recovers the entire algebraic split torus,
not merely the finite cyclic subgroup generated by d.

## 4. Irreducibility removes the unipotent radical

Suppose H<=Y^F acts irreducibly on F_q^n. The unipotent radical
R_u(L) is characteristic in L, hence normalized by Y and F-stable.
If it is nontrivial, its common fixed subspace in the natural
algebraic module is nonzero, by triangularization of connected
unipotent groups. It is proper because the given matrix action is
faithful. This subspace is Y-invariant and F-stable, and therefore
descends to a nonzero proper F_q-subspace invariant under H. That
contradicts irreducibility. Thus L is reductive.

This argument handles non-reductive family-4 groups directly; no
unproved assertion about a finite Frattini subgroup or an arbitrary
maximal torus is being used.

## 5. A reductive full-rank subgroup of SL_n is block diagonal

We recall the elementary type-A form of the root-subgroup description
of a connected reductive group containing the full diagonal torus T.
Such a group L is generated by T and its root subgroups. Its roots
relative to T are among e_i-e_j, the roots of SL_n. The corresponding
root subgroup is U_ij={I+aE_ij:a in the algebraic closure}.

For clarity this is a statement about algebraic root subgroups, not
just Lie algebras in positive characteristic. A T-root subgroup in
L embeds as the ambient U_ij: its nonzero differential has weight
e_i-e_j; equivariance allows matrix entries only of positive integral
multiples of this weight, and the ambient type-A root system has no
other such weights. The nonzero linear entry gives the full U_ij.

Reductivity makes the root set symmetric: U_ij occurs if and only if
U_ji occurs. For distinct i,j,k, the matrix identity

    [I+aE_ij, I+cE_jk] = I+acE_ik

(with the commutator convention xyx^-1y^-1) shows that occurrence is
transitive. Thus the pairs i,j for which U_ij occurs partition
{1,...,n} into equivalence classes I_1,...,I_s. Root generation gives

    L = ( GL(V_1) x ... x GL(V_s) ) intersect SL_n,
    V_a = span{e_i : i in I_a}.

If L is proper, s>=2. The V_a are its pairwise nonisomorphic simple
natural submodules and are the uniquely determined simple summands.
This follows directly from the displayed full block groups and the
distinct diagonal-torus weights, including singleton blocks.
Every element normalizing L must therefore permute these subspaces.

The blocks are coordinate subspaces, so each is defined over F_q.
If an irreducible finite subgroup of N_X(L)^F had more than one orbit
on them, the sum over one orbit would be a proper invariant
F_q-subspace. It is therefore transitive on the blocks; they have
equal dimensions and give a block-monomial representation.

## 6. All proper irreducible overgroups are covered

Let H be any proper irreducible subgroup of G containing d. Choose
a maximal subgroup M of G containing H. It is also irreducible. As
d is outside B(q), M belongs to family 4, so M=Y^F for a proper
F-stable maximal-rank algebraic subgroup Y.

Section 4 makes L=Y^0 reductive. Sections 3 and 5 show that L contains
the algebraic diagonal torus and is a proper block-diagonal group,
and that H permutes its coordinate blocks transitively. Thus H is
block-monomial, as required. The blocks may depend on H; the selected
diagonal element d is the same for every proper irreducible H.

## 7. Internal audit and source boundaries

- The exact printed page23 was rendered and visually checked.
- n is fixed; q ranges over all sufficiently large prime powers.
- The positive-density set and the exceptional union are both counted
  in SL_n(q), using the simply connected version of the imported theorem.
- Density zero for each individual subgroup would be insufficient;
  Theorem3.2 controls the entire union, including every conjugate.
- Mere regularity of d would not place it in Y^0. Regularity of d^n!
  and the component-group calculation are both necessary steps.
- The argument allows small fixed characteristic and p dividing n!.
- A semisimple element lies in a torus of L only after reductivity has
  been established here; the application uses Section4 first.
- Descent of the unipotent fixed space and of the final blocks is
  explicit; irreducibility is over F_q, not absolute irreducibility.
- The type-A root argument uses actual root groups, avoiding the
  invalid inference that equal Lie algebras alone imply equal groups.
- Properness of Y ensures at least two final blocks. An arbitrary
  subgroup H of the maximal subgroup is handled by its own transitivity
  on these blocks, not an assumption that primitivity passes to subgroups.
- The author manuscript's entire Section3, including Lemma3.1's proof,
  and Section4 through Lemma4.1 were read. The maximal-torus Frattini
  argument in Theorem2.3's proof was checked separately. No full-paper
  audit or independent proof of its cited classification is claimed.
- In the initial arXiv version, all of Section3 and the opening of
  Section4 through Proposition4.2 and Lemma4.3 were read, as well as
  Section5's stated generation results. The author copy has additional
  material and different subsidiary numbering; our citation uses it.
- In Fulman--Guralnick2012, the introduction and Theorems7.2--7.3 with
  their proof were read, together with the subsequent Aschbacher table
  and Theorem7.7 statement. The latter requires large rank and is not
  substituted for the fixed-rank input.
- Targeted searches for the exact problem number and diagonal/block-
  monomial wording found the Notebook and related material, without an
  identified explicit attribution of this consequence. This limited
  search cannot establish novelty. We conservatively count it as a
  consequence of prior machinery, not an additional new candidate.
- No finite computation is needed to establish this asymptotic
  consequence. The source dependency and all elementary steps above
  remain available for independent mathematical review.
