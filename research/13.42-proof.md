# Problem 13.42: a class-three free nilpotent group with non-nilpotent tensor completion

11 September 2026. Complete affirmative candidate; independent review
and priority checks remain pending.

## The theorem and the category

Let A=Z[t] and let N=F(x,y)/gamma_4(F(x,y)) be the free nilpotent
group of rank two and class three. Its unrestricted tensor A-completion
is not nilpotent. In fact, for every r>=1,

    [x^t, y, ..., y] != 1 in N^A,                    (1)

where there are r copies of y, and commutators are left-normed with
[u,v]=u^(-1)v^(-1)uv.

We use the A-group axioms in Myasnikov--Remeslennikov, *Exponential
groups 2: Extensions of centralizers and tensor completion of CSA-groups*,
Definition 1, page 1. They require ordinary power rules, compatibility
with conjugation, and (gh)^alpha=g^alpha h^alpha when g and h commute.
Their Definition 2 and Theorem 1, page 3, give the universal tensor
completion for these A-groups. These definitions were read and viewed:
https://web.stevens.edu/algebraic/alexeim/Publications/All_files_new/expo2jj_29.pdf .
The source's later CSA-group construction is not used.

In particular, the category does not require faithful scalar action,
or absence of A-torsion. Our scalar ring A is an integral domain, and
the target group below is torsion-free as an ordinary group. Its scalar
action factors through A/(t^2). No quotient closure of the category
of A-groups is assumed.

## A rational nilpotent group

Let L be the rational BCH group of the five-dimensional Lie algebra
with basis X,Y,U,V,W and nonzero basic brackets

    [X,Y]=U,       [X,U]=V,       [Y,U]=W.

The vectors V,W are central. We write elements of L as
exp(aX+bY+cU+dV+eW), abbreviated (a,b,c,d,e), with rational
coordinates. Multiplication is

    (a,b,c,d,e)*(A,B,C,D,E)
      = (a+A, b+B, c+C+s/2,
         d+D+(a*C-c*A)/2+s*(a-A)/12,
         e+E+(b*C-c*B)/2+s*(b-B)/12),               (2)

where s=a*B-b*A. The identity is the zero tuple, the inverse negates
all coordinates, and integer powers multiply all coordinates by that
integer. The map beta:L->(Q,+), beta(a,b,c,d,e)=b, is a homomorphism.

This group can be defined without an infinite series. In the unital
free associative rational algebra on X,Y with every word of length
four set to zero, take the Lie subspace spanned by X,Y,[X,Y],
[X,[X,Y]],[Y,[X,Y]]. Define exp(Z)=1+Z+Z^2/2+Z^3/6. Direct expansion
gives (2), so it defines an associative group of class at most three.
The inverse coordinate map is log(1+Z)=Z-Z^2/2+Z^3/3, and the five
indicated Lie vectors are linearly independent. The class bound follows
from [1+J^i,1+J^j] being contained in 1+J^(i+j), where J^4=0.
Only class at most three is needed for the map from N below.

Two elementary consequences of (2) will be used repeatedly:

1. If l=(a,b,c,d,e) and h=(A,B,C,D,E), the first three coordinates
   of l^h=h^(-1)lh are

       (a, b, c+a*B-b*A).                          (3)

2. The elements l and h commute exactly when

       a*B-b*A=0,   a*C-c*A=0,   b*C-c*B=0.        (4)

Thus their triples (a,b,c) and (A,B,C) are linearly dependent.
For commuting elements their product has these triples added.
Both facts also follow by subtracting the two orders of (2).

## A conjugation-equivariant map

Let M be the additive group of Q[Q]. Explicitly, M consists of finite
sums of rational multiples of basis vectors T^q, q in Q, with
T^q*T^r=T^(q+r). Distinct exponents label linearly independent vectors.
Let L act on the right on M by

    v^l = v*T^(beta(l)).

Define a map F:L->M by

    F(a,b,c,d,e) = a*T^(c/a)  if b=0 and a!=0,
                   0         otherwise.           (5)

The division in this expression takes place in Q. We record four
properties, valid for all l,h in L and all integers n.

    F(l^h)=F(l)*T^(beta(h));                        (6)
    F(l^n)=n*F(l);                                 (7)
    F(lh)=F(l)+F(h) if [l,h]=1;                     (8)
    F(l)*T^(beta(l))=F(l).                         (9)

For (6), conjugation preserves a,b. When b=0 and a!=0, (3) changes
c/a to c/a+beta(h); when the condition fails, both values are zero.
For (7), multiply the coordinates by n; treat n=0 separately.
For (8), (4) makes the triples proportional. The function of a triple
in (5) is homogeneous over Q (including negative scalars and zero),
hence additive on any one-dimensional subspace. Finally, the only
case of nonzero F(l) has beta(l)=0, proving (9).

## The semidirect product and its exponentiation

Use the right-action convention

    H = L semidirect M,
    (l,v)*(h,w)=(lh, v*T^(beta(h))+w).              (10)

Write i(v)=(1_L,v), and define f:H->i(M) by

    f(l,v)=i(F(l)).

Conjugation on i(M) by (h,w) is multiplication by T^(beta(h)).
Thus (6)--(9) give

    f(g^h)=f(g)^h,         [g,f(g)]=1,              (11)
    f(gh)=f(g)f(h) whenever [g,h]=1.                (12)

Moreover, for all integers n,m,

    f(g^n f(g)^m)=f(g)^n.                          (13)

Indeed, the projection of g^n f(g)^m to L is l^n, so this is exactly
(7). In particular f(1)=1 and f(f(g))=1. Notice also that if g,h
commute, (11) implies [f(g),h]=1 and [g,f(h)]=1.

For a polynomial P(t) in A, put a=P(0), b=P'(0), and define

    g^P = g^a f(g)^b.                              (14)

Here the powers on the right are ordinary integer group powers.
We verify every A-group axiom explicitly.

- **Unit and zero:** P=1 and P=0 give g^1=g and g^0=1; f(1)=1
  gives 1^P=1.
- **Addition:** By (11), g and f(g) commute. Adding the two coefficients
  in (14) therefore gives g^(P+Q)=g^P g^Q.
- **Multiplication:** Write Q(0)=c and Q'(0)=d. Equation (13) gives
  f(g^P)=f(g)^a. Hence

      (g^P)^Q = (g^a f(g)^b)^c (f(g)^a)^d
               = g^(ac) f(g)^(bc+ad) = g^(PQ),

  because (PQ)(0)=ac and (PQ)'(0)=ad+bc.
- **Conjugation:** Ordinary integer powers respect conjugation and
  (11) gives (g^h)^P=(g^P)^h.
- **Commuting products:** If [g,h]=1, equations (11)--(12) permit all
  necessary rearrangements, and

      (gh)^P = g^a h^a f(g)^b f(h)^b = g^P h^P.

Thus H is an A-group in precisely the source category. It is also
torsion-free: a finite-order element would project to a finite-order
element of L and then lie in the torsion-free abelian group i(M).

## The non-nilpotent image of the completion

Let x=(exp X,0), y=(exp Y,0) in H. The subgroup they generate lies
in L and has nilpotency class at most three. Therefore sending the
free generators to x,y defines a homomorphism N->H. By the defining
universal property it extends to an A-homomorphism N^A->H.

But (5) and (14) give

    x^t=i(T^0),        beta(y)=1.

For every v in M, formula (10) yields

    [i(v),y]=i(v*(T-1)).

Consequently the image of the left side of (1) is

    i((T-1)^r).

This is nonidentity for every r>=1: the coefficient of T^r is one,
and the vectors T^0,...,T^r are distinct basis vectors of M. Thus
(1) holds in N^A. A nilpotent group cannot have these nontrivial
commutators of arbitrary length, proving the theorem.

The same fixed pair x^t,y generates a non-nilpotent subgroup, so the
completion is not even locally nilpotent.

No assertion that the entire target H is generated over A by x,y is
needed; the image of the completion already contains the displayed
commutators. Nor is injectivity of N->H required. The use of a free
nilpotent source follows from its defining universal property, not
from an unproved identification of the matrix or Lie subgroup.

This is an unrestricted tensor completion. Hall's completion within
nilpotent groups, and a completion restricted to nilpotent A-groups,
are different universal constructions and do not supply counterarguments.
