## Scope and mathematical audit

1. Printed page 139 was visually checked. The symbol is >=, and the
   inputs of each star-commutator have coprime prime-power orders.
   The question does not require the output orders to be primary.
   The candidate proves the conclusion even if the inequality is
   required only for pairs with primary outputs.
2. In the normalizer lemma, both tested elements are legitimate
   star-commutators. In particular b=[a,z] has inputs a and z of
   distinct-prime-power orders. It lies in the normalized p-group,
   so its output order is also coprime to |a|. The identity
   a[a,z]=a^z has the stated commutator convention.
3. The hyperfocal theorem is used in its explicit finite-group
   element-normalizer formulation. O^p(G) is the p-residual.
   It is never confused with the p-core O_p(G).
4. A p'-element of a normalizer splits into commuting primary
   components inside the same cyclic subgroup, hence all components
   still normalize Q. The expansion [x,uv]=[x,v][x,u]^v gives
   actual primary-input commutator factors inside Q. Taking powers
   of commutator outputs is neither assumed nor used.
5. Every restricted value dies in a p-group quotient because at
   least one of its input primes differs from p. This proves the
   opposite inclusion in the Sylow generation lemma. Perfection
   then makes O^p(G)=G without needing G simple.
6. The hypothesis is hereditary to subgroups. No quotient inheritance
   of the order inequality is invoked. Minimality gives perfection;
   the soluble radical equals Phi(G) by the maximal-subgroup
   argument. Phi(G)'s nilpotence is reproved using the Frattini
   argument. Hence the simple quotient is minimal simple.
7. Each Sylow subgroup of Phi(G) is characteristic there and normal
   in G. Its centralizer is normal. Containing the Sylow subgroups
   at every other prime gives p-power index, so perfection forces
   this centralizer to equal G. This proves Phi(G)=Z(G).
8. The list of five minimal-simple families is imported from
   Thompson. The local subgroup existence is also prior in
   Bastos–Monetta, Proposition 18. Explicit constructions establish
   the precise odd-prime-order element used in this proof.
9. For odd PSL2, the two square sets intersect by their cardinalities.
   The trace pairing proves independence of I,i,j,k in every odd
   characteristic. The element (-I+i+j+k)/2 has determinant one
   and order three, including in characteristic three. Its image
   modulo {I,-I} is nontrivial and cycles the projective involutions.
10. The Suzuki matrices use exactly Bray–Bäärnhielm's convention.
    With this convention the b-axis scales by lambda^(t+2), not
    lambda^(t+1). Direct matrix calculation and the congruence
    t^2=2q verify the formula; gcd(t+2,q-1)=1 ensures nontrivial
    action for every selected prime divisor of q-1.
11. The preimage E of V has central derived subgroup and is therefore
    nilpotent. Its unique Sylow 2-subgroup maps onto V. A lift of
    ybar can be replaced by its q-component without changing its
    image; this operation is applied to an input, not an output.
    Thus a=[u,y] is a primary restricted commutator whose image is
    an involution. No assertion that |a|=2 is needed.
12. Baer–Suzuki is applied in S, where O_2(S)=1. The product of the
    resulting two involutions has an odd prime divisor in its order.
    Its corresponding odd-prime-order power is inverted by abar.
    This power need not be a commutator: it is only used to specify
    the cyclic subgroup in the quotient.
13. The full preimage of that cyclic subgroup is abelian because
    its kernel is central. Its unique Sylow r-subgroup is normal
    in the dihedral preimage and maps onto the cyclic subgroup.
    Therefore the primary restricted commutator a normalizes it.
    The normalizer lemma contradicts the inversion action in S.
14. The final forbidden pair can be written a and [a,z], with z in
    that Sylow r-subgroup. Their input and output order requirements
    are all satisfied; their product is a conjugate of a. Thus the
    contradiction is to the exact Notebook hypothesis.

## Completed exact controls

`scripts/verify_19_56.g` enumerates all nonidentity primary inputs of
distinct primes, retaining all resulting values, with the identity
included by convention. It checks every coprime-output pair, both
with and without the primary-output restriction. The coverage is
all **586 groups through order 64**, followed by seven named groups:
S3, A4, S4, A5, SL2(5), PSL2(7), and A6. This includes intentional
duplicates for comparison with the independent implementation.

Across the **593 cases**, **159,220 input pairs** are evaluated.
There are **843 Sylow generation checks**, using the subgroup
generated by all p'-elements as an independent computation of
O^p(G). All pass. The inequality holds in **582 cases** and fails
in **11 cases**. In every case its truth agrees with nilpotence of
the nilpotent residual, and with the primary-output-only condition.
The residual is computed through the lower central series, not
through the commutator set being tested.

The GAP matrix checks cover fifteen odd fields, five even fields,
PSL3(3), and Suzuki parameters 8,32,128: **24 examples** in total.
All **17,472 Suzuki additive-product identities** and every torus
conjugation check pass. At q=8 the displayed matrices generate an
actual group of order 29,120 and the local V lies in that group.

Three separate central-lift checks in SL2(5), SL2(7), and SL2(9)
construct a=[u,y] of order four and an odd-primary z with
|a[a,z]|=4. Their [a,z] orders are 5,3,3 respectively. They check
the abelian cyclic preimage, the normal Sylow subgroup, its image,
and the explicit violation. These examples specifically guard
against treating the lift a as an involution in G.

`scripts/verify_19_56.py` constructs the seven named groups directly
as literal permutations or two-by-two matrices over prime fields.
It does not read GAP data. All **176,580 multiplication entries**
are reconstructed; the star-value counts, input-pair counts,
inequality outcomes, and Sylow generation data agree exactly with
GAP. Every negative case includes four explicit restricted inputs
and the two output values. Twelve independent odd-prime matrix
checks, including characteristic three, verify the quaternion and
order-three identities. Both final processes were observed to exit
zero, and the complete logs contain their expected completion data.

An initial GAP control stopped on the trivial group because
`FactorsInt(1)` returns a list containing 1, which was mistakenly
passed to SylowSubgroup. Its output contains an error and has no
completion marker. Notably, GAP under this launcher returned OS
status zero despite the error. Its script, log and process record
are preserved as `results/19.56-initial-*` and are explicitly
excluded from pass evidence. The corrected run filters prime
divisors with IsPrimeInt, checks the trivial group, and completes.
The final audit requires both exit status and clean completion
markers, and hash-binds the exact scripts and logs.

These bounded computations corroborate the local arguments. The
general result rests on the written proof and the imported theorems.

  The same material in the earlier author preprint was read.

  Guo–Revin, DOI 10.1007/s13373-017-0112-y, introduction and the
  opening list of the minimal-simple classification section.
  Guo–Revin's separate subgroup-classification proof is not imported.
* Bray–Bäärnhielm, arXiv:1706.05697v1: page 1 read in full,
  including the standard matrices and the stated unipotent/torus
  subgroup membership. Their recognition algorithm and its proof
  are not needed and were not audited.
* Baer–Suzuki: the exact p-element statement was checked in the
  abstract and opening page of Guralnick–Malle, *Variations on the
  Baer–Suzuki theorem*, author preprint dated 22 June 2014.
  The final references were read. The classic theorem's proof is
  imported; the paper's new variations are not used.
* Monakhov arXiv:1704.01746v1: all four pages read. Its ordinary
  primary-output theorem, normalizer lemma and central-quotient
  argument are prior. The original ordinary focal argument does
  not by itself establish restricted-input Sylow generation.
* Monakhov's 2017 star-commutator paper, Russian original pages
  253–256: all four pages read, including the two theorem proofs
  and the final question, which is exactly 19.56. Theorem 2 assumes
  solubility. Its earlier Schmidt/formation structure dependencies
  are imported and have not all been independently audited.
* Bastos–Monetta arXiv:1811.10025v1: all fifteen pages read.
  Theorem A and its full proof were examined for the present scope.
  Its k=2 inputs may have arbitrary coprime orders and its hypothesis
  is equality. Proposition 18 and the analogous local lifting
  strategy are explicitly credited. Theorem B and its later claims
  are not used. In particular we do not import the preprint's
  Lemma 14 literally without a value-set hypothesis on x: S3
  already shows that the unrestricted wording cannot be correct.
* Li–Lei–Gao 2022, pages 372–374: all three pages read. The
  conditions on all small primary elements do not imply the
  restriction to star-commutators required here.
