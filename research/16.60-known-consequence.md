# Problem 16.60: an existing affirmative claim, with a direct proof

Status: excluded from the new-candidate count. Yerrapati--Dixit--Shukla,
*Complex Representations of Groups and Involutions of its Automorphisms*,
arXiv:2605.23195v1 (22 May 2026), Theorem3.1, states the full affirmative
answer. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23195v1 . The introduction, Lemmas2.19
and2.20, and Theorem3.1 were read. The rest of the paper was not audited.

Their Lemma2.19 passes from an average of chi(g alpha(g)) to a scalar
Cauchy--Schwarz expression in chi(g) and chi(alpha(g)) without explaining
that step. Character values are not multiplicative, so that displayed
step should not be accepted as a scalar Cauchy--Schwarz proof by itself.
The following elementary tensor argument supplies the needed bound and
establishes the theorem independently of that step. This is not a claim
of priority for the already stated answer or its standard ingredients.

## Direct proof for arbitrary automorphisms

Let rho:G->U(V) be a complex irreducible representation of degree d,
with character chi. Define the unitary representation

    R(g)=rho(g) tensor rho(alpha(g))

on V tensor V, and let E=|G|^(-1) sum_g R(g). This is the orthogonal
projection onto the invariant vectors. Their dimension is

    dim Hom_G(V*, V composed with alpha),

which is zero or one by Schur's lemma. Let F(v tensor w)=w tensor v
be the unitary flip. The identity Tr((A tensor B)F)=Tr(AB) gives

    iota_alpha(chi)=|G|^(-1) sum_g chi(g alpha(g))=Tr(EF).

For an orthonormal basis of the image of E, this trace is a sum of
at most one matrix coefficient of a unitary operator. Therefore

    |iota_alpha(chi)|<=rank(E)<=1.

No involutivity assumption on alpha is needed, and E need not commute
with F. Finally, the regular character identity gives

    |{g:alpha(g)=g^(-1)}| = sum_(chi in Irr(G)) chi(1) iota_alpha(chi).

Indeed, the regular character at g alpha(g) is |G| if that element is
one and zero otherwise. Taking absolute values proves the desired
bound by T(G)=sum chi(1).

## A second reduction

One can alternatively take H=Fix(alpha^2). Every alpha-inverted element
lies in H, and alpha restricts to an involutive automorphism of H.
The usual twisted Frobenius--Schur argument gives |S_alpha|<=T(H).
Also T(H)<=T(G): the restriction to H of the direct sum of all
irreducible G-modules contains every irreducible H-module, by induction
and Frobenius reciprocity. Its dimension is T(G). This proves the same
bound from the classical involutive case.

These arguments justify excluding16.60 as an existing-result target;
they do not certify unrelated later statements in the2026 paper.
