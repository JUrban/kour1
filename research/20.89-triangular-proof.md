# Almost Engel elements of a triangular group

**Partial result for 20.89, also numbered 19.83.** Let G be any subgroup
of the invertible upper triangular n by n matrices over a field F. The
almost Engel elements of G form a normal subgroup P. In fact, P is
finite-by-nilpotent, and there is one finite normal subgroup K of P that
is an Engel sink for every element of P relative to the whole of G.
No assertion of new priority or of a solution for general linear groups
is made.

Conjugation is x^g = g^-1 x g and [x,g] = x^-1 x^g. A finite sink for g
must work for every x in G, with an input-dependent entry time.

## 1. Scalar lemma

Let B be an additive subgroup of F and let a in F^* satisfy aB = B.
Suppose that all sufficiently late iterates of b -> (a-1)b belong to
one finite subset S of B, for every b in B. Then a=1 or B is finite.

Indeed, suppose a != 1 and take b != 0. The tail of the sequence
(a-1)^j b is finite, so two terms agree. Cancellation in the field
gives (a-1)^r=1 for some r>0. Every element of B is therefore periodic
under this map. Each occurs arbitrarily late in its own orbit and must
belong to S. Thus B is finite. If B=0 the conclusion already holds.

## 2. The entry filtration

Put U=G intersect UT_n(F). Order the pairs (i,j), i<j, by decreasing
j-i, breaking ties lexicographically, and write them as p_1,...,p_t,
where t=n(n-1)/2. Let V_s consist of unipotent upper triangular
matrices whose possibly nonzero off-diagonal entries are among
p_1,...,p_s. Put U_s=G intersect V_s, including U_0=1.

These are G-normal subgroups. Matrix multiplication and inversion
only create additional entries at larger superdiagonal distances;
conjugation by an upper triangular matrix has the same property.
For p_s=(i,j), the map taking a matrix in U_s to its (i,j) entry is a
homomorphism with kernel U_{s-1}. Its image B_s is an additive subgroup
of F. On B_s, conjugation by g acts as multiplication by

    chi_s(g) = g_jj / g_ii.

Terms other than this diagonal contribution have larger superdiagonal
distance and occur in U_{s-1}. This also proves [U_s,U] <= U_{s-1}.
The maps chi_s:G -> F^* are homomorphisms and U is in every kernel.
Finally, G/U is abelian, by taking diagonal entries.

Define

    P = intersection of ker(chi_s) over those s for which B_s is infinite.

The empty intersection means G. This is a normal subgroup containing U.

## 3. Necessity

Suppose g has a finite sink E relative to G. Starting with x in U_s,
every iterated commutator remains in U_s. Projecting its eventual tail
to B_s gives the scalar dynamics b -> (chi_s(g)-1)b with finite sink
the image of E intersect U_s. The scalar lemma forces chi_s(g)=1
whenever B_s is infinite. Consequently g belongs to P.

## 4. A finite-or-central-series lemma

We use these classical results in their precise forms:

* Hall: if gamma_{c+1}(X) is finite, then X/Z_{2c}(X) is finite.
* Baer: if X/Z_d(X) is finite, then gamma_{d+1}(X) is finite.

The c=0 instance of the first statement just says that a finite group
is finite. These forms and their use in this setting are recorded in
Wehrfritz, *Weak Engel Conditions on Linear Groups* (2019), p.146,
step (b), citing Robinson, *Finiteness Conditions and Generalized
Soluble Groups*, Theorem 4.25 and Corollary 2 to 4.21.

It follows that a group X with a finite normal series whose factors
are each finite or X-central is finite-by-nilpotent. Induct on the
length, with first nontrivial term A. If A is finite, lift the finite
normal kernel supplied by induction in X/A; its preimage is finite.
If A is central, induction gives finite gamma_{c+1}(X/A) for some c.
Hall gives finite (X/A)/Z_{2c}(X/A). The preimage of that upper-central
term is contained in Z_{2c+1}(X), since A is central. Hence
X/Z_{2c+1}(X) is finite, and Baer gives finite gamma_{2c+2}(X).
In either case the quotient by a finite normal subgroup is nilpotent.

## 5. Sufficiency and an ambient sink

In the series

    1 = U_0 <= U_1 <= ... <= U_t = U <= P,

each additive factor B_s is finite or P-central by the definition of
P. The last factor P/U is central in P/U, since it is abelian. The
lemma gives a finite normal subgroup K of P and an integer c>=0 with
gamma_{c+1}(P) <= K. We may take K=gamma_{c+1}(P) itself, so K is also
characteristic in P and normal in G.

For x in G and g in P, the first commutator [x,g] belongs to U, hence
to P. Its next c commutators with g belong to gamma_{c+1}(P), hence
to K, and all later ones remain in K. Thus K is a finite sink for g
relative to all of G. This proves that P is exactly the set asked
about, including inverse and product closure. It also proves the
stronger uniform bound [x,_{c+1} g] in K for every x in G and g in P.

For n=1, U=1, P=G is abelian, and K=1 works directly.

## 6. Example separating finite and infinite layers

Over F_3(t), form the block diagonal group

    G = Aff_1(F_3) x (F_3(t)^+ semidirect {1,-1}),

using the usual 2 by 2 affine matrices in each block. The first
additive layer is finite; the second is infinite. The characterization
gives P=Aff_1(F_3) x F_3(t)^+, and K=Aff_1(F_3) x 1, of order six,
is a common sink. Directly, [x,_2 g] is in K for every x in G, g in P.
Thus the almost Engel subgroup can include non-Engel elements and a
nonnilpotent finite subgroup.

An element acting by -1 on the infinite additive factor is excluded:
in characteristic three its commutator map on that factor is
b -> (-1-1)b=b. Every translation is then a fixed commutator value,
forcing an infinite sink. The translations by t^k already suffice.

## 7. What this does not prove

An arbitrary linear group need not be triangularizable. Passing to a
triangular subgroup of finite index requires a separate argument for
the action of elements outside it. Still less does this proof treat a
general group with an insoluble connected component. The two original
problem numbers remain unresolved in their full stated scope.
