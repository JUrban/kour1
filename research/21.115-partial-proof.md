# Coset-union bounds for abelian groups and odd class-two groups

Status: retained special-case rediscoveries, developed 10 September 2026.
The subsequent literature check found Sambale's general resolution,
arXiv:2609.09052v1, submitted 8 September 2026. Its finite-group argument
has been checked. Problem 21.115 is therefore excluded from active targets
and from all new-solution counts. See `21.115-literature-audit.md`.

## Finite abelian groups

**Proposition 1.** Let A be a finite abelian group, and let C_1,...,C_n be
cosets of subgroups of A whose union is not all of A. Then

    |A minus union_i C_i| >= |A|/2^n.

Choose x_0 outside the union. For each C_i=g_i H_i, the image of
x_0 g_i^(-1) in A/H_i is nontrivial. Characters of a finite abelian group
separate its elements: this follows directly by decomposing the group
into cyclic factors. Hence there is a complex linear character chi_i,
trivial on H_i, with chi_i(x_0) != chi_i(g_i).

The function

    f(x)=product_i (chi_i(x)-chi_i(g_i))

vanishes on every C_i and is nonzero at x_0. On expansion it is a linear
combination of at most 2^n characters (possibly fewer because products
coincide or coefficients cancel).

For completeness, any nonzero function on A which is a linear combination
of t distinct characters has support at least |A|/t. Write
f=sum_{chi in T} a_chi chi, with |T|=t. Orthogonality and Cauchy--Schwarz
give

    ||f||_2^2 = |A| sum_chi |a_chi|^2,
    ||f||_infinity^2 <= t sum_chi |a_chi|^2,
    ||f||_2^2 <= |support(f)| ||f||_infinity^2.

Cancel the positive sum of coefficient squares to obtain the assertion.
Applying it to f gives Proposition 1.

This is the finite Fourier support uncertainty argument. Szegedy's proof
of Lemma 16 in *Coverings of abelian groups and vector spaces* uses the
same product of characters for complements consisting of one point,
followed by a cyclotomic norm argument giving a sharper result there:
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0411244 , published in J. Combin. Theory Ser. A
114 (2007), 20--34, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2005.10.007 .
We do not claim to have introduced that character-product technique.

## A nonabelian special case

**Proposition 2.** Proposition 1 remains true for every finite group G
of nilpotency class at most two whose derived subgroup has odd order.
In particular it holds for every finite group of odd order and class
at most two, with arbitrary exponent.

Use [x,y]=x^(-1)y^(-1)xy. The derived subgroup is central and of odd order,
so its squaring map is an automorphism. Define on the set G an addition

    x + y := xy [x,y]^(-1/2).

This is an abelian group operation, with zero 1 and additive inverse x^(-1).
Here are direct verifications. Since yx=xy[x,y]^(-1), the formula is
symmetric. The class-two identities [xy,z]=[x,z][y,z] and
[x,yz]=[x,y][x,z], together with centrality of all commutators, show that
both (x+y)+z and x+(y+z) equal

    xyz [x,y]^(-1/2) [x,z]^(-1/2) [y,z]^(-1/2).

The identity and inverse statements follow immediately. On the derived
subgroup this addition equals its original multiplication. The commutator
map is additive in each argument for the new operation.

Every original subgroup H of G is an additive subgroup as well: for
x,y in H, the odd-order element [x,y] lies in H, and its square root is
a power of itself and therefore belongs to H. Closure under the displayed
addition and inverses follows.

For fixed g, let T_g(x)=[g,x], considered as an additive map with central
values. It is an additive endomorphism and T_g^2=0. The map

    L_g=Id+(1/2)T_g

is an automorphism of the additive group, with inverse Id-(1/2)T_g.
The half is taken only on the odd-order derived subgroup, so no
divisibility assertion about the whole group is needed. In this notation
the original product satisfies

    gx = g + L_g(x).

Consequently each original left coset gH is an affine coset of the
additive subgroup L_g(H). The analogous formula with a minus sign handles
right cosets. All the sets therefore become cosets in one and the same
finite abelian group on the set G. Apply Proposition 1 to obtain the bound.

## Scope and source comparison

Read the full published Sambale--Tarnauceanu paper *On the size of coset
unions*, J. Algebraic Combin. 55 (2022), 979--987,
https://doi.org/10.1007/s10801-021-01079-x . It gives the bound for elementary
abelian groups (in a sharper prime-dependent form), cyclic groups, and
several other restricted settings, and poses the general conjecture.
Its nilpotent reduction left general p-groups unresolved at that time. These
arguments isolate further tractable cases but do not cover class-two
2-groups with nontrivial derived subgroup, or arbitrary finite groups.
