# Problem 15.92: continuum many alternating-residual triangle quotients

Complete proof candidate, 2026-09-11. Internal review and source checks are
recorded separately. Independent review and priority checks remain pending.

## Statement

For every integer r>6, the ordinary triangle group

    Gamma_r = <x,y | x^2=y^3=(xy)^r= 1>

has continuum many pairwise nonisomorphic **infinite** quotients residually
finite alternating. Here the latter property means that each nonidentity
element survives under an epimorphism onto a finite alternating group.

The Notebook was checked visually on PDF page 95. It omits '=1' at the end
of the displayed triangle presentation. The group it literally presents
surjects onto Gamma_r, so the result also answers that literal version.

The essential new deduction in this packet is the separating-kernel
argument below. The finite permutation constructions are imported from
Conder's 1980 thesis. Their existence is not asserted as new.

## A bounded-support criterion


Let Gamma be finitely generated. Suppose that there are epimorphisms
theta_i: Gamma -> A_(n_i), with n_i tending to infinity, and a single element
v of Gamma such that theta_i(v) is nonidentity and moves at most S points,
where S is independent of i. Then Gamma has continuum many pairwise
nonisomorphic quotients residually finite alternating.

All alternating groups below can be assumed to have degree at least five.

### 1. A marked limit with a nontrivial finitary normal subgroup

Enumerate the elements of Gamma. By successive subsequences and a diagonal
choice, pass to a subsequence on which each word is eventually always trivial
or eventually always nontrivial. Let R be the set of eventually trivial
elements. It is a normal subgroup, and G=Gamma/R is the marked limit.
The image vbar of v in G is nontrivial.

Choose a nonprincipal ultrafilter on the subsequence and let Omega be the
ultraproduct of the finite sets {1,...,n_i}. The natural permutation action
of G on Omega is faithful: for any word nontrivial in G, choose a moved point
in every sufficiently late coordinate. Conversely, an eventually trivial
word fixes every point in the ultraproduct.

The support of vbar has cardinality at most S. Indeed, S+1 distinct moved
ultraproduct points would, on an ultrafilter-large set of indices, be S+1
distinct points moved by theta_i(v), a contradiction. Nontriviality ensures
that this support is nonempty.

Let N be the normal closure of vbar in G and put

    Omega* = union over g in G of g(supp(vbar)).

This is a countable G-invariant set with at most S G-orbits. Every element of
N is a finitary permutation, N fixes the complement of Omega*, and its action
on Omega* is faithful.

Because N is normal in G, the N-orbits inside a fixed G-orbit have equal
cardinality. Thus there is a finite integer M bounding the sizes of all finite
N-orbits on Omega*: choose the maximum of the finite orbit sizes from the
finitely many G-orbits, or M=1 if there are no finite N-orbits.

### 2. Large alternating quotients of G kill vbar

Let Omega_fin be the union of the finite N-orbits. Write

    eta: N -> Sym(Omega_fin),       L = ker(eta).

Every element of eta(N) has order dividing M!, since each of its orbits lies
in a set of size at most M. This remains true if there are infinitely many
such sets.

Suppose phi: G -> A_m is onto and phi(vbar) is nontrivial. Simplicity gives
phi(N)=A_m. Let H be the kernel of phi restricted to N. This is a finite-index
normal subgroup of N. Every H-orbit in an infinite N-orbit is infinite: the
N-orbit is the union of finitely many translates of an H-orbit, using normality
and coset representatives for N/H.

For x in L and y in N, the finite support A of x lies entirely in infinite
N-orbits. Put B=supp(y). There is h in H which moves A disjoint from B.
Here is an elementary proof requiring no imported displacement theorem.
Every finitely generated subgroup of H is finite, because all its generators
have finite support. For each a in A choose finitely many elements of H
sending a to more than |A||B| different points. The finite subgroup F they
jointly generate has |F.a|>|A||B| for every a in A. For uniform h in F,
the probability h(a)=b is either zero or 1/|F.a|. The union bound shows that
the probability of any h(a)=b, with a in A and b in B, is less than one.
Thus a suitable h exists. Empty A or B is immediate.

The conjugate hxh^-1 therefore has support disjoint from y and commutes
with it. Since phi(h)=1, this implies that phi(x) commutes with phi(y).
As y was arbitrary and phi(N)=A_m has trivial center, phi(L)=1.
Consequently A_m is a quotient of eta(N), so its exponent divides M!.

Fix an odd prime ell>M. Whenever m>=ell, A_m contains an ell-cycle, whose
order does not divide M!. This contradiction proves that every sufficiently
large alternating quotient of G kills vbar.

For all sufficiently large i, theta_i does not factor through G, because
theta_i(v) is nontrivial. Equivalently, theta_i(R) is nontrivial, and hence
equals A_(n_i) by normality and simplicity.

### 3. Separating individual quotient kernels

Select indices i_1<i_2<... and elements r_1,r_2,... of R inductively.
At stage j choose i_j beyond the preceding cutoffs, and sufficiently large
that theta_(i_j)(R)=A_(n_(i_j)) and

    |A_(n_(i_j))| > product over k<j of |A_(n_(i_k))|.

Let

    L_j = R intersect intersection over k<j of ker(theta_(i_k)).

The index [R:L_j] is at most the displayed product. If theta_(i_j) killed
L_j, its restriction to R would give an epimorphism from the smaller finite
group R/L_j onto A_(n_(i_j)), impossible. Choose r_j in L_j not killed by
theta_(i_j). Since r_j belongs to R, every sufficiently late theta_i kills
it. Impose this as the new cutoff on all subsequently selected indices.

Thus theta_(i_k)(r_j) is nontrivial exactly when k=j. For each subset I of
the positive integers let

    K_I = intersection over j in I of ker(theta_(i_j)).

The element r_j lies outside K_I exactly when j belongs to I. All these
normal subgroups are therefore different. Each Gamma/K_I embeds in the
product of the indicated finite alternating groups, with onto coordinate
maps, so is residually finite alternating in the strong epimorphic sense.

There are continuum many kernels. A countable finitely generated group has
only countably many generating tuples of any fixed finite length. Therefore
only countably many epimorphisms from Gamma can have any one fixed
isomorphism type as their target. Dividing continuum many kernels into these
countable classes leaves continuum many isomorphism types. QED.

## The precise finite-image input

Write r=h+6d with h in {7,8,9,10,11,12} and d>=0, and set

    p=11 if h is 7,8,11;       p=13 if h is 9,10,12.

Conder's construction in Chapter 5 of his 1980 Oxford DPhil thesis gives
finite permutation actions on sets of arbitrarily large size n, with
permutations x_n,y_n,t_n satisfying

    x_n^2=y_n^3=(x_n y_n)^r=t_n^2=(x_n t_n)^2=(y_n t_n)^2=1,
    <x_n,y_n> = A_n.

In these same actions, w_n=x_n y_n t_n has one p-cycle, has no other cycle
whose length is divisible by p, and all its cycle lengths divide 720720.
These are properties of the construction in the proof, not consequences
of the bare assertion that A_n is a quotient.

The exact source locations are Theorem 5.1 on printed p. 90, the construction
on pp. 117-119, and its cycle and image assertions on pp. 119-120 (PDF
pages 97,124-127). The diagram tables appear on printed pp. 92-116. The
source allows x_n even in every case, including even r. In particular,
U(8,d) and U(10,d) themselves have odd x; adding W(8,d) or W(10,d) corrects
that parity. Each join inserts two transpositions and preserves parity.
Thus the ordinary triangle group has the asserted alternating images.

For clarity, one can use a chain of copies of S(h,d), attach a single
U(h,d), and add W only for h=8,10. The degrees are unbounded as the chain
length grows. The marked prime cycle is unaffected by further joins.
The source's useful-cycle argument and Jordan's theorem give the alternating
image. A separate arithmetic replay of these simplified families checks
the stated cycle data and parity, but does not replace the imported
existence and triangle-action assertions of Conder's construction.

Reference: Marston Donald Edward Conder, *Minimal generating pairs for
permutation groups*, DPhil thesis, University of Oxford, Trinity Term 1980,
[Oxford repository](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:75cd51df-8c16-4c00-85c2-32d85905164c),
DOI10.5287/ora-ppdogya80. The cached scan has 135 physical pages. The later
paper *More on generators for alternating and symmetric groups*, QJMath 32
(1981),137-163, DOI10.1093/qmath/32.2.137, is a corroborating bibliographic
lead; its full text is not used or claimed to have been read here.

## A single fixed word and the conclusion

The extended-triangle relations imply t_n x_n t_n=x_n and
 t_n y_n t_n=y_n^-1. Consequently

    w_n^2 = x_n y_n x_n y_n^-1.

Since

    720720 = 2^4 * 3^2 * 5 * 7 * 11 * 13,

the integer E=720720/p is even, is coprime to p, and is divisible by every
divisor of 720720 which is coprime to p. Therefore w_n^E fixes every point
outside its unique p-cycle and acts nontrivially on that cycle.

Define the following element of the **ordinary** triangle group, once and
for all, independently of n:

    v_r=(xyxy^-1)^32760       if p=11,
    v_r=(xyxy^-1)^27720       if p=13.

Its image under the epimorphism theta_n:Gamma_r->A_n is precisely w_n^E,
and thus is a single p-cycle. In particular, it is nonidentity and has
support at most 13 in every selected finite action.

Apply the bounded-support criterion to Gamma_r and these epimorphisms.
It gives continuum many distinct quotient kernels with alternating-residual
quotients. Restricting to infinite subsets I in the criterion gives infinite
quotients: each such quotient maps onto alternating groups of unbounded
order. There are continuum many infinite subsets, and the same countable
marking argument gives continuum many isomorphism types. This proves the
statement for every r>6. QED.

## Limits of the claim and validation

This does not claim that Gamma_r itself is residually alternating under
the selected family, nor that arbitrary infinite subsets of an arbitrary
family of alternating kernels have different intersections. The specially
selected witnesses r_j establish exactly the independence required.

The construction is an existence proof, using a diagonal subsequence and
a nonprincipal ultrafilter. No algorithm for enumerating all quotients is
claimed. The quotient family has the full continuum cardinality without
assuming the continuum hypothesis.

The supporting controls verify 150 parameter/chain cases from 13 diagram
tables, including d=0,1,2,3,10 and chain lengths 1,2,3,10,101. They check
degrees, involution parity, uniqueness of the selected prime cycle, the
720720 bound, and the resulting word support. Binary permutation powering
independently agrees with cycle-length arithmetic. All 240 divisors of 720720
are checked in the exponent argument. These are source-data consistency
checks, not finite tests of the infinite-group proof. Additional GAP controls
reconstruct the r=7 base diagrams directly from their drawn triangles and
edges. All five joined examples pass the exact primitive-group criterion
for A_n, in degrees 78 through 456. Four general group-order calculations
also pass; the redundant fifth is deliberately stopped and retained.
The final results and exact scope are in the review and manifest.
