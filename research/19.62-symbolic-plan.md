# Problem 19.62: symbolic inclusion experiment

12 September 2026. Exploratory; no general inclusion or solution claimed.

All 6,999 distinct derived carpets in the first finite screen satisfy
B_r^2 B_-r contained in B_r. Try to prove this inclusion by formal
monomial consequences of the original carpet conditions.

A derived parameter is an additive sum of signed terms C a^i b^j,
where a and b belong to specified original root subgroups. Expanding
the square times a second derived parameter reduces the desired
inclusion to two finite families: the square of one summand times one
opposite summand, and twice the product of two distinct summands times
one opposite summand. Variables remain independent even if their root
labels coincide. Root decompositions bound their total degrees.

For each target monomial, compute only coefficient ideals that have
explicit derivations from the carpet conditions. A variable initially
has coefficient one in its assigned root. Applying an (i,j,C) carpet
condition multiplies coefficient ideals with the corresponding powers.
Adding two certified multiples permits their integer gcd, by Bezout.
The same operations with a final nontrivial carpet condition certify
membership in the derived carpet. All divisors of the target monomial
form a finite directed calculation, ordered by total degree.

Success for every required term would prove the inclusion over arbitrary
commutative rings. Failure of this restricted monomial calculus proves
nothing negative: powers of sums can give further polynomial consequences
which it does not retain. No failed target may be called a counterexample.
Before a theorem claim, save explicit derivation certificates, audit
integer structure constants independently, verify square expansion,
and check the scope of the imported closedness criterion.
