# Problem 18.77: class-two and Lazard-group partial answers

Theorem 1.5. Priority of the small-class deduction is not established. No complete candidate increment.

We prove that the answer is affirmative for every finite p-group of
nilpotency class less than p, without an exponent restriction. We also
prove it for every class-two p-group, including p=2. The nonabelian
class-two case at p=2 only has e=1 under the problem's hypothesis.

## An alternating-form lemma over prime-power rings

Let R=Z/p^k, let D be a finite abelian p-group, and let d |-> M_d be an
additive map from D into the alternating n-by-n matrices over R. Here
alternating means diagonal zero and M^T=-M, also when p=2. Set

    |im(M_d:R^n -> R^n)| = p^(2 e_d),   e = max_{d in D} e_d.

If e<p, the elements d for which e_d=e generate D.

We recall two elementary facts about alternating forms. An alternating
matrix over R is congruent under GL_n(R) to blocks

    p^a J,  J=[[0,1],[-1,0]],  0<=a<k,

and a zero block. Choose a nonzero entry of smallest p-adic valuation,
put it in positions (1,2), scale its unit to one, and subtract multiples
of the first two basis vectors to eliminate their other pairings. Every
entry is divisible by the chosen p^a, so these operations are possible
over R. Iterate on the remaining coordinates. A block p^a J contributes
p^(2(k-a)) to the image order, proving the displayed even exponent.

For an integral p-adic alternating matrix Q of size 2r, with reduction
modulo p^k having image order at most p^(2h), we have

    v_p(Pf(Q)) >= rk-h.                                      (1)

Indeed, the same congruence reduction over Z_p gives nonzero blocks
p^a_i J. If Q is singular, its Pfaffian is zero and there is nothing
to prove. Otherwise v_p(Pf(Q))=sum_i a_i and the reduced image order
is p^(2 sum_i max(k-a_i,0)). The inequality follows by summing
k-a_i <= max(k-a_i,0). Congruence changes the Pfaffian by a unit.
For a principal submatrix, its image size is at most that of the full
matrix: its linear map factors through inclusion, the full map, and
coordinate projection. Thus (1) applies with h=e_d to every principal
2r-submatrix of a lift of M_d.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that all maximum parameters belong to a
proper subgroup of D. They then belong to a maximal subgroup H, of
index p. If e=0 every parameter is maximum, so assume e>0. Choose
u in H with e_u=e and v outside H. Make a fixed congruence change
putting M_u in the normal form above. Let r be its number of nonzero
blocks, with valuations a_1,...,a_r. Then

    e = sum_i(k-a_i),  1<=r<=e<p,  s=sum_i a_i=rk-e>=0.

Choose as an integral lift of M_u its displayed block matrix, and any
integral lift of M_v after the same change of coordinates. Let P(T) be
the Pfaffian of the leading 2r-submatrix of M_u+T M_v, using these
lifts. It is a polynomial in Z_p[T], of degree at most r, and
P(0)=p^s. For t=1,...,p-1, u+t v is outside H, so e_(u+tv)<=e-1.
Inequality (1) gives

    P(t) divisible by p^(s+1),  1<=t<=p-1.                   (2)

Moreover the coefficient of T^r is the Pfaffian of the corresponding
submatrix of M_v, so it too is divisible by p^(s+1).

All p values P(0),...,P(p-1) are divisible by p^s. Since deg P<p,
interpolation at 0,...,p-1 shows every coefficient divisible by p^s:
the Vandermonde determinant is a product of differences nonzero mod p,
hence is a unit in Z_p. Therefore Q=P/p^s is integral. Its reduction
mod p has constant term one, degree at most r-1, and, by (2), roots
1,...,p-1. But r-1<=e-1<p-1, an impossibility. This proves the lemma.

The proof does not presume that individual matrix entries are divisible
by p^s. It is interpolation that permits division of the Pfaffian
polynomial. The leading-coefficient argument is necessary in the boundary
case e=p-1.

## Application to finite groups of class less than p

The case p=2 is abelian and immediate. For p odd, use the Lazard
correspondence G=exp(L), where L is a finite Lie ring of class less
than p. Put D=Hom(L,C^*) for its additive character group, and choose
p^k annihilating L. Pull back the alternating bicharacter

    B_lambda(x,y)=lambda([x,y])

to a free R-module surjecting onto L. Identify the p^k-th roots of
unity with the additive group R. This gives an additive family M_lambda
as in the lemma. Its image order is |L:rad(B_lambda)|. To see this,
the pullback has radical equal to the inverse image of rad(B_lambda);
the first isomorphism theorem then computes the image order.

The finite Kirillov theorem states that coadjoint orbits Omega in D
parametrize irreducible complex characters, with

    chi_Omega(1)=|Omega|^(1/2)=|L:rad(B_lambda)|^(1/2),
    chi_Omega(exp z)=|Omega|^(-1/2) sum_(lambda in Omega) lambda(z).

For central z in L every member of an orbit has the same value on z.
Consequently the corresponding representation acts on exp z by the
scalar lambda(z). The lemma shows that the lambda attaining the largest
character degree generate D, provided e<p. Their common annihilator in
L is therefore zero. Hence the intersection N of all kernels of maximum
degree characters has trivial intersection with Z(G).

Finally any nontrivial normal subgroup N of a finite p-group meets Z(G)
nontrivially. Conjugation on N has nonfixed orbits divisible by p, so
|N intersect Z(G)| is divisible by p if N is nontrivial. Thus N=1,
as required.

The imported theorem is Boyarchenko--Sabitova, Theorem 2.6, with the
radical-index formulation in O'Brien--Voll, Theorem 3.1. The full proofs
of those imported orbit-method results are not reproduced here.

## Elementary application to all class-two p-groups

Let Z=Z(G), V=G/Z, and D=Hom(Z,C^*). For lambda in D define the
alternating bicharacter

    b_lambda(gZ,hZ)=lambda([g,h]).

Its radical in V is R_lambda. Every irreducible representation lying
over lambda has degree |V:R_lambda|^(1/2). Here is a direct argument,
including the existence of such representations. In C[G] take the
nonzero central idempotent

    e_lambda=|Z|^(-1) sum_(z in Z) lambda(z)^(-1) z.

The algebra e_lambda C[G] is semisimple, with a basis indexed by V.
Two basis elements commute exactly according to b_lambda. Its center
has basis indexed by R_lambda: conjugating a basis element multiplies
it by b_lambda against the conjugator, so only radical coefficients
can occur in a central element. This commutative semisimple center has
|R_lambda| primitive idempotents. Moreover the whole algebra is free
over its center of rank |V:R_lambda|, using representatives for
V/R_lambda and the nonzero multiplication constants of the basis.
Each central primitive summand consequently has dimension
|V:R_lambda| and is a full complex matrix algebra. Its matrix size is
the asserted character degree. All irreducibles arise from exactly
one lambda, since Z acts scalarly.

Choose k large enough that p^k annihilates V and Z. Pull back these
bicharacters to a free R-module surjecting onto V. The resulting
additive alternating matrices have image orders |V:R_lambda|. Apply
the lemma to D: the central characters supporting the maximum degree
generate D and thus separate Z. The normal-subgroup argument above
then proves that the maximum-degree irreducibles separate G.

This class-two proof uses complex semisimplicity and the elementary
structure of matrix algebras, but needs no orbit-method theorem.

## Limits and sharpness

None of this supplies an alternating-form parametrization for arbitrary
finite p-groups, so it does not solve the general Notebook question.
The strict inequality e<p is sharp for the algebraic lemma. Over F_p,
form a 2p-by-2p block diagonal alternating matrix with p blocks
(y-t x)J indexed by t in F_p. Its Pfaffian is

    product_(t in F_p)(y-t x)=y^p-x^(p-1)y.

Maximum image order p^(2p) occurs exactly when x=0 and y!=0. Those
parameters generate only one line of F_p^2. The same construction is
the alternating-form version of Passman's existing central-product
counterexample at e=p; it is not a new negative example.
