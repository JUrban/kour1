# 21.60: a counterexample of order twelve

Status: complete candidate negative answer to the proposed equivalence.
Outside review and novelty checks remain pending. The supplied Notebook
page 176 was visually checked. The counterexample uses p=2 and

    G = <a,b | a^3=1, b^4=1, bab^-1=a^-1>
      = C3 semidirect C4,

where C4 acts on C3 by inversion. Thus |G|=12. Put R=Z_(2), the
subring of Q consisting of fractions with odd denominator, and k=F2.

We prove two assertions:

1. Every simple kG-module is the reduction of a simple QG-module.
   Consequently every class of a finite-dimensional kG-module, including
   every projective indecomposable, is a nonnegative integral combination
   of the reductions specified in the question.
2. The ring RG is not semiperfect.

The obstruction in the second assertion is an explicit quotient order in
a division quaternion algebra. It does not require a Schur-index table.

## 1. All the simple modules in characteristic two lift

The central element z=b^2 has order two. On a simple kG-module,
(z-1)^2=0. Its kernel is nonzero and is G-invariant because z is central,
so z acts as the identity. Every simple kG-module therefore factors
through

    G/<z> = C3 semidirect C2 = S3.

We recall explicitly the two simple kS3-modules. In kS3 let

    e=1+a+a^2.

This is a central idempotent, because 3=1 in k and conjugation by b
interchanges a and a^2. The algebra e(kS3) is kC2: it has basis e,eb,
and a acts as 1. Its only simple module is the trivial module k.

The complementary algebra (1-e)(kS3), of dimension four, is M2(k).
An explicit isomorphism is supplied by the following representation:

    A = [[0,1],[1,1]],       B = [[1,1],[0,1]].             (1)

These matrices satisfy A^3=B^2=I and BAB^-1=A^-1, and
I+A+A^2=0. The four matrices I,A,B,AB are linearly independent
over k; for example, I+B=E12, A+AB=E22, and then the other two matrix
units follow. Thus the complementary four-dimensional algebra maps
isomorphically onto M2(k). Its only simple module up to isomorphism
is the natural two-dimensional module V. This proves that k and V
are all the simple kG-modules.

The trivial module lifts to the trivial simple QG-module. For V, take
the rational representation W with matrices

    A_Q = [[0,-1],[1,-1]],   B_Q = [[1,-1],[0,-1]].         (2)

Here A_Q^3=B_Q^2=I and B_Q A_Q B_Q^-1=A_Q^-1, so (2) defines a
representation of G, with b^2 in its kernel. It is simple over Q:
A_Q has irreducible minimal polynomial t^2+t+1 over Q, so there is
no invariant rational line. The lattice R^2 is G-stable, and reduction
of (2) gives exactly (1). The polynomial t^2+t+1 is also irreducible
over k, verifying directly that the reduction is simple.

Every finite-dimensional kG-module M has a composition series, and
therefore its class in the Grothendieck group is

    [M] = m[k] + n[V],            m,n nonnegative integers.

Both [k] and [V] are reductions of simple rational modules. Thus the
positive-combination condition in 21.60 holds for every M, and in
particular for all projective indecomposable kG-modules.

## 2. A division quaternion quotient of RG

Let E=Q(omega), where omega^2+omega+1=0, and denote its nontrivial
Q-automorphism by a bar. Form the quaternion algebra

    D = E + E j,       j^2=-1,       j u=bar(u) j (u in E).

Multiplication is explicitly

    (u+v j)(s+t j) = (us-v bar(t)) + (ut+v bar(s))j.

For u,v in E its quaternion norm is

    (u+v j)(bar(u)-v j) = u bar(u)+v bar(v).

This is a positive rational number whenever (u,v) is nonzero: if
u=x+y omega then u bar(u)=x^2-xy+y^2. Hence every nonzero element
has an inverse, obtained by dividing its quaternion conjugate by this
norm. In particular D is a division algebra and has no idempotents
other than 0 and 1.

Inside D let

    O = R[omega] + R[omega]j.

This is an R-order, free with basis 1,omega,j,omega j. It is closed
under multiplication because R[omega] is closed under the bar map.
Sending a to omega and b to j respects the presentation of G: omega
has order three, j has order four, and j omega j^-1=omega^-1.
It defines a surjective algebra homomorphism

    RG -> O.                                             (3)

Surjectivity follows from the displayed R-basis. The twelve elements
omega^i j^s for 0<=i<3 and 0<=s<4 are distinct, so this is also a
faithful group realization of G inside D^*. Faithfulness is not needed
for surjectivity of (3), but confirms the exact group being used.

## 3. Reduction of O splits into a matrix algebra

Modulo 2 the relations for omega and j become

    omega^2+omega+1=0,       j^2=1,       j omega=omega^2 j.

The assignment omega -> A and j -> B from (1) satisfies these relations.
The four basis elements map to I,A,B,AB, already shown independent.
Consequently

    O/2O is isomorphic to M2(k).                           (4)

In particular the quotient has nontrivial idempotents. One explicit
example is the residue of omega+omega j, whose matrix is E22. In O,
the element x=omega+omega j satisfies

    x^2+x+2=0;

its residue is idempotent, while O itself, as a subring of D, has no
nontrivial idempotent that could lift it.

To apply the definition of semiperfectness directly, we check that
J(O)=2O. For every y in O, the norm of 1-2y is congruent to 1 modulo
2R, and is thus a unit of R. Quaternion conjugation preserves O, so
the inverse formula shows that 1-2y is a unit of O. As 2O is an ideal,
the Jacobson-radical unit criterion gives 2O contained in J(O).
Conversely the image of J(O) in the semisimple quotient (4) is zero,
so J(O) is contained in 2O. This proves equality. The nontrivial
idempotent in (4) cannot lift to O, and hence O is not semiperfect.

Finally, a quotient of a semiperfect ring is semiperfect. For completeness,
if S maps onto T, the image of J(S) lies in J(T), so T/J(T) is a
quotient of S/J(S). When S is semiperfect this last ring is semisimple
artinian. Its quotients are direct factors, so any idempotent of T/J(T)
lifts to an idempotent of S/J(S), then to one of S, and finally to one
of T. This proves the assertion directly from the definition.

Apply it to (3). Since O is not semiperfect, RG cannot be semiperfect.
Combined with Section 1, this disproves the proposed “if” direction
in 21.60. QED.

## Relation to the published conjecture

This is the equivalence stated as Conjecture 9 in Johnston--Rumynin,
[*On a question by Roggenkamp about group algebras*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21316v2),
Journal of Algebra 687 (2026), 776--791,
[DOI 10.1016/j.jalgebra.2025.09.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2025.09.014).
The counterexample is modular, since 2 divides |G|. Its Sylow 2-subgroup
is not normal, so it is outside the normal-Sylow hypothesis of that
paper's Proposition 3.2. The paper's established necessary direction
is consistent with the example.

The example also conflicts with Proposition 3.3 as written in that
preprint: composition series here are filtrations of its stated kind.
The accompanying review identifies an explicit failure of the extension
lifting step used in its proof. We do not import that proposition.

In particular, equality of classes in the Grothendieck group is too weak
to ensure that a projective indecomposable module itself lifts. Here all
simple modules lift, but the quaternion quotient obstructs semiperfectness.

and the pending novelty check.
