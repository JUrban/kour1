# Problem 17.33: a candidate negative answer

Status: complete candidate negative answer, with a separate internal audit
and passing exact Python and GAP controls. Counted as the tenth complete
candidate. Novelty and outside review remain pending.

Let K=<a,b | a^(-1)ba=b^(-1)> be the Klein bottle group. We use its
standard integer model

    K=Z semidirect Z,  (u,k)(v,l)=(u+(-1)^k v,k+l).

The coordinate projection (u,k)->k has kernel Z. We construct, for
every r>=1, a finitely presented torsion-free virtually abelian group
Gamma such that every r-generated subgroup lies in q(K), but Gamma
itself does not. This rules out any axiomatization of q(K) by
quasiidentities using a bounded finite set of variables.

## The affine groups

Fix n>=2. Put E=F_2^n, and write its nonzero linear functionals as
chi in E*\{0}, with chi(e)=sum_j chi_j e_j. Index coordinates by

    I={(chi,j): chi in E*\{0}, 1<=j<=n},  m=|I|=n(2^n-1).

Let D_e act on Z^I by multiplication by (-1)^chi(e) in every
coordinate (chi,j). This is an action of E. Inside the semidirect
product Z^I semidirect E, define

    Gamma_n={(e,c): e in E, c in Z^I,
                    c_(chi,j) congruent to e_j modulo2 for all chi,j},

with multiplication

    (e,c)(f,d)=(e+f,c+D_e d).

Parity is preserved by this law and by inversion, so Gamma_n is a
subgroup. Its translation subgroup

    N={(0,2z):z in Z^I}

is free abelian of rank m. The quotient Gamma_n/N is E: a lift of
e_i is g_i=(e_i,c_i), where (c_i)_(chi,j)=delta_(ij).

In particular Gamma_n is finitely generated, using the n lifts g_i
and the m translations t_(chi,j)=(0,2e_(chi,j)). It is a finite
extension of a finitely generated abelian group and is finitely
presented; an explicit presentation check is supplied below.

## Torsion-freeness and finite abelianization

The square of an element is

    (e,c)^2=(0,(1+D_e)c).

Suppose e!=0. Since n>=2, there is a nonzero chi with chi(e)=0.
Choose j with e_j=1. The coordinate c_(chi,j) is odd, so the
corresponding coordinate of the square is 2c_(chi,j)!=0. Thus the
square is not the identity. If e=0 and c!=0, the square is also
nonzero. Any finite-order element would have square in the torsion-free
subgroup N, hence square one. Consequently Gamma_n is torsion-free.

On the other hand, its abelianization is finite. For every nonzero
chi, choose i with chi_i=1. Conjugation by g_i inverts each
t_(chi,j), and hence t_(chi,j)^2 belongs to the derived subgroup.
Thus 2N<=Gamma_n'. Since |Gamma_n:2N|=2^(m+n), the abelianization
is finite (indeed its exponent divides four).

Every homomorphism Gamma_n -> K is therefore trivial. Composing with
K->Z gives zero because Gamma_n has finite abelianization. The image
then lies in the translation subgroup Z of K, and the same argument
annihilates that image as well.

## Embeddings over every proper holonomy subspace

Let L<E be any proper linear subspace, and let Gamma_L denote its
full inverse image in Gamma_n. Choose a nonzero psi in E* that
vanishes on L. For each nonzero chi define

    ell_chi(e,c)=sum_(j=1)^n chi_j c_(psi,j).

On Gamma_L this is an integer-valued homomorphism: the coordinates
(psi,j) are fixed by D_e for every e in L. Its parity is

    ell_chi(e,c) congruent to sum_j chi_j e_j=chi(e) modulo2.

For each (chi,j) in I define a map into K by

    F_(chi,j)(e,c)=(c_(chi,j),ell_chi(e,c)).

This is a homomorphism. The first coordinate of a product in Gamma_L
is c_(chi,j)+(-1)^chi(e) d_(chi,j), precisely the multiplication in
K because ell_chi has parity chi(e); the second coordinate is additive.

The product F of these m maps is injective. If F(e,c)=1, all first
coordinates c_(chi,j) vanish. The defining parity conditions force
every e_j to vanish as well. Thus

    Gamma_L embeds in K^m,  for every proper L<E.

In particular, any subgroup generated by fewer than n elements has
holonomy contained in a proper subspace of E and embeds in K^m.
Every such subgroup belongs to q(K), since quasivarieties are closed
under finite products and subgroups.

## Why Gamma_n is outside q(K)

Use a finite presentation

    Gamma_n=<x_1,...,x_s | R_1=...=R_t=1>.

For a generator x_i representing a nonidentity element, the finite
quasiidentity

    (R_1=1 and ... and R_t=1) implies x_i=1

holds in K, because every homomorphism Gamma_n -> K is trivial.
It fails in Gamma_n on its defining generators. Every member of
q(K) satisfies every quasiidentity true in K, so Gamma_n is outside
q(K). This argument does not assume that arbitrary finitely generated
members of q(K) are residually K; finite presentation is used explicitly.

## No finite bound on the variables

Suppose q(K) were defined by a set Sigma of quasiidentities in at
most r variables, for some finite r>=1. Choose n>r, n>=2. For any
tuple of at most r elements of Gamma_n, its generated subgroup
embeds in K^m by the preceding construction, and hence satisfies
Sigma. Word evaluation at that tuple takes place in this subgroup,
so every sentence of Sigma also holds in Gamma_n. This contradicts
Gamma_n not in q(K). Thus q(K) has infinite axiomatic rank: the answer
to17.33 is negative.

## Explicit finite presentation

The generators are t_(chi,j) and g_i as above. Impose the following
finite families of relations, writing chi_i in {0,1}:

    [t_(chi,j),t_(eta,k)]=1;
    g_i t_(chi,j) g_i^(-1)=t_(chi,j)^((-1)^chi_i);
    g_i^2=product_(chi:chi_i=0) t_(chi,i);
    g_i g_j=(product_chi t_(chi,i)^(chi_j) t_(chi,j)^(-chi_i)) g_j g_i
        for i<j.

They hold in the affine model, by direct multiplication. Conversely,
the conjugation relations make the subgroup generated by the t's normal.
Modulo that subgroup, the g's commute and square to one. Negative
powers of g_i can also be removed using its square relation. Therefore
these relations collect every word to a translation monomial followed
by g_1^epsilon_1 ... g_n^epsilon_n, where each epsilon_i is zero or
one. If the affine image of such a normal form is one, its E-coordinate
forces all epsilon_i=0, and its independent translation coordinates
then force every translation exponent to be zero. Thus the presented
group maps isomorphically onto Gamma_n. This supplies the finite
presentation used in the separating quasiidentity.
