2026-09-10. This is the constructing agent's audit, not independent review.

The source was checked visually on supplied PDF page 187. It asks, for each
prime p, for a finitely generated residually finite p-group with nontrivial finite
centre, based on development of Golod's construction. It imposes no fixed
generator number and no prohibition on direct products. The construction
additionally gives an infinite group and satisfies the exact algebraic definition
of a Golod group in 9.76.

## Potential failure points checked

1. **Starting algebra really graded and nil, not merely graded-nil.** Every
   polynomial f_i, including inhomogeneous ones, is assigned a power; all its
   homogeneous components are imposed as relations. Hence the entire power
   vanishes and every element is nilpotent. The weighted relation count is
   summable, and the graded Golod–Shafarevich inequality gives infinite dimension.
   Ershov's Theorems 2.1, 2.4 and the countable-field proof in Section 2.2 were
   inspected in the primary survey https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.0490 .
2. **The annihilator union is proper.** Finite algebra generation is crucial.
   If both generators were in the union, a common bound would kill every long
   word. Thus the quotient is nonzero. This would fail without finite generation.
3. **The quotient has zero common right annihilator.** Test the two generators
   against a lift a. Membership of xa and ya in the union gives a common bound;
   then every sufficiently long word times a vanishes. This proves that a was
   already in the union, without appealing to a transfinite radical theorem.
4. **Residual finiteness survives.** The union is homogeneous. The quotient and
   triangular extension remain positively graded with finite-dimensional pieces.
   Degree truncations give explicit separating homomorphisms into finite p-groups.
   No arbitrary-quotient closure property of residual finiteness is used.
5. **Triangular extension is nil.** The displayed formula for (a,m)^k has second
   component a^(k-1)m, so one additional power beyond a's nilpotence index suffices.
   No matrix algebra over a nil algebra is being assumed nil.
6. **Centre computation.** Commuting with e kills the first coordinate.
   Commuting with the base algebra kills the second: its scalar part would make
   it invertible, while a zero scalar part is covered by the annihilator lemma.
7. **The centre of the finitely generated subgroup is trivial.** Its generators
   minus 1 generate the entire algebra. A group-central element therefore
   centralizes the algebra, not just an arbitrarily chosen smaller subalgebra.
8. **Infinitude of the subgroup.** The linear span of a finite group would be a
   finite-dimensional algebra, contradicting generation of the infinite algebra.
9. **Golod definition.** Adding the one-dimensional zero algebra realizes the
   final C_p factor inside a finitely generated non-nilpotent nil algebra. A
   presentation satisfying a positive Golod–Shafarevich deficiency is not asserted;
   the Notebook definition only requires the stated adjoint-algebra realization.

No defect located in this audit. The final direct-product step is elementary;
the substantive construction here is a centreless, residually p-finite Golod
group with the grading fully justified. Treat the resulting resolution as a
candidate until outside review and a broader novelty check.

## Comparison with the cited 2006 predecessor

Read the Russian original of V. A. Sereda and A. I. Sozutov, *Associative
Nil-Algebras and Golod Groups*, Algebra i Logika 45(2), 231–238 (2006),
English translation Algebra and Logic 45(2), 134–138,
https://doi.org/10.1007/s10469-006-0012-9 .
Reading copy obtained from the publisher's Math-Net archive:
https://m.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=al&option_lang=eng&paperid=144&what=fullt .

Theorem 1 states that every Golod group has a Golod quotient with trivial centre.
Its proof factors the underlying algebra by its Levitzki radical. The quoted
theorem does not establish residual finiteness of the resulting quotient or
homogeneity of that radical. The paper's later sections concern other residual
finiteness constructions, so the abstract alone was insufficient to settle this
point. Our proof neither assumes the quotient in Theorem 1 is residually finite
nor relies on Theorem 1.

The 2013 Timofeenko paper cited in 21.132 has now been inspected: its discussion
recalls known infinite and trivial centres, while its main construction concerns
infinite subgroups inside Golod groups. No finite-nontrivial-centre construction
was located. Its homogeneous-algebra realization is also satisfied by ours.
Primary source: https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dm1252 ; English translation DOI
10.1515/dma-2013-0034.

Remaining novelty work: look for prior homogeneous centreless constructions,
including known facts about grading of the Levitzki radical. If the requested
