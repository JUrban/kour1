# Candidate negative solution to Problem 21.121(a)

Developed 2026-09-10. Status: complete candidate argument; initial exact
computational checks and internal audit passed. Further novelty checks and
outside review remain outstanding.

We construct a countable 2-Jordan group Gamma whose 2-Jordan exponent is

    e_0 = log(24)/log(8) = 1 + log(3)/log(8),

but for which no constant J works at exponent e_0. Thus the infimum in
21.121(a) need not be attained. Part (b) is not resolved by this construction.

For a finite group H, write H_(2) for a Sylow 2-subgroup. The relevant index
is that of a normal abelian subgroup of odd order, exactly as in the Notebook.

## 1. A binary code with an elementary intersection bound

Fix k >= 1, put n=2^k-1, and index the n coordinates by the nonzero vectors
v in F_2^k. Define the binary linear code

    C_k = {(a dot v)_(v != 0) : a in F_2^k} <= F_2^n.

The defining map is injective, so C_k has dimension k. This is the binary
simplex code; no coding-theoretic theorem will be needed.

**Lemma 1.** Let L <= F_2^n be spanned by r nonzero vectors with pairwise
disjoint supports, and let c=dim(C_k intersect L). Then 2^c <= r+1.

**Proof.** Choose a basis w_1,...,w_c of C_k intersect L. It corresponds to
linearly independent vectors a_1,...,a_c in F_2^k. The surjective linear map

    v -> (a_1 dot v,...,a_c dot v)

shows that the coordinate columns of this basis include all 2^c-1 nonzero
vectors of F_2^c: every nonzero target has a nonzero preimage. On the other
hand, because each w_i belongs to L, its entries are constant on each of the r
disjoint supports, and zero outside their union. There are therefore at most r
different nonzero coordinate columns. Hence 2^c-1 <= r. This also covers c=0.

## 2. The finite groups

Let Q=Q_8, with central involution z. Let an element t of order 3 cyclically
permute the standard quaternion generators i,j,ij. This defines the group

    S = Q_8 semidirect C_3,

of order 24 (isomorphic to SL_2(3)). On Q/Z(Q), an F_2-vector space of dimension
two, t acts irreducibly and permutes its three nonzero vectors cyclically.

Identify Z(Q^n) with F_2^n by mapping a binary vector (epsilon_i) to the product
of the central involutions z_i with epsilon_i=1. Use this to regard C_k as a
central subgroup of S^n; the C_3 factors fix every z_i. Define

    G_k = S^n/C_k,
    P_k = Q^n/C_k,                 T_k = C_3^n.

Then G_k=P_k semidirect T_k, P_k is a normal Sylow 2-subgroup, and

    |G_k| = 24^n/2^k,             |P_k| = 8^n/2^k.             (2)

Write Z_0 for the image of Z(Q^n) in P_k. It is central, elementary abelian,
and fixed pointwise by T_k. The quotient

    V = P_k/Z_0 = direct-sum_(i=1)^n V_i

is the direct sum of n two-dimensional F_2 spaces. The i-th C_3 factor acts
faithfully on V_i and trivially on the other coordinates.

## 3. Uniform control of every subgroup of G_k

**Lemma 2.** For every H <= G_k there is a normal abelian odd-order subgroup
A_0 of H and an integer r >= 0 such that, writing u=|H_(2)|,

    [H:A_0] = u 3^r,             u >= 8^r/(r+1).              (3)

**Proof.** Put U=H intersect P_k. This is a normal Sylow 2-subgroup of H.
Choose a Sylow 3-subgroup A of H. Since only 2 and 3 divide |G_k|, orders give
H=UA and U intersect A=1. Its image in G_k/P_k is elementary abelian, so A is
elementary abelian of exponent 3. By the Sylow theorems A may be conjugated
into T_k, a Sylow 3-subgroup of G_k. The normal subgroup P_k is preserved
under this conjugation. Thus we may write H=U semidirect A with A <= T_k.

Set A_0=C_A(U). Since A is abelian and A_0 centralizes U, it is normal abelian
of odd order in H. Put |A/A_0|=3^r. The first equality in (3) follows.

Let W be the image of U in V. The kernel of A's action on W is exactly A_0.
One inclusion is immediate. For the other, if a in A fixes W, then for each
x in U we have x^a=x z_x with z_x in U intersect Z_0. Since a fixes Z_0
pointwise and a^3=1, iteration gives x=x^(a^3)=x z_x^3=x z_x; hence z_x=1.
Thus a centralizes U, as claimed.

Over F_2, the representation W of the elementary abelian 3-group A is
completely reducible. Its nontrivial irreducible constituents have dimension
two and correspond to nonzero linear characters

    alpha : A -> F_3,

with alpha and -alpha giving the same F_2 constituent. This description follows
by extending scalars to F_4, which contains the cube roots of unity. Each
operator is diagonalizable because X^3-1 has distinct roots there, and
commuting diagonalizable operators simultaneously diagonalize. Frobenius pairs
alpha with -alpha, giving the asserted two-dimensional summands over F_2.
The characters occurring in W span a space of dimension r,
because their common kernel is A_0.

Choose r linearly independent occurring characters alpha_1,...,alpha_r. For
each j choose a nonzero vector w_j in the corresponding F_2-isotypic summand
of W. Its support lies in the coordinate set I_j consisting of those V_i on
which A has character alpha_j or -alpha_j. These coordinate sets are disjoint.
Also choose a_j in A with alpha_j(a_j) nonzero, and a lift x_j in U of w_j.

In a single quaternion coordinate, if a vector v in Q/Z(Q) is nonzero and an
order-three automorphism acts nontrivially there, the commutator of lifts of v
and v^a is z: these are distinct nonzero vectors in a two-dimensional symplectic
space over F_2. It follows coordinate by coordinate that

    [x_j, x_j^(a_j)]

is the image modulo C_k of the binary support vector d_j of w_j. This calculation
is valid after the central quotient: choose arbitrary quaternion-coordinate
lifts of x_j; changing the lifts centrally does not change a commutator. All
nonzero entries on the support give z_i, and the other entries give 1.

The d_j are nonzero with pairwise disjoint supports, so their span L has
dimension r. Consequently [U,U], which is contained in U intersect Z_0,
contains an elementary abelian subgroup of dimension

    r - c,             c=dim(C_k intersect L).

Lemma 1 gives 2^c <= r+1. Furthermore dim_F2 W >= 2r, since the r selected
nontrivial constituents are distinct. Therefore

    |U| = |W| |U intersect Z_0|
         >= 2^(2r) 2^(r-c)
         = 8^r/2^c
         >= 8^r/(r+1),

which proves (3). For r=0 the same bound is simply |U|>=1.

## 4. Exponents greater than e_0 work uniformly

Fix any real e>e_0. Applying Lemma 2 to any H <= G_k gives

    [H:A_0]/|H_(2)|^e
      = 3^r/u^(e-1)
      <= (r+1)^(e-1) (3/8^(e-1))^r.                         (4)

Here e>e_0>1, so using the lower bound for u is legitimate. The base of the
last exponential is strictly less than one. Hence

    J(e) = sup_{r>=0, r an integer}
           (r+1)^(e-1) (3/8^(e-1))^r

is finite. It is independent of k and of the subgroup H. This proves a uniform
2-Jordan bound with exponent e for all subgroups of all G_k.

## 5. The limiting exponent fails

There is no nontrivial normal odd-order subgroup of G_k. Indeed, such a
subgroup N would commute with the normal 2-subgroup P_k, because [N,P_k] is
contained in N intersect P_k. Its image in T_k would then act trivially on V:
inner automorphisms from P_k act trivially on P_k/Z_0. The action of T_k on V
is faithful, so N is contained in P_k and therefore trivial.

Thus the only normal abelian odd-order subgroup of G_k is 1. Equation (2) gives

    |G_k|/|P_k|^e_0
      = (24^n/2^k)/(8^n/2^k)^e_0
      = 2^(k(e_0-1)),                                      (5)

which tends to infinity with k. No uniform constant J is possible at e_0.

## 6. One ambient group

Take the free product of the finite groups:

    Gamma = *_{k>=1} G_k.

Every finite subgroup of a free product is conjugate into one of its factors.
For completeness, use the usual free-product tree: vertices are the elements
of Gamma and the cosets gG_k, with an edge joining g to gG_k. Reduced-word
normal form makes this a tree. A finite subgroup fixes a vertex: the finite
subtree spanned by an orbit has a fixed centre, and the action preserves the
two vertex types, so an edge-centre cannot be inverted. Stabilizers of the
first vertex type are trivial and of the second type are conjugates of G_k.

Equation (4) therefore gives a 2-Jordan bound for Gamma at every e>e_0, while
the embedded factors G_k and (5) rule out e=e_0. Any bound with e<e_0 would
also be a bound with e_0, since Sylow subgroup orders are at least one. Hence

    e(Gamma)=e_0, and this infimum is not attained.

This is the requested negative answer to 21.121(a).

## Dependencies and boundaries

- Only the elementary Sylow theorems are needed for the subgroup decomposition.
  The required representation decomposition is explained using simultaneous
  diagonalization over F_4; no classification of finite groups is used.
- The finite-subgroup property of a free product is justified by its tree.
- The code intersection lemma is proved directly, including dimension-zero cases.
- No finite generation hypothesis on Gamma appears in 21.121. The construction
  is countable but is not claimed finitely generated.
- No assertion about the infimum for p other than 2, or about part (b), is made.
