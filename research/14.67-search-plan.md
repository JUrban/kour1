# Problem 14.67: overgroups of a largest centralizer

Status: exact search reduction; general problem unresolved. Begun
11 September 2026. No novelty claim or complete candidate is counted.

The question selects a nonidentity a in a finite group G with largest
centralizer order among all nonidentity elements. It asks whether every
nilpotent subgroup H normalized by C=C_G(a) lies in C. Equality is
allowed when choosing the largest centralizer.

## Exact reduction

For any finite group G and any a (without a maximality hypothesis), the
following are equivalent:

1. Every nilpotent subgroup normalized by C_G(a) centralizes a.
2. For every overgroup K with C_G(a) <= K <= G, a centralizes F(K),
   where F(K) is the Fitting subgroup of K.

Indeed, F(K) is nilpotent and normalized by C_G(a), proving one
direction. Conversely, for a proposed H set K=H C_G(a). Normalization
makes K a subgroup, with H normal in K. Its nilpotence gives H <= F(K).
This proves the converse. The finite-group Fitting theorem, that the
product of normal nilpotent subgroups is nilpotent, is the standard
structural input used by this reduction.

Thus it suffices to enumerate overgroups of a largest centralizer and
test their Fitting subgroups. Any failure gives the literal witness
H=F(K); a finite negative search has no implication beyond its range.

If Z(G) is nontrivial, every eligible a is central: a nonidentity central
element has centralizer order |G|, the maximum possible. The assertion
then holds immediately. In particular all groups of prime-power order
can be omitted by the nontrivial-centre theorem for finite p-groups.

There is also an elementary reduction to a of prime order. Choose a
nonidentity prime-order power b of an eligible a. The containment
C_G(a) <= C_G(b) and maximality of its order give equality of these
centralizers. The desired conclusions for a and b are therefore the
same. The initial code still checks every eligible conjugacy class,
retaining this redundancy as a useful control.

## Bounded experiment and independent controls

The initial range is every group of orders 2 through 511 in SmallGrp.
Prime-power orders are covered theoretically. Every other ID is either
logged as having nontrivial centre or explicitly tested. For each
centreless group, choose every conjugacy-class representative with
smallest nonidentity class size. The centralizer has the requisite
largest order by orbit-stabilizer. Enumerate both endpoints and all
intermediate overgroups, then test a against generators of each F(K).

The independent control enumerates actual subgroups and uses the
literal condition on every element of the directly computed
centralizer. Nilpotence is tested by the terminal lower central subgroup.
It compares both predicates on every nonidentity conjugacy-class
representative, including ineligible elements, so that positive and
negative cases are both exercised. Groups through order 32 and the
additional groups S4, A5, S5 and SL2(3) are included.

The main computation has a one-hour wall-clock bound and one GAP
workspace capped at 4 GiB. It is separate from the continuing 20.100
generator. Completion requires the actual subprocess exit code, a
complete coverage audit and passing independent controls. Scripts,
logs, source hashes and the exact command are retained locally.
