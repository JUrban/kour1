# Problem 4.56(b): cancellation gives isomorphic kernels

2026-09-11. A consequence of Bass's 1964 cancellation theorem, excluded
from the new-candidate count. Part (a) is not settled here.

The printed page 14 uses equality between the kernels. Under its
natural module-isomorphism interpretation, part (b) is affirmative.
Literal equality as submodules of the particular free module is false;
an elementary example is given at the end. The following proof treats
the substantive isomorphism assertion.

Let R be commutative Noetherian, let Lambda be a module-finite R-algebra,
and let d=dim(max(R))<infinity. Let T be the class printed in the
Notebook. Suppose U,V belong to T and have equal classes in its
Grothendieck group for short exact sequences. If

    phi: Lambda^n -> U,       psi: Lambda^n -> V

are epimorphisms and n>d, then ker(phi) and ker(psi) are isomorphic.

## 1. The defining kernels are projective

For U in T, choose a defining sequence

    0 -> P -> Lambda^m -> U -> 0,

where P localized at each maximal ideal of R is free of rank m over
the localized algebra. Since Lambda is finite over Noetherian R,
P is finite over R and finitely presented over Lambda.

Local freeness implies projectivity over Lambda. For completeness,
choose an epimorphism e:F->P with F finite free. Each localization
at a maximal ideal admits a splitting. Hom from the finitely presented
module P commutes with central localization. Clearing denominators in
a local splitting therefore gives an element s outside that maximal
ideal and a global map t:P->F with et=s id_P. The ideal generated by
all such s is contained in no maximal ideal, so a finite R-linear
combination of these s is 1. The same combination of the associated
t gives a global splitting of e. Thus P is finitely generated projective.

## 2. An Euler-class homomorphism

In the ordinary projective Grothendieck group K_0(Lambda), define

    epsilon(U)=[Lambda^m]-[P].

Schanuel's lemma shows this is independent of the chosen projective
presentation. It is additive on the exact sequences used to define
G(T). Indeed, in 0->U'->U->U''->0, lift the free cover of U'' to U
and combine it with the cover of U'. The kernel of the resulting free
cover of U is an extension of P'' by P'; it splits because P'' is
projective. Thus that kernel is P' direct-sum P'', proving additivity.
The same construction also proves that T is closed under extensions.
We obtain a well-defined homomorphism

    epsilon: G(T) -> K_0(Lambda).

Only this homomorphism is needed; injectivity, surjectivity and a full
description of G(T) are not asserted.

## 3. Arbitrary free covers have the requisite local rank

Put K=ker(phi). Compare the given cover Lambda^n->U with the defining
cover Lambda^m->U. Schanuel's lemma gives

    K direct-sum Lambda^m isomorphic to P direct-sum Lambda^n.

In particular K is finitely generated projective. At a maximal ideal
of R this becomes

    K_local direct-sum Lambda_local^m
      isomorphic to Lambda_local^(m+n).

The algebra Lambda_local is semilocal because it is finite over a
commutative local ring. Cancellation of free modules over semilocal
rings gives K_local isomorphic to Lambda_local^n. The same argument
applies to L=ker(psi).

Consequently

    epsilon(U)=n[Lambda]-[K],
    epsilon(V)=n[Lambda]-[L].

The assumed equality in G(T) implies [K]=[L] in K_0(Lambda).
By the group-completion definition of K_0, there is a finitely generated
projective Q such that K direct-sum Q is isomorphic to L direct-sum Q.

## 4. Bass cancellation

Bass's Theorem 9.3 applies to a module-finite algebra over a commutative
ring whose maximal spectrum is Noetherian of dimension d. It cancels
any finitely generated projective common summand when the remaining
module has a projective summand of local free rank greater than d.
Take that remaining module to be K itself. Its local free rank is n>d,
by Section 3. Hence K is isomorphic to L, as claimed.

The hypothesis concerns dim(max(R)), exactly as in the question,
rather than a substituted bound on dim(Spec(R)). Bass states his
theorem for right modules. For the left-module convention, apply it
to the opposite algebra, which has the same module-finiteness property.

## 5. Literal equality qualification

If the printed equality is required to mean equality of subsets,
take R=Lambda=Z, n=2>d=1 and U=V=Z/2Z. This module belongs to T by
the multiplication-by-2 presentation on Z. Let phi(x,y)=x mod 2 and
psi(x,y)=y mod 2. Then

    ker(phi)=2Z direct-sum Z,
    ker(psi)=Z direct-sum 2Z,

which are distinct submodules of Z^2 although isomorphic. This
formulation observation is also excluded from new priority.
