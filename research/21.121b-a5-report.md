# Exact exponent in a nonsoluble locally finite example

12 September 2026. Follow-up partial result for 21.121(b). The number
of complete candidates remains 46; no outside review or priority
certificate is claimed.

The [proof](21.121b-a5-proof.md) gives the restricted direct power of
A5 the exact attained 2-Jordan exponent log(60)/log(4), between two
and three, with constant one. It has no soluble subgroup of finite
index. The main point is control of every subgroup of every finite
power, including diagonal subgroups, rather than just the full powers.

For a subgroup H of A5^n, divide its coordinate projections into full
and proper ones. The full projections give a product of diagonal A5
factors; the proper projections give a soluble group B. A common
quotient would be both perfect and soluble, so H is their direct
product. The previous soluble bound applies to B with J=1. The full
powers then prove the matching lower exponent. This does not answer
the unrestricted upper-bound-three question.

## Mathematical review and controls

The same-agent review checked the simple-subdirect induction, its
trivial-coordinate cases, the common-quotient splitting, normality of
the chosen odd-order subgroup, and the uniformity of the exponent
bound. The proof of the small subgroup facts about A5 is included.
No gap was identified; outside scrutiny is still needed.

GAP enumerated nine conjugacy classes representing all 59 subgroups
of A5, and 113 classes representing all 8,381 subgroups of A5^2.
For each class representative it checked the decomposition, soluble
factor, actual Sylow order, and minimum normal abelian odd-order
index. These data and the bound are invariant under conjugation.

The independent program builds A5 as even permutations of five
letters and enumerates all 59 subgroups. It reconstructs the subgroups
of A5^2 from all pairs of projection subgroups, all normal kernels,
and all isomorphisms of their quotient tables. Isomorphisms are found
by enumerating generator images of the correct orders and verifying
the complete right-generator transition graph. There are 8,381
distinct Goursat data sets and distinct reconstructed subgroups;
87 quotient-table combinations are cached. Duplicate subgroups would
cause a failed assertion. No GAP subgroup list is imported.

To compute the minimum index independently, intersect each reconstructed
H with the product of the two odd-order radicals of its projections.
Those radicals are abelian. The intersection is therefore a normal
abelian odd-order subgroup, and every other normal odd-order subgroup
lies in it by projection. This proves that the computed index is the
minimum, rather than only a selected upper bound.

The native and independent histograms agree in all fields: subgroup
order, Sylow 2-order, minimum admissible index, number of simple
factors, soluble-factor order and its Sylow 2-order. The control is
exhaustive through two factors; the written induction supplies the
unbounded number of factors in the theorem.
