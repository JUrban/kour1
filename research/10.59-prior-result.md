# Problem 10.59: a prior solution in the residually finite case

Status: prior affirmative result for periodic residually finite p'-groups;
the unrestricted Notebook question remains unresolved here. No new
complete candidate is counted. Audited on 2026-09-11.

## Source and precise scope

Alfonso Di Bartolo, Kivanc Ersoy and Giovanni Falcone,
*A residually finite analogue of Kegel's theorem on splitting automorphisms*,
[arXiv:2604.01011v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01011v2),
11 June 2026; first submitted 1 April 2026. The arXiv record also supplies
DOI 10.1007/s00013-026-02278-3; the publisher version was not consulted.

Theorem 1.3 on PDF page 2 proves that a periodic residually finite p'-group
with a splitting automorphism of prime order p is nilpotent with class
bounded by p. The additional orbit-generated-subgroup hypothesis in the
Notebook is unnecessary in this restricted setting. The proof occupies
PDF pages 5--6 and uses the classical bounded-class theorem for finite
groups with a fixed-point-free automorphism of prime order.

The arXiv abstract **metadata** inspected on 11 September omits p' from
this theorem's hypothesis. The PDF abstract, HTML full text, theorem and
proof all retain it. The stronger metadata wording is not imported.

All mathematical text in the seven-page paper was read. The theorem and
main proof on pages 2, 5 and 6 were also checked visually, as was the
Notebook question on PDF page 43. The classical finite-group theorem and
the original construction of Tarski monsters remain imported results.

## Audited deduction

Let c(p) be the classical class bound. For each finite-index normal
subgroup M of G, intersect its p translates under the automorphism phi:

    N = intersection for 0<=i<p of phi^i(M).

Then N is normal, phi-invariant, of finite index, and contained in M.
The splitting identity descends to Q=G/N. Since every element of G has
finite order prime to p, the same holds in Q, so Q is a finite p'-group.
For a fixed point a of the induced automorphism, the splitting identity
gives a^p=1, hence a=1. If Q is nontrivial, the induced automorphism has
order exactly p; an identity automorphism could not be fixed-point-free.
Thus Q has class at most c(p). The trivial quotient causes no exception.

Consequently gamma_(c(p)+1)(G) is contained in every such N, hence every
M. Residual finiteness makes their intersection trivial. This proves the
class bound and the affirmative answer in this case.

## A direct check of the Tarski-monster exclusion

The paper also excludes a periodic Tarski monster as a counterexample.
Its proof invokes a theorem on power automorphisms. The following short
argument verifies that special step directly.

Let T be an infinite simple group whose proper nontrivial subgroups all
have one fixed prime order q. If each orbit-generated subgroup under phi
is nilpotent, it is proper in T and equals the cyclic subgroup of its
starting element. Hence phi preserves every cyclic subgroup.

Fix y!=1 and choose x outside <y>. Write phi(x)=x^a and phi(y)=y^b,
with a,b nonzero modulo q. Since phi also preserves <y*x*y^-1>, for some
nonzero c modulo q one has

    y^b*x^a*y^-b = y*x^c*y^-1.

It follows that y^(b-1) normalizes <x>. Its normalizer is proper, since
T is simple, and therefore is <x>. The distinct subgroups <x> and <y>
intersect trivially. Thus b=1 modulo q and phi(y)=y. As y was arbitrary,
phi is the identity, contrary to its required prime order.

This exclusion is a prior result, not an answer for groups that merely
have a Tarski-monster subgroup or section. The paper's Example 3.4 makes
that distinction explicit and does not satisfy the p'-hypothesis.

## Internal review

1. Residual finiteness is used for a common class bound, not to infer
   nilpotence from arbitrarily many nilpotent quotients of unbounded class.
2. Periodicity and absence of p-torsion ensure that finite quotients have
   order prime to p; absence of p-torsion alone would not ensure this.
3. The induced automorphism can lose order on a quotient. The fixed-point
   argument above handles the identity case rather than assuming exact
   order descends.
4. The invariant intersection remains finite-index without finite
   generation of G.
5. The Tarski argument proves preservation of each whole cyclic subgroup;
   nonzero powers generate because that subgroup has prime order.
6. The Tarski normalizer step uses simplicity and the precise subgroup
   structure, not an assertion about arbitrary periodic groups.
7. No assertion of a general solution to 10.59, or of new priority, is made.
