## Mathematical import and its elementary consequence

The classical Glauberman theorem is imported: if an r-group A acts
coprimely on G, its correspondence is a bijection from Irr_A(G) to
Irr(C_G(A)); the correspondent is the unique constituent of the
restriction with multiplicity prime to r. We do not give a new proof of
that theorem. All selected finite models check the multiplicity rule
and bijectivity directly from their certified character bases.

For completeness, the following is the elementary consequence of the
restriction formula, already credited above. Write

    chi_C = e psi + r Delta,        r does not divide e,

where psi is the correspondent and Delta is a character or zero. If psi
is linear, psi(c) is a root of unity for every c. A zero of chi_C would
give Delta(c)/psi(c) = -e/r. The left side is an algebraic integer; a
rational algebraic integer is an integer, contradicting r not dividing
e. Thus a linear correspondent always gives a nonvanishing restriction.

Consequently, in the prime-power action range searched here, the count
in the question is at least |C/C'|. Its excess is exactly the number of
nonlinear correspondents whose original character is nevertheless
nonvanishing on C. In this range the counting question and the pointwise
question are equivalent, using the imported correspondence. This is an
elementary consequence of the prior direction, not a new resolution.

## GAP algorithm limitation

The local GAP 4.16.1 documentation at `lib/ctblsolv.gd`, the `IrrConlon`
declaration, describes an irreducible-character algorithm for
supersolvable groups. We inspected its implementation in
`lib/ctblsolv.gi`; no vendor files were changed.

`scripts/probe_21_100_conlon.g` reproduces a concrete limitation in this
installation. On SmallGroup(729,268), standard `Irr` first gives 105
characters with sum of squared degrees 729. `IrrConlon` returns 153
entries, whose 105 distinct characters equal that basis. Calling Conlon
first also causes a subsequent `Irr` call on that same group object to
return the 153-entry list, with squared-degree sum 2025. We report the
observed cache-order effect, without asserting a general cause or a fix
in other GAP versions.

The exporter obtains the standard basis first, deduplicates Conlon's
output, checks equality with that basis, and checks norms and degrees.
More decisively, a separate Python verifier constructs genuine induced
characters from certified linear homomorphisms and proves that its
distinct irreducibles are complete. The screen scripts call `Irr`
directly and never call `IrrConlon`. No external bug report was sent.
