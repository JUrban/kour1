## Audit plan and outcome

The earlier review left one precise question open: is the Levitzki radical in
Sereda--Sozutov's centreless quotient homogeneous when the starting Golod algebra
is positively graded? The audit therefore checked the Notebook definition,
the actual quotient in the 2006 proof, the hypotheses of a published radical
homogeneity theorem, and the separating finite quotients. All four checks pass.
The relevant source scopes and retained files are listed below. No finite
experiment can prove this infinite construction, and none is substituted for
the argument.

## Exact scope

The supplied Notebook, printed p.187, asks for each prime p for a finitely
generated residually finite p-group with finite nontrivial centre, based on
Golod's construction. There is no condition forbidding a direct factor. We
produce an infinite group and also meet the algebraic Golod-group definition
in 9.76: the algebra is finitely generated, nil, and not nilpotent. A positive
deficiency condition on a presentation of the final group is not required.

## Classical inputs

1. The graded Golod construction gives a two-generated, infinite-dimensional
   nil algebra A over F_p, generated in degree one, with a positive grading
   A = direct-sum_{n>=1} A_n and finite-dimensional A_n. Section 1 of
   `21.132-proof.md` gives the homogeneous-relation construction explicitly,
   using the classical graded Golod--Shafarevich inequality. In particular,
   every element, including every inhomogeneous element, is nilpotent.
2. The Levitzki radical L(R) is the largest locally nilpotent ideal, and
   L(R/L(R))=0. These standard radical properties are imported. A nonzero
   nilpotent ideal is locally nilpotent and hence is contained in L(R).
3. For a ring graded by the additive positive integers, L(R) is homogeneous.
   This is an instance of the theorem attributed to Jespers--Krempa--Puczylowski
   (1982) in Hong--Kim--Madill--Nielsen--Ziembowski (2018), introduction and
   Proposition A. Positive integers form a commutative cancellative torsion-free
   semigroup. The source expressly allows rings without an identity. There is
   no assumption of an infinite coefficient field or of a strong grading.

The centreless quotient by L(A) is exactly the construction in
Sereda--Sozutov (2006), Theorem 1. The following details explain why its use
on this graded A supplies the extra residual finiteness required here.

## The quotient algebra

Put B=A/L(A). The ideal L(A) is proper: otherwise the algebra A, which has
two algebra generators, would itself be locally nilpotent and thus nilpotent.
That contradicts its infinite dimension and finite algebra generation.
Consequently B is nonzero. It is nil, generated by the two images b_1,b_2,
and has no nonzero nilpotent ideals because L(B)=0.

Homogeneity gives the positive grading

    B_n = A_n / (A_n intersect L(A)).

Each B_n has finite dimension. If B were finite-dimensional, only finitely
many B_n would be nonzero, so the positive grading would make B nilpotent.
That would make B a nonzero nilpotent ideal of itself, a contradiction.
Thus B is infinite-dimensional.

In fact the commuting centre Z(B) is zero. Suppose z is central, with z^m=0.
In the unitization B^1, the ideal I=B^1 z B^1 of B contains z. Since z is
central, I=B^1 z and I^m=B^1 z^m=0. Thus I=0 and z=0. Using the unitization
here also handles elements annihilated by B; no faithfulness assumption on
the regular representation is needed.

## The adjoint subgroup and its separating quotients

Let H=<1+b_1,1+b_2> inside 1+B. This is a group because every b in B is
nilpotent, so (1+b) has a finite geometric-series inverse. For sufficiently
large e, the characteristic-p identity (1+b)^(p^e)=1+b^(p^e)=1 shows that
every element of H has p-power order.

If 1+b belongs to Z(H), it commutes with both 1+b_i; hence b commutes with
both algebra generators b_i and therefore with all of B. Thus b=0, and
Z(H)=1. Moreover H is infinite. Otherwise its F_p-linear span in B^1 would
be a finite-dimensional subalgebra containing 1 and both b_i, hence containing
B, which is impossible.

For each N>=1, let B_{>=N}=direct-sum_{n>=N} B_n. This is a two-sided ideal,
and B/B_{>=N} is a finite-dimensional nilpotent F_p-algebra. The map

    H -> 1+(B/B_{>=N})

is a homomorphism into a finite group of order p^(dim(B/B_{>=N})). These
maps separate H: for 1+b different from 1, choose N larger than the largest
degree occurring in the finite homogeneous support of b. Its image is then
nonidentity. Thus H is residually a finite p-group. This proof uses the
grading of the quotient algebra, rather than an invalid general assertion
that arbitrary quotients preserve residual finiteness.

## Finite nontrivial centre and the Golod definition

Let E=F_p z be the one-dimensional zero-multiplication algebra and set
C=B direct-sum E, with componentwise multiplication. Then C is a nil algebra,
is not nilpotent, and is generated by (b_1,0),(b_2,0),(0,z). The subgroup of
1+C generated by one plus these three generators is exactly

    G = H x (1+E) = H x C_p.

It is infinite, at most three-generated, residually a finite p-group, and
Z(G)=C_p. Residual p-finiteness follows by using a separating quotient of H
and the finite second factor. Since C is a quotient of the free nonunital
algebra on these generators, G satisfies the exact definition in 9.76.
This proves every requested property for every prime p, including p=2.
