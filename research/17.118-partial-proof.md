# 17.118: class bounds and sharp small-prime answers

Status: partial result, 12 September 2026. The full question for arbitrary
finite p-groups and p >= 5 remains unresolved here. No new complete
Notebook candidate is added. Priority of these partial observations is
unconfirmed. Write “exponent p” for the identity x^p = 1, allowing the
trivial subgroup.

The original question asks whether a finite p-group G with a subgroup H
of exponent p and index p must have a characteristic subgroup of exponent
p whose index is bounded in terms of p alone.

We prove the following statements.

1. If H has nilpotency class at most c, the required index is at most
   p^(c+1). If G itself has class at most c, the index is at most p^c.
2. For p = 2 the optimal universal bound is 4. For p = 3 it is 27.
   The upper bound for p = 3 imports Laffey's 1976 root-count theorem.
3. For every prime p there is an explicit finite example in which H has
   class p-1 and every characteristic exponent-p subgroup has index at
   least p^p. The bound p^p is attained in that example. Thus statement 1
   is sharp when c = p-1, and any affirmative answer to the full question
   must allow at least p^p.

## 1. A deletion lemma for nilpotent groups

**Lemma.** Suppose G has class at most c and is generated by a set Z
together with x_1,...,x_r. Suppose that for every i the subgroup generated
by Z and all x_j with j != i has exponent p. If r > c, then G has
exponent p.

The important point is that deletion of a chosen generator need not
define an endomorphism of G. We construct a universal group on which it
does define an endomorphism.

Let F be the free nilpotent group of class at most c on formal symbols
for Z,x_1,...,x_r. Let R be the normal subgroup generated by w^p for all
words w that omit at least one distinguished symbol x_i. Put T = F/R.
The hypotheses give a surjection T -> G. On F let e_i fix every generator
except x_i, which it sends to 1. The maps e_i commute and are idempotent.
They preserve R: deletion cannot introduce a symbol absent from w.
Consequently they induce commuting idempotent endomorphisms of T.
Every element of e_i(T) is represented by a word omitting x_i, and thus
has p-th power 1. Hence, for every g in T,

    g^p belongs to D := intersection_i ker(e_i).

We claim D is contained in gamma_r(T), the r-th lower central subgroup.
For each k let L_k = gamma_k(T)/gamma_(k+1)(T), written additively. It is
an abelian group generated by the simple Lie brackets of weight k in
the chosen generators. The e_i induce commuting idempotent group
endomorphisms E_i of L_k. If k < r, each generating bracket omits some
x_i, so E_i fixes it. Therefore

    P = (1-E_1)...(1-E_r)

annihilates every generating bracket and is zero on L_k. If d lies in
D intersection gamma_k(T), its class v in L_k satisfies E_i(v)=0 for
every i, whence P(v)=v. It follows that v=0 and d lies in gamma_(k+1)(T).
Induction from k=1 proves D <= gamma_r(T). Since r > c, gamma_r(T)=1.
Thus g^p=1 for all g in T, proving the lemma. This argument uses the
ordinary lower central graded Lie ring over Z and requires no division
by p and no assumption c < p.

## 2. Characteristic intersections and class bounds

Take the family of all automorphic images of H and let K be its
intersection. This is a characteristic subgroup of exponent p. Each
member is normal of index p, so G/K is elementary abelian. Write
[G:K]=p^r. Choose r members H_1,...,H_r whose defining linear forms form
a basis of the dual of G/K. Their intersection is K. There are lifts
x_1,...,x_r of a coordinate basis such that x_j belongs to H_i for j != i.
Together with any generating set Z of K, they generate G.

If G has class at most c and r > c, the lemma gives exponent p for G;
then G itself is the required characteristic subgroup. Otherwise r <= c
and K has index at most p^c. This proves the bound under the class
assumption on G.

Now assume only that H has class at most c. Every H_i has the same
property. If r > c+1, each simple left-normed commutator of weight c+1
in Z,x_1,...,x_r omits some x_i. All its entries lie in H_i, so it is 1.
This implies that G has class at most c. For completeness, the generator
criterion follows by induction: if all commutators of weight c+1 vanish,
every simple commutator of weight c centralizes each generator, hence is
central. Quotient by the central subgroup they generate. In the quotient
all commutators of weight c vanish; induction gives class at most c-1.
The original group therefore has class at most c. The base c=1 says
that pairwise commuting generators generate an abelian group.

Applying the lemma now shows G has exponent p. Thus either G itself
works or r <= c+1 and K works, proving [G:K] <= p^(c+1). If c=0 then
H=1 and G has order p, so the conclusion also holds.

The parameter c is not shown to be bounded in terms of p. This is the
precise obstruction to using this argument as a full answer to 17.118.

## 3. Sharp universal bounds for p=2 and p=3

For p=2, H is elementary abelian, of class at most 1. Section 2 already
gives the upper bound 2^2=4. Here is also a direct argument. If H is
characteristic, it works. Otherwise choose a distinct automorphic image
H'. Both are abelian and G=HH', so H intersection H' lies in Z(G) and
[G:Z(G)] <= 4. If G is nonabelian, Z(G) lies in H: a central element
outside H would imply G=HZ(G) is abelian. Thus Z(G) has exponent 2 and
works. If G is abelian, its subgroup {g:g^2=1} is characteristic and
contains H, so has index at most 2. Sharpness follows from Section 4
(or from the dihedral group of order 8).

For p=3 we use the following exact prior theorem: if a finite 3-group G
does not have exponent 3, then

    |{g in G : g^3=1}| / |G| <= 7/9.

This is the theorem of T. J. Laffey, *The number of solutions of x^3=1
in a 3-group*, Math. Z. 149 (1976), 43-45,
[publisher source](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/BF01301629.pdf).
The full short proof has been read, but its classical imported theorems
are not independently reproved; see the source audit.

Assume G does not have exponent 3, and form K and the independent
H_1,...,H_r as in Section 2. Each H_i consists of roots of x^3=1. Their
images in G/K are independent coordinate hyperplanes. The fraction of
G in their union is exactly 1-(2/3)^r. If r >= 4 this is at least
65/81, exceeding 7/9=63/81, a contradiction. Thus r <= 3 and K is a
characteristic exponent-3 subgroup of index at most 27. If G has
exponent 3, choose G itself. Section 4 supplies sharpness.

More generally, a uniform positive gap delta(p) in the fraction of roots
of x^p=1 for finite p-groups not of exponent p would imply
(1-1/p)^r >= delta(p), hence a bound on r. No such general gap is proved
or assumed here for p >= 5.

## 4. An explicit sharp family for every prime

Fix a prime p and p letters x_0,...,x_(p-1). Let A be the associative
unital F_p-algebra with basis the empty word 1 and all words in distinct
letters, of lengths 1 through p. Multiplication is concatenation when
the sets of letters are disjoint, and zero otherwise. Associativity also
follows by viewing A as the free associative algebra modulo the monomial
ideal generated by words with a repeated letter.

Let J be the span of positive-length words. Then J^(p+1)=0 and

    dim J = D = sum_(k=1)^p p!/(p-k)!.

The set G=1+J is a group: multiplication is inherited from A and
(1+u)^(-1)=1-u+u^2-...+(-u)^p. It has order p^D. The degree filtration
satisfies [1+J^a,1+J^b] <= 1+J^(a+b), so G has class at most p.
The linear-part map is a surjective group homomorphism

    pi : G -> V = J/J^2 = F_p^p.

Put Omega = sum of all p! words of length p. These are distinct basis
vectors, so Omega != 0 even though p divides p!. If the linear part of
u is sum a_i x_i, then

    (1+u)^p = 1+u^p = 1+(product_i a_i) Omega.                (1)

Indeed the binomial identity holds because 1 and u commute. In a
product of p positive-length terms, any term of degree greater than 1
forces total degree above p and vanishes. Among products of linear
terms, precisely the permutations of all p letters survive, each with
coefficient product_i a_i. In particular G has exponent p^2, since (1)
is nontrivial for a_i=1 and every element in 1+J^p has order dividing p.

Let H_i = pi^(-1)({a_i=0}). This has index p and exponent p by (1).
It has class at most p-1: a commutator of weight p has possible
nonzero terms only in degree p, determined by the Lie bracket of its
linear parts. Those linear parts use at most p-1 letters; each length-p
word therefore has a repetition and is zero. In fact H_i has class
exactly p-1. The commutator of 1+x_j over the p-1 distinct letters
other than x_i has a nonzero leading Lie bracket in degree p-1. In
that bracket the word listing the letters in the chosen order has
coefficient 1; distinct letters prevent its cancellation. The same
argument with all p letters shows G has class exactly p. For p=2,
the assertion about H_i is simply that it is nontrivial abelian.

Set K=ker(pi)=1+J^2. It has exponent p and index p^p. It is
characteristic in the abstract group G, not just invariant under the
automorphisms visible from A. Namely,

    K = {g in G : (gh)^p=h^p for every h in G}.              (2)

If pi(g)=0, equality follows from (1). Conversely let pi(g)=a != 0.
Choose j with a_j != 0. Set b_j=0 and, for each k != j, choose
b_k != -a_k. Then product b_i=0 whereas product(a_i+b_i) != 0.
Choose h with pi(h)=b; equation (1) gives (gh)^p != h^p. This proves
(2), an intrinsic condition preserved by every automorphism.

To prove the lower bound, let C be any characteristic exponent-p
subgroup of G. Cycling the p letters induces an algebra automorphism
sigma and an automorphism of G, acting as a p-cycle on V. Thus
W=pi(C) is a sigma-invariant F_p-subspace. If W != 0, choose 0 != w
in W. Since (sigma-1)^p=0, the last nonzero vector in the sequence
w,(sigma-1)w,... is fixed by sigma. The fixed vectors of a p-cycle
on F_p^p are precisely the constant vectors. Hence W contains
(a,...,a) for some a != 0. A lift in C has nontrivial p-th power
1+a^p Omega by (1), contradicting the exponent of C.
Therefore W=0, so C <= K and [G:C] >= p^p. Since K itself is
characteristic, the minimum index is exactly p^p.

These examples have arbitrarily large p but do not give unbounded
indices for a fixed p. They are compatible with an affirmative answer
to the full question.

## 5. A smaller finite example for p=3

Inside the preceding 16-dimensional unital algebra for p=3, take
Q=<1+x_0,1+x_1,1+x_2>. The formulas and cycling automorphism restrict
to Q, and pi(Q)=F_3^3. Thus the proof of (2) and the invariant-subspace
argument apply unchanged: Q has a characteristic exponent-3 subgroup
of index 27, and no such subgroup of smaller index.

The saved exact algebra model contains 6,561 distinct elements of Q,
each with a word witness, and is closed under multiplication by the
three generators. Independent replay of these witnesses and every
generator edge certifies |Q|=3^8. The kernel of pi has order 243;
each coordinate hyperplane has preimage of order 2,187 and exponent 3.
Exactly 1,944 elements have nontrivial cubes. Thus the root fraction
is 19/27, below Laffey's extremal value 7/9.

A separate GAP p-quotient construction yields the same invariants
(class 3, exponent 9, derived subgroup order 243, third lower central
subgroup order 9). A faithful algebra matrix computation links the
two models. Neither a claim of minimal possible group order nor a
claim that the initial finitely presented group is finite is needed.

All finite calculations are controls on an explicit example. The
universal claims in Sections 1-4 are established by the arguments
printed above, with the single stated Laffey import for the upper
bound at p=3.
