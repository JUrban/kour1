## Why a sink-size-only bound will not suffice

The following elementary family rules out a bound on the order of the
nilpotent residual of the normal closure of a in terms of |E(a)| alone.
It does not rule out a bound that also depends on the linear degree.

Fix an odd prime p and let V=F_p^d. Let H be the signed permutation
group C_2^d semidirect S_d in its natural action on V, and put
G=V semidirect H. This is an affine linear group of degree d+1 over
F_p. Let a negate the first coordinate and fix the others.

In G/V, every commutator with a lies in the abelian sign subgroup,
which centralizes a. Thus every second commutator with a lies in V.
More precisely it lies in the first coordinate line: writing a first
commutator as v h with h in the sign subgroup, its commutator with a
has translation part in (a-1)V, and sign matrices preserve that line.
On that line the commutator map is multiplication by -2. Since p is
odd, this is a permutation of the p elements. Every point of the line
is therefore periodic and occurs in every eventual sink, while every
starting element reaches that line in at most two steps. Consequently

    E_G(a) = F_p e_1,              |E_G(a)| = p.

The normal closure of a projects onto the entire sign subgroup, by
permutation conjugacy. It contains the first coordinate line because
[V,a]=F_p e_1, and hence contains every coordinate line by normality.
Conversely V semidirect C_2^d is normal and contains a. Thus

    <a^G> = V semidirect C_2^d = (C_p semidirect C_2)^d.

The nilpotent residual of this group is V, of order p^d: its quotient
by V is abelian, and repeated commutation with the coordinate involution
preserves each coordinate line by the same nonzero multiplier -2.
The normal closure of E_G(a) is also V. Taking p=3 leaves sink size
three while both of these orders grow without bound.

This generalizes the already retained degree-three, order-72 control.
It is an algebraic obstruction, not a newly executed enumeration or a
counterexample to almost Engel product closure in linear groups. Its
linear degree grows with d. It changes the remaining quantitative route:
a successful uniform bound must use additional structure, such as degree.

## Source scopes and unresolved steps

The primary article *Finite groups with Engel sinks of bounded rank*
([Cambridge PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0D236B891C2CBEF721467DBC64CC8013/S0017089517000404a.pdf/finite_groups_with_engel_sinks_of_bounded_rank.pdf),
Glasgow Math. J. 60 (2018), 695--701) states its main bounds under a
condition on every element. The indexed introduction and relevant
statements were inspected in this follow-up; the complete proof was not
newly audited. Such a universal hypothesis cannot be replaced here by a
condition on selected generators or their conjugates without proof.

The alternative route through infinite locally finite linear sections
still needs an individual almost Engel automorphism theorem and the
appropriate centralizer-density statements. No such theorem was proved
or imported with verified sufficient hypotheses. The all-elements
argument in Shumyatsky's paper cannot be applied to the subgroup
generated by almost Engel elements before closure has been established.

These routes remain unresolved. The newly repaired handoff archive and
its exact hash observations do not change that mathematical status.
