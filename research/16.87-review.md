# Internal audit of the candidate answer to 16.87(a),(b)

Date: 2026-09-10. No outside reviewer has yet checked this construction.

## Mathematical checks

1. **Exact scope.** The supplied PDF, page 103, was inspected visually.
   It asks about every finite free rank, pointwise fixing by arbitrary
   endomorphisms, and a *nonperiodic variety* in (b). It does not require
   its relatively free groups to be torsion-free. The same wording also
   appears in the online twentieth edition. Both parts are answered by
   the same variety M_p.
2. **Nonabelian and nonperiodic.** UT_3(F_p) is a nonabelian member;
   Z is an infinite-order member. A variety defined by identities
   automatically has the required closure properties. We are not using
   a class defined by an existential restriction.
3. **Abelianization.** The epimorphisms between G_r/G_r' and Z^r are
   inverse because each fixes the named basis. Thus exponent sums are
   well-defined over Z, without hidden p-torsion in this quotient.
4. **Central element.** x_i^p is central because commutators are central
   and have exponent dividing p. This does not assert that G_r itself
   has finite exponent. Its p-th powers have infinite order when their
   exponent-sum vector is nonzero.
5. **All proposed test sets.** The rational nullspace argument applies
   to any k<r integer exponent vectors, including dependent vectors and
   zero vectors. Primitive normalization makes the homomorphism to Z
   onto. Arbitrary central/commutator components in the original elements
   are allowed.
6. **The map exists.** g -> g c^{f(g)} is a homomorphism directly from
   centrality, so no endomorphism lifting or unsupported presentation
   calculation is needed. It need not fix c itself.
7. **Exact fixing and noninvertibility.** f(S)=0 gives exact equality
   phi(s)=s. Meanwhile f(phi(G))=(1+p|w_i|)Z is proper in Z, so phi
   cannot be surjective. The independent determinant computation gives
   the same integer obstruction.
8. **Upper bound and rank one.** The r named generators are a test set.
   When r=1 the empty set fails using the (p+1)-power endomorphism of Z.

## Independent exact arithmetic

Command: `python3 scripts/verify_16_87.py`.
Result: `results/16.87-verification.json`, status PASS.

The checker uses integer exponent coordinates with central coordinates
modulo p, for p=2,3,5 and r=1,2,3,4,6,8. It includes every one-element
test-set candidate with both exponent coordinates in [-3,3] and every
central coordinate at r=2. Larger cases use the fixed random seed 1687.
The finite checks comprise:

- 1,156 target sets and independently computed integer determinants;
- 2,398 exact fixed-element checks;
- 3,468 generator-substitution comparisons and homomorphism checks;
- 720 associativity checks and 2,880 central-power checks.

The substitution evaluator actually multiplies the images of an element's
collected generator/commutator word. It does not use the proposed closed
formula to produce those images. Negative exponents, commutator torsion,
and nonzero central components are included. The all-ranks theorem still
rests on the proof, not these finite checks. GAP is unnecessary for it.

## Literature and novelty audit

No prior resolution was found in targeted English and Russian searches
for 16.87, test rank of nonperiodic varieties, finite commutator subgroups,
central powers, and virtually abelian groups. This is limited search
evidence and does not establish novelty.

The main nearby primary paper inspected was C. K. Gupta, V. A. Roman'kov,
E. I. Timoshenko, *Test ranks of free nilpotent groups*, Communications in
Algebra 33 (2005), 1627--1634,
[DOI 10.1081/AGB-200061040](https://doi.org/10.1081/AGB-200061040).
The author-uploaded manuscript's introduction and both theorem proofs
were read via its
[full-text page](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/266968985_TEST_RANKS_OF_FREE_NILPOTENT_GROUPS).
Its groups are free in the full nilpotent varieties, hence torsion-free;
their test ranks are one or two. It also treats F_r x Z^n. Those theorems
do not give the present variety, whose derived subgroup has exponent p.
The direct-product lower bound does use the related principle of an
endomorphism of an abelian factor fixing too few selected elements.

The abstract and introductory results of Gupta--Timoshenko's
[*The test rank of a soluble product of free Abelian groups*](https://m.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=sm&option_lang=eng&paperid=3852&wshow=paper)
(2008) were inspected. Their relatively free soluble products are built
from torsion-free abelian factors, with a different test-rank formula.
This paper has not been exhaustively audited here.

C. K. Gupta's 2013 survey *Certain combinatorial topics in group theory*,
International Journal of Group Theory 2(1), 91--107, was located and its
indexed test-rank statements screened. The attempted CiteSeer PDF fetch
returned 404; this is not recorded as a full-document review. Its
[indexed full-text page](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/268301997_Certain_combinatorial_topics_in_group_theory)
contains no match for 16.87 or “periodic.”

Remaining uncertainty is chiefly bibliographical: this elementary
central-endomorphism observation could have been published elsewhere,
or the proposer could have intended a torsion-free restriction absent
from the printed question. No evidence of such a qualification has been
found. Unlike the separate 12.69 formulation issue, the present example
does not contradict an accompanying claimed theorem. It is provisionally
counted as a complete candidate resolution of the problem as printed.
