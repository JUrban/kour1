# Report on the central-extension investigation for21.114

2026-09-12. **Full question unresolved.** The companion proof supplies an
order8192 weakly ab-maximal group of derived length3, with two independently
verified sets of input models. This proves that a universal bound, if one
exists, is at least3. No assertion of minimal order, nonisomorphism of the
two outputs, or new priority is made. The complete-candidate count remains43.

## Mathematical result

`21.114-central-products.md` proves a general theorem for a cyclic central
kernel of order p^k. The initially proposed extraspecial construction did
not suffice for the discovered examples: their useful central kernels have
order4. Replacing the extra factor by a Heisenberg group over Z/p^k Z
supplies the required extension. An isotropic-subgroup estimate works for
nonfree modules and for p=2.

The same note proves an obstruction to iteration. In these examples the
last nontrivial derived subgroup lies in the common centre. Every central
extension of the constructed group still has derived length at most3.
Thus the finite examples and central-cover construction do not yield the
unbounded family requested by a negative answer to21.114.

The pilot considers all catalogue groups in the five specified orders,
and tests weak ab-maximality when the nilpotency class is at least3.
It suffices to check subgroups containing the centre. GAP enumerates
conjugacy classes of subgroups in the central quotient and pulls them back;
conjugate subgroups have the same abelianization order. A failed row records
the order and abelianization order of a violating subgroup.

All177 positive cases are metabelian. There are13,209 tested subgroup

147.664 seconds (tool chunk1a975c). This screen depends on the GAP

Schur covers of the177 positive groups were then examined. The covers
of SmallGroup(128,854) and SmallGroup(128,860) have orders2048 and4096,
kernel invariants[2,2,4] and[2,4,4], and derived length3. The other175
computed covers are metabelian. The cover probe returned actual exit0,
empty stderr and its marker in6.637 seconds (5c95b3). Only one cover per
group was computed; this is not an enumeration of all central extensions.

In each exceptional cover, a central quotient preserves the nontrivial
second derived subgroup and leaves a cyclic kernel of order4. The resulting
group P has order512 and quotient Q of order128. The exporter returned
actual exit0 with clean stderr and its marker in5.485 seconds (42039c).
Both P groups themselves fail weak ab-maximality: their abelianizations
have order16, while an explicitly certified abelian subgroup has order64.
The central-product step is therefore essential for these inputs.

## Independent finite proof

The exported JSON files give generators as literal permutations, a Cayley
edge list, a central subgroup and a complete quotient map. The P models
have three permutation generators, of degrees128 and64 respectively.
Their second JSON catalogue-field entries are0, meaning unnamed models;
no SmallGroup ID0 is asserted. Q's labels retain their discovery names,
but no property of the final finite proof depends on those identifications.

The Python checker uses actual permutation composition to verify every
Cayley edge, reconstructs every element and multiplication table, then
checks all512^2 products for each quotient homomorphism. It proves that
the kernel is cyclic of order4 and central, and computes the derived
series with orders512,32,2,1 directly from commutators.

All subgroups of Q are enumerated by adjoining generators to previously
found subgroups. This is exhaustive because each subgroup has a finite
generating sequence. Skipping the remaining elements of a coset is valid
since adjoining x or sx to a subgroup S gives the same generated group.
The two quotients have380 and396 subgroups, all with abelianization order
at most16. Their full preimages also establish the exact maximum64 for
subgroup abelianizations in P: adjoining the central kernel does not
change a subgroup's derived group and can only increase its order.

The final independent run checks4,352 permutation edges,557,056 table
entries,524,288 homomorphism products and4,035,842 commutator pairs.
Five mutations of a permutation, Cayley edge, kernel, quotient map and
claimed order are rejected. Actual exit0, clean stderr and the marker
were recorded in1.605 seconds (fade7a).

A separate native GAP construction starts from the certified permutations,
builds the64-element Heisenberg factor by matrices over Z/4Z, and forms
the diagonal central quotient. It verifies order8192, abelianization
order256, derived length3, nilpotency class5 and the surviving512-element
embedded factor for both inputs. Actual exit0, empty stderr and the
marker were recorded in1.751 seconds (97d798). The proof of weak
ab-maximality of this larger group is the general theorem; its whole
subgroup lattice was not enumerated.

## Sources and review limits

The exact21.114 wording was read in the supplied Notebook. The main
source is Lisi--Sabatini,
[On groups with large verbal quotients, arXiv:2203.12021v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12021v4),
13March2024, published in J. Group Theory27(6) (2024),1203--1217,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/jgth-2023-0088). The retained author PDF is
191,323 bytes, SHA256
`dd2ab9d166257f03e6de26fe3cd7f65138721f0bcf16fabfbb59e49112c340a0`.
See `results/21.114-source-download.json` for retrieval metadata.

Read the introduction, Lemmas2.1--2.4, the proof of Theorem1.2,
Subsection3.1, and Sections4.1--4.2. Question A on author p.11 was
also viewed as an image. The original proofs of the imported Rose and
Keller--Yang theorems were not independently audited. The journal final
text was not obtained in this pass.

Two slips in the author version's example discussion are not used here:
the claim that every binomial coefficient binomial(p^a,i),0<i<p^a,
is divisible by p^a is too strong (binomial(9,3)=84); the desired
odd-prime power congruence does follow by the standard elementary
valuation argument. Also, the cyclic-complement identification in
Example3.8 needs an odd-prime restriction, since the Sylow2 subgroup
of Aut(C16) is C4 x C2. The odd-prime family suffices for the cited
unbounded-nilpotency-class background. Neither calculation enters our
central-product theorem or finite certificates.

The primary abstract and introductory statement of Eberhard--Sabatini's
[2025 probabilistic construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05821)
were checked. They concern strict ab-maximality and class-two extremal
examples, not an unbounded derived-length construction for21.114.
Its full probabilistic proof was not audited. Searches using both
ab-maximal and the older weakly-top terminology did not locate the
specific central-product consequence; absence of a search result is
not a novelty guarantee.
