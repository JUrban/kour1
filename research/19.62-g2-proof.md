# Problem 19.62 for G2 over every commutative ring

12 September 2026. Complete candidate for the G2 case of 19.62, with an
explicit finite arithmetic certificate. Other root types are not covered
by this packet, so the full-problem candidate count remains unchanged.

**Theorem.** Let A be an elementary carpet of type G2 over a commutative
ring with identity, and let B be its derived carpet. Then B is closed.

We prove the stronger intermediate statement

    B_r^2 B_-r is contained in B_r, for every root r,                 (1)

where the square denotes pointwise squares. This applies in arbitrary
characteristic and does not require either 2 or 3 to be invertible.
Closedness then follows from Nuzhin's 2023 Theorem 1, whose type
restriction concerns symplectic systems and does not exclude G2.
That published sufficient criterion, including its earlier Lie-ring
input, is an explicit imported dependency. The inclusion (1) is the
new certified calculation here. Priority remains unconfirmed.

## The finite reduction

For every nonopposite pair of roots r,s, use the Chevalley formula with
factors ordered by (i+j,i,j). Write c=|C_ij,rs|. A summand of B_k has
the form

    c x^i y^j,  x in A_r, y in A_s, k=i r+j s.                      (2)

Signs do not affect an additive subgroup. There are 156 ordered
nontrivial rules (r,s,k,i,j,c), with c equal to 1, 2 or 3. Their exact
values are verified as polynomial matrix identities over Z[t,u], not
inferred from a finite-field coincidence: see the constant check below.
The integral adjoint Chevalley model is the same previously checked
model used in the G2 finite experiments.

Let z be a finite sum of summands (2) at root k, and let w be a finite
sum at root -k. Expanding z^2 w shows that (1) follows from the following
two families, with all variables independent:

1. For rules (r,s,k,i,j,c) and (u,v,-k,h,l,d), certify

       c^2 d x^(2i) y^(2j) z^h w^l in B_k.

2. For any two rules at k and one at -k, certify twice the product
   of their three summands in B_k.

The second family includes equal rule types, because distinct summands
can use independent elements of the same two original root subgroups.
Finite sums, including negative summands, are allowed by additivity.
The diagonal and off-diagonal terms of a square are therefore all covered.

Rename independent variables by sorting the pairs (assigned root,
target exponent); equal pairs still denote separate variables. When
several targets become identical after this renaming, asking for their
coefficient gcd is sufficient for all of them. This produces 2,712
targets: 534 with four variables and 2,178 with six. Every root is
included explicitly, without an appeal to Weyl symmetry. A separate
checker rebuilds the targets using all ordered pairs for the second
family and compares the entire resulting coefficient dictionary.

## What an arithmetic certificate proves

Fix one target and formal commuting variables X_1,...,X_n, with X_d
assigned to A_(r_d). A node carries a root r, a monomial M in these
variables, and a positive integer coefficient a. Its assertion is

    a M belongs to A_r for every allowed choice of the variables.

An initial node is X_d with coefficient one at its assigned root.
The only noninitial operation is the following. Suppose two preceding
nodes certify a M in A_r and b N in A_s. A carpet rule yields

    c a^i b^j M^i N^j in B_(i r+j s), and hence in A_(i r+j s).       (3)

Here membership in B follows directly from its definition, and its
inclusion in A follows from A's carpet condition. If two applications
of (3) produce the same destination monomial with coefficients u and v,
their gcd times that monomial also belongs to B: choose integers h,j
with hu+jv=gcd(u,v) and use additivity. This is an integer Bezout
argument. No division inside the coefficient ring is used.

Each saved noninitial node supplies precisely two such applications,
and claims their exact coefficient gcd. The independent checker verifies
the source roots, destination root, exponent-vector equality, integer
products and gcd, and that all parent nodes have already been proved.
Its final node is noninitial and its coefficient divides the requested
target coefficient. Multiplying by the integer quotient proves the
target in B_k. Thus induction on the finite list proves every assertion
in each certificate over every commutative coefficient ring.

All 2,712 targets pass. The certificates contain 62,835 nodes in total,
with at most 70 in any one target proof. Four corruptions are rejected:
a changed claimed coefficient, a cyclic dependency, a wrong carpet
rule, and a missing final node. The verified expansion proves (1), and
the cited sufficient criterion completes the theorem.

## Constants, files and independent checks

The preparer locates the 156 constants using the frozen G2 matrices
modulo nine and verifies every scalar parameter pair there. This is a
search aid. A separate exact checker constructs both sides of each of
the 120 nonopposite ordered-root commutator identities as sparse
polynomial matrices over Z[t,u]. It tries the signs of the proposed
absolute constants and requires a unique exact equality. All identities
pass, covering 3,508 nonzero polynomial matrix entries and total degree
up to nine. Every possible root-string term is independently reconstructed
from the root coordinates and matched to the rule list.

The C++ search computes coefficient ideals among monomial consequences.
It is not part of the logical trusted calculation: the small Python
checker reads the saved explicit derivations and validates ordinary
integer operations. Search bounds cannot turn a failed target into a
proof. Variables remain distinct even when their root labels coincide.
The canonical commutator-factor convention is used throughout. Changes
of Chevalley basis signs have no effect on additive subgroups.

The source of the closedness implication is Ya. N. Nuzhin,
*On the Closedness of Carpets of Additive Subgroups Associated With a
Chevalley Group Over a Commutative Ring*, JSFU 16(6) (2023), 732–737,
Theorem 1. The full six-page text was read; the definition and derived
carpet discussion were also inspected as images. Its cited Lie-ring
invariance theorem is imported rather than independently reconstructed.
The later 2026 symplectic extension is not needed for this G2 theorem.
