## Original scope and terminology

The original question was freshly read visually in
`results/pdf-statement-review/21.121-statement.png`. Part (b) concerns
arbitrary p-Jordan groups. The present result covers finite soluble
subgroups, uniformly bounded indices of soluble subgroups, and the
separate normal-Sylow hypothesis. These are genuine restrictions.
Part (a)'s earlier nonattainment candidate remains a different result.

The primary source Fei Hu, *Jordan property for algebraic groups and
automorphism groups of projective varieties in arbitrary characteristic*,
[arXiv:1804.10946v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10946v2), 25 September
2018, published in Indiana Univ. Math. J. 69 (2020), 2493–2504, was
downloaded with HTTP 200 and retained with hashes. Its introduction and
Section 2.1, including Lemmas 2.1–2.2 and their proofs, were read from
the extracted text; the proofs of Theorems 1.3 and 1.7 and Remark 3.1
were also read. The entire paper and its algebraic-group inputs were
not independently audited, and no new visual page inspection of it is
claimed in this follow-up.

Definition 1.6 in v2 agrees with the Notebook. The weaker condition on
p'-subgroups alone is “generalized p-Jordan” in v2. In v1 these were
called “strongly p-Jordan” and “p-Jordan,” respectively. The v1
introduction and group-theoretic lemmas were also inspected via the
primary PDF's web extraction. An attempted v3 URL returned 404; the
arXiv record confirms v2 is the last version. No nonexistent version
is cited as evidence.

Hu's Lemma 2.2 uses Schur–Zassenhaus and cites Robinson, *A Course in
the Theory of Groups*, Theorem 9.1.2, for the complement theorem. That
standard theorem is imported here. The proofs provide the Frattini
kernel argument, the abelian coprime automorphism bound, and the
deduction of Hall p'-subgroup existence in finite soluble groups.

Remark 3.1 recalls the Chermak–Delgado characteristic abelian subgroup
bound and warns that the characteristic subgroup need not remain
prime to p if constructed in a mixed-order group. Our proof supplies
the measure argument explicitly and applies it **inside O_(p')(G)**.
This placement is essential, not a notational convenience. The
Fitting theorem is the other standard finite-group input; the needed
self-centralizer consequence is proved explicitly in the proof file.

## Bounded searches

Queries combined p-Jordan with soluble/solvable, normal Sylow, affine,
exponent two, Hall subgroups, and Fitting subgroups. Primary results
also led to Chen–Shramov's *Automorphisms of surfaces over fields of
positive characteristic*, Geom. Topol. 28 (2024), whose indexed
Theorem 1.4 restates the linear exponent-three theorem. Only that
indexed statement and the indexed discussion of exponents were
inspected; no theorem from the paper is used here.

No exact prior statement of the present general soluble-group
deduction was located in these bounded searches. That observation
does not establish priority. The affine family and the underlying
finite-group arguments are classical and may already be recorded
in equivalent form elsewhere.

## Same-agent proof review

1. The Frattini automorphism kernel acts freely on the finite set of
   lifts of a fixed generating basis, proving its order is a p-power.
   The abelian acting quotient is therefore faithful on the Frattini
   quotient. No false bound on all automorphisms of a p-group is used.
2. The product of finite field unit-group orders is at most p^d-1.
   Repeated constituents do not compromise the bound; their dimensions
   are included in the full module dimension.
3. In the first construction A is normal in the complement H, and
   C_A(P) is normalized by H and centralized by P. This proves
   normality in G, not merely in the complement.
4. Chermak–Delgado's least maximum-measure subgroup is characteristic
   and abelian. Applying it to the p'-radical preserves p'-order and
   gives characteristic, rather than just normal, invariance.
5. Quotienting a finite G by its p'-radical preserves the weak Jordan
   constant: the preimage of a p'-subgroup is itself a p'-subgroup.
   This assertion is not made for arbitrary quotient kernels.
6. The quotient has p-group Fitting subgroup; its self-centralizer
   property uses solubility. A5 has trivial Fitting subgroup and is
   an explicit negative control against deleting that hypothesis.
7. The Hall p'-subgroup acts faithfully on that Fitting subgroup.
   Its abelian normal subgroup has index at most the same J, giving
   the quotient's order bound without a derived-length factor.
8. The p-part of group order is unchanged on quotienting by a p'-group.
   The factors J, J^2 and |G_(p)|^2 therefore have exactly the claimed
   combined bound, with the trivial quotient treated separately.
9. For bounded soluble index, take a core before applying the
   characteristic conclusion. An abelian subgroup merely normal in
   the core would not have sufficed for normality in G.
10. The affine family over the algebraic closure of F_p is locally
    finite and has abelian p'-subgroups. Each complete finite affine
    subgroup has no nontrivial normal p'-subgroup, which proves the
    lower exponent bound. No finite experiments prove this limit.

and priority assessment are still required.
