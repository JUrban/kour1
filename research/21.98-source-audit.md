## The exact prior reduction

G. Cutolo and C. Nicotera, *Verbal sets and cyclic coverings*, J. Algebra
324 (2010), 1616-1624, DOI10.1016/j.jalgebra.2010.06.025.
The [author's six-page version](https://www.dma.unina.it/cutolo/papers/JAlg10.pdf)
was retrieved. All mathematical text was read, and page 2 was viewed.
Lemma 1.1 states finite generation, finite conjugation action and finite
torsion subgroup for the verbal subgroup of any word under finite cyclic
coverage. Its proof groups infinite-order values by commensurability and
uses Dietzmann's lemma and standard FC-group facts. Those classical
inputs are not independently reproved here. Theorems B and C concern
lower central words; they do not settle the arbitrary multilinear word.
The paper's nilpotent square-word example also shows why a finite
kernel must be allowed in a theorem for every word.

This corrects the earlier triage's missing FC reduction by identifying
its precise prior source. The remaining rank-one step is not supplied
by that lemma.

## Input to the polynomial path proof

S. A. Jennings, *The group ring of a class of infinite nilpotent groups*,
Canadian J. Math.7 (1955),169-187, DOI10.4153/CJM-1955-022-5,
[publisher PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/CBFFF3CF11D2D7BE0F0F77FBEE8D8DD1/S0008414X00030303a.pdf/the-group-ring-of-a-class-of-infinite-nilpotent-groups.pdf).
Theorem5.2 on page182 gives a faithful finite augmentation-power
quotient for a finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group. The
earlier14.26 audit read Sections4-5 and viewed page182; this theorem was
rechecked in the cached text. Its substantial proof remains imported.
Page171 also recalls that a finitely generated nilpotent group's torsion
elements form a finite subgroup and that the quotient is torsion-free.
The same reading scope is preserved in `research/14.26-sources.md`.

The proof in `21.98-nilpotent-case.md` uses finite-dimensional nilpotent
algebras and an elementary univariate polynomial argument. It does not
require general algebraic-group geometry. The assertion that a rational
polynomial vanishing at infinitely many integers is zero supplies the
step forcing all word-value logarithms onto a single line. No numerical
experiment is used as a substitute for that argument.
