# Problem 11.15: the simple word sequence already fails for odd p

Checked 11 September 2026. Bounded source audit, no resolution or new
candidate. The requested result is an explicit two-variable sequence
detecting abelian Sylow p-subgroups for odd p; existence is already part
of the problem statement.

## Brandl's example

Let q_1,q_2,... list the primes other than p and set
m_k=(q_1 ... q_k)^k. The tempting sequence is

    a_k(x,y) = [x^(m_k), y^(m_k)]^(m_k).

Brandl's 1981 paper already proves both of the following.

1. If a finite group satisfies even one of these identities, its Sylow
   p-subgroups are abelian (Lemma 2).
2. For odd p, G=SL(2,p^2) has abelian Sylow p-subgroups and fails every
   identity a_k=1 (the example on printed p.466).

For clarity, the second assertion is verified directly. In GF(p^2)
choose c with c^2=-4, and put

    x = [[1,1],[0,1]],   y = [[1,0],[c,1]].

With [x,y]=x^-1 y^-1 x y, direct multiplication gives

    [x,y] = [[c-3,c],[4,1-c]].

It has determinant 1 and trace -2. Its off-diagonal entry 4 is nonzero,
so it is a non-scalar Jordan matrix with eigenvalue -1. Its order is
2p. Both x and y have order p. Since p does not divide m_k, there
exist x_0 in <x> and y_0 in <y> such that x_0^(m_k)=x and
y_0^(m_k)=y. Thus a_k(x_0,y_0)=[x,y]^(m_k) is nonidentity. The inputs
x_0,y_0 can depend on k; fixing x,y as inputs for every k is unnecessary.
The upper unitriangular subgroup of SL(2,p^2), of order p^2, is an
elementary abelian Sylow p-subgroup.

For p=2, Brandl proves this very sequence works in all finite groups
(Theorem B, using Walter's theorem). For any p it works in the
p-soluble class (Theorem B*, using the p-length-one structure theorem).
Neither statement extends the sequence to arbitrary finite groups
when p is odd.

## Consequence for the search

A generic enumeration of finite groups and their identities does not
by itself supply the new kind of explicit sequence requested here.
Brandl's Theorem A already gives eventual identity sequences for all
finite varieties and preserves a bound on the number of variables
when the class is recognizable in that number of generators. His
proof of that general theorem is referred to a separate paper. This
audit gives neither a new formula nor a proof of impossibility for
other formulas. The specific simple commutator-power route is closed.
