# Almost Engel elements of characteristic-zero linear groups

This is partial coverage of Kourovka 20.89 / 19.83. The theorem below
covers every characteristic-zero field and does not assume finite
generation, solubility, or virtual solubility of the ambient group.
Positive characteristic remains unresolved here. Priority and outside
review are pending; this is not a new complete-candidate count.

Write [x,a]=x^-1 a^-1 x a and use left-normed iterated commutators.
An element a is almost Engel in G if there is one finite subset E of G
such that, for each x in G, [x,_n a] belongs to E for all sufficiently
large n. The entry time may depend on x and a.

**Theorem.** Let G be a subgroup of GL_n(F), where char(F)=0. Its almost
Engel elements form a characteristic subgroup L. There is a finite
subgroup K normal in G, contained in L, such that L/K is locally
nilpotent. In particular K is a common finite Engel sink for every
element of L, relative to the whole group G. One may choose K
characteristic in L and hence characteristic in G.

The proof imports the classical linear-group results and the precise
Shumyatsky statements listed in the source audit. It proves the lifting
and quantifier steps explicitly.

## 1. A local property established before taking products

Let S be the set of elements almost Engel relative to G, and initially
define L=<S>, without assuming S is a subgroup. Conjugation and every
automorphism of G preserve S, so L is characteristic in G.

The reduction in [20.89-residual-reduction.md](20.89-residual-reduction.md)
proves the following, over any field:

> Every subgroup generated by finitely many elements of S is
> finite-by-nilpotent.

Here finite-by-nilpotent means having a finite normal subgroup with
nilpotent quotient. This result uses residual finiteness of finitely
generated linear groups, Gruenberg's Engel-radical theorem, and
Shumyatsky's Theorem 3.3; it does not assume closure of S.

Consequently **every finitely generated subgroup of L is
finite-by-nilpotent**. Indeed, express each of its finitely many
generators as a finite word in S and its inverses, and collect the
finitely many S-elements used. The given subgroup lies in the resulting
finite-by-nilpotent group. If H lies in M and D is finite normal in M
with M/D nilpotent, then H/(H intersect D) embeds in M/D. This proves
the necessary inheritance by subgroups.

We call the displayed local property (P) for the rest of this proof.
It is inherited by subgroups and homomorphic images. In particular L
contains no nonabelian free subgroup: a free group of rank two is
torsion-free, so finite-by-nilpotence would make it nilpotent, which
it is not.

## 2. Two abstract finite-kernel lemmas

**Lemma 1 (invariant finite kernel).** If X has a finite normal
subgroup D with X/D locally nilpotent, it has such a subgroup
characteristic in X.

Choose D of least order. For any automorphism sigma, the group
X/(D intersect sigma(D)) embeds in the product X/D times X/sigma(D).
A finitely generated subgroup of this product lies in a product of
two finitely generated nilpotent groups and is therefore nilpotent.
Thus the product, and its subgroups, are locally nilpotent. Minimality
gives D=D intersect sigma(D), and equality of orders then gives
D=sigma(D). This proves the lemma.

**Lemma 2 (torsion-free lift).** Suppose X has property (P), N is a
torsion-free normal subgroup of X, and X/N is finite-by-(locally
nilpotent). Then X is finite-by-(locally nilpotent). Moreover, if a
group of automorphisms of X preserves N, a finite kernel can be chosen
invariant under that group of automorphisms.

Choose, by Lemma 1, a finite characteristic subgroup D of X/N with
(X/N)/D locally nilpotent. Let R be its preimage in X. Thus R/N is
finite, X/R is locally nilpotent, and R is invariant under every
automorphism under consideration.

In any group Y with property (P), the torsion elements form a locally
finite characteristic subgroup T(Y). To see this, take finitely many
torsion elements y_1,...,y_r and put H=<y_1,...,y_r>. A finite normal
subgroup E of H has nilpotent quotient H/E. The latter is finitely
generated by torsion elements, hence is finite: in a nilpotent group
the torsion elements form a subgroup, and a finitely generated torsion
nilpotent group is finite. Thus H is finite. This proves closure under
products and inverses as well as local finiteness and characteristic
invariance of T(Y).

Apply this observation to R and put K=T(R). Since N is torsion-free,
K intersect N=1. The homomorphism K -> R/N is therefore injective,
so K is finite. It is characteristic in R, and is consequently
invariant under the automorphisms under consideration.

We claim X/K is locally nilpotent. Take any finitely generated H<=X.
Property (P) supplies a finite normal E of H with H/E nilpotent; hence
gamma_i(H)<=E for some i. Also the image of H in X/R is a finitely
generated locally nilpotent group, so gamma_j(H)<=R for some j.
For m=max(i,j), gamma_m(H) is a finite subgroup of R and hence is
contained in T(R)=K. Thus H/(H intersect K) is nilpotent. Every
finitely generated subgroup of X/K is an image of some such H, proving
the claim and the lemma. No assertion that T(X) itself is finite is
used or implied.

## 3. The characteristic-zero algebraic quotient

Extend F to an algebraic closure k, retaining the given faithful
representation. By Tits's Theorem 1, the absence of a nonabelian free
subgroup makes L virtually soluble. This version of the theorem
assumes characteristic zero and places no finite-generation
restriction on L.

Let A be the Zariski closure of L in GL_n(k). Its identity component
A^0 is soluble. For completeness, take a soluble finite-index
subgroup B of L. Its Zariski closure is soluble, and finitely many
translates of that closure cover A. Hence its index in A is finite
and it contains A^0. These are the standard closure and component
properties of linear algebraic groups.

Let U be the unipotent radical of A^0. The structural theorem for
connected soluble algebraic groups says that U is characteristic
in A^0, consists of unipotent matrices, and A^0/U is a torus.
Because L is normal in G, conjugation by G preserves A, A^0 and U.
Set N=L intersect U. Thus N is normal in G.

In characteristic zero, a nonidentity unipotent matrix has infinite
order. For example, the finite polynomial logarithm identifies a
unipotent matrix u with a nilpotent matrix log(u), and
log(u^r)=r log(u) for every positive integer r. Thus u^r=1 implies
log(u)=0 and u=1. Consequently N is torsion-free.

The algebraic quotient A/U is a linear algebraic group, with the
abelian group A^0/U of finite index. The image of L in this quotient
has kernel N. We obtain a **linear virtually abelian group L/N**.
Only this quotient, rather than the original ambient group G, is
being asserted virtually abelian at this point.

## 4. Almost Engel closure in the quotient and lifting

Each image of an element of S is almost Engel relative to L/N:
first restrict its ambient sink to L, and then apply the quotient map.
These images generate L/N.

In every virtually abelian group the almost Engel elements form a
subgroup. This is the independently proved consequence of
Shumyatsky's Lemma 3.2 in
[20.89-metabelian-proof.md](20.89-metabelian-proof.md), Sections 2--3.
Specifically that lemma makes the normal closure of each almost Engel
element finite-by-(locally nilpotent); Lemma 1 makes its finite kernel
normal in the whole group, and the Hirsch--Plotkin radical theorem
then proves product and inverse closure.

It follows that **all elements of L/N are almost Engel in L/N**.
We may now apply Shumyatsky's Theorem 4.2: an almost Engel linear
group is finite-by-hypercentral. Hypercentral groups are locally
nilpotent, so L/N satisfies the quotient hypothesis of Lemma 2.
Applying that lemma to X=L, with the automorphisms induced by G,
gives a finite subgroup K normal in G such that L/K is locally
nilpotent. The order of these applications matters: Theorem 4.2
was not applied to L before closure had been proved.

Finally let l belong to L and x belong to G. The commutator [x,l]
lies in L because L is normal in G. In L/K the two elements
[x,l]K and lK generate a nilpotent subgroup. Hence sufficiently
long repeated commutation of the former with the latter is trivial.
Equivalently [x,_n l] lies in K for every sufficiently large n.
Thus K is one finite sink for l relative to all of G. This proves
L<=S, and the reverse inclusion was its definition. Therefore S=L.

Apply Lemma 1 again to L to choose a finite characteristic kernel
with locally nilpotent quotient. Since L is characteristic in G,
this kernel is characteristic in G and the same commutator argument
makes it a common sink. This proves the theorem.

## 5. Scope and failure of the characteristic-p shortcut

Two uses of characteristic zero have been explicit: the unrestricted
Tits alternative, and torsion-freeness of unipotent matrices. In
positive characteristic an infinite unipotent subgroup may be an
infinite torsion group, so T(R) need not inject into finite R/N.
The full Notebook question does not follow by changing the field in
this proof.

The theorem gives a finite common sink, not a uniform entry time.
Nor does it assert that all torsion elements of L form a finite
subgroup; an infinite central torsion subgroup is compatible with
the conclusion. The common kernel need not equal an individual
minimal sink, and individual minimal sinks need not be normal.
