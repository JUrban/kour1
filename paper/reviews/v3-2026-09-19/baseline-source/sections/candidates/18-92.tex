\subsection{Complete lattices of formations: Problem 18.92}
\label{cand:18.92}

The question defines a complete lattice of formations as
an intersection-closed nonempty family with a largest
member. Joins are internal to that family. Under this
definition both requested examples follow from prime sets.

Let \(\mathcal P\) be the set of primes and let
\(\mathcal F_S\) be the finite groups whose order has
all prime divisors in \(S\subseteq\mathcal P\).
This is a formation: quotients and finite subdirect
products introduce no new prime divisor. Moreover
\[
 \mathcal F_S\subseteq\mathcal F_T\iff S\subseteq T,\qquad
 \bigcap_i\mathcal F_{S_i}=\mathcal F_{\cap_iS_i}.
\]
The cyclic group of order \(p\) witnesses the reverse
implication in the first assertion.

For part~(a), take
\(\Theta=\{\mathcal F_S:S\text{ finite}\}\cup
\{\mathcal F_{\mathcal P}\}\).
Its joins are union of prime sets when that union is
finite and the top otherwise. Every nonbottom element
fails to be compact. Choose a prime \(q\) in its set
and enumerate \(\mathcal P\setminus\{q\}\).
The formations for successive finite initial segments
have join the top in \(\Theta\), whereas no finite
subjoin contains the cyclic group of order \(q\).
The bottom is therefore the only compact element,
and \(\Theta\) is not algebraic.

For part~(b), use the five formations with prime sets
\[
 \varnothing,\quad\{2\},\quad\{2,3\},\quad\{5\},
 \quad\mathcal P.
\]
They are closed under intersections, and their order is
the pentagon \(N_5\). Explicitly, writing
\(A=\mathcal F_{\{2\}},B=\mathcal F_{\{2,3\}},
C=\mathcal F_{\{5\}}\), we have \(A<B\) but
\[
 A\vee(C\wedge B)=A,\qquad (A\vee C)\wedge B=B.
\]
Thus the lattice is not modular.

Both examples contain the formation of all finite groups
as top, so the empty-intersection convention causes no
problem. These statements concern the chosen families;
they do not assert either property for the full lattice
of all formations. Source:
