\subsection{11.115: equal commutator subgroups from a proper power}
\label{cand:11.115}
\problemstatement{11.115}
Let \(X=F(a,b)\), fix \(m\ge2\), and put
\[
 N=\langle\!\langle a^m\rangle\!\rangle_X,\qquad
 T=\ker(X\longrightarrow C_m),\quad a\mapsto1,\ b\mapsto0.
\]
Then \(1<N<T<X\): \(a^m\) is nontrivial, \(a\notin T\), and
\(b\in T\setminus N\), as the exponent-sum map to \(\Z\) in \(b\)
shows. In particular \(N\) is not maximal in \(X\).
Nevertheless
\[
 [T,N]=[X,N].
\]
To prove this, set \(K=[T,N]\). Both \(T,N\) are normal in \(X\),
so \(K\trianglelefteq X\). In \(X/K\), the image of \(a^m\) commutes
with \(b\in T\), and it already commutes with \(a\).
It is therefore central. Its normal closure, the image of \(N\),
is central too, giving \([X,N]\le K\). The reverse inclusion is immediate.
This disproves the proposed strict containment.

More explicitly,
\[
 K=\langle\!\langle[a^m,b]\rangle\!\rangle_X,\qquad
 X/K=\langle a,b\mid[a^m,b]=1\rangle.
\]
This quotient is \(F(s_0,\ldots,s_{m-1})\rtimes\Z\), where a generator
of \(\Z\) cyclically permutes the \(s_i\)'s:
send \(a\) to that generator and \(b\) to \(s_0\).
The inverse map uses \(s_i=a^iba^{-i}\); the final cyclic step is
just \([a^m,b]=1\).
Thus \(N/K\cong\Z\) is central and \(X/N\cong C_m*\Z\).
The common subgroup \(K\) is nontrivial because \([a^m,b]\) is
a nonempty reduced word, and \(K<N\) by exponent sum in \(a\).
For the optional stronger condition that \(T\) itself not be maximal,
take \(m=4\) and insert the kernel of the map to \(C_2\).

The supplied question imposes no torsion-free quotient or
primitive-element assumption. Those extra conditions would exclude
this example. The short proof is self-contained; its intended scope
and historical priority still warrant outside scrutiny.
