\subsection{Order dimension of Chernikov subgroup lattices: Problem 11.116}
\label{cand:11.116}
\problemstatement{11.116}

Suppose \(D\trianglelefteq G\), \([G:D]=m<\infty\), and
\[
 D=C_{p_1^\infty}\times\cdots\times C_{p_r^\infty},
\]
with distinct primes \(p_i\) and \(r\geq1\).
Then the order dimension of the entire subgroup lattice of
\(G\) is at most \(2rm\).

Here is a coordinate proof. Write \(C_{p^\infty}\)
additively as \(\Z[1/p]/\Z\). Its unique terminating
base-\(p\) expansion
\(x=\sum_{i\geq1}d_i p^{-i}\), \(0\leq d_i<p\),
defines a bijection to \(\mathbb N_0\) by
\[
 b_p(x)=\sum_{i\geq1}d_i p^{i-1}.
\]
A coset of the subgroup of order \(p^n\) has all
digits beyond \(n\) fixed. Its image is an integer
interval \([ap^n,(a+1)p^n-1]\); the whole group maps
to \(\mathbb N_0\). Inclusion is therefore determined
by two endpoint inequalities, left endpoints ordered
in reverse and right endpoints in the usual order.

Every subgroup of \(D\) is the product of its primary
components: Chinese remaindering recovers each component
as an integer multiple of an element. Thus the product
of these bijections sends every coset of a subgroup of
\(D\) to a nonempty box. Its inclusion order is determined
by \(2r\) endpoint coordinates. Include the empty set
by giving it left endpoints \(+\infty\) and right
endpoints \(-\infty\).

Fix representatives \(t_q\) of the \(m\) cosets of \(D\).
For \(H\leq G\), its fiber
\[
 S_q(H)=\{d\in D:dt_q\in H\}
\]
is empty or a coset of \(H\cap D\). If \(d_0\) lies
in the fiber, multiplying \(dt_q\) by
\((d_0t_q)^{-1}\) proves that the fiber is
\((H\cap D)d_0\). The fibers partition \(H\), and
\(H\leq K\) exactly when every fiber of \(H\) is
contained in the corresponding fiber of \(K\).
This embeds the subgroup lattice into a product of
\(2rm\) chains.

For clarity, a poset embedded in \(d\) chains has order
dimension at most \(d\): use the full coordinate
lexicographic order to break ties, and for each
coordinate order first by that coordinate. All \(d\)
orders extend the poset, and any failure of comparison
is detected in at least one coordinate. Their
intersection is exactly the original order.

A Chernikov group has such a finite-index divisible
subgroup. The question's exclusion of two quasicyclic
factors for the same prime ensures the required distinct
primes. Finite groups have finite subgroup lattices
and cause no exception. The extension need not split.
