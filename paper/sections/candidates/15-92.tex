\subsection{15.92: continuum many alternating-residual triangle quotients}
\label{cand:15.92}
\problemstatement{15.92}
For every \(r>6\), the triangle group
\[
 \Gamma_r=\langle x,y\mid x^2=y^3=(xy)^r=1\rangle
\]
has continuum many pairwise nonisomorphic infinite quotients in which
every nonidentity element survives an epimorphism onto a finite
alternating group. The proof combines Conder's finite permutation
constructions with the following separating-kernel argument.

\begin{lemma}
Suppose a finitely generated group \(\Gamma\) has epimorphisms
\(\theta_i:\Gamma\to A_{n_i}\), with \(n_i\to\infty\), and one
element \(v\) whose image is always nontrivial and moves at most
a fixed number \(S\) of points. Then \(\Gamma\) has continuum many
pairwise nonisomorphic infinite alternating-residual quotients.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By diagonal subsequence selection, arrange that each element of the
countable group \(\Gamma\) is eventually always trivial or eventually
always nontrivial under \(\theta_i\).
The eventually trivial elements form a normal subgroup \(R\);
let \(G=\Gamma/R\), with \(1\ne\bar v\in G\).
The action on a nonprincipal ultraproduct of the underlying finite
sets is faithful: a word eventually nontrivial has a sequence of
moved points. The support of \(\bar v\) has at most \(S\) elements,
since \(S+1\) distinct moved ultraproduct points would contradict the
coordinate support bound on an ultrafilter-large set.

Let \(N=\langle\bar v^G\rangle\). On the union \(\Omega_*\) of all
translates of \(\operatorname{supp}(\bar v)\), the group \(N\) acts
faithfully by finitary permutations. There are at most \(S\) \(G\)-orbits.
Because \(N\trianglelefteq G\), its orbits within each \(G\)-orbit have
equal size. Thus a finite \(M\) bounds all finite \(N\)-orbit sizes.
Let \(\eta\) be the action of \(N\) on the union of its finite orbits,
and put \(L=\ker\eta\). The exponent of \(\eta(N)\) divides \(M!\).

Suppose \(\phi:G\to A_m\) is onto, \(m\ge5\), and
\(\phi(\bar v)\ne1\). Then \(\phi(N)=A_m\).
Set \(H=\ker(\phi|_N)\), a finite-index normal subgroup of \(N\).
Every \(H\)-orbit inside an infinite \(N\)-orbit is infinite, since
the latter is a finite union of translates of the former.
For \(x\in L\) and \(y\in N\), there is \(h\in H\) moving
\(\operatorname{supp}(x)\) disjoint from \(\operatorname{supp}(y)\).
Indeed \(H\) is locally finite as a finitary permutation group.
For finite sets \(A=\operatorname{supp}(x)\), \(B=\operatorname{supp}(y)\),
choose finitely many elements of \(H\) so that the finite subgroup
they generate has every orbit of \(a\in A\) larger than \(|A||B|\).
A uniform element of this finite subgroup avoids all equalities
\(h(a)=b\) by the union bound. Empty supports are immediate.
Thus \(hxh^{-1}\) commutes with \(y\), and \(\phi(h)=1\) gives
\(\phi(x)\in Z(\phi(N))=1\).
Therefore \(A_m\) is a quotient of \(\eta(N)\), of exponent dividing
\(M!\). For \(m\) at least an odd prime greater than \(M\), this is
impossible because \(A_m\) contains a cycle of that prime length.
All sufficiently large alternating quotients of \(G\) consequently
kill \(\bar v\).

For sufficiently large \(i\), \(\theta_i\) cannot factor through \(G\).
Hence \(\theta_i(R)\ne1\), and normality and simplicity give
\(\theta_i(R)=A_{n_i}\).
Inductively select indices \(i_j\) and elements \(r_j\in R\) so that
\[
 \theta_{i_k}(r_j)\ne1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k=j.
\]
At step \(j\), choose \(i_j\) beyond the cutoffs for all preceding
\(r_k\), with
\(|A_{n_{i_j}}|>\prod_{k<j}|A_{n_{i_k}}|\).
The subgroup
\(L_j=R\cap\bigcap_{k<j}\ker\theta_{i_k}\) has index in \(R\)
at most this product, so it cannot be killed by the surjection
\(\theta_{i_j}|_R\). Choose \(r_j\in L_j\) with nontrivial image.
Since \(r_j\in R\), all sufficiently late maps kill it, supplying
the next cutoff.

For each infinite \(I\subseteq\mathbb N\), put
\(K_I=\bigcap_{j\in I}\ker\theta_{i_j}\).
The witnesses give \(r_j\notin K_I\) exactly when \(j\in I\);
hence there are continuum many different kernels.
Each quotient embeds in the corresponding product of alternating
groups with onto coordinates, and is infinite because their orders
are unbounded. For each fixed countable quotient isomorphism type,
there are only countably many epimorphisms from a finitely generated
\(\Gamma\), as images of a finite generating tuple determine a map.
The continuum many kernels therefore give continuum many types.
\end{proof}

We specify the finite input, which is stronger than mere existence
of alternating quotients.
Write \(r=h+6d\), \(h\in\{7,8,9,10,11,12\}\), \(d\ge0\), and choose
\[
 p=\begin{cases}11&h=7,8,11,\\13&h=9,10,12.\end{cases}
\]
Conder's thesis \cite[Chapter~5, pp.~117--120]{conder-thesis}
constructs arbitrarily large actions of the extended triangle group
with generators \(x_n,y_n,t_n\) satisfying
\[
 x_n^2=y_n^3=(x_ny_n)^r=t_n^2=(x_nt_n)^2=(y_nt_n)^2=1.
\]
The parity choice makes \(\langle x_n,y_n\rangle=A_n\), including
when \(r\) is even. In these same actions \(w_n=x_ny_nt_n\) has
one \(p\)-cycle, no other cycle length divisible by \(p\), and all
cycle lengths divide \(720720=2^4\,3^2\,5\,7\,11\,13\).
The displayed choice of \(11\) or \(13\) is the length of the unique
exceptional prime cycle in the respective diagram; it is not a free
choice. These cycle and parity facts are read from the construction,
not inferred from its headline generation theorem.
The finite transcription, diagram reconstruction and 150 cycle controls
are indexed in \artifact{results/15.92-summary.json} in the public
snapshot (\reproductionlocation{}); the external follow-up
reports replaying both controls.

The relations give \(w_n^2=x_ny_nx_ny_n^{-1}\).
The even integer \(E=720720/p\) is coprime to \(p\) and divisible
by every other occurring cycle length, so \(w_n^E\) is a single
nontrivial \(p\)-cycle. Thus the one fixed word
\[
 v_r=(xyxy^{-1})^{32760}\quad(p=11),\qquad
 v_r=(xyxy^{-1})^{27720}\quad(p=13)
\]
has nontrivial support at most \(13\) in all the selected alternating
images of the ordinary triangle group.
The lemma proves the result.
The presentation printed in the Notebook omits the final \(=1\);
that literal group also surjects onto \(\Gamma_r\), so the same
quotients answer it.
