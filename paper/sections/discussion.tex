\section{What the experiment shows}
\label{sec:discussion}

The experiment produced a substantial body of mathematical work in a
fixed time, and a second model was able to assess much of it by detailed
reading and independent reconstruction. Explicit small examples made
some arguments particularly accessible. Structural reductions and finite
certificates made other claims checkable despite a much larger search
behind them. The mathematical contribution of the run includes partial
theorems and useful deductions from prior work, as well as candidate
answers to the selected Notebook questions.

The review phase adds a different contribution. Claude confirmed many
arguments and computations, exposed gaps in what had been supplied to
readers, and prompted a stronger geometric example. Codex's response
also corrected mistaken objections. Preserving that exchange shows how
two different language models can contribute to mathematical scrutiny:
checking a result, challenging its hypotheses, consulting the original
source, and revising a judgment. The resulting evidence is stronger than
the producing agent's initial self-assessment.

The scope of the experiment remains specific. The agent chose its own
problems, so the 46 entries do not estimate a success rate on a random
sample of open questions. Shared inputs link several answers, and
prior exposure through training or literature is not fully observable.
Independent checking supports correctness of the claims examined;
discovery priority needs its own source assessment. The public archive
also has a stated limit for the largest certificate. These qualifications
accompany the mathematical statements and the review record without
changing their value as inspectable results.

For a further experiment, the main improvements would be a predetermined
comparison set, separate records for correctness and novelty, and
measurement of the time needed for independent review. Selected
formalizations could strengthen the most consequential reductions and
finite checks. Appendix~\ref{app:evaluation} develops these design and
measurement questions; the present paper supplies the arguments and
review history on which such work can build.
