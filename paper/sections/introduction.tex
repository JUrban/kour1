\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Kourovka Notebook~\cite{kourovka21} collects open problems in group
theory and neighboring areas, ranging from concrete questions about finite
groups to structural and logical problems about infinite ones. We present
a collection of arguments for its entries, developed in a 48-hour research
experiment and subsequently subjected to independent review by a second
language model. The work includes explicit counterexamples, structural
arguments, algorithms, finite certificates and deductions from earlier
theorems. We give the mathematics together with an account of its
development and review.

Michael Kinyon and Josef Urban conceived and oversaw the experiment.
They gave OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, running through Codex, access to GAP and
the literature and allowed it to choose which previously unsolved problems
to pursue. This setting tests a research process: selecting a useful
question, interpreting its hypotheses, finding a construction or reduction,
and deciding what has actually been proved.

The experiment has two phases. During 10--12 September 2026, Codex
selected problems, developed arguments and checked its own work. After
manuscript preparation, Anthropic's Claude Opus[1m], running through
Claude Code, independently reviewed the paper and then responded to the
authors' reply and supporting material. Claude examined all 46 entries
on the deadline candidate list, verified a majority in detail, and
independently rebuilt many of the finite constructions and calculations.
This substantial external review strengthens the evidence for the
mathematical results. Its corrections, confirmations and exchanges with
Codex form a second part of the experiment, described in
Section~\ref{sec:external-review}.

\subsection{Mathematical outcomes}

Table~\ref{tab:highlights} gives a selection of the results. Four complete
arguments appear in Section~\ref{sec:examples}; the remaining candidate
proofs, partial results and prior-work deductions follow in

a two-element definable set in the integral Heisenberg group to
constructions using geometric group theory and universal integer
identities for Chevalley groups. Their imported ingredients are credited
where they enter the proofs.

The number 46 records the problem entries classified as complete solution
candidates at the deadline. It counts specified subparts once per entry,
and some entries share a construction or a substantial imported theorem.
The inventory in Section~\ref{app:inventory} gives the covered parts and
convention-sensitive hypotheses. Subsequent review provides evidence of
correctness; novelty is a separate question. In particular, we acknowledge
contemporaneous work on 21.106, and retain known results and deductions
under their prior-work status. Several substantial partial theorems,
including the distinct-powers results, are outside the 46-entry count.

\subsection{Motivation and reading routes}

Urban's motivation for the shared time budget was the 2006/7
MPTPChallenge: attempt related problems and reuse discoveries across
them~\cite{mptpchallenge}. MaLARea pursued this approach through repeated
proving and learned premise selection~\cite{malarea-2015}. Here the agent
could also interpret informal statements, choose representations, consult
sources and abandon unproductive approaches. Given the breadth and rapid
development of learning-guided proving and LLM-based AI for mathematics,
we do not attempt a comprehensive related-work survey.

Mathematical readers can proceed from the review account to the four
representative proofs and use the inventory to find their areas of interest.
Readers interested in AI and automated reasoning will find the research
process in Section~\ref{sec:trajectory} and its implications in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}. Exact instructions, detailed chronology,
usage accounting and reproduction procedures are collected in the
appendices, followed by the change record and review correspondence.
