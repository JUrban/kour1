\section{Version 3: a withdrawn claim and a statement audit}
\label{app:statement-correction}

\subsection{Problem 10.35 and the correction}

On 19 September 2026, Evgeny Khukhro identified a statement error in
our claimed answer to 10.35. The supplied Notebook, page~40, asks whether
every finitely generated torsion-free subgroup of \(\GL_n(\C)\) is
residually in the class of torsion-free subgroups of
\(\GL_n(\overline{\Q})\). Our transcription replaced
\(\overline{\Q}\), the algebraic numbers, by \(\Q\).
The closure bar is visible in the PDF but absent from its extracted text.

The proposed example cannot refute the printed assertion. Its generators
\[
 U(1)=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T=\operatorname{diag}(2i,2)
\]
lie in \(\GL_2(\Q(i))\). The generated group is torsion-free,
as proved in Section~\ref{cand:10.35}, and already embeds in a permitted
target. Its inclusion therefore separates every nonidentity element.
The matrix lemma used for the rational-field obstruction assumes that
the field contains no square root of \(-1\); it does not apply over
\(\overline{\Q}\).

We withdraw the claimed resolution and retain the valid result over
\(\Q\) and \(\R\) as a separate observation. This is not a partial
solution of 10.35. The elementary fact that a nonscalar two-dimensional
matrix has a commutative centralizer algebra remains valid over any
field. Its use in the characteristic-two construction for 20.90 is
unaffected; that argument does not use the failed application to 10.35.

The error passed two stages of review. Codex's original statement audit
incorrectly asserted that the supplied page had been inspected and that
neither field had a closure bar. Claude's report subsequently accepted
the 10.35 entry and checked its matrix lemma. Verifying the lemma did
not verify that its field hypothesis matched the question. The reports
remain reproduced as historical documents, with this correction taking
precedence over their acceptance of the entry.

\subsection{What was rechecked}

Following the correction, Codex inspected rendered excerpts for all
46 historical candidate statements and compared their hypotheses,
quantifiers and counted subparts with the manuscript's stated scope.
The record in \artifact{paper/reviews/v3-2026-09-19/} contains a separate
assessment for each entry, the source PDF hash, page coordinates and
the rendered statements. It preserves the v2 source baseline and PDF,
and the original erroneous audit as evidence of the review failure.

This pass found no further transcription mismatch requiring withdrawal.
Existing scope qualifications remain explicit. For example, 16.28(a)
allows a reducible closed set and a field of positive characteristic
with a transcendental element; 15.65 defines separability by the minimal
polynomial; 18.92 defines joins within the chosen family of formations;
and 20.92(a) specifies a particular brace average. Convention-dependent
entries, including 9.4, 13.42, 14.26 and 16.20, retain their cited
definitions and stated limits. The audit is a check of statement fidelity,
not a new verification of every proof, its imported theorems or its novelty.

Khukhro described his own reading as cursory and proposed listing the
submissions as unverified AI solutions. We credit the specific correction;
we do not treat that communication as human specialist acceptance of
the remaining portfolio. It also does not change the separately reported
specialist confirmations of alternative papers discussed in Appendix~\ref{app:contemporary-comparison}.

\subsection{Counts and interpretation}

The frozen ledger retains 46 candidates submitted at the deadline.
The current assessment is one withdrawn claim and 45 remaining candidates.
These are distinct from counts of correct new theorems or independently
discovered solutions. The eleven contemporary overlaps studied in
version~2 remain credited; 10.35 is not among them. The abstract,
introduction, inventory and review discussion now state the withdrawal.
Appendix~\ref{app:review-changes} records the exact v3 changes.

The correction limits the conclusions drawn from model agreement.
The independently reconstructed calculations remain evidence for their
stated claims, but neither their success nor agreement between models
ensures that the original problem was transcribed correctly. A future
evaluation should record statement fidelity, proof checking and novelty
separately, with the original typeset question available at each stage.
