\begin{abstract}
We report an experiment in mathematical research and independent review
by two language models. OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, running through Codex, had
48 hours to investigate questions listed as open in the supplied Kourovka
Notebook, using GAP, exact programs and mathematical literature. The
resulting archive records 46 entries classified as complete solution
candidates at the deadline, together with partial theorems, rediscoveries
and bounded searches. Version~3 withdrew one claimed resolution after
the Notebook's editor identified a misread field, leaving 45 candidates.
We present explicit counterexamples, structural arguments and
finite certificates, with their hypotheses and imported ingredients.
We compare eleven entries with contemporary solutions, identifying shared
arguments, different constructions and stronger alternatives.
Anthropic's Claude Opus[1m], running through Claude Code, subsequently
reviewed all 46 entries, reported verifying a majority in detail, and independently
reconstructed many finite examples and calculations. Its two reports
and the exchange with Codex confirmed substantial portions of the work,
corrected interpretations and attribution, and led to a stronger
counterexample with a globally principal fixed divisor. Both model reviews
missed the field error; its correction illustrates the need to check
problem transcription separately from the resulting argument. The paper
presents both the mathematical portfolio and the research-and-review
process, with exact records and verbatim correspondence in the appendices.
Version~4 also supplies a mathematical edition using the same statements,
proofs and attribution notes.
\end{abstract}
